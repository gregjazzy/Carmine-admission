The North Carolina residency rule

Before any question of grades or essays, NCSSM applies a legal condition. The school is part of the University of North Carolina System, and state law, G.S. 116-235(b)(1), allows only bona fide legal residents of North Carolina to attend. Non-residents are ineligible, however strong.

Who counts as a resident

  • A student whose custodial parent or legal guardian is a legal resident of North Carolina, as defined by state law.
  • A student whose parent is an active-duty member of the armed services, living in North Carolina because of that service when the application is made.

Board of Trustees policy adds a date: the custodial parent or guardian must have established permanent legal residency in North Carolina by 1 December of the school year in which the student applies. A family moving into the state in January of the application year is too late for that cycle.

The RDS process

Every applicant to the Residential program, in Durham or Morganton, or to NCSSM Online must complete the North Carolina Residency Determination Service process at ncresidency.org. Applicants to Summer Ventures alone do not. For the Class of 2029, the RDS application is due by 30 January 2027, and it uses the same login as the CFNC account used for the NCSSM application. Without an RDS number, the NCSSM application on CFNC cannot be completed.

The questions, answered by the student and the parents, serve only to verify legal residence. The system returns an in-state or out-of-state determination.

If the answer is "out of state"

  1. Request a reconsideration from RDS.
  2. If needed, file an RDS appeal, and then an appeal to the State Education Assistance Authority.
  3. Once those appeals are exhausted, the family can appeal in writing to NCSSM within ten days of the decision.

Families with complex situations, such as separated parents in different states, a recent move, or a military posting, should start the process early so that there is time for these steps.

Why it matters so much

NCSSM costs families nothing for tuition, room, board or textbooks, and its Residential graduates can benefit from guaranteed UNC System admission and a UNC tuition grant. All of this is funded by the state for its residents. The residency rule is the condition on which everything else rests.
