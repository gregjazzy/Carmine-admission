The Best Primary Schools in Paris, and the One That Suits Your Child

Parents looking for the best primary school in Paris are right to think about it early: the school sets the language in which a child learns to read and write, their pace in mathematics, and often the collège (middle school) that follows. But the best school is only the best if it suits the child. Every year I help families with this choice, and I meet many of those children again when they enter the leading collèges.

The leading private schools in Paris, and what they are really worth

Families aim first for École alsacienne, Jeannine Manuel, Saint-Jean-de-Passy, Franklin, Stanislas or Sévigné: private schools under contract with the French state, which take children from nursery and, for most of them, keep them through to the baccalaureate. Their real doors open early, in nursery and in CP (the first year of primary, age 6); after that, places come almost only from children leaving.

A primary school is chosen for what children learn there, and for what it opens up afterwards. Behind comparable reputations, these schools do not shape the same children. One gives a six-year-old time to find their feet; another expects them to keep up from day one. Here, a child genuinely learns to read and write in two languages; there, English is limited to a few hours a week. The level reached by the end of CM2, in French as in mathematics, varies a great deal from one school to another, whatever the fees. And above all, their pupils do not get into the same collèges: some schools lead naturally to the most selective, others far less than they suggest.

None of this shows on a school visit. I know it because I follow these children: I have pupils in most of these schools, and I see every year which leading collèges they get into. That is what allows me to help each family find, among the best schools, the one that suits their child.

Example: the application rounds for September 2027. Saint-Jean-de-Passy, petite section and CP to CM2 from 15 September 2026. Franklin, CP to CE2 from 15 September 2026. École alsacienne, a round of about eight weeks, from November to early January. Jeannine Manuel, closing on 12 January 2027 for families living in France. These dates change every year: check each school's website from the end of the summer.

The most sought-after schools open their applications in the autumn of the year before. Tell me your child's age and the school you are aiming for: I will tell you when to apply, and how to prepare.

What is at stake at each age

In nursery and CP, the leading schools open most of their places: École alsacienne in moyenne section (age 4) and CP, Jeannine Manuel in moyenne section, grande section (age 5) and CP. At this age, the child is observed in a small group and it is mainly the parents who are met. Jeannine Manuel asks for a speech and language assessment for grande section and CP, and CP is the last year in which it requires no English.

From CE1 to CM2 (ages 7 to 10), places are rare and depend on children leaving. École alsacienne is the exception, with a real door in CM1, the only primary year in which it sets a written test. Jeannine Manuel does not accept beginners in English from CE1 onwards.

Age counts by calendar year: in France, a child enters CP in September of the year they turn six, whatever their month of birth. Several international schools place children differently, so the same child born late in the year can end up a year higher or lower depending on the school.

Entry to CP or CM1 at a selective school needs preparing. Have your child assessed in French, mathematics and English before you choose.

Other routes: state schools, international schools, the IB

State primary schools are free and zoned: the town hall of your arrondissement assigns the school for your address. Paris has eight international sections at primary level (German, Arabic, Chinese, Italian, Japanese, Russian), none in English. A child who does not speak French joins the class for their age in nursery, and a reception class from age six.

English-speaking and international schools (American School of Paris, British School of Paris, Marymount, International School of Paris, ICS Paris) keep the child in an English-language track; only ICS Paris and the International School of Paris offer the IB Primary Years Programme. Their cost is in another league: in 2026-2027, from €25,000 to €35,000 a year, plus entry fees, against €10,260 at Jeannine Manuel, which is under contract with the state.

The choice above all commits the language of writing. A child who learns to read and write in English will have a gap in written French to close if they later join a leading French collège, and that is precisely what their entrance tests assess.
