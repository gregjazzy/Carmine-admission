How to Prepare for the Jeannine Manuel Entry Test

Photo: École Jeannine Manuel

EJM Admission February 2026 · updated November 2026 5 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. Has worked with students of École Jeannine Manuel for more than ten years.

Book a conversation

École Jeannine Manuel's assessments cannot be prepared in three weeks. What makes the difference is not revising a syllabus, but giving the child time to change the way they work. Every year I prepare children for these assessments, and for more than ten years I have followed students of the school: here is how I organise preparation over time, month by month.

Why time matters more than anything

Most candidates who fail are not weak students. They often come from another system, American, British or IB, and have what it takes to succeed. What they lack is method: proving in mathematics rather than only calculating, writing French with the expected rigour, moving from conversational English to written English. These habits take months to build, not weeks.

Preparing for the assessments

A programme built around your child's assessment date, starting with a measure of what they are missing: that is the work I do with every family. Let's start with a first conversation.

When to start

Six months before the assessment is comfortable; three months is the minimum, and only for a child already solid in all three subjects. Since applications close in mid-January for families living in France, and the assessments follow the pre-selection of files, preparation should start by September at the latest, and over the summer if the gap is large.

Six months before the test is the time to know where your child stands. Ask for an assessment: three tests at the school's level, in French, English and mathematics.

The plan, month by month

Months 1 and 2: measure and lay the foundations

Everything starts with an assessment in all three subjects, to see exactly where the gaps are. The programme is then built on them, and only on them. In mathematics, the first work is on the French method; in French and English, on writing.

Months 3 and 4: work in depth

This is the longest phase. Regular exercises in each subject, corrected and reworked, until the new method becomes natural. Timed work is introduced step by step.

Proofs, notation, writing it up the French way: I teach the maths myself. Tell me about your child's level.

Months 5 and 6: practise under real conditions

Full mock assessments, in the conditions of the day, then a review of every paper: what keeps coming back, what costs time, where to start. The child also learns not to get stuck on one exercise.

Preparing for the Jeannine Manuel tests

I prepare each child for the maths, French and English papers and for the interview, with a programme built around the date of the test.

Mental preparation

It is rarely discussed, yet it is a real cause of failure. A child facing an unfamiliar place, an unfamiliar format and high stakes can lose part of their means, even when ready. Mock assessments serve that purpose too: on the day, the exercise is no longer a surprise.

What I do for your family

Every year, I see the vast majority of applicants leave without a place. My job is to do everything I can so that your child is not one of them, starting early enough. What the school says about its assessments by age is gathered in the Jeannine Manuel entry test guide.
