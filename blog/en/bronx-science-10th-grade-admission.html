Entering Bronx Science in 10th grade

The Bronx High School of Science, New York. Photo Bxsstudent, Wikimedia Commons, public domain

Most students enter Bronx Science in 9th grade, through the SHSAT taken in 8th grade. But the test is open a second time to students in their first year of 9th grade, for entry into 10th grade. Bronx Science describes this entry as "a handful" of students a year.

The rules
WhoNew York City residents in 9th grade for the first time (not repeating)
RegistrationMySchools, 6 to 30 October 2026
TestSaturday 21 November 2026, at a central site; an alternative date may be possible
FormatThe same SHSAT: English and math, computer-adaptive, 180 minutes
OffersBy score and ranked list, as in 8th grade

Who it suits

  • Students new to New York who arrived after the 8th grade test.
  • Students who sat the test in 8th grade and fell short, and have progressed since.
  • Students who did not test in 8th grade and now want a specialized high school.

A student already admitted to a specialized high school would have little reason to sit again, unless aiming at a different school on the list.

A second attempt, better prepared

For 9th graders, I fit SHSAT preparation around a full high school workload, with priority on the section that held the score back in 8th grade.

Weighing the odds

With about 750 places in 9th grade and only a handful in 10th, the second attempt is far narrower than the first. A family should treat it as a real but limited chance, and keep the student fully engaged in their current high school, whose grades will matter for college whatever happens. If an offer does come, in March, the student joins a class in September that has already spent a year together, including the research course every Bronx Science 9th grader takes.

Is your child in 9th grade and thinking of Bronx Science? Let's look at the numbers together.

Preparing in 9th grade

A 9th grader has a year more mathematics and reading than an 8th grader, which helps on content. The constraint is time: preparation must fit around homework and activities. The best plan starts over the summer before 9th grade, and keeps the autumn for timed practice.

My approach

I have students at Bronx Science, and for seven years I have worked with around fifteen families a year aiming at the most selective schools. For a 10th grade attempt, I begin with the 8th grade score report if there is one, and target the points that were missing.
