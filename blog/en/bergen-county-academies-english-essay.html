Book a conversation

BCA asks for two pieces of writing, and families often confuse them. The first is the personal statement in the online application, written at home before 8 November. The second is the essay written under test conditions in late November. This article is about the second: 45 minutes, a passage read on screen, and an argument about it.

The six criteria

CriterionWhat it means in practice
ComprehensionThe essay shows that the student has understood the passage, including what is implied
InsightA clear thesis: one arguable idea, stated early
OrganisationParagraphs built properly and in a logical order
SupportRelevant facts and details from the passage back the thesis
StyleA clear, mature voice
Grammar and spellingCorrect, careful English

No notes, phones, smart watches or other devices are allowed.

Why good readers lose marks

Many strong students read well and still write a weak test essay. The most common reason is the absence of a thesis: the essay retells the passage, or lists observations, without arguing anything. The second is support: quoting the text loosely, or not at all, rather than choosing the details that prove the point. Both come from a lack of practice under time, not from a lack of ability.

A thesis in the first five minutes

I teach students a method for the 45 minutes: read with a question in mind, commit to a thesis, plan three paragraphs, and leave time to reread.

Book a conversation

Using the 45 minutes

  • About 10 minutes: read the passage, then reread it looking for what it suggests rather than what it says.
  • About 5 minutes: write the thesis in one sentence and choose the evidence for each paragraph.
  • About 25 minutes: write.
  • The last 5 minutes: reread for grammar and spelling, which are scored.

The other essay

The application statement is different: in 400 words or fewer, the student explains who they are and why they want each academy they have chosen, then lists activities and awards in up to 100 words. Anything beyond 500 words is not read. BCA asks applicants to write it as independently as possible.
