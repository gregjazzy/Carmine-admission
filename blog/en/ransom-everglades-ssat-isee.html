Choosing the date

Because the ISEE allows one sitting per season, a first attempt in October or November keeps a second available in December or January. A first attempt in January leaves no second chance within the cycle. The SSAT is more flexible, but the same logic applies: a first sitting in the autumn, a review of the score report, then targeted work before a second. Scores must arrive in time for the checklist, so the last sitting should come early enough for results to be released and sent.

Which test suits your child

Both tests measure verbal and quantitative reasoning, reading and mathematics, and include an unscored writing sample sent to schools. They differ in question style and timing. A practice test of each in the early autumn usually shows which one a student is more comfortable with. If other schools on the family's list accept both, one well-prepared test can serve every application.
