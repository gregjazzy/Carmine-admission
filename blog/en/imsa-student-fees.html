IMSA is a public school funded through the Illinois General Assembly, built on the principle that any student, whatever their family's resources, should be able to attend. For Illinois residents, the state covers tuition, housing and meals. What families pay is a student fee set by income.

What an Illinois family pays, Class of 2029 schedule

ItemAmountWhen
Application$0With the application
Seat reservation fee$250, non-refundableOn acceptance
One-day orientation$65 per person, one parent per studentJune before entry

Over three years, the total runs from about $2,259 to $22,987, depending on the family's tier.

How the fee is set

The Student/Class Fee is assessed on a six-tier graduated scale based on gross family income and household size, using a formula derived from the federal lunch programme. Families eligible for free or reduced-price lunch can have certain fees waived, including the seat reservation fee. A fee reduction application becomes available in June. Most importantly, no Illinois resident is refused admission because of inability to pay the fees. Eligible students can also borrow a laptop at no cost.

Free for most, selective for all

Because cost is rarely the barrier for Illinois families, the competition is academic. I prepare applicants for the SAT or ACT, the essays and the activities section.

Out-of-state students

IMSA admits a small number of tuition-paying students from outside Illinois. They should expect to cover annual tuition, room and board of about $55,000 to $57,000. They follow an earlier calendar, with a 15 January deadline and decisions on 8 March.

What families still budget for

Beyond fees, families of residential students budget for travel between home and Aurora, personal spending, and a laptop if they buy one rather than borrow. Admitted students must attend orientation in June with at least one parent, and some are invited to EXCEL, a three-week summer programme in July, depending on their placement test.
