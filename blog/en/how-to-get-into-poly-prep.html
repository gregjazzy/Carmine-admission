How to get into Poly Prep

Poly Prep Country Day School teaches children from Nursery to Grade 12 on two Brooklyn campuses, the Lower School in Park Slope and the Middle and Upper Schools on a 25-acre campus in Dyker Heights. It accepts applications to every grade, but four grades carry most of its intake, and that distinction shapes the whole process: in those grades every applicant is interviewed, in the others only some are.

I work with families applying to Poly Prep and to the other ISAAGNY schools of New York. Here is how admission works for 2027-28.

In the main entry years, every applicant who submits the preliminary application on time is interviewed in person. In the other years, interviews are by invitation, chosen from the preliminary applications. A child may apply to only one grade in a given cycle.

Two stages

The application runs on Ravenna. The preliminary application comes first: November 6, 2026 for most grades, November 20 for Grades 5, 6 and 9. The supporting materials follow by January 8, 2027: the ISAAGNY forms shared by New York independent schools, teacher recommendations requested after November 1, transcripts and, from Grade 2, test scores. Poly Prep accepts the SSAT or the ISEE with no preference and superscores repeated attempts. Decisions follow the ISAAGNY calendar in February.

What the school reads

Poly Prep calls its review holistic: test results are read alongside transcripts, essays, recommendations and the interview. Families can also add videos or photos of a serious interest, in athletics, performance, debate, art, music or dance. For siblings and children of alumni, the criteria are the same, and admission is not guaranteed.

Cost and aid

Tuition and fees for 2026-27 run from $39,478 for a half-day Nursery to $71,852 in Grade 12, with busing, lunch and grade trips included. More than 24 percent of students receive need-based aid, totalling over $14.5 million; there are no merit scholarships, and the aid application is due December 15.
