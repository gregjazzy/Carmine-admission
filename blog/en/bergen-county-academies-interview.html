The interview is Phase 2 of BCA admission, and not every applicant reaches it. The committee first reviews the application, the test and the school record; by late February, every applicant can see on the studentrecs.bergen.org portal whether they continue. No status is ever given by phone.

What Phase 2 involves

AcademyPhase 2
All academiesAn individual interview with a team of teachers and/or guidance counsellors
AVPA MusicInterview and an audition
AVPA TheatreInterview and an audition
AVPA Visual ArtsInterview and a visual arts workshop, with a portfolio

Details of auditions and portfolios are sent only to those invited, through the portal, and change every year. Students who asked to be considered for another district school may be interviewed there before February; their BCA application continues in parallel.

What the interview is for

BCA describes the interview as a forum for its faculty to meet prospective students and learn about their interests. Since the academy is chosen at application and cannot be changed, the conversation naturally turns on why this academy: what the student has done, read, built or explored in its field, and what they hope to do there.

Interest you can show

I help students talk clearly and honestly about what draws them to their academy, with examples from their own work, not memorised lines.

The pledge applies here too

Applicants pledge not to discuss their interview questions with anyone, just as for the test. Lists of supposed BCA interview questions found online should be read with that in mind.

Mid-April, and the wait list

Only interviewed students receive an updated admissions status, in mid-April. Those interviewed but not accepted are placed on the wait list. It has no ranking, and a place opens only when an accepted student declines; the committee then looks for a qualified candidate from the same district for the same academy. BCA cannot predict if or when a student will be called.

Then Phase 3

An accepted student moves to Phase 3: a declaration of intent with the required documents, and placement tests in mathematics and in a world language. These do not decide admission; they set the level at which the student starts in 9th grade. A student who prepared well for the entrance test usually has little to add, apart from reviewing the language studied in middle school.
