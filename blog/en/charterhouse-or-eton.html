Charterhouse or Eton

Charterhouse, Godalming, from the air. Photo Tim Sheerman-Chase, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 4.0

Charterhouse and Eton often appear together on a family's list, and the overlap is real: both read the ISEB Common Pre-Test in the autumn of Year 6, both invite a selection of candidates to a second stage in the spring, and both make their 13+ offers conditional on what happens next. Around that shared test, though, the two processes are built differently, and the differences decide how a family should plan.

The calendar trap

Eton's registration closes at the end of Year 5, a full year before Charterhouse's recommended deadline. A family that discovers Eton in Year 6 has missed it at 13+. Families considering both should register for Eton in Year 5 and for Charterhouse by 30 September of Year 6; the Pre-Test, sat once, is shared with both schools.

Two different second stages

At Eton, the second stage is individual: a one-to-one interview and an online test of academic ability and potential. At Charterhouse, it is collective as much as individual: group activities, friendly interviews and a piece of creative writing, designed to see the complete child. A boy who shines alone may find the Charterhouse group tasks unfamiliar; a child who is lively in a group may find Eton's online test the harder part. Preparing for both means practising both.

What confirms the place

Both schools make offers two years before entry, and both keep a check in Year 8. At Charterhouse, the check is a positive reference, for work and behaviour, from the child's school during Year 8. At Eton, the conditional place is confirmed through the King's Scholarship, Common Entrance or the Eton Entrance assessment. The Eton route therefore keeps an academic examination at the end; the Charterhouse route relies on the school's judgement of how the child has continued.

Beyond 13+

Charterhouse also admits at 11+, through its prep schools, at 14+ with around 20 Removes, and at 16+ with around 70 Specialists. Eton's other main entry is the Sixth Form. A family whose child is not ready at 13 has more doors at Charterhouse.
