The Best High Schools in Paris, and the One That Suits Your Child

Parents looking for the best lycée (high school) in Paris are right to aim high: the lycée decides which schools and universities will open up after the baccalaureate. But a leading lycée is only the best for a child if it suits them. I always have students in most of these lycées, and every year I prepare children to get into the most selective of them. Their applications start in September of the year before entry and close in the spring: it pays to know early which ones to aim for.

The leading lycées in Paris, and what they are really worth

The Paris lycées that come top of the official baccalaureate results:

  • State: Louis-le-Grand, Henri-IV, Lavoisier, Charlemagne, Fénelon, Jean-Baptiste-Say, Condorcet.
  • Private under contract: Stanislas, Franklin (Saint-Louis-de-Gonzague), Jeannine Manuel, La Tour, Blomet, Notre-Dame de Sion, Fénelon Sainte-Marie, the École internationale bilingue.

Based on the lycée results indicators published by the French ministry of education (2025 baccalaureate). The detail, school by school, is in Best high schools in France.

A lycée is chosen for the quality of its teaching, and for the doors it opens after the baccalaureate. Published rankings stop at the bac, and the honours rates of the leading lycées sit within a few points of each other. With the same honours rate, one sends most of its students to the French classes préparatoires and grandes écoles, while another also knows how to get them into London, Lausanne or American universities. One helps a strong student progress without crushing them; another wears down those who are not at the top. The international sections and bilingualism on display do not mean the same thing everywhere. And an average does not carry the same weight from one lycée to another: prépa admissions panels and universities abroad know it, families much less so.

None of this is in the brochures. I know it from the inside: I always have students in these lycées, I have had many before them, I often know their teachers, heads of year and sometimes head teachers, and I see every year which schools and universities their students get into. That is what allows me to help each family find, among the best, the one that suits their child. The procedures are detailed school by school: Jeannine Manuel, Stanislas, Franklin, Sévigné, École alsacienne, and in the state sector, Henri-IV and Louis-le-Grand.

Applications to the leading lycées open from September of the year before. Tell me which year you are aiming for: I will help you keep the dates and prepare the tests and interviews.

How to get into a leading Paris lycée

  • Private schools under contract choose their students. You apply directly to the school, often very early, then confirm the place in Affelnet, the online allocation system: the head remains free to accept or not.
  • Henri-IV and Louis-le-Grand have two routes. A pupil in troisième (year 9) at a Paris collège lists them in Affelnet as first choices. Everyone else submits a file on a dedicated platform that opens in January and closes in early April for seconde, late April for première and terminale. The stated criteria: academic excellence, commitment and motivation.
  • International sections recruit on a file, a written test and an oral in the section's language, before mid-March.
  • The catchment state lycée goes through Affelnet in May: the score adds a catchment bonus, troisième marks and social bonuses.

Other routes: international sections and the IB

International sections lead to the French international baccalaureate (BFI), a full French bac about 40% of whose mark comes from section-specific subjects. In the state sector they are free apart from the section association's contribution: in English, Camille-Sée (15th arrondissement), Honoré-de-Balzac (17th) and Maurice-Ravel (20th); outside Paris, the Lycée international de Saint-Germain-en-Laye. In private schools under contract, Jeannine Manuel, the École active bilingue Étoile and Sévigné offer a BFI track.

The IB Diploma is only taught in private schools, for a teenager schooled in English who will study abroad. In 2026-2027 it costs from €29,000 to €41,400 a year depending on the school, plus entry fees, against €10,275 for Jeannine Manuel's BFI lycée. I compare the two diplomas in IB or French baccalaureate.

Arriving in première or terminale

A student from a foreign school not recognised by France sits tests that decide their year group. The bac is then prepared over two years: continuous assessment in première and terminale counts for 40% of the mark, and the early exams at the end of première, French and now maths, are added for a student who arrives directly in terminale. All première marks count in Parcoursup. Arriving in terminale is possible, but it is the heaviest year in which to do it.

Is your child arriving from a foreign school and facing placement tests? Have them assessed first: three tests in French, English and mathematics, and a plan to close the gaps.
