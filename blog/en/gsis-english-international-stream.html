The English International Stream is the half of German Swiss International School that most international families apply to, and the school describes it as very selective. Every applicant must pass all parts of the relevant entrance assessments, and English is expected to match that of a native speaker of the same age. German is not required for entry, but every EIS student learns it.

I prepare children for the EIS at every entry point. Here is what the stream asks and offers.

The assessments by entry point

KG1 and Year 1 children are assessed individually within a group. From Year 2 there are written English and mathematics papers and a verbal English reading comprehension; from Year 3 to Year 6, a computer-based CAT4 comes first. Years 7 to 10 sit the CAT4 and written English and mathematics. Year 12 applicants are interviewed, with transcripts and a school recommendation. From Year 2, a confidential recommendation form from the current school is required, and no place is offered without it. Papers are confidential and no samples are published.

Invitations to assess follow admissions priority and are not guaranteed in a long waitlist; an uninvited child keeps the original application date. A child who does not pass is deferred for at least two years.

Languages in the EIS

German is the first foreign language from Year 2 to Year 11, which gives EIS students a sustained exposure to German that few international schools offer. Mandarin is a second foreign language in Kindergarten and from Year 4, and French is available from Year 7. Once enrolled, a student cannot switch to the German stream; during the application, the family can still transfer between streams.

What native-level English means for families

For many applicants the deciding factor is English. A child who has grown up with English at home and school usually meets the bar; a child who speaks English well but has been taught mainly in another language often does not yet, especially in reading aloud and in writing. Building that level takes months, sometimes years, which is why the planning of an EIS application should start with an honest look at the child's English.
