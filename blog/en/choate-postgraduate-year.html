A postgraduate year at Choate

A postgraduate year is a thirteenth year of school, taken after high school graduation and before college. Choate admits about fifteen to twenty postgraduates a year; there are seventeen this year, beside 230 returning seniors. They follow an individual program, earn a completion certificate, walk at graduation with the senior class and become Choate alumni.

Who it suits

The reasons are listed plainly: more academic preparation, more time for activities, a year of growing up in a residential setting, or more time to develop as an athlete and be recruited. In practice, the students I see considering a PG year fall into a few groups.

  • The student who graduated young, or feels young, and wants one more year before college.
  • The student whose record does not yet match their ability, and who needs a year of demanding work in a school colleges know well.
  • The athlete who needs another season in front of college coaches.
  • The student from another system who wants a year inside American education before applying to American universities.

For athletes there is one hard limit. Choate sets no age limit for PG applicants, but NEPSAC rules do not allow an athlete to turn twenty before or during a playing season. A family planning a PG year for sport should check the birth date against the season before anything else.

A year that has to change the file

A PG year is worth its cost only if it changes what colleges will see. I work with the student on the Choate application, then on the college applications that follow, at the level of the most selective universities.

What the year looks like

PG students take five courses each term, chosen with the input of their college counselor and their dean, according to their interests. Each one works individually with an assigned college counselor, and the work starts before arrival, with a webinar in June. They live in the senior dorms, usually sharing a room with another PG, and they have the same permissions and traditions as the seniors.

The catalogue is wide: more than 300 courses, mathematics beyond Calculus BC (linear algebra, multivariable calculus, differential equations), and honors electives across history, economics, philosophy and the sciences. A PG who arrives with a clear idea of what they want to show colleges can build a year around it.

The calendar problem

The PG year is short, and the college process does not wait for it. A student who arrives in September applies to college in the fall of that same year. That means the first trimester at Choate is the one colleges will see, and the Choate counselor will have known the student for only a few weeks when the applications go out. The PG year has to be prepared before it begins: the courses, the tests, the list of colleges.

How the application differs

The file is the same as for other grades, with two differences. Teacher recommendations may come from current or previous-year teachers, which matters for a student who has already graduated. And the accepted tests are the PSAT, SAT or ACT, dated from June two years before the application. Testing is optional, as for every grade. For a non-native speaker, a TOEFL, IELTS or Duolingo is strongly recommended.

The deadlines are those of every applicant: a complete file by January 15, decisions on March 10.

Where Choate students go

Over the last five classes, the most frequent destinations included the University of Chicago, NYU, Columbia, Cornell, Yale and Georgetown, with smaller numbers at Harvard, Princeton, MIT and Stanford, and 22 students at St Andrews. Six went to West Point. A PG shares those counselors and that network for one year, which is the real point of the year for many families.

How I work with PG candidates

I have students at Choate, and for seven years I have worked with around fifteen families a year aiming at the most selective schools and universities. With a PG candidate, I start from the end: what the college file lacks today, and whether a year at Choate can supply it. If it can, I prepare the Choate application, the interview and the essays, then the SAT or ACT and the college applications, so that the one year is used in full.
