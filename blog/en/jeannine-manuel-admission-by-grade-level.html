Jeannine Manuel Admission in 6eme, 4eme, or 2nde: Key Differences

Photo: École Jeannine Manuel

EJM Admission January 2026 · updated November 2026 5 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. Has worked with students of École Jeannine Manuel for more than ten years.

Book a conversation

The families who contact me almost always ask the same question: when should we try for Jeannine Manuel? For more than ten years, I have worked with students of the school, from their admission all the way to higher education, and every year I prepare children to get in. The answer depends on the level, because the school does not recruit the same way in sixième (the first year of middle school, age 11), quatrième (age 13) and seconde (the first year of lycée, age 15).

Three levels, three situations

Sixième and seconde are entry years: the school opens whole classes there, in middle school and then in lycée. Quatrième is not one. As in cinquième and troisième, a place only exists if a student leaves, and the number changes from one year to the next.

The procedure is the same at every level: online application, application fee, documents, pre-selection, then an assessment and an interview for the applications that are retained. I go through it in detail in the complete guide to getting into Jeannine Manuel. What changes from one level to the next is the number of places, the requirements and the way to prepare.

Preparing for entry to Jeannine Manuel

The right entry level, a complete file on time, a child prepared for the tests and the interview: that is the work I do with every family. Let's start with a first conversation about your child.

Entry in sixième

This is the main door into middle school. All students are in the American international section, and the school asks for a solid level of English, written and spoken. French is not required: a child who does not speak it yet can join through the adaptation programme.

After a pre-selection on the file, the school meets the candidates and their parents, and asks them to sit additional assessments. Decisions arrive by email in March, earlier than for the other middle-school years.

The application opens in the autumn of CM2, the last year of primary school, with the school reports from CE2, CM1 and the current year. A weakness in mathematics or written English spotted at the start of CM2 can be worked on; spotted in January, it is too late.

Is your child aiming for sixième? I give them three tests at the school's level, in French, English and mathematics, to see where they stand. Ask for an assessment.

Entry in quatrième

In quatrième, the classes have existed for two years. A place only opens when a student leaves, and some years there are very few. After pre-selection, candidates may sit one or more written tests; only then are some of them invited in with their parents. Decisions arrive in April.

Many families try for quatrième by default, because sixième slipped past them. An application in quatrième only makes sense if it is prepared for that level, and if the child has other schools ready to take them on the day no place opens up.

Entry in seconde

Seconde is the main door into lycée. It leads to two programmes for première and terminale, both to be followed at the school: the French International Baccalaureate (BFI), in the American section with Advanced Placement courses, or the International Baccalaureate (IB). The school requires near-native English, and for the IB, a sufficient command of French, which remains a compulsory subject there. There is no entry at all afterwards into terminale, the final year.

The choice between the two tracks also shapes the budget. In 2026-2027, lycée fees in the BFI track are €10,275 a year; in the IB track, the seconde year (Foundation year) costs €24,865, then première and terminale €32,560 a year. A first-time registration fee of €1,650 is added.

The application includes a motivation letter from the student. After pre-selection, the school meets some candidates with their parents and gives them assessments. Decisions arrive in April. In mathematics, the level of the school's own troisième students is high; I know it well, since I work with its top students.

In seconde, mathematics, French and English must be at the right level from the first test. Tell me about your child's background and we will see together what still needs work.

My advice: apply as early as possible

I am often asked whether it is better to try for sixième, when places are plentiful, or to wait for seconde, when the BFI or the IB give the plan its full meaning. My answer does not change: as early as possible.

A child who joins in sixième has seven years ahead to find their pace in a demanding school. They reach lycée on familiar ground, with their friends, their work habits and an English shaped by years of lessons. A student who joins in seconde, however bright, has to catch up on everything: a group that has known each other for four years, and a very heavy workload. I have seen many succeed, but it is harder.

Waiting until a child is "more mature" is often a false good idea. Part of the maturity the school expects in seconde is something its own students have gained there.

What I do for your family, depending on the level

I always start with an assessment: three tests at the school's level, in French, English and mathematics, an interview with your child, a written report and a conversation with you. For sixième, I ideally do this at the start of CM2, then prepare your child for the assessments and the interview. For quatrième, I tell you frankly whether applying is worth it this year, and in parallel we prepare the backup schools, the ones that genuinely suit your child and that I know from the inside. For seconde, we choose between the BFI and the IB together, I work on the motivation letter with your child and prepare them for the interview.

Every year, I see the vast majority of applicants leave without a place. My job is to do everything I can so that your child is not one of them. The school publishes neither the format of its tests, nor their level, nor what is really at stake in the interview, nor the profiles it chooses. That is exactly what I know: ten years working every week with its students and preparing children for admission to Jeannine Manuel. I pass this on to every family I work with, so that their child arrives prepared, with the best possible chance of getting in.
