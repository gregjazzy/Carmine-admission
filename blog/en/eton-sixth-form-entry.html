Entering Eton in the Sixth Form

Most Etonians arrive at thirteen, but each year the school takes about twenty more boys at sixteen, straight into the Sixth Form, where they are known as Specialists. These fee-paying places are open to boys from UK independent schools, UK state schools and schools overseas. For boys who missed the 13+ route, or whose families only began to look at English boarding later, it is the last door, and one of the narrowest.

I have students at Eton and prepare candidates for this entry. What follows is the process and what it really asks of a boy.

Twenty places, and a minimum that is not the bar

The formal requirement is six GCSEs at grade 7, or the overseas equivalent. In practice, the level of successful candidates is well above that. Academic strength is not sufficient on its own: the school looks for boys likely to make a notable contribution to school life in sport or the arts. There is no bursary support on this route; the funded entry at sixteen is the Orwell Award, reserved for boys from UK state schools.

A boy with a string of 8s and 9s predicted and nothing else to show is therefore an ordinary candidate here. A boy with the same grades who plays in a serious orchestra, rows competitively or has led something real in his school is a different one.

A file worthy of Eton

At sixteen, Eton chooses twenty boys from a field of very strong applicants. I prepare candidates for the tests and the interview, and help shape a file that shows the boy as he really is at his best.

October: the written application

The form must be completed by early October of the year before entry, so in Year 11 for a boy at an English school. It asks for predicted GCSE grades (or their international equivalent) and the most recent set of school reports. It also asks a series of questions in the style of a personal statement, about why Eton and about the boy's interests and achievements outside the classroom.

These answers are the only place where the boy speaks for himself before the tests. Answers that list activities are common; answers that show what an activity has taught him, and what he would bring to a house, are much rarer and much more useful to the people reading them.

November: the assessments at Eton

In the second half of November, candidates come to Eton for an online cognitive test and tests in specific subjects. Those who are shortlisted then have an online pastoral interview, and Eton asks their school for a reference. A boy who wants to be considered for an honorary Music Award says so on the form and, if shortlisted, is auditioned by the Head of Music. Results are announced in early December.

For the subject tests, the useful preparation is depth in the subjects a boy intends to take at A level rather than a wider spread. The pastoral interview is about judgement more than knowledge: whether this boy, at sixteen, will settle into a house, take his share of responsibility and use what Eton offers.

Arriving in a house already formed

Each of Eton's twenty-five houses holds about fifty-five boys, around eleven per year group, most of whom have lived together since F Block. A Sixth Form entrant arrives with his own room like everyone else, but into friendships three years old. He also chooses, before his first Specialist Half, the Masters he would like as his Specialist Tutor, for weekly sessions held in the tutor's own home. Boys who make the most of these two years usually arrive knowing what they want from them.

The calendar

Applications for entry in September 2028 open in June 2027, when Eton also holds its Sixth Form tours; registration for 2027 entry is closed. The registration fee is £480. For boys at UK state schools, Orwell Award applications open on 1 July 2027, with assessments in November or December, and up to fourteen awards a year.
