At CDNIS, a successful assessment does not always mean an offer. When all places in a grade are taken, applicants who passed are placed in the waiting pool for the year they applied for. For a family, this is both good news and an uncomfortable wait.

How a child ends up in the pool

CDNIS screens in two stages. In Stage 1, the assessment or interview, school results and the reference form produce a ranking: Excellent, Good, Marginal or Not suitable. Applicants ranked Excellent or Good go on to Stage 2, where priority is given to debenture holders or potential holders, siblings of current students and Canadian citizens. Offers follow, subject to available seats. Applicants who passed but did not receive an offer go into the pool.

The rules of the pool

  • No guarantee. Placement from the pool is never guaranteed.
  • One year only. The pool applies to the academic year applied for. Applications and pool status do not carry over.
  • Reapplication. A family that still wants a place for a later year must submit a new application, with a new fee.
  • A small advantage next time. For Nursery and Early Years 1, a child who passed the assessment and was waitlisted the previous year is among those given priority for an assessment slot.

Why places open, or do not

Hong Kong international school places move with family relocations: some families leave during the summer or the school year, others arrive. A place may open between offer rounds or even after the school year starts, for students joining mid-year, who then pay prorated tuition and the full levy and entry fee. Priorities such as debentures, siblings and Canadian citizenship remain part of the picture.

What families can do

  • Keep the school informed of any change: a new address, new reports, a strong result.
  • Secure a good alternative place, so that the child's year is not on hold.
  • If the place does not come, prepare the next cycle early, remembering that deadlines for Early Years 1 to Grade 5 fall on 2 October.

When the pool becomes the strategy

For Grade 6 and above, where CDNIS considers applications after 31 December only if places remain, a family arriving in Hong Kong mid-year may find the pool the only realistic route.
