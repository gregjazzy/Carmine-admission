What sets Columbus Academy apart

Columbus, Ohio, home of Columbus Academy. Photo Kevin Payravi, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

US independent schools November 2026 5 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

Book a conversation

Families comparing independent schools in Central Ohio usually ask the same question: what does Columbus Academy offer that justifies its fees and its selection process? The answer lies partly in its model, the country day school it has followed since its founding in 1911, and partly in concrete choices about space, spending and support.

I help families weigh schools like this one. Here is what stands out.

A country day school

The country day model rests on the idea that children are best served when home and school work together, and Columbus Academy treats parents as integral members of its community. That shows in the admissions process itself: at every level there is a conversation with the parents, not just an assessment of the child. The school states its aim plainly: not to mould young minds but to light them on fire.

Values and community

The school names five core values: honesty, respect, compassion, responsibility and fairness. It sets out a commitment to diversity, inclusion, equity and justice as measures of its excellence, approved by its board in 2013, and backs it with its aid budget and the Malone programme. The stated aim is a student body drawn from a wider range of backgrounds than fees alone would allow.

What it means for an application

A school built on partnership with families pays attention to the parents as well as the child. The parent conversation at each level is a real part of the file, and so are the student's own pieces from sixth grade: the supplement, the interview and the shadow day. Families who arrive knowing what they want from the school, and able to say it, make the most of those conversations.
