Andover tuition and need-blind aid

A boarding year at Andover costs $79,800 in 2026-2027, and a day year $63,840. For many families, they are the least important figures on this page. Andover admits students without looking at whether they can pay, meets the full need of every family it admits, and does so with grants. Understanding how that need is calculated matters more than the price.

Need-blind, in practice

The admission committee decides without regard to the family's finances, and applying for aid does not affect the decision. Andover has run this policy for close to two decades, and it is one of very few independent schools to do so. The consequences are concrete: 47% of students receive aid, 12% are on full scholarship, and the school spends about $30 million a year on it. Loans have been replaced by grants, so students leave without school debt.

There is no income ceiling for a full scholarship. Because the applicant pool is large and spread across every income level, Andover does not set a threshold above which a family cannot receive full aid; each case is calculated.

The decision is made on the file

At Andover, no family buys a place and none is refused for its means. Everything rests on the file, and that is what I prepare, at the level of the most selective schools.

How the calculation treats a family

  • The home: the value of the family's home is not counted as an asset up to $650,000.
  • Renters: families who do not own a home receive a rental allowance in the calculation.
  • Special circumstances: each file is reviewed for concerns that the numbers alone do not show.
  • Both parents: Andover requires financial information from every living parent. Divorced or separated parents each complete their own application, and nothing is shared between them. A single parent provides proof of that status.

The method is Andover's own, applied by its financial aid office, and it looks at the family as a whole rather than at a single line of the tax return.

The rule that catches families out

A family that might ever need aid must apply in the first year. Andover asks every family considering current or future assistance to apply at admission, so that it can budget. A family that is comfortable today but could face a change in the coming years should think carefully before leaving the box unticked; applying carries no disadvantage at admission.

Small details cause delays every year: a birth date entered differently on two forms, a slightly different spelling of the child's name between the admission and aid files. They are worth checking before submitting.

Paying, and the small print

Charges not paid on time can carry a late fee of 1.5% a month. Families abroad can pay through Convera or Flywire, and a monthly plan is available through FACTS. The application fee is $60, or $125 for international applicants. Families applying for aid can request a waiver of the test fee through the Andover portal, but late registration charges for the SSAT or ISEE, which are substantial, are not covered.

Why the price is not the question

I have students at Andover, and for seven years I have worked with around fifteen families a year aiming at the most selective schools in the United States, England and Switzerland. At Andover the question is never whether the family can pay; the school has removed it. The question is whether the file earns the place, and that is where I work: the test, the graded paper, the essay, the video, the interview, each taken to the level of the pool.
