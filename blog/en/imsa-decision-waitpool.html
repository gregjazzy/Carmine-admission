IMSA has a fixed number of places, set by the number of rooms in its residence halls, typically about 200 to 250 a year. When the decisions arrive, applicants learn whether they are accepted, placed in the waitpool, or not admitted. The waitpool is the part families understand least, and it can remain open until the very start of the school year.

What accepted and waitpool students must do

  • Respond by the deadline, accepting or declining, to keep the place or the position in the waitpool.
  • Sit the placement test on one of the dates from 27 to 29 May 2027.
  • Complete the required registration forms in PowerSchool by 31 May.
  • Accepted students: attend orientation on 11 or 12 June with at least one parent; Illinois families pay the $250 seat reservation fee on acceptance.

Waitpool students take the placement test and complete forms like admitted students, so that they are ready if a place opens.

Ready if the call comes

For waitpool students, I prepare the placement test as seriously as for admitted ones, so that a late place can be taken up without a gap.

How long the waitpool lasts

Students can be called from the waitpool until convocation, at the start of the school year, which IMSA treats as the last day to pull students from it. A family may therefore hear in May, in July, or a few days before move-in. That makes a parallel plan essential: a confirmed place at the local high school, with the knowledge that it may change late.

If the answer is no

A student who applied in 8th grade and was not admitted can apply again in 9th grade, the last year of eligibility, and still enter IMSA as a sophomore. A stronger SAT or ACT score, a year of further math and science, and sharper essays can change the outcome.
