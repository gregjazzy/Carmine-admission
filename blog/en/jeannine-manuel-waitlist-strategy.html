Jeannine Manuel Waitlist: What to Do

Photo: École Jeannine Manuel

EJM Admission February 2026 · updated November 2026 5 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. Has worked with students of École Jeannine Manuel for more than ten years.

Book a conversation

École Jeannine Manuel has replied, and it is neither a yes nor a no: your child is on the waiting list. It is often the most uncomfortable moment of the whole application, and the one where families make their worst decisions. Every year, some of the families I prepare for the school receive this answer; here is how I advise them to get through the wait.

What the waiting list means

The school puts it simply: candidates on the waiting list are those who were not offered a place for lack of room. It is not a disguised rejection. It is the school that places a candidate on the list; families cannot ask to be put on it.

Places can open up. A family admitted elsewhere that chooses another school, a job transfer, a departure during the year: each withdrawal frees a place. The main decisions come at the end of March for CP and sixième, from mid to late April for the other year groups, and the school then keeps informing families as places come up.

But a strategy cannot be built on hope. While you wait, you have to hold two fronts at once: keep the application alive, and seriously prepare other schools.

Getting through the wait

Keeping the Jeannine Manuel application alive while preparing other schools: that is the work I do with families on the waiting list. Let's start with a first conversation.

What you can do while you wait

One letter of interest, only one

A short letter, one page at most, can confirm that the school remains your first choice and bring in what is new since the file was submitted. Tone matters: factual, restrained, no pressure. If nothing has changed, invent nothing; if your child has progressed, this is the moment to say so.

A letter of continued interest is written with care, and only once. Have yours reviewed before you send it, along with the new elements of your child's file.

Keep the file up to date

A clearly better school report, a language exam passed, a prize in a competition: send it to the admissions office, briefly. Nothing more.

What you must not do

Chase constantly. Repeated calls or emails do not move a file forward. They count against it.

Bring in connections. Someone who "knows someone" and offers to put in a word: at best it is ignored, at worst it creates unease.

Send documents nobody asked for. A video, a portfolio, one more letter: it clutters the file without strengthening it.

Let your child carry the wait. The waiting list is an adult matter. They should not have to carry your anxiety.

Plan B, in parallel

The waiting list does not excuse you from anything. Other schools have their own deadlines, and waiting for the outcome before contacting them often means missing them. If Jeannine Manuel does offer a place in the end, you can always turn the others down. The closest alternatives, from Saint-Germain-en-Laye to the Lycée Balzac, are described in the article on alternatives after a rejection.

If the place does not come

Being on the waiting list one year carries nothing over to the next: to try again, you must submit a new online application. The year in between is then used to close the gap, in mathematics, written French or written English, in a way that shows in the school reports. What the school expects at each stage is set out in the guide to getting into Jeannine Manuel.

Reapplying next year? I measure where your child stands with three tests at the school's level, in French, English and mathematics. Ask for an assessment.
