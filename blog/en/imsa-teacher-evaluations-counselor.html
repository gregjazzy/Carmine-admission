Grades and test scores say what a student has achieved. The evaluations IMSA asks for say how they achieve it. They come from four people at the student's current school, and they all fall into a short window at the start of the year.

What is required

FormFromWhat it shows
Teacher Evaluation, mathCurrent math teacherThe student at work in mathematics
Teacher Evaluation, scienceCurrent science teacherThe student at work in science
Teacher Evaluation, EnglishCurrent English teacherThe student at work in English
Counselor FormCurrent counselor or the administrator in charge of transcriptsContext on the school and its courses, and official transcripts for the last 2.5 years

If earlier transcripts are held by a previous school, an optional Previous School Counselor Form requests them. IMSA does not accept transcripts sent by students or families.

The window

Students can send the forms from 4 January 2027. Everything must be received by the deadline: 15 January for out-of-state applicants, 5 February for Illinois residents. For out-of-state families, that leaves eleven days, at the end of the winter holidays.

Every piece on time

I help IMSA applicants plan the January window: teachers told in advance, forms sent on day one, and the checklist followed until every piece is in.

Making sure the forms arrive

  • Tell teachers in December that an evaluation request will arrive in January, so they are ready.
  • Send on 4 January, not at the end of the month.
  • Check the portal, which admissions staff update manually; delays are common at busy times.
  • Use the student's personal email for the application, as IMSA recommends; some school systems block outside messages.
  • If a teacher has not received the request, IMSA's troubleshooting page explains what to do.

Do your child's teachers know an IMSA evaluation is coming? Let's plan January together.

What the evaluations add

IMSA's teacher forms ask how the student approaches learning, comes prepared, contributes to discussion, works through challenges and collaborates. These are the qualities it looks for in a residential STEM community, and teachers who see the student every day are the best witnesses.
