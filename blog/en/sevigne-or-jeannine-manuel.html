Sévigné or Jeannine Manuel: two bilingual schools, two ways of being one

Collège Sévigné, rue Pierre-Nicole, Paris 5th. Photo LPLT, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0

Many families looking for a bilingual school in Paris hesitate between Sévigné and Jeannine Manuel, and many apply to both. It is often a good idea, provided you understand that the two schools do not select the same child, and do not offer them the same thing.

Bilingualism, two ways

At Jeannine Manuel, English is the backbone of the school: a child entering after CP must already master it, and the school turns away beginners in English from CE1. In lycée it prepares the BFI and the IB, and its students are among the best prepared in Paris for English-speaking universities. At Sévigné, bilingualism is recent, more flexible, and organised in tracks: in collège, a bilingual English track for children from a bilingual primary, and an English + track open to all, with no English test on entry; in lycée, a Monde track towards the BFI and a Europe track for non-bilingual students.

The direct consequence: a child with ordinary school English has a real door at Sévigné, and almost none at Jeannine Manuel outside the entry years.

Two different selections

Jeannine Manuel recruits mainly at five levels, from the second year of nursery to seconde, on application then assessments and interview, with a mid-January deadline for families living in France. Sévigné opens applications in the autumn, invites sixième candidates to tests on Wednesday afternoons between December and February, and answers in waves: first the bilingual track, then English +. The two calendars overlap, which makes it possible to apply to both; but two different tests have to be prepared.

The right choice, seen from the inside

I have had students at Jeannine Manuel for more than ten years and I have students at Sévigné, and every year I support around fifteen families with these applications. The difference I see lies less in level than in atmosphere. Jeannine Manuel is a large, intense international school, strongly turned towards the world; it suits children who thrive on pace and stimulation. Sévigné is a small secular school, demanding but without rankings, deeply attached to the humanities; it suits children who give their best when they feel trusted. The right choice depends on the real child, their written English, and what the family imagines after the bac.

After the bac

For a plan clearly aimed at British or American universities, Jeannine Manuel keeps an edge, through the IB, the density of written English and its familiarity with those applications. For an open plan, France or abroad, Sévigné, with its BFI and its Europe track, offers a very good balance. Both prepare the BFI: the real difference is the path.
