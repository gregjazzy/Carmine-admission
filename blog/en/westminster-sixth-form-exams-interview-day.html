Westminster Sixth Form exams and interview day: how to prepare

College, the King's Scholars' house at Westminster School, from the garden. Photo stevecadman, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 2.0

London schools November 2026 6 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

Book a conversation

Westminster's Sixth Form selection fits into two Saturdays in November. On the first, every candidate sits four entrance papers. On the second, about 190 of the 600 or so candidates return for a day of interviews and assessment. Both are short, both are demanding, and both reward a kind of preparation that has little to do with revising the GCSE syllabus.

The papers: four times 45 minutes

Each candidate is examined in the four subjects chosen for A level, with a 45-minute paper in each, between 9am and 2pm at Westminster (7 November 2026 for 2027 entry). Overseas candidates may sit them at their local British Council, on the day itself or with permission on the day before or after; candidates offering Music must come to Westminster. Dictionaries are not allowed.

Forty-five minutes is short for a subject paper, and the papers are written to discriminate among very able sixteen-year-olds who have covered only one year of their GCSE courses. In practice that means questions that ask for understanding rather than recall: a mathematics problem that combines two ideas in an unfamiliar way, a science question that asks the candidate to reason about an experiment they have never done, a history or English question that tests how they read a source or a passage. Departments publish sample papers or guidance for some subjects, and every candidate should read what is available for their four.

How to use the months before November

  • Go beyond the course in each of the four subjects: a few serious books or problem sets per subject, chosen to deepen rather than to race ahead.
  • Practise under 45-minute conditions, by hand, so that pacing is automatic.
  • For mathematics and the sciences, work on multi-step problems, including olympiad-style questions at an accessible level.
  • For essay subjects, practise planning quickly and writing a sharp, argued answer rather than a long one.

Ready for both Saturdays

We prepare candidates for Westminster's four subject papers and for the interview day, so that they arrive in November with depth in each subject and the ease to discuss it.

The interview day

Invited candidates come to Westminster in person on the interview day (28 November 2026 for 2027 entry), from 9am to 2pm. There is no remote option, including for candidates from overseas. The day has three elements:

ElementLengthWhat it tends to probe
Subject interviews, one per subject4 × 15 minutesHow the candidate thinks in the discipline when faced with something new
General interview15 minutesInterests, reading, motivation, contribution to school life
Interview Day Assessment45 minutesA further written task under time

Fifteen minutes per subject is enough for one good problem or text and a real discussion. At this level, an interviewer may well hand over something the candidate has not seen and watch them work on it aloud. The candidates who do well say what they notice, try an approach, accept a hint gracefully and build on it.

After the day

Offers are sent in December, conditional on at least eight (I)GCSEs at grade 7 or above, four of them at 8 or 9 in the A level subjects. Music candidates are auditioned in November, and up to four King's Scholarships are awarded on the overall performance across exams and interviews.

How I prepare candidates

From the summer, I work subject by subject at a level beyond GCSE, with timed 45-minute papers by hand. In the weeks before the interview day, I run mock interviews in each of the candidate's four subjects, using unseen problems and texts, and a general interview on their reading and interests. The goal is a candidate who enjoys being stretched, because that is what the day is designed to find.
