The Selective High School Placement Test

For Year 7 entry to James Ruse and every other NSW selective high school, there is one test. The Selective High School Placement Test is set and run by the NSW Department of Education. For entry in 2028 it takes place on May 13 and 14, 2027, with each child attending one of the two days, and a make-up test on June 11 for students with an approved illness or misadventure request.

I help families prepare for this day calmly and properly. Here is how it works.

Four sections, equal weight

OrderSectionFormatTime
1Reading17 multiple-choice questions, 38 answers in all45 min
2Mathematical Reasoning35 questions, five options each40 min
3Thinking Skills40 questions, four options each40 min
4WritingOne open response30 min

Each section counts for 25 percent. All answers, including the writing, are entered on a computer supplied at the centre; students get two A4 sheets for working and can ask for more. Several versions of the test exist so that it can run over two days and at the make-up session, and some common items, which do not count towards the score, keep the versions comparable.

What the test is designed to measure

The Department describes the test as an assessment of intellectual ability, built on concepts from the NSW curriculum up to Year 6 and reviewed so that no extra knowledge is needed. Free practice tests, online and in PDF, are provided for everyone.

A child who has spent a year drilling question banks is not better placed than one who reads widely, writes often and thinks about problems. What good preparation does is precise: make the format familiar, so that the first sight of a Thinking Skills question is not on test day; work on written expression, which a timed thirty-minute task exposes; and help children educated outside NSW check that the Year 6 concepts are in place.

Test day

Children are allocated to a test centre, usually a local public high school, according to their primary school, and every student from the same school sits on the same day. The Test Admission Ticket arrives two weeks before; it must be printed, as a digital copy is not accepted. On a typical timetable students arrive at 9:30, sit Reading, a short break, Mathematical Reasoning, a twenty-minute break with a snack, Thinking Skills, another short break, then Writing, and leave around 1:55. They wear school uniform, bring two 2B pencils, an eraser, a sharpener and clear water bottle, and may not bring calculators, rulers, pens or pencil cases.

Illness, absence and attendance

A child who is unwell should not come. The family submits an illness or misadventure request within five business days, and an approved request gives access to the make-up test; a child who misses the make-up test cannot be placed. One less obvious rule: the Department tells the selection committee about students with many unexplained absences from school, and the committee may decline to place them.
