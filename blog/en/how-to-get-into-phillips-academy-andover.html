How to get into Phillips Academy Andover

Samuel Phillips Hall, Phillips Academy Andover. Photo Daderot, Wikimedia Commons, CC0

Andover asks an applicant for more than most schools do. A test score is required. So is a piece of writing already graded by a teacher, an essay, a short video, an interview in one of three formats, a parent statement, three years of grades and four recommendations. Every piece is due on February 1. The length of the list is the first thing families notice. What matters more is that the committee reads these pieces against each other, and a child who looks different in each of them raises a question.

I have students at Andover and prepare candidates for it every year. Here is how the file is put together and where it holds or breaks.

A school that wants every quarter

Phillips Academy was founded in 1778 and is the oldest incorporated boarding school in the United States. Its constitution asks it to be open to "youth from every quarter", and the school takes that literally: admission is need-blind, 47% of students receive aid, and this year's 1,167 students come from 43 states and territories and 56 countries. There are 858 boarders and 309 day students, a twelfth grade that includes 20 postgraduates, and an average class of about twelve or thirteen.

Every piece at Andover's level

Three views of the same mind

Few schools combine a graded paper, a fresh essay and a video. Together they give Andover three views of how the child thinks: in class, under a teacher's eye; alone, on a subject of their choosing; and aloud, on camera. Add the test, and the committee also has a common measure against thousands of other applicants.

The trap is inconsistency. A graded paper with careful but ordinary prose, next to an essay that reads like a professional's, does not make the child look better; it makes the reader wonder who wrote the essay. A video in which the child recites, next to an interview in which they speak freely, raises the same doubt in the other direction. The strongest files are not the ones where every piece is polished. They are the ones where every piece sounds like the same person.

Would your child's paper, essay and video sound like the same person? Tell me about them.

The test is not optional here

Unlike several of its peers, Andover requires a score from every applicant. The family chooses the test: the Upper Level SSAT or ISEE, the PSAT, the SAT or the ACT. For a child at a school abroad that does not teach in English, a TOEFL, IELTS or Duolingo comes on top, and most admitted international students score at least 100, 7.5 or 135. No minimum SSAT score is published, but in a pool this strong the score has to stand comfortably beside the rest of the file.

The grades Andover will see, and the ones it gives

Andover reads the first term of the current year and the two years before; applicants to twelfth grade or the postgraduate year send their full high school record. Once admitted, students are graded from 0 to 6, are not ranked, and carry five courses a term, at six to nine hours of work per course each week. The rhythm is demanding, and a file should show a child who already works steadily, not one who has peaked in the months before applying.

How I work on an Andover application

I have students at Andover, and for seven years I have worked with around fifteen families a year aiming at the most selective schools in the United States, England and Switzerland. For Andover, I begin with an assessment of English and mathematics at the level of the Upper Level SSAT, which tells us which test to sit and how long to prepare. Then we choose the graded paper together, work on the essay through several drafts, practise the video and the interview in the chosen format, and plan the recommendations. Each piece is checked against the others before it goes in.

Where Andover students go next

Over the last three years, more than thirty students each went to Cornell, Columbia and the University of Chicago, and between twenty and twenty-nine to Harvard, MIT, Stanford, Brown, Georgetown and NYU, among others. For the classes of 2025 to 2027, the middle half of SAT scores ran from 1370 to 1530. These numbers describe the room a child joins, not a bar to clear at fourteen.
