UWCSEA runs two campuses in Singapore with the same learning programme in five elements, academics, activities, outdoor education, personal and social education, and service, the same language support and the same university advising. The choice between them is about location, setting and, in the older grades, language options. It also has a practical side: once a place is accepted, there is no transfer between campuses.

The question families underestimate: the commute

The daily journey weighs on family life. Dover suits families in the west; East is the more accessible campus from the central, eastern and northern districts. A family with young children on the wrong side of the island will feel it every day. A family considering Dover for a young child should also check where the campus will be after August 2032.

The right campus, then the right file

I help families decide between Dover, East or a dual application, then prepare the file and, for older students, the Virtual Immersion Programme.

One campus or both?

Day applicants choose. A dual campus application costs S$1,254 instead of S$627 and places the child in the pool of eligible applicants for both campuses, which means being considered for more places. It does not necessarily mean better chances: each campus typically receives two to three applications per place. UWCSEA recommends a dual application only to families who would be equally happy with either campus; a family with a strong preference, because of location or a language course offered on one campus, should apply to that one alone.

Unsure between Dover and East? Tell me where you live and what matters to your child.

Boarders

Boarding applicants are always considered for both campuses, for a single application fee of S$841. They can state a preference, but there is no single-campus boarding fee, and boarders attend one Virtual Immersion Programme whichever campus the interviewer belongs to.

One question before all others

For seven years, I have worked with around fifteen families a year on applications to very selective schools. For UWCSEA, I ask families one question before anything else: would you accept a place on the other campus? The answer decides between a single and a dual application.
