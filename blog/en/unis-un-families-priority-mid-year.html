UNIS for UN and diplomatic families: priority and mid-year entry

UNIS was created in 1947 by UN parents for their own children, and it still serves that purpose: about half of its families are affiliated with the United Nations, the other half are New York families from every background. A posting to New York rarely respects the admissions calendar, so the school has kept a specific promise for these families. Understanding exactly what it covers avoids two opposite mistakes: assuming a place is automatic, and assuming it is too late.

Who can be given priority

Priority applies to families working for the United Nations, for a Mission to the UN or for a consulate, and to families who have relocated to New York from abroad. For them, UNIS may grant priority admission after the regular deadline or during the school year.

The wording matters. Priority means the file can still be read when the January deadline has passed, or when the family arrives in March. It does not mean admission without evaluation: the child still meets UNIS staff, the school still asks for reports, teacher forms and, from 5th grade, a test score, and a place still has to exist in the grade.

Families not linked to the UN are welcome too

The other half of the school is made up of New York families of every origin who choose UNIS for an international education and the IB. Their applications follow the standard calendar, with decisions in February. For them, the deadlines are real, and the advice is simple: apply on time.

How a mid-year application works

UNIS accepts applications during the year when places are available. The steps are the same as in the main round, division by division: Ravenna account and application, evaluation or meeting, teacher forms and reports, test from 5th grade. The difference is that there is no calendar: the decision comes shortly after the file is complete. In practice, that puts the pace in the family's hands. A file that is complete in ten days gets an answer in ten days; one that waits three weeks for a report from the previous school waits three weeks.

Places do sometimes open during the year, but none is guaranteed in a given grade. Applying to a second school in parallel is prudent.

Preparing the file from abroad

  • Reports and teacher forms. Ask the current school before you leave, not after; teachers who have moved on to a summer break or a new class answer slowly. If the reports are not in English, arrange translations early.
  • The test. From 5th grade, MAP results from the past year are accepted, and many international schools give MAP: check whether yours does. Otherwise, the SSAT is offered at test centers in many countries and the ISEE is available online or on paper depending on location. The SSAT at home is open only in the United States and Canada.
  • The meeting. Plan it for the first days in New York, or discuss options with the admissions office if the child is still abroad.
  • Grade placement. A child coming from a southern-hemisphere calendar or a different system may fit one grade up or down. Raise it at the start.

The financial side for UN and diplomatic families

UNIS's financial aid process has specific rules for these families. UN staff provide a recent salary statement and, where relevant, US tax returns; mission and consulate staff provide a statement signed by their mission giving salary and housing or education allowance. Aid never affects the admission decision.

Settling in

A child arriving in February joins classes that have been together since September. UNIS is used to this, with students from more than a hundred countries and the New2UNIS parent group for new families, and the English Language Learners program supports children still learning English. The adjustment is still real, and a child who arrives with the test behind them and a clear idea of what to expect settles faster.
