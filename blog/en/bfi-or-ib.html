BFI or IB: which to choose depending on where your child will study

BFI or IB October 2026 7 min read

Families torn between the BFI and the IB often frame the question in terms of prestige. That is not the right criterion. Both diplomas are recognised by the world's best universities. What changes is how each university reads them, and that depends on the destination. The right choice therefore starts with the question: where is my child likely to study after school?

A reminder: the BFI (baccalauréat français international) is a full French baccalauréat plus teaching in the language of an international section. The IB (International Baccalaureate) is a separate diploma, built around six subjects, three or four of them at higher level.

For France: the BFI keeps every door open

For Parcoursup, France's national higher-education platform, the BFI is a French baccalauréat: committees read reports and marks as they always do. The IB is accepted, but it remains a less natural route into the prépas, the two-year intensive programmes whose syllabus extends the French lycée's. For a student aiming at a science or business prépa, the BFI is the safer choice. I explain this difference in IB or French baccalaureate.

For the UK: both, with an English advantage for the British BFI

British universities know both diplomas well and set requirements in the terms of each. UCAS's profile of the BFI likens 16 out of 20 in a subject to an A* at A level, and 15 to an A. It also states that the British version of the BFI is widely accepted as proof of English, with no further test. For the IB, higher-level subjects are what count: Cambridge, for example, requires Analysis and Approaches at higher level for mathematics. Procedures are detailed in UCAS with the French baccalaureate.

For Switzerland: two very different grading logics

EPFL and ETH Zurich publish precise requirements for each diploma. With a French baccalauréat, and therefore a BFI, ETH asks for a final average of at least 16 out of 20 and 14 out of 20 across maths, a science and the language. With the IB, ETH asks for 38 points out of 42 excluding the core, with maths, a science and a language at higher level. Either way the bar is high; only the form changes. See ETH Zurich with the French baccalaureate and EPFL with the French baccalauréat.

For the United States and Canada: both, with no preference

American universities have no preferred curriculum. Stanford says so explicitly for its international applicants, and Harvard advises finishing the curriculum you started rather than switching. In Canada, McGill assesses baccalauréat applicants on their reports from the last two years of lycée. What counts is the difficulty of the path taken and the results, not the name of the diploma.

In short. French prépas and grandes écoles: preferably the BFI. UK: both, with an English advantage for the British BFI and care over higher-level subjects in the IB. Switzerland: both, with demanding thresholds. United States and Canada: both, no preference. Uncertain plans: the BFI keeps the most doors open in France without closing those abroad.

Choose, and stick with it

The choice is often made at the end of middle school or in the first year of lycée, together with the choice of school. Switching midway is costly, both in curriculum and in the application file.
