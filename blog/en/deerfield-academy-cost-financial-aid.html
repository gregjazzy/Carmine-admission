Deerfield cost and financial aid

US boarding schools November 2026 6 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

Deerfield's legendary headmaster, Frank Boyden, asked families to "pay what you can". The school still describes its aid in those terms, and the numbers back it up: 38% of current families receive aid, 35% of those pay nothing at all, and the average boarding grant is $68,235. For a family weighing the cost, the useful question is not the sticker price but which rules apply to them.

The price and what it includes

For 2026-2027, boarding costs $79,940 and day $57,850. The figure is more inclusive than at many schools: room and board, textbooks, the use of a MacBook Air and the health center are all covered. When the school becomes fully boarding in fall 2027, it plans a single rate that is truly comprehensive, with no additional fees or hidden costs.

After admission, the family signs the enrollment contract and pays a non-refundable deposit, credited to the first bill. Tuition can be paid in one installment on August 1, in two on August 1 and December 1, or monthly for a small fee, through FACTS.

Three situations, three answers

FamilyWhat Deerfield commits to
Lives and works in the US, W-2 income only, up to $150,000Pays nothing for tuition and fees
Same profile, above $150,000Pays no more than 10% of total income, per student
Self-employed, business owners, international familiesEligible for need-based aid up to 100%; full demonstrated need met if admitted

The first two rows require two years of verifiable W-2 income and assets consistent with that income. A family with a business is not excluded; it simply goes through the standard need analysis rather than the fixed formula.

Money opens the door, the file decides

At Deerfield the aid is generous, and the competition for places is fierce. I prepare children to win the place: the test, the three essays, the interview and the recommendations, at the level of the strongest applicants.

"Will applying for aid affect admission?" "Possibly."

Deerfield answers this question with one word, and it is an honest one. The aid budget is very generous but limited, so the school cannot always accept every admissible applicant who needs aid. In other words, Deerfield is not strictly need-blind. Families should not let that stop them from applying for aid; more than 40% of prospective families do. It does mean the rest of the file has to be strong enough that the committee wants the child, cost included.

The calendar of the aid file

  • September 1: the Clarity application opens; the fee is $65 and Deerfield does not waive it.
  • January 15: application submitted; Canadian families upload their T-4 and return, other international families a current bank statement, salary verification or non-US tax filing.
  • February 15: US W-2 filers upload their latest W-2s.
  • March 10: aid decisions arrive with admission.
  • April 10: deadline to accept the aid offer.

All legal parents and guardians, step-parents included, complete an application. Families who do not apply at admission can apply later only after a significant and unpreventable change in their finances.

Beyond tuition

Some students also receive help with airfare, health insurance, music and dance lessons, and athletic or global studies travel. There is no merit aid of any kind, academic or athletic. The application fee itself, $60 or $125 from abroad, can be waived on the basis of need until January 31.

Where the real effort goes

Several of my students are at Deerfield, and for seven years I have worked with around fifteen families a year on admissions to the most selective schools in the United States, England and Switzerland. I am not a financial adviser, and the school's own office handles aid. My part is the file that earns the place. At Deerfield that means a graded essay chosen for what it shows, a test prepared early, an interview where the child talks about what they are learning, and teachers who can write about it.
