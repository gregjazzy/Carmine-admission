Harrow admissions timeline: from Year 5 to the first day in the Shell

Harrow's 13+ process stretches over four school years, from a first visit at nine or ten to the first day in the Shell at thirteen. Few of its dates can be moved, and the most important ones fall early. The calendar below goes year by year, with what each one asks of a family.

Year 4 or Year 5: visit

Harrow recommends visiting when a boy is in Year 4 or 5. Open Mornings run from 9am to 1pm on twelve Saturdays a year: a talk by the Head Master, questions to the Director of Studies and some current boys, a meeting with the Registrar, a tour of the School and of a boarding House. Families who cannot come may be offered an individual visit, depending on availability.

This is also the year to check two things at home: is the boy's current school able to support a 13+ application, with a reference and, if needed, the Pre-Test; and is he reading widely in English. Registering at birth brings no advantage, so nothing is lost by deciding now.

Everything that decides the offer happens in this one year, and much of it in the autumn. A boy who starts preparing for the Pre-Test in October is already late; the summer before Year 6 is the right moment.

Late registration, and its limits

A boy at a UK school who registers after 30 September of Year 6 and before 1 January of Year 7 is a late applicant: he sits the Pre-Test as soon as possible and, if his reference and score are both very strong, the Harrow Test in September or January of Year 7, or September of Year 8. Harrow advises registering as early as possible within that window, since testing and offers are made on a rolling basis. Boys at school overseas cannot apply late.

Your son is starting Year 6 and nothing is booked yet? Write to me now; there is still time if we move quickly.

Year 7: settling the offer

Boys with offers are invited to Experience Harrow days in the summer term and to School events through the year. This is the year to decide whether to stay at the current school or move to a UK prep school for Years 7 and 8, as about half of overseas entrants do, and to start working on whatever Common Entrance or scholarship papers lie ahead. Sport scholarship candidates should note that their application deadline falls on 30 September of Year 8.

Year 8: scholarships, then Common Entrance

  • September 30: deadline for Sport scholarship applications.
  • October to November: Sport scholarship assessment.
  • By 22 November: deadline for Music, Art, Drama, DTE and Academic scholarship applications, with the Music pre-audition at least six months earlier.
  • January to February: Academic and other scholarship assessments; Academic results in March.
  • May and June: Common Entrance, unless excused by a strong Academic Scholarship performance; French oral in May where possible.
  • June: welcome afternoon with the House Master and the other new boys of the House.

Year 9: the Shell

In September, around 160 boys arrive as Shells. Their starting sets reflect their Common Entrance or scholarship results, which is the last reason not to coast through Year 8.

How I accompany families along this calendar

I work with families across the four years: planning the registration and the reference in Year 5, preparing the Pre-Test over the summer and the Harrow Test over the autumn, then, after the offer, the scholarship papers or the Common Entrance subjects that set a boy's level. At each stage, the boy knows what is coming and the family knows what to send, and when.
