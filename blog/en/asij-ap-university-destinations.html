ASIJ's High School follows the American model: Advanced Placement courses rather than the International Baccalaureate Diploma. For families in Tokyo comparing ASIJ with IB schools, this is one of the real differences, and it shapes how a student builds a transcript for university.

I work with families planning a High School path toward selective universities. Here is how it works at ASIJ.

AP and IB compared

PointAP at ASIJIB Diploma
StructureIndependent one-year coursesSix two-year subjects taken together, three at Higher and three at Standard Level
ExamsOne final exam per course, no limit on the numberCoursework plus several final papers
CoreNone required; AP Capstone optionalTheory of Knowledge, CAS, 4,000-word extended essay
Scores1 to 5 per exam1 to 7 per subject, up to 45 points

ASIJ students may sit the AP exams for the courses they take; AP Chinese, Japanese and Spanish exams are the exceptions.

The AP Capstone Diploma

For students who want a research component, ASIJ offers AP Capstone: AP Seminar, then AP Research for students who scored 3 or higher in Seminar. A student who scores 3 or higher in both and in at least four other AP exams earns the AP Capstone Diploma. It gives an AP transcript some of the inquiry and extended writing that the IB builds in through its core.

Where graduates go

The largest share of ASIJ graduates go to universities in the United States; others go to Canada, the United Kingdom, Australia and Japan. Each student works one-on-one with a college counsellor, and more than 100 university admission officers visit the campus every year. Recent acceptances listed by the school include Harvard, Yale, Princeton, Stanford, MIT, Columbia and the University of Chicago in the United States, McGill, the University of Toronto and the University of British Columbia in Canada, and Keio, International Christian University and Osaka University in Japan.
