At Poly Prep, the interview is where admission divides in two. In the main entry years, Nursery and Grades 5, 6 and 9, every applicant who submits the preliminary application on time must register for an in-person interview; it is a mandatory step. In every other grade, places depend on departures, and the school interviews only the applicants it selects from the preliminary materials.

I prepare children and parents for these interviews. Here is what each one looks like.

When the interview is not guaranteed

For non-entry grades, the preliminary application decides whether a family is invited at all. For Grades 7-8 and 10-12, that preliminary file is substantial: report card, a Changing Schools essay, the ISAAGNY parent answers, interests form and common essay, and for Grades 11 and 12 any PSAT or SAT results. Invitations go out by email with a code to book a slot, on a rolling basis; the school cannot interview every applicant in these years, and the number of slots changes from year to year.

Applying to a non-entry grade? Tell me about your child.

Preparing without rehearsing

Whatever the age, the school wants to meet the real child. Preparation means familiarity: knowing what the visit will contain, having practised talking about two or three genuine interests, and, from Grade 5, having written short pieces under time. Parents, who meet the committee at every age, do best when they can describe their child with specific examples and explain why Poly Prep in particular.
