Choate or Exeter

The Andrew Mellon Library, Choate Rosemary Hall, Wallingford. Photo Daderot, Wikimedia Commons, public domain

US boarding schools November 2026 7 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

Choate and Exeter often appear on the same family's list, and it makes sense: both are among the most selective boarding schools in New England, both close their files on January 15, and both post decisions on March 10. The two applications, though, are built on different assumptions about what a file should contain. A family applying to both is preparing two different exercises on the same calendar.

The test: required at one, a choice at the other

This is the deepest difference. Exeter requires a score at every grade: the Upper Level SSAT or ISEE for ninth grade, and its readers look closely at the SSAT writing sample. Choate makes testing optional, reads files without scores on grades, recommendations, interview and activities, and keeps the best score in each section if several sittings are sent.

For a family applying to both, the child has to take the test anyway, for Exeter. The score is then sent to Exeter in any case, and to Choate only if it helps the file. The preparation is the same; the decision is made school by school.

The interview: one format against three

Choate interviews online only, for forty-five minutes, the first twenty to twenty-five of them alone with the child. Exeter lets the family choose between a campus interview, a live online conversation and recorded video answers to questions the school provides. Each school grants one interview per applicant.

A child applying to both will therefore do at least one interview online. If the family chooses the campus format for Exeter, the child prepares two different exercises: a conversation in a room and a conversation on a screen. If it chooses recorded video, it is a third exercise again, with no one to respond to. I usually advise preparing the Choate interview first, because it fixes the online format, and building the Exeter one on that base.

Money: two policies, one practical difference

Both schools meet the full demonstrated need of the families they admit, with grants and no loans, and neither offers merit scholarships. The difference is at the moment of decision. At Exeter, a family's finances have no bearing on admission. At Choate, files are first read without regard to aid, but when total need exceeds the aid budget, need may weigh. Exeter also offers free tuition to US families earning under $150,000 who meet its conditions. For families who need substantial aid, that difference deserves attention.

The classroom: what the child is choosing

Every class at Exeter takes place around an oval Harkness table, with at most twelve students and a teacher, and the lesson is a discussion. Exeter offers no AP courses. Choate offers more than 300 courses in six departments, eight Signature Programs, from robotics to government, of which a student may join only one, and its students sat 389 AP exams last year. Neither is better in the abstract. A child who thinks best aloud, among others, will find Exeter's table natural; a child who wants to go deep in one direction may find what they need in a Choate Signature Program.
