Many families who apply to Bangkok Patana meet the waiting pool. The school states that many year groups are full and that some age groups are regularly oversubscribed; when it cannot make an offer straight away, a complete international application is placed in a pool rather than refused.

A pool, not a queue

The word matters. In a queue, the first to apply is the first served. In Patana's pool, the order in which applications arrived is the last thing considered. When a place becomes available, the school looks at the candidates in the pool against six factors, in this order:

  1. The applicant's ability to thrive within and contribute to the learning community.
  2. The availability of support for English as an Additional Language, Learning Support or other needs.
  3. A prior connection: sibling of a current student, returning student in good standing, sibling or child of alumni.
  4. Other siblings applying for a place at the same time.
  5. Balance within the year group in gender, nationality and language.
  6. The date the application was completed.

A stronger file in the pool

A child who will need no English support removes a constraint the school has to manage. I prepare that English, and the writing sample that shows it.

What a family can influence

Two of the six factors are within reach. The first, the ability to thrive and contribute, is read through reports, the confidential recommendation, the writing sample and any assessment. The second, support needs, depends largely on English: when EAL places in a year group are taken, a child who needs that support must wait, while a child who does not can be placed. Connections, siblings and national balance cannot be changed.

Complete, or not considered

An application enters consideration only once every document has reached the admissions office and the application fee is paid. A file missing one report is not in the pool at all, and the date of completion, the sixth factor, only starts counting from that moment.

Thai nationals

The pool applies to international applicants. Thai nationals follow a once-a-year process; without a place, they are invited to update their application for the following year.
