UK boarding schools

Millfield fees 2026-27

From September 2026, a boarder in the Millfield senior school, from Year 9 to the Upper Sixth, pays £20,205 a term, VAT included; a day pupil pays £13,155. Over three terms, that is £60,615 and £39,465 a year. International families pay the same as British ones. Before comparing these figures with other schools, it is worth looking at what they cover.

What the fee includes

Millfield keeps extras to a minimum. The day fee covers:

  • tuition and most activities at school;
  • a three-course lunch, and supper for pupils staying on for activities;
  • games and prep;
  • textbooks, on loan;
  • personal accident insurance;
  • a contribution to travel and accommodation when representing the school;
  • internet access.

The boarding fee adds accommodation, breakfast, a three-course supper and laundry. At many schools, several of these items appear as extras on the termly bill. Riding is charged separately, under an equestrian fee schedule.

The fee follows the place

At Millfield, a place is won at interview, in the reasoning test and through the reference, and a scholarship at the January assessments. I prepare children for each.

Scholarships: an honour first

Senior scholarships, for entry into Year 9 or the Lower Sixth, carry a fee reduction of up to 10%. Families who do not need financial help are encouraged to accept the award on an honorary basis. The scholarship's real value lies in what it opens: a family in genuine need can then apply to have it augmented by a bursary.

Bursaries

Bursaries take the form of a fee reduction, up to 100% in exceptional cases, set according to the family's means after a confidential financial circumstances form. The assessment can look at the family's capacity to raise its income, capital that could be released, including equity in property, siblings at the school, the contributions of both parents after a separation, and help from relatives. A limited number of bursaries may also go to pupils who do not meet the scholarship standard. Awards depend on the school's budget and are reviewed each year.

Paying from abroad

International families pay through Flywire, which accepts payments from almost any country. Millfield also warns that it will never email or text a change of bank details; any such message should be treated as fraud.

My view

I work each year with around fifteen families, as I have for seven years, aiming at very selective schools. At Millfield, the inclusive fee makes budgeting simpler than elsewhere; the scholarship, worth little on its own, is often the key to a bursary. Preparing for it is worth the effort.
