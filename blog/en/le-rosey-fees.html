Le Rosey: tuition fees 2027-2028

The Château du Rosey, Rolle. Photo Eric Bajart, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0

Le Rosey is often described as the most expensive school in the world. What matters to a family is less the figure than what it implies, and what it does not buy. Fifteen of my students have been admitted to Le Rosey, and not one got in thanks to their parents' wealth: the school accepts neither donations nor paid intermediaries.

2027-2028 at a glance

For a student from class 5 to terminale, tuition including boarding amounts to CHF 164,400 for 2027-2028, paid in three terms of CHF 54,800. The Junior campus, for classes 9 to 6, has its own conditions. Students already at the school renew in two instalments, the first two terms being due by 31 March. In the final two years, an annual exam-related charge is added: CHF 13,900 for the International Baccalaureate, CHF 4,900 for the French Baccalaureate. There is also a health charge of CHF 3,950 a year up to the age of eighteen, pocket money of CHF 20 to 120 a week depending on the section, and personal expenses.

The payment timeline, to know before the offer

The application starts with a one-time, non-refundable fee of CHF 3,000, covering the file, the exams and the visit. Once admitted, the family has ten days to pay CHF 72,800: the first term, non-refundable in case of withdrawal, and an CHF 18,000 guarantee deposit for extra expenses. Without that payment, the enrolment lapses. For a family still waiting on another boarding school, ten days is very short: the decision has to be made beforehand.

Once a student is enrolled, enrolment renews automatically each year. A family wishing to withdraw their child must notify the school in writing by 31 March before the year concerned; after that date, the first term paid is not refunded. The guarantee deposit is only returned at the end of the term following the student's departure, and the health charge rises sharply after eighteen, to CHF 8,620 a year. These are clear rules, but they commit a family for several years.

Why money does not buy the place

This is the part families most often misunderstand. Le Rosey states it in black and white: it will not ask for, or even accept, donations to any development fund, and it never pays agents or intermediaries, who cannot influence an admission decision in any way. Tuition fees are its only source of income. Beds are limited, nationality quotas are strict, and a brilliant, well-connected candidate can be refused for lack of space. What decides is the campus day, the interview, and the conviction that the child will flourish in Rolle. Families who use an adviser pay that adviser themselves; the school never does.

Has someone promised you a place for a fee? Let's talk: that is not how it works.

The cost people forget

The cost of a poorly prepared application is not counted in francs: it is a lost year, a refusal to explain to a child, sometimes a fallback school chosen in a hurry. Le Rosey does allow candidates to reapply the following year, and its weekly committee and rounds mean an answer comes quickly. But no family wants to get there. The real investment is made before the file goes in: knowing the school's level in each of its languages, the weight of the mother-tongue paper, and the moment when the quota for your child's nationality is still open.
