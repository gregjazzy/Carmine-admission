Le Rosey: tuition fees 2027-2028

Le Rosey is often said to be one of the most expensive schools in the world. Its figures, at least, are clear: for 2027-2028, boarding and tuition for a student from class 5 to terminale cost CHF 164,400, or CHF 54,800 per term. What is less clear is everything that comes on top, and above all what money cannot obtain.

Every year I support families aiming for Le Rosey and the other leading Swiss boarding schools. Here is what I explain to them before we even talk about admission.

What you pay, and when

The application starts with a one-time, non-refundable registration fee of CHF 3,000, covering the file, the exams and the visit. If the child is admitted, the family has ten days to confirm the place by paying CHF 72,800: the first term, non-refundable in case of withdrawal, and an CHF 18,000 deposit for extra expenses. Otherwise the place is cancelled. The second term is due at the end of March, the third at the end of January of the school year.

On top of this come, in the last two years, an annual charge of CHF 13,900 for the International Baccalaureate or CHF 4,900 for the French bac, a health charge of CHF 3,950 a year for the compulsory insurance, pocket money of CHF 20 to 120 a week depending on the section, and extras: private lessons, mid-term trips, outings.

A place money cannot buy

Le Rosey does not sell its places: it gives them to applicants who succeed in its exams and its day on campus. I prepare your child for these tests, in maths, English or French, and for the interview, to the standard of a school that turns away excellent applicants every year. My aim: that they get in on their own merit.

What the price includes

The fee is genuinely all-inclusive for the essentials: classes, books, accommodation and meals in Rolle and at the Gstaad winter campus, the ski pass, golf, riding and sailing, music ensembles, theatre, compulsory mountain expeditions, and all university counselling.

Comparing Le Rosey with other Swiss boarding schools? Let's talk about your child: I will tell you which ones suit them, and what each really costs.

What money does not do

Two sentences in Le Rosey's financial conditions deserve slow reading. The school accepts no donations, for a development fund or anything else. And it never pays agents or intermediaries, who, it writes, have no influence over its admission decisions.

In other words, at Le Rosey neither wealth nor connections get a child in. Their exams, their day on campus, their interview, their profile and the nationality quotas decide. That is exactly why serious preparation changes the outcome, and why any promise of "privileged access" is worthless.

How I prepare applicants for Le Rosey

For seven years, I have supported around fifteen families a year with their admissions to the most selective schools, in France and Switzerland, and fifteen of my students have already been admitted to Le Rosey. For Le Rosey, My support rests on an assessment of your child in maths, English and French at the level of the school's exams. I prepare them for the tests and the interview on the campus day, help you choose the right moment to apply given their year group and the quotas, and build with you the list of other boarding schools to aim for.
