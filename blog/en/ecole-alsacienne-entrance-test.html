École alsacienne: the entrance test for CM1 and sixième

The École alsacienne, Paris 6th. Photo Celette, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

Paris schools November 2026 6 min read

There are only two entry points at the École alsacienne where the child sits a written test: entry into CM1 (age 9) and entry into sixième (age 11, the first year of collège). They are also two of the school's five real entry points, with 28 places in CM1 and around sixty in sixième, for an average of seven applicants per place. The test is therefore decisive, and it is almost always misunderstood.

Families prepare for it like a class test: revise the curriculum, do exercises. What I see, following students at the school and children applying to it, is that the test does not separate those who know from those who do not. It separates children who almost all know.

A test only for applications already shortlisted

The test is not the first step. Before it, the school reads the school reports from the current and previous year closely, and sets aside a large share of applications. Those who sit the test, in French and maths, therefore already have solid reports. Those who pass it then have an interview of about ten minutes with a teacher, alone, without their parents.

In other words, the test comes in the middle of the selection, not at the start. A child who arrives poorly prepared does not lose one chance among others: they lose the place their reports had opened for them.

Arriving at the test at the top of their level

The criterion families underestimate: consistency

The École alsacienne says it in one sentence that almost nobody reads with the attention it deserves: consistency of results is valued. In my view, that is the key to this test and to the whole selection.

In practice, a child who is excellent in maths and fragile in French does not have the advantage their parents imagine. Against an applicant who is solid everywhere, without any particular brilliance, it is often the second who gets through. The school is not looking for a champion in one subject: it is looking for a child able to follow an education that gives the same weight to literature, sciences and the arts. An imbalance, however brilliant, shows in the test even more than in the reports.

Is your child as solid in French as in maths? Have them assessed at the school's level: I will tell you exactly where they stand, subject by subject.

What the test really assesses

In French, it is not general knowledge that makes the difference, it is writing: understanding a text in depth, answering precisely, writing a few clear and correct lines. A child who reads a lot but writes little, or who speaks perfect French at home after schooling abroad, is often less at ease than expected.

In maths, it is not arithmetic, which almost all have mastered, it is reasoning: understanding a problem never seen before, choosing an approach, explaining it. Children trained to apply methods find themselves at a loss as soon as the question steps outside the familiar frame.

The exact format, length and type of exercises are written down nowhere. That is precisely where the gap opens between a child discovering the test on the day and a child who knows what to expect.

How I prepare applicants for the École alsacienne

For seven years, I have supported around fifteen families a year with their child's admission to the most selective schools in Paris, and I have students at every one of them, the Alsacienne included. For this school, my support covers everything that decides a place:

  • An assessment at the school's level: three tests, in French, maths and English, and an interview with your child. You know exactly where they stand, and what separates them from an offer.
  • Preparation for the test: writing in French, reasoning in maths, at the level and in the spirit of the test.
  • Preparation for the interview: talking about themselves, alone, in front of a teacher, without reciting anything.
  • The application and the calendar: school reports that show the consistency the school is looking for, an application submitted on time.
  • The other schools: with seven applicants per place, the schools to aim for in parallel, chosen for your child.

When to start

Applications for September 2027 are open from 13 November 2026 to 3 January 2027, and the school asks for the current year's reports around mid-January. The test follows. For entry into sixième, the reports that will open the door are built from CM1, and you need to know where the child stands from the start of CM2: a gap in writing or reasoning takes several months to close, not a few weeks.
