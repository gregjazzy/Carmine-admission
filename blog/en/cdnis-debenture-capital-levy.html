Debentures and the capital levy

At many Hong Kong international schools, the word "debenture" causes more anxiety than the entrance assessment. At CDNIS, the rules are clear, and they matter for two questions families mix up: what they will pay each year, and what helps their child get in.

The basic rule

Every student admitted from Early Years 1 upward must either pay an annual capital levy or be covered by a debenture. Nursery students need neither. The capital levy is a non-refundable HK$43,000 a year, used for the school's capital needs, campus development and infrastructure, and it may be reviewed from time to time. A debenture holder's nominated child is exempt from the levy. One debenture covers one student.

A closed market

CDNIS introduced its debenture programme in 1993 to fund the campus and long-term projects, and it no longer issues new debentures. Today, debentures change hands only between existing holders and current or newly admitted students without a debenture. The school is neither buyer nor seller and has no plan to buy debentures back, but it is the only authorised facilitator; private transactions without its confirmation are not recognised.

Buyers are matched in the order their transfer forms arrive, and a transfer can go ahead only once the buyer's child holds a firm place. Transfers are processed in batches that follow the admission cycles.

The assessment comes first

A debenture never replaces a strong assessment at CDNIS. I prepare children for the assessment and interview that decide whether priorities apply at all.

What a debenture does for admission

A debenture is not an admission requirement. Applicants who hold one, or have applied for one, receive priority, but only once they meet the assessment requirements. In practice, the CDNIS screening has two stages: Stage 1 ranks every applicant on the assessment, school results and reference, as Excellent, Good, Marginal or Not suitable. Only Excellent and Good move to Stage 2, where debentures weigh alongside siblings and Canadian citizenship. A debenture cannot lift a Marginal applicant into the school, and it never guarantees a place.

Levy or debenture: a financial choice

For most families, the comparison is simple arithmetic: HK$43,000 a year for each year of enrolment, against the cost of buying a transferable debenture on the second-hand market and the priority it brings for that one child. Families with several children, or employers buying corporate debentures, face a different calculation. Detailed questions go to the school's Debenture Programme Manager.
