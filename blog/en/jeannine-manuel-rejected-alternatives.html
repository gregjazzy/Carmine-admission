Rejected by Jeannine Manuel: Alternatives and Rebound Strategy

Photo: École Jeannine Manuel

School Integration February 2026 · updated November 2026 6 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. Has worked with students of École Jeannine Manuel for more than ten years.

Book a conversation

Your child was not offered a place at École Jeannine Manuel. The email has arrived, and with it disappointment, often anger, and one question: "what did we miss?" I get these calls every spring. For more than ten years, I have followed students of the school through to higher education, and every year I also see very good candidates leave without a place. A rejection rarely says what parents think it says.

Why strong students are turned down

The school generally does not explain its decisions in detail, which leaves families guessing. Here is what I see most often.

Timing

For families living in France, applications close in mid-January, and most places are decided on that calendar. For families moving from abroad there is no deadline, but the school itself recommends applying as early as possible: a late application arrives when places are already partly allocated.

An imbalance between subjects

The school assesses English, French and mathematics, depending on the child's age. A marked weakness in one can be enough, even when the other two are excellent: a child at ease in English but weak in French-style mathematics, or a strong mathematician whose written French has suffered after years abroad.

The make-up of each class

This is the hardest reason to hear, because it says nothing about the child. The school values the cultural diversity of its classes and keeps places for families coming from abroad for a limited time. Between equivalent applications, it also has its priorities: students of its sister schools in London and Lille and their siblings, children of staff and of alumni. An excellent child can be turned down one year and admitted the next.

The day of the assessment

An assessment happens on a given day. A tired, intimidated or simply uneasy child can perform below their real level.

Bouncing back after a rejection

Let the disappointment pass, then decide

For parents, a rejection often feels like a personal failure: we should have started earlier, prepared better. For the child, especially if friends were admitted, it is a blow to their confidence. The disappointment is legitimate on both sides. But it must not become a label: the child turned down yesterday has the same qualities today. The families who bounce back best give themselves a few days, then look calmly at what is still open this year and what can be prepared for the next.

Serious alternatives

Fixating on one school makes families forget that there are other excellent bilingual and international schools in and around Paris. The most common comparison is covered in Jeannine Manuel or Saint-Germain.

Lycée International de Saint-Germain-en-Laye

Often the most natural alternative for a student who was aiming for Jeannine Manuel. This state lycée has many international sections, including American and British, and prepares for the BFI. Tuition is free, apart from the contribution asked by the section associations. Each section has its own tests and calendar: check them straight away.

Lycée Honoré-de-Balzac, in Paris

For a family that needs to stay in Paris, this state lycée in the 17th arrondissement has several international sections, including a British one, that also prepare for the BFI. Entry is on file, then written and oral tests in the language of the section, on the education authority's calendar.

EIB Monceau

Not to be confused with Jeannine Manuel, even though the two schools share a common origin. The École Internationale Bilingue is another bilingual Paris school, with its own teaching approach and admissions process.

English-speaking international schools

For a family whose goal after school is only abroad, the English-speaking international schools of the Paris region are an option, with the IB or A-levels. The trade-off is distance from the French track, so this only makes sense when the destination is clearly defined.

Reapplying next year or applying elsewhere now: let's talk about your child's plan and choose the schools to apply to.

Reapplying the following year

A new application is possible: it is submitted online, like the first. But reapplying without changing anything is pointless. You need to know what was missing and be able to show, a year later, that it has changed: the weak subject worked on, rising school reports, a language exam passed, new responsibilities at the current school. The new file looks forward; it does not dwell on the rejection.

Reapplying only works with an honest diagnosis of what went wrong. Tell me about your child's application: I can assess their level and prepare the tests and interview again.

The lycée is not the destination

A student who does very well at another good lycée, with a BFI and strong results, builds a university application as solid as a Jeannine Manuel student's. Universities look first at grades, the student's path, essays and recommendation letters. I have seen students from other lycées get into Columbia, Oxford or EPFL. The lycée matters, but how the next step is prepared matters just as much. Outcomes are covered in university outcomes after Jeannine Manuel.
