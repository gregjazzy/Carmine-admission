The Gordonstoun assessments

Many British schools put every candidate through the same test on the same day. Gordonstoun does not. The interview, the current school report and a referee form the core of every application; additional assessments come on top, when the school needs them to complete its picture of a child. Five subjects can be asked for.

Mathematics

A maths assessment checks that a child has the foundations the year group will build on. It matters most for children arriving from another curriculum, where the order of topics differs: a pupil can be ahead in algebra and behind in geometry, or the reverse. Gaps of that kind are easy to close before entry, and hard to close in the middle of a boarding term.

English

For a native speaker, the English assessment reads comprehension and writing: whether the child follows a text closely and writes with control. Writing is where an otherwise good file most often shows strain, because a report grade says little about what a child produces alone, in a limited time.

Verbal reasoning

Verbal reasoning works on vocabulary, word relationships, codes and logic expressed in words. Outside the British system, it is rarely taught, and a capable child meeting the format for the first time loses time on the instructions rather than on the problems. Familiarity counts for a great deal here.

Non-verbal reasoning

Non-verbal reasoning uses shapes, sequences, rotations and patterns. It is the least dependent on language, which is why it can show the potential of a child whose English is still developing. It also rewards practice: the types of question repeat, and children who have seen them work faster and with more confidence.

English for non-native speakers

This is the assessment that matters most for families abroad. A third of Gordonstoun pupils come from overseas, from more than forty countries, and the school teaches in English from the first morning. Its English as an Additional Language department supports pupils once they arrive, but the admission still depends on the child being able to follow lessons and share a boarding house in English straight away. Spoken English improves quickly through immersion; written English much more slowly, and that is usually where preparation is needed.

For pupils of 15 or 16 from another system with good English, the school also runs a one-year GCSE course in Year 11, rather than the usual two years.
