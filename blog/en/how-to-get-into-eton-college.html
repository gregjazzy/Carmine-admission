How to get into Eton

Most boys arrive at Eton at thirteen, in F Block. The place itself is decided when they are eleven, and the file is opened before they turn ten. Families who start thinking about Eton in Year 7 have usually already missed the main door.

I follow boys at Eton and prepare candidates for its assessments every year. What follows is the route as it actually works, and the points where good candidates fall away.

A file opened three years ahead

Registration is online and closes on 31 August of Year 5, the school year in which the boy turns ten. For entry in September 2029, the deadline was 31 August 2026. These dates are not negotiable, and an incomplete file (the unabridged birth certificate is required, along with the £480 registration fee) is not processed and may be discarded after a month.

Registering at birth brings no advantage: every registered boy sits the same initial assessment. The list is not a queue, which is good news for families who come to Eton late in Year 5, and bad news for those who assumed an early name secured anything.

Money enters the file just as early. Every family, whether or not it needs help, must return a financial declaration by 25 July of the summer after Year 5. A late form is not accepted.

Stage 1: a filter, not a verdict

In the autumn of Year 6, between September and the end of November, every registered boy sits the ISEB Common Pre-Tests: four adaptive online tests in maths, English, verbal reasoning and non-verbal reasoning, usually taken at his own school. Eton adds a report from his head teacher on his academic strengths, his interests and his character. Results arrive in mid-December.

Stage 1 does not choose the boys who will come to Eton. It removes those who cannot keep up. Adaptive tests are built to find the ceiling of a child quickly, and a boy who has never met a non-verbal reasoning question under time pressure gives away marks that say nothing about his intelligence. That is the part of Stage 1 that preparation changes most.

Ready for each threshold

Eton takes boys from around 120 schools each year and turns away far more who were excellent in theirs. I prepare candidates so that their real level shows on the day it counts, from the Pre-Tests to the interview.

Stage 2: where the place is decided

Boys who pass Stage 1 are invited to Eton in the spring of Year 6, in groups by age. They have an individual interview and sit an online test built to predict academic ability and potential. Offers are then made in tranches according to the boy's birthday in the school year, between March and July; boys whose families need a bursary of 80% or more hear in February.

This is the day that matters. The online test is designed so that cramming helps little, which leaves the interview as the part where boys differ most. What Eton is weighing is a boy's current and potential ability, and whether he will be happy in a boarding house. An eleven-year-old who can follow an unexpected question, admit he does not know and then reason his way forward makes a very different impression from one who has learned answers. Families tend to underestimate how much this can be worked on, without making the boy sound rehearsed.

After the offer: two more years to hold the place

A successful Stage 2 brings a conditional place, not a certain one. It is confirmed in Year 8, at twelve or thirteen, by passing the King's Scholarship, Common Entrance or Eton Entrance examinations. In between, during Year 7, the family meets several House Masters to find a boarding house, and pays the acceptance fee by the end of March.

Some boys receive a waiting-list place instead. They come back in the summer of Year 7 for a second interview and sit the online test again. Eton takes some of them every year, in a number that varies. For these boys, the eighteen months after Stage 2 are not a pause but a second chance, and the most useful thing a family can do is treat them that way.

If 31 August has passed

A boy who was not registered in time still has two doors. In Year 8 he can sit the King's Scholarship, about fourteen places a year, or apply for a Music Award; neither requires prior registration, and both are fiercely competitive. At sixteen, Eton admits about twenty fee-paying boys into the Sixth Form, and funds up to fourteen Orwell Award places for boys from UK state schools. Each of these routes asks more of a candidate than the 13+ entry, not less.
