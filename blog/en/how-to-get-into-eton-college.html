How to get into Eton College

Eton has around 1,350 boys aged 13 to 18, all boarders. Most join at 13, in Year 9, but everything is decided well before: a boy must be registered by the end of Year 5, the year he turns 10, and he is assessed in Year 6. For a family new to the English system, that is the first surprise: at Eton, the application starts three years before the first day.

The stages

Registration. It is done online, with the full birth certificate and a £480 registration fee. The deadline is 31 August of Year 5: for entry in September 2029, a boy had to be registered by 31 August 2026. These deadlines are strict.

Stage 1. In the autumn of Year 6, the boy sits the ISEB Pre-Tests, four online tests in English, maths, verbal and non-verbal reasoning. Eton also asks his school for a report. On these two pieces, the school selects the boys who go forward.

Stage 2. Between January and March of Year 6, the selected boys come to Eton for interviews and tests.

The conditional place. Boys who succeed at Stage 2 are offered a conditional place, confirmed at 12 or 13 by passing the King's Scholarship, Common Entrance or Eton Entrance examinations. Others are offered a waiting-list place, with a further assessment in Year 7.

Preparation for every stage

What Eton looks at

The school says it looks at a boy's current and potential academic ability, and at whether he will be happy in a boarding environment. It also expects every boy to make his own contribution to school life. In other words, good results are not enough: the Stage 2 interview carries real weight, and it can be prepared.

Is your son 8 or 9? Tell me about him: registration closes before he turns 10, and preparation starts early.

The other ways in

After the registration deadline, only two doors remain: scholarships in Year 8, including the King's Scholarship, and Sixth Form entry at 16, with about twenty fee-paying places a year.
