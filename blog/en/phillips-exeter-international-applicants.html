Applying to Exeter from abroad

Exeter's students come from 38 countries, and the school applies one process to all of them. An applicant from Paris, Seoul or São Paulo fills in the same Candidate Profile, pays the same $60 fee, meets the same January 15 deadline and is read by the same committee as an applicant from Boston. Nothing is adapted, and nothing is closed.

What differs is the work around the file. I work every year with families outside the United States who apply to Exeter, and these are the points where distance costs them time.

Who counts as international

Exeter treats as international both students who need a Form I-20 and an F-1 visa to study in the United States, and students who live outside the country even with an American passport. The distinction matters after admission, for the visa and the health insurance, not for the decision itself.

Documents that must arrive in English

Reports for the current and previous year must reach Exeter in English, sent through Gateway by the school or by a licensed translator. A family cannot send them itself. For a school that reports in another language, that means two years of reports to translate, and a school office that has to learn how Gateway works. Both take longer than families expect.

The recommendations follow the same route. The English teacher who writes is the child's current English teacher, which at a school abroad usually means the teacher of English as a foreign language. That letter weighs as much as the mathematics one, and the teacher writing it often has never been asked for an American recommendation before. A short conversation in September about what Exeter reads makes a visible difference.

Exeter, from wherever you live

A family abroad faces the same committee as an American family, with less of the context. I prepare the child for the test, the essays and the interview at Exeter's level, and keep the documents and dates under control from a distance.

English: the test that is not required

For students who do not speak English as a first language and have not been at an English-speaking school for the last two years, Exeter strongly recommends the TOEFL, IELTS or Duolingo English Test, without requiring it. A good score is worth sending. In any case, English shows everywhere in the file: in the writing sample of the SSAT or ISEE, in the two essays and in the interview. A child whose English is solid but still school English needs time to practise writing and talking in it, not only a test score.

Testing and interviewing from a distance

The SSAT at Home is open only to students in the United States or Canada on test day. Abroad, the SSAT is taken on paper in an international centre or on computer in a Prometric centre, at $339 for the Upper Level, and the ISEE is offered online or on paper depending on the country. The dates of the nearest centre decide the calendar and are worth checking before the summer.

The interview can be held online, as a live conversation, or by recorded video, answering questions supplied by the school. For most families abroad the online interview is the natural choice; the recorded video suits time zones that make a live slot difficult, but it is the harder format for a child. Each applicant has one appointment, to be completed by January 15.

Families abroad can apply for need-based aid, through Clarity, by January 15, with their foreign tax return or employer statement in the original and in official translation. The tuition-free programme for families earning under $150,000, on the other hand, requires living and working in the United States with W-2 income only, and does not apply to families based abroad.

After March 10: visa and arrival

Instructions for the I-20 are sent to the student's Exeter email in late April, and the form cannot be issued more than 120 days before the start of school; the family then takes it to a visa appointment at the American embassy. New international students are invited to an orientation held over the three days before term. During the year, the school provides free transport to Boston Logan, Manchester and New York for vacation departures and returns, which simplifies flights home.

How I prepare families applying from abroad

I have students at Phillips Exeter and for seven years have worked with around fifteen families a year, many of them outside the United States, aiming for the most selective schools in the United States, England and Switzerland. For a family abroad, I prepare the child for the test, the essays and the interview at the level Exeter expects, and I plan the parts that distance complicates: translations, recommenders unfamiliar with American files, the test centre, the visa.

Distance adds steps, not obstacles. Write to me while there is time for all of them.
