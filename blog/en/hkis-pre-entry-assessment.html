The HKIS pre-entry assessment

Book a conversation

At HKIS, not every applicant is assessed. Invitations depend on the applicant's priority group, the number of applications and the expected number of places in each grade. Families who are invited receive an email and book a date through the OpenApply portal. The format then depends on the child's age.

What is assessed, by age

GradeFormatWhat is observed
Reception One and TwoIn person, preferredFine and gross motor skills, developmental readiness, behaviour, expressive and receptive English
Lower PrimaryIn person, preferredPhonics, phonemic awareness, numeracy, behaviour, developmental readiness, expressive and receptive English
Grades 3 to 12Standardised, computer-based and adaptiveMath, reading, writing and oral language, including speaking and listening comprehension

For the youngest

At four or five, the assessment is about readiness. Children entering Reception One must be four by 31 August of their year of entry, and Reception Two five. What helps is ordinary: a child used to listening to an adult, following simple instructions in English, sitting for a short task, and playing alongside others. Drilling a four year old rarely helps and sometimes hurts.

Ready for an adaptive test

I prepare HKIS applicants from Grade 3 for the adaptive assessment: reading, writing and math at grade level and above, and confidence speaking English with a new adult.

From Grade 3: the adaptive assessment

Older applicants take a comprehensive, computer-based adaptive assessment. As with other adaptive tests, questions adjust to the student's answers, so a strong student meets harder material quickly. The assessment covers core domains in math and literacy and includes oral language, both speaking and listening comprehension. For a child whose first language is not English, the oral and writing components are where the gap shows most.

Has your child been invited to assess? Let's prepare together.

What the committee does with it

The Admissions Committee reviews the assessment with the rest of the file: report cards, teacher recommendations and personal statements. It weighs English proficiency, academic achievement or readiness, personal characteristics, fit with the school's Student Learning Results, and proven success in similar school environments. A good assessment is necessary, but it is read in that context.

Four useful habits

  • Read in English every day, and talk about what was read.
  • Write short pieces regularly: a paragraph explaining an idea, a short story.
  • Keep math fluent at grade level, with some problems a little beyond it.
  • Practise speaking in English with adults outside the family.

Where I focus the work

For seven years, I have worked with around fifteen families a year on applications to very selective schools, many in Hong Kong. For HKIS, I focus on English, written and spoken, which the assessment examines at every age, and on math for older applicants.
