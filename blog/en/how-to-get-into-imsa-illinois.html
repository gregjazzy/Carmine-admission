How to get into IMSA

The Illinois Mathematics and Science Academy in Aurora is a public residential high school for Grades 10 to 12, funded by the Illinois General Assembly. Students enter as sophomores and live on campus for three years. The number of places, typically about 200 to 250, is set by the number of rooms. Admission is competitive but holistic, and there is no fee to apply.

Eligible applicants

  • Students in 8th or 9th grade; tenth to twelfth graders are not eligible.
  • Students who have completed, or are at least halfway through, Algebra 1 and a high school-level science course.
  • Illinois residents, plus a small number of tuition-paying students from elsewhere in the United States.

A holistic reading

IMSA reads each file as a whole: classroom performance, test scores, extracurricular activities and leadership, alongside the qualities it looks for in a student, passion for STEM, curiosity, the ability to work with others, and the capacity to thrive living away from home. Geographic and demographic variables are also considered, to build a diverse class.

From October to April

The application opens on 26 October 2026. Teacher evaluation and counselor forms become available on 4 January 2027. The deadline is 5 February 2027 for Illinois residents and 15 January for out-of-state applicants. Decisions are released on 5 April for Illinois and 8 March for out-of-state applicants. Admitted and waitpool students then take a placement test in late May and attend orientation in June.

Paid by the state, mostly

For Illinois residents, the state pays for tuition, housing and meals. Families pay a student fee on a six-tier sliding scale, from about $698 to $7,450 a year, and no Illinois resident is refused for inability to pay. Out-of-state students should expect about $55,000 to $57,000 a year.
