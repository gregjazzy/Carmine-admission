Bilingual mastery is the core of The ISF Academy's programme, for children from Chinese-speaking and non-Chinese-speaking homes alike. Immersion is led by specialists in bilingual education, and the school treats every teacher as a language teacher: a mathematics teacher teaching in English is also an English teacher. The balance between the two languages shifts as children grow, and that shift explains much of what the admissions assessments look for.

I work with families on both languages for ISF entry. Here is how the programme is built.

What it means for applicants

Foundation Year has no language requirement. From Grade 1 to Grade 11, applicants need grade-appropriate proficiency in both Chinese, with oral work in Putonghua, and English. That is a high bar for most families: a child who is strong in English but whose Chinese has been a weekend subject, or a child from a Chinese-medium school whose English is not yet at grade level, will find one half of the assessment hard.

The assessments mirror the programme. From Grade 2, written papers cover Chinese, mathematics in Chinese, English and mathematics in English in primary; in secondary, Chinese, English and mathematics in English. Orals and group activities run in both languages.

Is one of your child's two languages weaker? Tell me which: that is where to start.

Choosing the moment of entry

Because Putonghua dominates the early years, a non-Chinese-speaking child has the easiest entry at Foundation Year, before any language requirement applies. Later entry demands Chinese at grade level in a programme where, in Grades 1 to 3, most teaching is in Putonghua. For a child strong in English, the language balance of secondary is more forgiving, but Chinese is still assessed.

How I prepare children for ISF

For seven years I have worked with around fifteen families a year aiming for the most selective schools around the world. For ISF, I assess the child's level in both languages against their grade, work on the weaker one in writing and speaking, and prepare the written papers and group activities in Putonghua and English.
