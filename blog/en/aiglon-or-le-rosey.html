Aiglon or Le Rosey: how to choose

Villars-sur-Ollon, home of Aiglon. Photo Bledard92, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0

Boarding schools November 2026 5 min read

Many families hesitate between the two, and it makes sense: Aiglon and Le Rosey are among the most renowned boarding schools in Switzerland, and of comparable size, around five hundred students from sixty-five to seventy countries. But knowing them from the inside through my students, they are two schools that do not shape children in the same way.

Language and programmes

Le Rosey is bilingual: each student chooses French or English as their main language and takes at least one subject in the other, before sitting the International Baccalaureate or the French bac. Aiglon is English-speaking: IGCSEs in Years 10 and 11, then the International Baccalaureate, and the school's own diploma. For a French-speaking child who wants to keep French, or aim for the bac, Le Rosey is the natural choice. For a child who wants to step fully into an English-speaking curriculum, Aiglon is more so.

Choosing, then getting in

I help you see which of the two truly suits your child, then prepare their application to one, the other or both: fifteen of my students have been admitted to Le Rosey, and I have students at Aiglon. My aim: that they get into the one where they will thrive.

The spirit of the school

Le Rosey lives on two campuses, Rolle on Lake Geneva and, from January to March, Gstaad, where afternoons are given over to winter sports; it is a school of traditions, run for five generations by its owners, with clearly set Codes and discipline. Aiglon lives in the mountains all year round, in Chesières; it is a not-for-profit school founded in 1949 on the balance of mind, body and spirit, where expeditions, skiing and meditation are part of daily life. One shapes students through international community life and high standards; the other, in addition, through physical effort and mountain challenge.

Admission

Le Rosey sets exams in maths, language and the mother tongue during a day on campus, decides weekly, and applies strict nationality quotas; for the older classes it decides in rounds. Aiglon uses an interview and online assessments, says they need no preparation, and decides monthly. In both cases, what makes the difference is the child: their real level, their maturity, and their fit with the school's spirit.

Aiglon or Le Rosey for your child? Tell me about them: I will tell you which one fits, and how to prepare each application.

After graduation

Aiglon students mostly go to the United States: 60% of the class of 2026. Le Rosey's are spread more between London, Europe and North America, and its French bac graduates also mostly head to the US or the UK. Both schools achieve excellent IB results, averaging around 36 points.
