La Tour: entering sixième, and choosing three languages

Paris schools November 2026 4 min read

Sixième (age 11) is the main entry point to La Tour, a Catholic collège-lycée in the 16th arrondissement. Its applications open earlier than those of other years, and they involve a choice few schools ask for at that age: three languages.

The calendar

Sixième applications open in mid-September of the CM2 year, a year before entry; other years only open in January. The process starts with online pre-registration, followed by a complete file: application form, school reports from the two previous years and the current one, a motivation letter to the head of school, an application fee. The answer comes within six weeks, with an appointment for shortlisted files, attended by the child and both parents.

Applying in September rather than in spring means being read while places remain.

A place in sixième at La Tour

The sixième application turns on the CM1 and CM2 reports, the letter and the appointment. I know La Tour through my students, and I prepare applications of excellence, submitted at the right time.

Three languages, from sixième

La Tour offers ten languages and puts languages at the heart of its project. Every student takes three languages, chosen in sixième and kept through collège and up to the bac. English can be taken in the anglophone section, advanced English or reinforced English; then come European modern languages, German, Spanish, Italian, Russian, and ancient and oriental languages, including Arabic, Chinese and Japanese depending on the year. The most demanding sections open by exam, and a language commitment form accompanies enrolment.

Choosing well before applying

I have students at La Tour, and every year I support around fifteen families towards the most selective collèges in Paris. Before sixième at La Tour, the decisive question is languages: anglophone section or not, and which third language. It commits the expected level, the budget and the child's timetable for seven years. I work on it with families from the child's real level, then prepare the file and the appointment.

An adaptation year

At La Tour as elsewhere, sixième is first a year of adaptation: 26 to 29 hours of lessons a week, new methods, one teacher per subject. Add the weekly hour of pastoral education, planned for every student from sixième to terminale, and projects focused on group cohesion. A well-prepared child settles in easily; a child who arrives borderline in maths or French soon feels it.
