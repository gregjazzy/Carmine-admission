How to get into ASIJ

The American School in Japan teaches more than fifty nationalities on two Tokyo campuses: the Early Learning Center in Roppongi for the youngest, and the Kindergarten-to-Grade 12 campus in Chofu. It admits new students in every grade each year, between 15 and 20 percent of its enrollment, but on conditions that do not depend on the child alone.

I work with families in Japan and abroad applying to ASIJ and to Tokyo's other international schools. Here is how admission works for August 2027.

The two conditions

At least one parent must speak, read and write English fluently, because English is the language of all the school's communication with families. And the child must live in Tokyo with their parents for the whole time they attend ASIJ: it is a day school with no boarding or homestay. There is no citizenship requirement. Families already living in Tokyo cannot transfer in mid-year; families new to Japan can ask about mid-year places.

How the school decides

There is no general entrance examination. The school reads the reports, test results and recommendations, and may ask a child to sit an academic, English or other screening before deciding. Its stated criteria go beyond the child's record: the fit between the family's educational goals and the school's approach, balance across each grade, the resources available to support the child, and ASIJ's founding principle of serving students with an established need for an English-medium education.

Timing and cost

Applications open in mid-November for the following August, and decisions start in late February or early March and continue through the summer, so earlier files meet more open places. In 2026-27, tuition runs from ¥3,143,000 to ¥3,826,000 a year, and new families also pay one-time fees of ¥1,825,000. ASIJ offers no scholarships or financial aid to new applicants.
