The Deerfield interview

The Main School Building, Deerfield Academy, Massachusetts. Photo John Phelan, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 3.0

US boarding schools November 2026 6 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

Book a conversation

At Deerfield the interview is required. It is a one-on-one conversation with an admission officer, presented as a chance for the family to learn about Deerfield and for Deerfield to learn about the child. It can be booked only once the Candidate Profile has been submitted, through the Deerfield Admission Portal or by contacting the office.

Summer tour-and-interview appointments exist for families applying for the following fall. During the school year, space tightens in December and January, so booking early matters.

The campus format: the tour is part of it

On campus, the child walks around Deerfield with a current student before the interview. Children often underestimate that hour. It gives them something concrete to talk about afterwards: a class glimpsed, the dining hall, a question they asked their guide. A child who arrives in the interview with an observation of their own has already started the conversation.

An interview prepared, not rehearsed

I prepare children for the Deerfield interview in the format they will face, in English, so that they talk about what they are learning with real substance. Parents preparing the Zoom format get their own briefing.

The Zoom format: the parents speak too

On Zoom, part of the hour is for the parents. Families often prepare the child and forget themselves. The parents do not need a speech. They need to be able to say simply why Deerfield, why now, and what they hope the school will give their child, in terms consistent with what the child said a few minutes earlier. A parent who contradicts the child, or who talks over them about achievements, does more harm than good.

Campus or Zoom? Tell me about your child and your situation, and we will choose.

What to talk about

Deerfield's whole application leans one way: towards a student who responds to feedback, revises, and develops ideas. The interview is the place to show that aloud. The best material is rarely a prize. It is a piece of work that went wrong and was redone, a teacher's comment that changed how the child writes, a problem they could not solve and came back to. A child who can describe that process, with real detail, is answering the question Deerfield cares about even if no one asks it directly.

Deerfield is also a school of shared meals, discussion and debate across disagreement. A child who can listen, disagree politely and build on what the other person said shows they would fit at those tables.

What does not work

Recited answers, lists of activities, and a child who answers every question in one sentence. The interviewer is trying to have a conversation; a child who closes every topic forces them to keep opening new ones, and the hour passes without anyone learning much.

How I prepare the interview

Students of mine are at Deerfield today, and over seven years I have prepared around fifteen families a year for the most selective schools in the United States, England and Switzerland. For the interview, I work with the child in short sessions in the chosen format, starting from their real schoolwork: what they are proud of, what they found hard, what they redid. For a Zoom interview, I also spend time with the parents. The aim is a conversation, not a performance.
