Getting Into the Ivy League From France

Every year, French families aim for Harvard, Princeton, Columbia or Yale for a brilliant child, and many come away without an admission. Rarely for lack of ability. Most often because a French application, however excellent, is not read in Princeton the way it would be in Paris. An Ivy League university does not choose the best grades: it chooses a student, and judges them through what their school, their teachers and their own writing say about them.

Since 2021, students I have supported have been admitted to Columbia, UPenn, Stanford, Pomona and Harvard. I know the lycées they come from, how those lycées grade, and how their applications are read on the other side of the Atlantic. That is the reading I prepare with each student, well before terminale (the final year).

What an Ivy League university reads in an application

Ivy League universities practise holistic review, an overall assessment of the applicant. Grades are not the ceiling of an application but its floor: an average of 18 out of 20 does not set a student apart, it earns them a reading. What decides next is everything else.

The Common App, the platform shared by more than a thousand American universities, asks for a 650-word Personal Essay in which the student tells who they are, not what they have achieved; a ten-line activities list, where a passion built over several years shows, or its absence gives the student away; and, for each university, Supplemental Essays that demand a precise reason for applying. For a student trained in the French dissertation, structured and impersonal, writing "I" with the right touch is an entirely new exercise.

Add to this two teacher letters and one from the school. An American letter tells the story of a student: how they think, what they bring to the class, specific scenes. "A serious, hard-working student" carries no weight.

The Personal Essay and Supplemental Essays take several months of work. Tell me about your child: I support them on their essays, from the first idea to the final version.

Harvard, Columbia, Stanford: an application worthy of them

I build with your child an application of excellence, designed to be read by the admissions committees of the most selective American universities: a coherent profile, essays with a voice, tests at the top of their level. My aim: that theirs is the application that stands out.

What the French lycée changes in the application

An American application is never read on its own: it is read through the school that sends it. In France, 14 out of 20 is a very good mark and 16 is rare; converted naively, an average of 15 looks like 75%, a mediocre grade in the United States. Everything then rests on the school profile, the document in which the school explains its grading, and on how familiar the university is with that school.

This is where lycées differ most, far more than in the league tables. Some are known to American universities, know how to present their students, and have teachers used to writing for them. Others, among the best in France, send applications whose harsh grades are explained nowhere and letters that read like certificates. I have followed students in these lycées for years, and I know how each one grades and how its applications are received across the Atlantic: that is what makes it possible to compensate for what needs compensating, before the application goes out.

Tests and timing: it all starts in seconde

After a few years in which they were optional, the SAT and ACT are required again at the most selective universities. For entry in 2027, Harvard, Yale, Penn, Brown, Cornell and Dartmouth require them again, as do MIT and Stanford; Columbia and Princeton return to them for the following entry. The SAT Reading and Writing section is where a French student has the most work to do; the maths section is where they can aim for the top of the scale, as I explain in SAT Math from a French lycée and taking the SAT from France.

Early Decision and Early Action applications are due in November of terminale, Regular Decision in January, and answers arrive between December and April, based on school reports, before the bac. The application itself is built over three years: the specialities chosen in seconde, activities that grow in première, the tests, then the essays. A family that turns to it at the start of terminale has already lost most of its margin. The cost, and the financial aid that changes it

A year at Harvard costs more than $90,000 all-in for 2026-2027. But the richest universities cover the full demonstrated financial need, including for international students, and about ten admit international students without looking at their ability to pay, including Harvard, Yale, Princeton, MIT, Dartmouth, Brown and Amherst. The others take it into account in their decision: the distinction matters when choosing universities. For international students, the request goes through the College Board's CSS Profile, which translates the family's income and assets into American categories.
