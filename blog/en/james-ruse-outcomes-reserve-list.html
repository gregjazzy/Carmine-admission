Selective schools in Australia

Outcomes and the reserve list

Selective high school outcomes for Year 7 arrive in August of Year 6, in the family's application dashboard. For entry in 2027, the date was August 19, 2026. Each school choice shows one of a small number of results, and the way those results combine follows strict rules that are worth understanding before choosing the order of schools, not after.

I help families read these results and decide what to do next. Here is how they work.

How offers are made

Places are offered on test performance and on the order of school choices. Each school's threshold depends on the number of applicants, their results and the number of places, so it differs from school to school and year to year. When outcomes are first released, a family can receive an offer from one school only: if the child's result reaches the level for several schools, the offer comes from the highest choice. The child may also be placed on reserve lists for schools ranked above the offer.

The practical consequence is that the order of choices matters. Listing James Ruse second behind a school the family wants less can mean receiving an offer from that first school and only a reserve place at James Ruse.

Reserve lists and reserve bands

A reserve position can turn into an offer if places become vacant, up to the end of Term 1 of Year 7. For students who sat the 2027 test, the window extends to the start of Term 4 of Year 7 if the child is attending a comprehensive public high school, and Year 8 places from 2029 will also be filled from the reserve list for children in that situation. The dashboard shows a reserve band, an estimate based on when students in a similar position were offered places the previous year. It is a guide, not a promise.

The performance report

The report places the child in one of four bands for each section: top 10 percent of candidates, next 15 percent, next 25 percent, lowest 50 percent. It shows no raw score and no rank, and the Department does not release either. Read across the four sections, it shows where the child stood relative to that year's cohort: useful for a family deciding whether to try again by another route, and for understanding a reserve position.
