The Reading Test opens the Selective High School Placement Test. It lasts 45 minutes and counts for a quarter of the result. On paper it has 17 questions, but three of them have several parts, so the child gives 38 answers in all, a little over a minute each, while reading every text on a screen.

I work with children on reading for this test every year. Here is what the section contains and what makes the difference.

The texts

The material is deliberately varied: non-fiction and fiction, poetry, magazine articles, reports and extracts from books. A child who reads only one kind of text, novels, for instance, meets genres in the test that they have rarely read closely. Poetry and reports can unsettle a child who rarely reads them, for opposite reasons: one says a great deal in few words, the other buries its point in detail.

One question type deserves attention. In comparison questions, the child reads two short extracts on the same theme and answers questions about how they relate: what they share, where they differ, how each writer treats the subject. That asks for more than understanding each text; it asks the child to hold both in mind at once.

The pace is manageable for a fluent reader and tight for a slow one. Reading on screen adds a small cost: children who are used to annotating a printed page need to get used to holding the text in mind while moving between passage and question. The free online practice tests reproduce the software and are the right place to get used to it.

What builds reading

Reading for this test is not a technique learned in a few weeks. It is built by reading a lot, across genres, and by talking about what one reads: why a writer chose a word, what a character's silence means, what a report is really claiming. Parents can do much of this at home. The Department's own advice is to read anything, any genre, any topic.

Where focused work helps is in precision. Many errors come not from misunderstanding the text but from choosing an answer that is partly true. Practising with the official questions, and explaining why each wrong option is wrong, sharpens exactly that.
