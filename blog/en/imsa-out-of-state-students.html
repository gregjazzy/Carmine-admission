Applying to IMSA from outside Illinois

IMSA is an Illinois public school, but it also accepts a small number of tuition-paying students from outside the state, giving families elsewhere in the United States access to its residential STEM programme. The process is the same as for Illinois applicants, but the calendar is earlier and the cost is very different.

The bar is the same

An out-of-state applicant must live in a US state or territory other than Illinois, be in 8th or 9th grade, and show at least a year of algebra and a year of high school science, taken at school or through an accredited online provider or community college. Beyond that, nothing is lowered or added: the file, the test scores and the evaluations are those an Illinois resident submits.

The cost

Out-of-state families should be prepared to cover annual tuition, room and board of about $55,000 to $57,000. Illinois residents, by contrast, have tuition, housing and meals paid by the state and pay a student fee by income. Invoices are issued in July and first or full payments are due on 1 August.

How out-of-state students are chosen

Out-of-state files are read by the same holistic standard as Illinois ones, and IMSA also weighs geography and demographics when it builds a class. Because places for non-residents are few, a family outside Illinois should treat the file as competitive from the first draft: test taken early, essays finished before the holidays, every evaluation requested on the first day.

The tightest point

Teacher and counselor forms open on 4 January and everything must be in by 15 January. For families outside Illinois, that is eleven days at the end of the holidays. Teachers should be warned in December, and the SAT or ACT taken in the autumn so that official scores are already in.
