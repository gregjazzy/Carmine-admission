French school years vs UK years, US grades and the IB: the table, and the birth-date trap

Equivalences October 2026 6 min read

A family moving countries, or switching from a French school to an international one, always runs into the same question: which year will my child be in? The answer fits in a table, with one nuance that often costs or gains a year: systems do not group children by the same birth dates.

The correspondence between the French lycée and British years is the one UCAS, the UK universities' platform, uses: seconde is presented as Year 11, première as Year 12, terminale as Year 13. The IB programmes cover three age bands: the Primary Years Programme from 3 to 12, the Middle Years Programme from 11 to 16, over five years, and the Diploma Programme from 16 to 19, over two years.

The birth-date trap

In France, children are grouped by calendar year of birth: all those born in the same year start CP together. In England, the school year starts on 1 September, and a child belongs to the cohort born between 1 September and 31 August. In the United States, the rule depends on the state, but the cut-off usually falls around the end of summer.

The consequence: a child born between September and December is one year ahead in France compared with a British or American system. A child born in November who is in CE1 in Paris would be in Year 2, the equivalent of CP, in London. For a family leaving France, that can mean repeating a year; for a family coming back, skipping one, or on the contrary asking to stay back. The table above applies to children born between January and August; for the others, shift by one row, and discuss it with the school.

IB and the French baccalaureate: what the table does not say

A year equivalence is not a curriculum equivalence. A student arriving from the MYP into seconde has not necessarily covered the same maths chapters as a French student, and the method differs: written proof on one side, exploration and investigation on the other. A student moving from the French system into the Diploma Programme must choose six subjects and their levels, which shapes their university options.
