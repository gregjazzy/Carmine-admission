Entering Stuyvesant in 10th grade

Stuyvesant High School on the Hudson, Manhattan. Photo Tdorante10, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

A student who misses Stuyvesant in 8th grade has one more chance. New York City residents in 9th grade for the first time, not repeating, can take the SHSAT again for entry into 10th grade. The rule is the same as in 8th grade: the score is the only criterion, and seats go in score order according to the student's ranked list.

What is different in 9th grade

Who it suits

  • A student who scored close to the cutoff in 8th grade and has clearly progressed since.
  • A student who moved to New York City after the 8th grade test.
  • A student who did not register in 8th grade and now wants a Specialized High School.

It is less suited to a student who scored well below the line in 8th grade: the content is the same, and a year of 9th grade alone rarely closes a large gap.

A second attempt, planned properly

Preparing during a high school year

A 9th grader has a full high school timetable and new grades that matter for college. Preparation should be steady rather than heavy: a diagnostic in spring of 8th grade or early summer, regular work through the summer, and full timed practice in September and October. The test view offered in the spring after the 8th grade test is a valuable starting point, because it shows exactly where points were lost.

The ranked list, again

Registration for 9th graders happens in MySchools in October, like 8th graders, with the same ranked list of testing schools. A student who wants only Stuyvesant can list only Stuyvesant, but every school they would attend should be listed, because an unlisted school can never make an offer.
