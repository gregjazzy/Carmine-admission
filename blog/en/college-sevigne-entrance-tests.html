Collège Sévigné entrance tests: what you need to know

Collège Sévigné, rue Pierre-Nicole, Paris 5th. Photo LPLT, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0

Paris schools November 2026 5 min read

Book a conversation

Sévigné calls its tests "indicative". Many families hear "secondary". That is a mistake: they are the only tests in the whole procedure, and they cover the subjects that most separate applicants. I have students at Sévigné, as at the other leading Paris schools, and every year I prepare children for these tests.

What applicants sit

The tests depend on the track. For sixième (age 11), the bilingual English track tests oral and written English comprehension, French and maths; the English + track tests only French and maths, with no English. Sixième tests take place on a Wednesday afternoon, by invitation, between December and February. Students who do not live in the Paris region sit the tests at their current school, which Sévigné sends them to. For lycée entry into the international section, the school also asks for an English questionnaire completed by the student's current English teacher.

Preparing for Sévigné's tests

I prepare each child for Sévigné's tests, in French, maths and, depending on the track, English, with work timed to the date of their invitation. Let's start with a first conversation about your child.

What the school does not say

Sévigné gives the subjects. It says nothing about the format of the tests, their length, the exercises set, or the level that makes the difference. Yet these details decide, and they are what I know, having followed students at the school and prepared applicants for its tests.

Why strong students lose their place

Fragile foundations in arithmetic and problem solving

Maths separates candidates more than anything. A child who arrives in December of CM2 with shaky arithmetic or little practice with multi-step problems has no time left to rebuild them.

Will your child's foundations hold? Ask for an assessment: three tests at the school's level, and what remains to strengthen.

The wrong track

Aiming for the bilingual track for its prestige, without the written English it requires, means sitting a test one cannot pass. Conversely, English + is not a second choice: French and maths do the selecting, and the bilingual track remains accessible later.

Written French that lags behind

Sévigné gives the humanities a strong place. A child at ease orally but fragile in writing, especially if schooled abroad, pays for it in the French test.

When to start

Example: the round for September 2027. Applications opened on 3 November 2026. For September 2026, sixième tests were held on 3 and 10 December, 14 and 28 January, 4 and 11 February, at 1:30 pm, with answers in mid-February for the bilingual track and mid-March for English +.

Foundations should be checked at the start of CM2, not on the eve of the invitation: a gap in arithmetic or writing takes several months to close.
