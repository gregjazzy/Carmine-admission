Applicants to Ransom Everglades' Upper School add two writing pieces to the file that Middle School applicants do not: a graded paper from the current school year, and the Upper School Applicant Statement. Both are part of the checklist due January 29, and the statement has a further rule: it must be completed before the interview.

I work with Upper School applicants on both. Here is what each involves.

Choosing the graded paper

The paper is not written for the application: it is a piece of schoolwork the student has already done this year, sent with the teacher's grade and comments. That makes the choice the main decision. A good choice is a paper that shows the student arguing or analysing, not summarising; one where the teacher's comments are specific; and one recent enough to reflect the student's current level. Because only work from this school year counts, students applying in the autumn should keep their essays and look for opportunities to write a strong one early in the year.

The statement before the interview

Because the statement is completed before the interview, it can be read before the conversation and become part of it: what a student writes about their interests and reasons for choosing Ransom Everglades will often be the starting point of the questions. A statement that is specific, honest and clearly the student's own makes for a better interview than one that sounds polished but general. Writing it early also helps a student think through what they want to say in person.
