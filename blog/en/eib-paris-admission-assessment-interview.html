EIB Paris: the admission assessment and interview, what you need to know

Paris schools November 2026 5 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

Book a conversation

EIB takes students all year round, and many families conclude that admission is a formality. It is not: the meeting and the assessment decide entry, but also the class, the track and sometimes an extra-cost language programme. I follow students at EIB, and every year I prepare children for admission to Paris bilingual schools.

What the school says

After an online request, the family is called back for a first conversation. Then comes a meeting with the head, at school or by video, often with the child. Depending on the school, a short assessment exercise may be offered: at Grenelle it sometimes happens, the interview being enough in most cases; at Trocadéro an assessment may be offered from CE1 (age 7); at La Jonchère every student is assessed from the second year of nursery, in French, English and maths. In collège and lycée, the head's decision rests on the file and, depending on the profile, on tests and an interview. The €300 application fee is paid before the meeting.

Preparing for EIB admission

I prepare each child for EIB's assessment and interview, in French, English and maths, so that they join the class and track that suit them. Let's start with a first conversation about your child.

What the school does not say

EIB presents the assessment as a way to know the child and offer the most suitable track. True. It does not say that this track may be an immersion class or a reinforcement programme, at extra cost, nor what tips a family towards another school in the network when the class is full. That is what I know through my students.

Why children miss the right place

Overestimated language level

From CE1 to CM2, the half-English schools require good understanding in both languages. A child at ease in only one is offered reinforcement, or another school.

Will your child's English and French hold up? Tell me about them: I will tell you which track to aim for.

Maths lagging behind

EIB follows the French curriculum, often with the Singapore method in primary. A child from another system may be out of step, and the assessment shows it.

A meeting taken lightly

The head meets the family as much as the child. A family that knows what it expects from a bilingual school, and why this one, stands out.

When to start

Requests for the following September open in October, and November is the most sought-after time; the last places often go from January. The assessment is therefore prepared from the start of the school year.

What I do for your family

Every year I see children placed in a class or track below their potential, for not having prepared the assessment. My job is to avoid that. It starts with an assessment in French, English and maths at EIB's level; I then prepare the child for the assessment and the family for the meeting.
