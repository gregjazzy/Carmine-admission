Best lycée in France: what the rankings say, and what they keep quiet

Book a conversation

Every spring, families search for the best lycée in France (the lycée covers the last three years of school, from seconde to terminale, ending with the baccalauréat) and find league tables where Louis-le-Grand, Henri-IV, Stanislas and Franklin compete for first place. These lists have their use: they say which schools succeed at the bac. They do not say which one will take your child furthest, or which one will open the schools and universities they are aiming for. I know several of the lycées at the top of these tables from the inside, and every year I see what the rankings leave out.

Where the rankings come from

The only public data are the lycée value-added indicators, published every spring by the French Ministry of Education for the previous bac session: the pass rate, the honours rate, and the access rate, the share of students entering seconde who obtain the bac in the same school. For each, the ministry calculates an expected result given the students the school takes in; value added is the gap between the two. It states that these indicators "are not intended to establish a ranking". Press league tables are not ministry publications.

For Jeannine Manuel, the ministry publishes no value added for 2024 or 2025. At Louis-le-Grand, 230 of the 309 candidates obtained the highest honours (mention très bien), 83 of them with the jury's congratulations.

The raw rates are all close to 100%: they separate no one. Value added hovers around zero, which means these results were expected given the students these schools recruit. The access rate is what separates them: 85% with a gap of −8 means that some students who entered in seconde did not sit the bac in the school, whether they left of their own accord or were asked to. It is the indicator to look at when you wonder whether a school keeps the students who struggle.

The ministry also publishes each school's social position index, calculated from the parents' occupations: 120.2 on average nationally in September 2025, 137.7 in Paris, and from 142.3 to 160.2 for the schools in this table. The lycées at the top of the league tables are first of all those that recruit the most privileged students with the best grades. That says nothing against them: a class where everyone works fast pulls a good student upwards.

What the rankings keep quiet

A league table stops at the bac, which all these schools pass. What matters to a family starts afterwards. Behind identical honours rates, some lycées send most of their students to the top prépas, others also know how to get them into London, Lausanne or the United States, and their reputation outside France is not the same. Nor is their grading: an average earned in one is not read like the same average earned in another, and admissions panels know it. Some carry a good student who is not at the top; others let them wear out in the bottom quarter of the class, and the access rate sometimes keeps a trace of it.

None of this appears in any table. It is known by following students in these schools year after year, knowing their teachers and leadership teams, and seeing where they are admitted afterwards. The best lycée for what?

A student aiming for a top scientific prépa (the two-year classes that prepare for the grandes écoles) should look at where the students admitted to those classes come from: that is the subject of Which lycée for a top prépa, with Parcoursup data. A student aiming for London, Lausanne or the United States will need a school that knows how to write recommendation letters in English and offers an international section or the BFI. The two do not always overlap: the best lycée in France for one is not the best for the other. For Paris, school by school, see the best high schools in Paris.

Best lycée in Paris: how to get into seconde

In Paris state schools, entry into seconde goes through Affelnet, the online allocation system. Its points scale gives the main weight to catchment: 32,640 points for a school in the student's first sector, against at most 9,600 points for grades. Henri-IV and Louis-le-Grand are the exception. A student in troisième (the last year of middle school) at a Paris collège and living in Paris applies to them through Affelnet, as first and second choices only; the two schools then count as their first sector wherever they live, and the ranking is decided on results, with a bonus for scholarship students and a bonus linked to the social index of their collège.

Students who are not at school in Paris, or do not live there, submit an application on a dedicated platform, open from mid-January to early April. The education authority assesses it on three criteria: academic excellence, commitment and motivation, scholarship status. This is the route for families returning from abroad.

In the state-contracted private sector, each school sets its own procedure: application, school reports, sometimes tests and an interview, with a calendar that often starts in the autumn of troisième. They are detailed for Stanislas, Franklin and Jeannine Manuel.
