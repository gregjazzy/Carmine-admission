Lycée International or a private international school: how to choose

Schools near Paris November 2026 4 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

Book a conversation

For an international family living west of Paris, the hesitation is classic: the Lycée International, a state school, or a private international school, the American School of Paris, the International School of Paris or the British School of Paris. The two worlds do not cost the same, and above all do not lead to the same place.

Cost

At the Lycée International, the French part of teaching is free; only section hours are paid: in 2026-2027, from €4,051 to €5,746 a year in the British Section and from €5,100 to €6,750 in the American Section when the family pays itself. In the region's private international schools, lycée tuition exceeds €30,000 a year.

The right school for your child

Lycée International or a private school: the choice commits the curriculum, the diploma and the universities open. I know both worlds, and I tell you which will truly serve your child.

Curriculum, and diploma

The Lycée International follows the French curriculum, with section hours in the child's language, and leads to the BFI. The workload is heavy: maths and science are taught in French, at the national pace. Private international schools follow an international programme and generally prepare the IB or an English-language diploma. For a child likely to stay in France or to hesitate between France and abroad, the BFI keeps more doors open; for a child who will soon move on, an international programme ensures continuity.

Torn between state and private? Tell me about your child: I will tell you which path serves them best.

Admission

At the Lycée International, admission turns on the section's language, through a test, on a tight calendar. Private schools recruit by their own rules, often throughout the year. A child with fragile French can enter the Lycée International through the Français Spécial class, provided their mother tongue is strong.
