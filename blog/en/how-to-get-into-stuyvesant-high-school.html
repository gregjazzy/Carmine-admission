How to get into Stuyvesant

Most selective schools weigh many things. Stuyvesant High School, on Chambers Street in Lower Manhattan, weighs one. Admission to New York City's eight testing Specialized High Schools is decided solely by the Specialized High Schools Admissions Test, the SHSAT. Grades, recommendations, interviews and activities play no part.

Who can take the test

  • A New York City resident.
  • In 8th grade, or in 9th grade for the first time, not repeating the year.
  • From any kind of school: public, charter, private, parochial or homeschooled.

The sequence

  1. Register in MySchools between early October and 30 October, ranking the testing Specialized High Schools in order of preference.
  2. Test in November. Eighth graders at public middle schools ending in 8th grade test at school on a weekday; other eighth graders test at a central site on a weekend; ninth graders test on a separate Saturday.
  3. Receive an offer in March, or no offer, together with the score.

How seats are allocated

The student with the highest score receives an offer to their first-choice school, then the next highest, and so on down the list. When a student's first choice is full, they are considered for their second, then third. A student is never considered for a school they did not list. For 2026 admission, the lowest score that earned a Stuyvesant seat was 561, the highest cutoff of the eight schools.

The test, from fall 2026

The SHSAT is now taken on computer and is adaptive: the difficulty of later questions depends on earlier answers. It has two sections, English Language Arts and Mathematics, with 50 questions each and a standard time of 180 minutes. Students choose which section to start with. Scaled scores from the two sections are added into a composite score that sets the ranking.

Other routes

Students from disadvantaged backgrounds who score just below the cutoff may be invited to a summer Discovery program leading to a seat. Test preparation is also offered through the city's DREAM program for eligible 7th graders. And a student who misses in 8th grade can test again in 9th grade, once, for entry in 10th grade.
