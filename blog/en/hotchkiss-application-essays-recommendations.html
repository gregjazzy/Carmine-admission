The Hotchkiss application: Gateway, essays and four recommendations

The main building of The Hotchkiss School, Lakeville, Connecticut. Photo Adrien Delessert, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 2.5

US boarding schools November 2026 6 min read

Book a conversation

Hotchkiss uses Gateway to Prep Schools, the online application shared by many independent schools. It has two parts: the Candidate Profile, with basic information about the student and family, which Hotchkiss would like to receive before the interview; and the Candidate Project, the formal application with essay questions and activities. Around them sit the transcript and four recommendations. Each piece is weighed equally, and each file is read and discussed by several members of the committee.

The pieces, and who sends them

PieceWho provides it
Candidate ProfileThe family, as early as possible
Candidate Project: essays, activitiesThe student
Transcript, current and past gradesThe school, directly
Principal or counselor recommendationThe school, directly
English and math recommendationsLast year's or this year's teachers
Personal recommendationAn adult who knows the student well
Arts or athletics supplements, optionalThe student, through SlideRoom or the Athletic Interest Form

Uploads through Gateway are limited to one file per form and under one megabyte; teachers who struggle can email documents to the admission office.

The essays

Hotchkiss looks for strong writing skills, and the essays are the only place the committee reads the student directly. The good ones share a pattern: one specific subject rather than a summary of a life, told with concrete detail, ending somewhere the reader did not expect. A paragraph about a single afternoon in a lab, or a disagreement with a teammate, says more about independence and character than a page of adjectives.

Two mistakes recur. Essays polished by adults until they no longer sound fourteen; and essays that repeat the activities list. The committee has both the list and the recommendations; the essay should add what neither contains.

Essays in the student's own voice

We help students find their subject, draft and revise their Gateway essays without losing their voice, and present their activities so that the whole file tells one coherent story.

The recommendations

Four voices: the principal or counselor, who places the student in their class; the English and math teachers, who describe the student at work; and a personal recommender, who sees the student outside school. Hotchkiss asks families to send requests through Gateway by November 1. The best way to help recommenders is simple: ask early, ask teachers who know the student well rather than the most senior ones, and give the personal recommender a short note of what the application already covers, so their letter adds something new.

Arts and athletics

Students with a serious interest in the visual or performing arts can submit work through SlideRoom; athletes can complete the Athletic Interest Form, with video. Meetings with coaches or department heads are arranged directly with them. These supplements matter when the talent is real and sustained; otherwise they add little.

Reading the file as the committee does

Most admitted students have A-range grades, excellent recommendations, strong writing and involvement in their community, with tests in support. A file works when its parts agree: the essays show the curiosity the English teacher describes, the activities match the interests mentioned at interview, the counselor's letter confirms the independence the student claims. That coherence is what several readers, discussing the file together, notice.

How I work on Hotchkiss applications

I help the student choose essay subjects, then work through drafts with questions rather than rewrites, so the final text is theirs. I review the activities section for clarity and emphasis, and help families plan the recommendations by November 1. The goal is a file whose pieces support each other.
