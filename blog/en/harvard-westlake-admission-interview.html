The Harvard-Westlake interview

Book a conversation

The interview is the part of a Harvard-Westlake application where the school meets the applicant in person. It is also the part with the strictest logistics, and families who treat it casually can lose it altogether.

Securing the interview

  • Submit the application form by 13 November. For 7th and 9th grade applicants, a form submitted by that date at 11:59 pm Pacific time guarantees an invitation to interview.
  • Accept the time you are given. Interviews are scheduled on weekdays during school hours. The school asks families to make every effort to accept the appointment offered, and cannot guarantee an interview if a family declines or reschedules.
  • Plan for a school day off. The applicant will miss some class at their current school; it is worth warning teachers early.

Two formats

The interview can take place in person or on Zoom. Families who come in person also receive a personal tour of the middle school campus, led by a current student ambassador, on the day of the interview. That tour is a chance for the applicant to see classes in session and arrive at the conversation with something concrete to say.

Questionnaire and interview, together

I prepare Harvard-Westlake applicants for the questionnaire and the interview as one conversation, so that what they write and what they say form a clear picture of who they are.

The questionnaire comes first

The student questionnaire, a set of short-answer questions about the applicant and their interests, must be submitted at least two days before the interview. The interviewer is likely to have read it. That has two consequences: the answers should be the student's own, and the student should be ready to talk about anything they wrote. A questionnaire written by a parent creates an awkward interview.

Is your child's interview booked? Let's prepare the questionnaire and the conversation.

What the conversation looks for

Harvard-Westlake seeks kind, curious and engaged students eager to contribute to the school. An interview that shows curiosity is one where the applicant asks questions back, talks with real detail about something they love, and listens. Kindness shows in how a student speaks about classmates, teammates and teachers. Engagement shows in what they do when nobody asks them to.

Preparing without rehearsing

  • Talk at home about the questionnaire answers, so the student can expand on them naturally.
  • Arrange a couple of conversations with an unfamiliar adult, in person and on video.
  • Avoid scripts: an eleven or fourteen year old who recites sounds like it.

How I prepare applicants
