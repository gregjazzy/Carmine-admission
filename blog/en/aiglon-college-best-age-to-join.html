Aiglon College: what is the best age to join?

Villars-sur-Ollon, home of Aiglon. Photo Bledard92, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0

What is the right age to join Aiglon? Families often ask with school level in mind. In Villars, the answer depends as much on a child's maturity as on their grades, and on how the school organises its early years. I have helped students win places at Aiglon and Rosenberg, and fifteen at Le Rosey, and every year I see families arrive either too early for their child or too late for the school.

Three ways in

Aiglon takes students from Year 3, at around seven, up to Year 12. In between, three moments stand out. Entry to the junior school, for the youngest. Entry at the start of senior school, as the school prepares students for IGCSEs. And entry in Year 12, for the International Baccalaureate, the latest possible, which requires coming to campus for the interview and assessments.

How a child is assessed changes with age too. The youngest have a "Wonder Years experience": a lesson with other pupils, break time, a short written piece, some reading and a maths quiz. From Year 5 to Year 11, the interview and assessments can take place online if the family cannot travel. In Year 12, everything happens in Villars. Choosing the age of entry also means choosing the kind of assessment your child will face.

Day pupil first, boarder later

One point many families miss: in Years 3 and 4, pupils are day students only. Boarding opens from Year 5, at around nine. For a family that does not live near Villars, the realistic entry point is therefore nine at the earliest. About three in four students board, and the junior school has around a hundred pupils, against four hundred in the senior school.

Why the school prefers early arrivals

Aiglon is clear that the ideal is to join early and stay for most of one's schooling, because its curriculum is designed to move smoothly from junior to senior school, then from IGCSE to IB. A student who arrives at ten knows the school, its demands, mountain life; one who arrives at fifteen has to learn everything at once, the working language, the pace and boarding life. Both can succeed, but the second has less margin.

Joining for the IB, the narrowest door

Arriving in Year 12 remains possible, but it is the most demanding case. The student must come to campus for the interview and assessments, sit an additional mathematics test, and settle within barely two years into a programme others have been preparing for a long time. I rarely advise against it, but I never recommend it to a child whose written English is not yet solid.

Visiting before deciding

The simplest way to test the right age is often to go and see. Aiglon offers an initial visit of about an hour and a half, with the admissions team and a campus tour, before any application is made. Watching a nine-year-old or a twelve-year-old walk through the junior or senior school tells a family a great deal. Decisions are then made every month, so a well-prepared family does not lose much time once the right year is chosen.

The right age for your child

The ideal age cannot be read off a chart. A curious, independent nine-year-old who enjoys group life can thrive in boarding very early; another child, at twelve, may still need home. I look at three things: maturity, written English, and a taste for effort and the outdoors, which count at Aiglon more than anywhere else.
