Aiglon College: what is the best age to join?

Villars-sur-Ollon, home of Aiglon. Photo Bledard92, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0

Boarding schools November 2026 4 min read

Aiglon welcomes children from age 7, in Year 3. But the age of entry is not neutral: it decides boarding or day status, the admission tests, and how the child will live the school. And on this the school gives clear advice.

The landmarks

In Years 3 and 4, pupils are day students only. Boarding opens in Year 5, around age 9, in the two junior school houses, La Baita for boys and La Casa for girls, each home to up to about thirty pupils. From Year 9, students join one of the eight senior school houses, of around fifty students each. The school accepts new students at any stage up to Year 12, the start of the International Baccalaureate, but never in Year 13: the IB and the Aiglon diploma are two-year courses.

Joining at the right age, ready

I help you choose the moment when your child has the best chance of being admitted and thriving at Aiglon, then prepare them for the interview and assessments that match their age. My aim: that they join at the right time, and stay.

Why the school advises arriving early

Aiglon says it plainly: for the full benefit of its curriculum, it is ideal to join early and stay for most of one's schooling. The programme is designed as a progression, from junior school to the IGCSEs and then the IB, with expeditions that grow in difficulty year after year. A student who arrives in Year 12 goes straight into the two busiest years, must do their admissions visit in person, and has not lived the rest. They can succeed, but they discover Aiglon just when the school asks the most of them.

At what age should your child apply? Let's talk: I will tell you where their chances, and their balance, are best.

What each age requires

Young children are assessed through a morning of class, reading, writing and arithmetic; older ones through an interview and online assessments of reasoning and language; in Year 12, through a compulsory visit and an additional maths test. The older the student, the more their written English matters, since they will follow the IGCSEs and then the IB in English.
