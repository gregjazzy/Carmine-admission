University Outcomes After Jeannine Manuel: Ivy League, Oxbridge, EPFL

Photo: École Jeannine Manuel

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. Has worked with students of École Jeannine Manuel for more than ten years.

École Jeannine Manuel is often described as "a good lycée". That undersells it: what sets it apart is where its students go next. For more than ten years, I have followed students of the school through their schooling and met them again when they apply to university. Here is what those outcomes reveal, destination by destination, and what they mean for a family still deciding whether to apply.

American universities

The school states that it sends more students to Ivy League universities, and to Oxford and Cambridge, than any other lycée in France. Several things work in its students' favour. The BFI is taught in the American section, with Advanced Placement courses that American universities know. The school also offers the IB, an international diploma. And it prepares its students early: from troisième (age 14), they attend presentations on higher education and on the countries where they might study.

The rest depends on the student: grades, activities, essays. For the choice between the two lycée programmes, see what the BFI changes after graduation.

British universities

In the UK, offers are conditional: each university sets, for the French baccalauréat, an average and subject grades to achieve in July. For Oxford and Cambridge, depending on the course, an admissions test and an interview come on top. None of this can be improvised in the final year: the personal statement is built on what the student did the year before, and the interview takes months of practice.

The UCAS application, the personal statement and the Oxbridge interview all need preparation. Let's talk about your child's ambitions: I guide them through their applications.

Switzerland: EPFL and ETH Zurich

EPFL admits holders of the French baccalauréat on their grades, with requirements in mathematics and physics; there is no admission on a portfolio. The specialities chosen in the last two years therefore count directly. ETH Zurich, which teaches its bachelor's programmes in German, has its own, stricter conditions. I cover EPFL in getting into EPFL from a French high school.

French prépas and grandes écoles

A student who takes the BFI obtains a French baccalauréat: the file goes through Parcoursup, the French admissions platform, without conversion, towards the classes préparatoires, Sciences Po or double degrees. That is a real difference from the IB, which the school also offers, and whose file French panels read less directly. For a family aiming at both sides of the Channel or the Atlantic, the choice between BFI and IB at the end of seconde matters as much as the choice of school.

Why the choice is made early

If you are reading this, your child is probably in primary school, middle school or early lycée. That is the right time. Joining Jeannine Manuel in sixième or seconde shapes outcomes that play out five or seven years later, and waiting until the final year to think about universities means arriving long after the race has started.

The families who get the most from it choose the school for what comes after, not for the school years themselves. For families returning from abroad, see joining Jeannine Manuel when returning from abroad; for what sets the school apart, why Jeannine Manuel is one of the best schools in France.
