Westminster School goes co-educational: what changes for girls

College, the King's Scholars' house at Westminster School, from the garden. Photo stevecadman, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 2.0

London schools November 2026 6 min read

Book a conversation

For most of its history Westminster was a boys' school with girls in the Sixth Form, a pattern it has followed for more than fifty years. It is now becoming fully co-educational. Westminster Under School and Westminster School are both moving to mixed year groups, and by 2030 every year, from Reception to the Upper Sixth, will include girls and boys. For families of daughters, a school that used to be reachable only at 16 is opening up at every entry point.

The Under School has added Chapter House, a new building on its central London site designed equally for girls and boys aged 11 to 13.

What does not change

Selection remains academic, and every application is treated in the same way. At 13+, girls follow exactly the process described for boys: registration from Year 5, ISEB Pre-Tests in Year 6, Westminster's own papers and an interview, an unconditional offer in February. At 16+, Westminster notes that the great majority of its candidates are already girls, with a significant number of boys, and that boarding or day status makes no difference to selection.

Choices that open up for a girl

Until now, a family wanting Westminster for a daughter waited for the Sixth Form, with its 600 or so candidates for about 80 places. A girl can now aim earlier. For a daughter at a primary school ending in Year 6, the 11+ route offers a through place; for one at a prep school running to Year 8, 13+ from 2028. A family weighing Westminster against London's established girls' schools, such as Godolphin and Latymer or North London Collegiate, now faces a real choice at 11 and 13, not only at 16.

The first cohorts of girls at 13+ will be the first in their houses and year groups. Some girls will relish being pioneers; others would rather join a structure already settled. That is a question of temperament worth discussing at home and at an open day.

The rules that still apply

  • A child applying at 11+ cannot apply at 13+ at the same time.
  • Before 16, international pupils need a parent living in the UK; Westminster takes no international boarders under 16.
  • Boarding is weekly and term-time only; King's Scholars, girls and boys alike, board in College.
  • Bursaries depend on strict London residence conditions.

How I prepare girls for Westminster

The preparation is the same as for any candidate, because the bar is the same: problem solving in mathematics, wide and ambitious reading, writing with precision, and the ease to talk about real interests at interview. For the Sixth Form, the four subject papers and the interview day. For the most able, The Challenge in Year 8 and a King's Scholarship.
