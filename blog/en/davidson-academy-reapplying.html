Reapplying to Davidson

The University of Nevada, Reno campus, home of the Davidson Academy. Photo NevadaMarCom, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

A "not this year" from the Davidson Academy is not final. Any student who does not score high enough on the Academic Readiness Assessment is welcome to apply again the following academic year, and the school notes that it is not unusual, though certainly not guaranteed, for students to get in on their second try. The year in between is what makes the difference.

I work with families preparing a second application. Here is how it works.

What the year should change

The assessment is scored by area: mathematics 30 points, science reasoning 30, language proficiency 10, critical reading and discussion 25, written expression 25, affective traits 25. The Academy looks for 80 percent, and accepts 70 percent with a strong file. A declined student was below that line somewhere, and the year should target that place: expository writing for most students, since very few applicants have been taught it; Algebra I or Geometry readiness for others; for some, the work habits the proctors observed.

The bar moves too. A student who reapplies a year older is compared with older Academy students, so standing still is falling behind. Progress has to be real, not just familiarity with the format.

Keeping the file strong

Recommendations, grades and test results carry weight, especially for a student near the 70 percent line. A year of visible progress, a stronger test score if a new one is required, and recommenders who can describe how the student has grown all help the second application read differently from the first.

How I help students reapply to Davidson
