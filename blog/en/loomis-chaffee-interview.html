The Loomis Chaffee interview

Grubbs Quadrangle, Loomis Chaffee, Windsor, Connecticut. Photo Daderot, Wikimedia Commons, CC0

US boarding schools November 2026 4 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

Book a conversation

At Loomis Chaffee, the interview is optional on paper and close to essential in practice. The school says it is not required but strongly encouraged, and in a holistic process it is the one moment when the committee hears the student speak for themselves.

Three formats, one chance

FormatDetail
On campusTour then interview with an admission officer or faculty member; about 90 minutes in all; weekdays when classes are in session, until mid-January
OnlineWeekdays; same format as on campus
Off campusIn US and international cities the team visits, with an admission officer, as time allows; registration link sent by email in advance

Each student may have only one interview, whatever the format, and only for the coming school year.

How the conversation runs

The interview lasts about 30 minutes with the student alone, followed by about 15 minutes with parents or guardians. Loomis strongly recommends that at least one parent attend, both to give context on the candidate and to ask how the school might fit the family's values and goals.

Thirty minutes that count

I help students prepare to talk naturally about their interests, their reasons for Loomis and what they would bring, without a script.

Book a conversation

Booking it

  • First, the inquiry form: it triggers an email with the link to schedule a tour and interview.
  • Book early: campus places are limited and run only until mid-January.
  • After 1 December: only students who have submitted the Candidate Profile and the application fee can book.
  • International residents: must submit the English Proficiency Form before scheduling.

What a good interview shows

Loomis looks for students who will contribute to a close community, and who are, as its students describe each other, glad to see others succeed. A candidate who can speak with specific examples about what they love doing, how they work with others and why Loomis rather than another school gives the interviewer what a transcript cannot.

How I help

I have students at Loomis Chaffee, and for seven years I have worked with around fifteen families a year aiming at the most selective schools. Before an interview, I run one or two practice conversations, then help the student refine their examples and questions, so that the real one feels like a conversation rather than a test.
