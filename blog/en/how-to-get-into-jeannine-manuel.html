How to Get Into Jeannine Manuel: The Complete Admission Guide

Photo: École Jeannine Manuel

EJM Admission January 2026 · updated November 2026 6 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. Has worked with students of École Jeannine Manuel for more than ten years.

Book a conversation

For more than ten years, I have worked with students of École Jeannine Manuel, from their admission to the school all the way to the university or grande école they are aiming for. Every year, around fifteen families entrust me with preparing their application. Here is how admission works, from choosing the year group to the school's decision, and what the school's website does not say.

Before the application: choosing the right year group

The school only truly opens its doors at five levels: moyenne section (age 4), grande section (age 5), CP (the first year of primary school, age 6), sixième (the first year of middle school, age 11) and seconde (the first year of lycée, age 15). These are its entry years. From CE1 to CM2, from cinquième to troisième and in première, a place only exists if a student leaves. There is no entry at all into terminale, the final year.

Families are rarely told this, yet it is the first decision. Applying for CM2 often means applying for a place that will not open up, whereas sixième, one year later, opens a whole class. A family that is not English-speaking has every reason to aim for CP, the last year in which the school requires no English: from CE1 onward, it does not accept children who are beginners in English. For a child who does not yet speak French, there is an adaptation programme from CE1 to troisième, with places allocated through the year, outside the usual calendar.

Which year to aim for, this year or the next: tell me about your child's situation and I will tell you where their real chances lie.

The procedure, step by step

  1. The information session, in person or by webinar, in French or in English.
  2. The online application, on the OpenApply platform, which opens at the end of September for the following school year. For families living in France, it closes in mid-January.
  3. The application fee: €350, non-refundable.
  4. The documents. School reports for the last two years and the current year; a letter about the family, written by someone close to it; a letter from the child's current school, unless that school is under contract with the French state; a speech and language assessment for grande section and CP; a motivation letter from the student for seconde and première; a standardised test if the child attends a private school outside the French national curriculum that offers one. An application only goes forward to pre-selection once it is complete.
  5. The assessment and the interview, for the applications that are retained.
  6. The decision, by email.

Example: the 2027 admissions round. Applications close on 12 January 2027 for families living in France; there is no deadline for families moving from abroad. Decisions at the end of March for CP and sixième, from mid to late April for the other year groups.

What the school assesses, by age

From moyenne section to CP, the child takes part in small-group activities led by two teachers, in French or English as the family prefers, and the school meets both parents for half an hour. From CE1 to CM2, the applications that are retained lead to a short academic assessment in Paris, in English, French or mathematics. From cinquième onward, written tests come first, and the student attends the interview with their parents. In seconde, the motivation letter and assessments in all three subjects are added.

Find out where your child stands before the tests: I give them three tests at the school's level, in French, English and mathematics. Ask for an assessment.

What the school does not publish

The school's website lists the subjects. It says nothing about the format of the tests, how long they last or the level expected at each age. Nothing either about what is really at stake in the interview, who conducts it, or what tips the balance when two candidates have the same grades. No admission rate is published, nor the number of places that open up outside the entry years.

That is exactly what I know. Ten years working every week with students of the school, seven years preparing children for these tests and interviews: I know what they are asked, how the interviews go and which profiles the school chooses. I pass this on to every family I work with, so that their child arrives prepared, with the best possible chance of getting in.

The mistakes I see every year

Underestimating the level in mathematics

Good grades at another school say little about the level expected at Jeannine Manuel. In mathematics, its students are well above average, and a child who shines elsewhere can find themselves struggling on the day of the assessment.

A family letter that reads like a self-recommendation

The school asks for a portrait of the family by someone who knows it. A letter signed by a friend but clearly written by the parents stands out, and it weakens the application.

A file that drags on

An application is only reviewed once it is complete. A speech and language assessment booked too late, a letter from the current school that never arrives, and the application waits while others move ahead.

Only one school

A child placed on the waiting list who wants to try again must submit a new application the following year. Families who have prepared nothing elsewhere find themselves without a solution in April.
