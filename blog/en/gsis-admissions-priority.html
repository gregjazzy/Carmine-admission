At German Swiss International School, passing the entrance assessment is necessary but rarely sufficient. Places, especially in the English International Stream, go to applicants in an order set by the school's admissions priority, and assessment invitations themselves are sent in that order. A family that understands the priority list knows early how realistic a place is, and what it can change.

I go through this list with every family I accompany to GSIS. Here it is.

The six levels

PriorityWho
1German, Swiss or Austrian passport holders; Infrastructure Debenture holders
2Siblings of a student currently at GSIS
3Children of GSIS staff
4Children of GSIS alumni
5Holders of an existing, vacant debenture
6Everyone else, by the date the application was received

Priority applies after a successful assessment, and in both streams. The school keeps the final decision on every application, and does not disclose an applicant's position on the waitlist or other applicants' priority.

What the priority means in practice

For a family at level 6, the date of application is the only lever, which is why applications are accepted year-round, from birth. Applying when a child is two rather than four can change their position considerably for Kindergarten entry. For the English International Stream, where demand is highest, invitations to assess are not guaranteed; a child who is not invited because of the waitlist keeps the original application date and is deferred to the next available year group.

Debentures sit inside this list in two places. An Infrastructure Debenture, at HKD 6,000,000 and bought before the annual admissions process, gives first priority, equal to the passports. An existing debenture that is vacant gives level 5 once upgraded to its current value. The Capital Levy, the annual alternative to a debenture for new students, carries lower priority than any debenture.

Priority is not admission

Priority decides the order in which applicants are considered, not the result. Every applicant must pass the assessment, and in the English stream must pass all parts of it, with English expected at the level of a native speaker of the same age. A level 1 applicant who does not meet that bar is not admitted, and a child who fails is deferred for at least two years.
