Collège Sévigné: entering sixième, bilingual English or English +

Collège Sévigné, rue Pierre-Nicole, Paris 5th. Photo LPLT, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0

At Collège Sévigné, you do not enter sixième (age 11), you enter a track. The school offers two, each with its own entrance test. The choice is not an administrative detail: it decides the tests, the expected level and what follows. For September 2027, applications opened on 3 November 2026.

Two tracks, two tests

The bilingual English, humanities, sciences track assumes the child has already practised English intensively in primary school: it becomes a language of schooling, written and spoken. New entrants take a test assessing their oral and written English, but also their French and maths. The English +, humanities, sciences track is open to all profiles: a placement test in French and maths, without English, shows where the child stands, and the track offers stronger exposure to English, especially speaking.

The right track, and the test passed

I help you choose the track that suits your child, then prepare them for Sévigné's test, in French, maths and, if needed, English. My aim: that they join the track where they will give their best.

How to choose

A family whose child attended a bilingual primary school will naturally aim for the bilingual track. A family whose child has ordinary school English will often do better with English +: French and maths do the selecting there, and the bilingual track remains accessible later. The trap would be to aim for the bilingual track for its prestige, without the English it requires: the test shows it quickly.

Bilingual or English + for your child? Tell me about them: I will tell you which track opens the best door.

What the school looks for

Sévigné says it does not look for uniform profiles but for distinctive personalities, and claims "excellence without competition". It is a deliberately small, secular school, where philosophy is taught from the last year of nursery, and which gives real weight to the humanities and classical languages. The test measures the level; the whole application shows the student.
