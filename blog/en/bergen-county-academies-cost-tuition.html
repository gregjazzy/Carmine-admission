For a school with an average SAT of 1466 among its seniors and research laboratories with electron microscopes, BCA costs families nothing in tuition. It is a public career and technical education high school for Bergen County residents, and the money flows between public bodies rather than from parents.

Who pays what

ItemPaid by
TuitionThe student's home school district (the "sending district")
Transport to and from schoolThe home district; families ask their local Board of Education
Application and entrance testNo fee is mentioned in BCA's application materials
Transport to sports practiceProvided from each campus; no late bus after practice ends

Students from private or charter schools

Because tuition and transport come from the home district, a student at a private, parochial or charter school must complete a form signed by the local Board of Education, confirming that they are registered in their town of residence. If admitted, every student must be registered in that district, whichever middle school they attended.

Free, and fiercely contested

With no tuition and an acceptance rate of 19.5 %, every seat is contested. I prepare students for the test, the essay and the interview that decide.

The real price: time

The school day runs from 8:00 a.m. to 4:10 p.m., and later for students who play a sport. Wednesdays include two hours of interdisciplinary projects in the first three years, and seniors complete an internship. BCA tells families plainly that it seeks those fully committed to this extended day, and that a child with substantial obligations outside school, especially ones that clash with it, may not be the right fit.

What families still pay for

Day-to-day costs remain those of any high school: supplies, clubs and trips where they carry a charge, sport equipment, and getting home after practice when no late bus runs. For many families, the comparison is with private schools in the region, and on tuition alone BCA wins by a wide margin.
