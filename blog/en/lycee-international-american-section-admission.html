American Section of the Lycée International: admission, tests and cost

Schools near Paris November 2026 5 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

Book a conversation

The American Section of the Lycée International, founded over half a century ago on Dwight Eisenhower's initiative, is one of the most sought-after of the fourteen sections. Its students follow the full French curriculum at the Lycée International and, on top, teaching in English by English-speaking teachers. At the end, the BFI: a 100% pass rate for the class of 2026, with 38% highest honours and 8% with the jury's congratulations.

Whom it admits

Admission rests on overall academic achievement and the likelihood of success in a bilingual, bicultural programme. The section looks for students with native or near-native English, vocabulary, language skills and reading included, and an affinity with American culture. French is not required for candidates to the Français Spécial class, but specific academic criteria apply. Favourable recommendations go to the headmaster, who decides.

The tests

In primary, an individual English assessment of 30 to 60 minutes with a section teacher. From sixième to terminale, a written English exam, in groups, of 45 to 90 minutes, on site or online for candidates living outside the region; selected candidates then have a 30-minute interview with a teacher.

Example: the round for September 2027. Online applications from 1 December 2026, until 8 January 2027 for sixième to terminale and until 22 January 2027 for nursery to CM2. No mid-year entries in 2026-2027.

What it costs

In 2026-2027, per the section's grid: €5,100 a year from nursery to CM2, €5,260 in collège, €6,750 in lycée when the family pays itself, roughly double when an employer covers the fees. Application: €225 up to CM2, €250 after. On admission, €2,000, of which €1,000 is an advance on fees.
