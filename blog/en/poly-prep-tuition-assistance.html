In 2026-27, more than 24 percent of Poly Prep students received tuition assistance, sharing over $14.5 million. All of it is based on need: the school awards no merit scholarships for academic, athletic or extracurricular achievement. It also says plainly that requests exceed the funds available, which makes the timing and completeness of an application matter as much as the figures in it.

How the award is set

Clarity builds a financial profile from income, savings and investments, monthly expenses, debts and tax information, and proposes an initial figure. The school's Financial Aid Committee then reviews each case in context, with no single formula, and sets an award according to demonstrated need and the funds still available. Poly Prep notes that many families with moderate or dual incomes receive partial support, and encourages any family for whom full tuition would be a hardship to apply.

Aid beyond tuition

An award at Poly Prep can reach well past tuition: after-school programs in the Lower and Middle Schools, domestic and international travel, athletic trips, music lessons, one-on-one SAT preparation and tutoring, college visits, summer academic courses, graphing calculators and devices. The school's aim is that a student on aid can take part in everything the school offers.

Because the budget is fixed and allocated in one round, the school has no flexibility to add grants once it is spent. A complete file on December 15 is the only safe plan.
