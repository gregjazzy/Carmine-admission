Westminster Sixth Form entry: 75 to 85 places at 16+

College, the King's Scholars' house at Westminster School, from the garden. Photo stevecadman, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 2.0

London schools November 2026 7 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

Book a conversation

Westminster's Sixth Form has admitted girls for more than half a century, and its 16+ entry remains one of the most sought-after in the country: 600 to 650 candidates sit the entrance exams each year, about 190 are interviewed, and between 75 and 85 receive offers, with a waiting list. Most candidates are girls, though a significant number of boys apply, and every application is treated in the same way. Selection is entirely academic: wishing to board neither helps nor hinders.

Four subjects, chosen at registration

When registering, each candidate chooses the four subjects they intend to study at A level, and these are the four subjects they will be examined in: a 45-minute paper in each. The subjects are arranged in teaching blocks designed around the combinations most Westminster pupils need for their university choices, so the choice has to fit the blocks. Westminster takes into account that candidates have covered only one year of their GCSE syllabus when papers are set; some departments publish sample papers, others describe what to expect.

The strategic point: the four subjects are both the exam and the future timetable. A candidate should choose the subjects they will genuinely take, in a combination the blocks allow, and in which they can show depth beyond the GCSE course.

The interview day

Candidates who perform most strongly in the exams are invited to Westminster in late November; others are told by email at the same time. Everyone invited, including candidates from overseas, must attend in person. The day comprises a 15-minute interview in each of the four subjects, a 15-minute general interview and a 45-minute Interview Day Assessment. Westminster writes to the head of the current school for a reference beforehand.

The offer and its conditions

Offers go out in December. They are conditional on at least eight (I)GCSE passes at grade 7, of which at least four at grades 8 or 9 in the subjects to be studied at A level. Candidates on other qualifications are judged on satisfactory progress and further results. In March, offer holders spend a day at the school, by then allocated to houses, and meet their housemasters.

Day or boarding

At 16+, the choice is made before registering. Boarding is weekly and term-time only, with school on Saturday mornings; some Sixth Form boarders stay at weekends, though the site is quiet on Saturday nights. International candidates join as boarders.

Costs and awards

For 2026-27, Sixth Form day fees are £17,482 a term and boarding fees £23,094. Up to four King's Scholarships are awarded at 16+, on the overall performance in the exams and interviews; King's Scholars board in College and pay the day fee. Up to four Music Scholarships, each worth 10 percent of the day fee, are awarded after auditions in November.

How I prepare Sixth Form candidates

I start with the subject choice and the blocks, then prepare the four papers at the depth Westminster expects, beyond the GCSE syllabus, especially in mathematics and the sciences. Before the interview day, I run mock subject interviews, where the candidate is pushed on an idea in each discipline, and a general interview, so that the 15-minute slots feel like conversations between people who enjoy the subject.
