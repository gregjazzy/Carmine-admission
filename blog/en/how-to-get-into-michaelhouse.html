How to get into Michaelhouse

In 1897, a year after founding Michaelhouse, Canon James Cameron Todd told his first Speech Day that the school's aim was to make "not accountants, not clerks, not clergymen, but men". Nearly 130 years later, in Balgowan in the KwaZulu-Natal Midlands, Michaelhouse is one of the few schools in the world where every pupil is a boy and every boy boards. Its admission process reflects that: it looks at a boy's record, but it also wants to meet him.

The four parts of the file

  • Previous academic performance: the boy's results over the preparatory years, read as a trajectory rather than a single mark.
  • A personal interview: with the Rector or a Deputy Rector, during Grade 6, together with a tour of the school for the boy and his parents.
  • Recent school reports: a copy of the most recent report.
  • A confidential recommendation: written by the Head of the boy's current school, and never seen by the family.

The calendar in brief

A boy can be registered at any time from birth, and Michaelhouse encourages registration by the end of Grade 6. The interview and tour take place during Grade 6. Registration for a given Grade 8 intake closes on 31 January of the boy's Grade 7 year, together with scholarship and financial assistance applications. Families who miss that date go onto a waiting list. Sons, brothers and grandsons of Old Boys receive early offers at the end of February; the other offers of place, and of scholarships and financial assistance, follow in early April, about nine months before the boy arrives.

A file the Rector remembers

I prepare boys for the interview with the Rector, and for the scholarship assessments where their profile allows it, so that the boy the school meets is the one his teachers know.

Other entry points

Grade 8, called E Block, is the main entry. Places in D and C Blocks, Grades 9 and 10, are offered only when one becomes available, on the same four criteria. Scholarships are reserved for E Block entry.

What the school is looking for

Michaelhouse's admission policy is non-sectarian, and the school takes boys from 22 countries and from every province of South Africa. Its values are integrity, humility, courage, compassion and service, and its education is built on breadth: sixteen sports, seven cultural programmes, chapel, a campus of 680 hectares, and teachers who live on site. A boy who arrives at the interview able to talk about what he does outside lessons, and why it matters to him, is speaking the school's language. A boy who can only list his marks gives the Rector less to work with.

Before registering, a practical point

Michaelhouse reserves the right to run credit checks on applicants, to make sure families can meet the financial commitment. Families who will need financial assistance should say so on the application form itself, where the request begins.
