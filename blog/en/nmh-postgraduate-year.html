The postgraduate year at NMH

Northfield Mount Hermon takes students for a postgraduate year: a fifth year of high school, after graduation, before college. The school is clear that it is not meant as "another year". It is a program with its own advising structure, designed for motivated students who have a precise reason to spend a year at NMH before going on.

I work with families considering a PG year at NMH and at other New England boarding schools. Here is what the year offers and what the application needs to show.

Three reasons to do a PG year

ProfileWhat the year gives them
Student-athleteA year to develop athletically and academically in a demanding setting before competing in college
International studentA year to strengthen English and live inside an American school before applying to American universities
Student who needs more timeA year to reach their full level before starting college

The application should make the family's reason explicit. A PG applicant who knows why they want the year, and what they intend to do with it, reads very differently from one who is simply not ready to choose.

How the year is organized

Each postgraduate has an academic advisor and also a postgraduate advisor who works with the whole PG cohort. College counseling is done with a counselor who has experience guiding postgraduates through university applications. PGs are full members of the senior class, in classes, dorms, teams and activities.

The academic schedule suits a one-year stay. NMH's college-model program gives three core courses per semester in seventy-minute blocks, six over the year, and allows a motivated student to cover two years of a subject in one, for example two levels of a language. A PG can use that structure to fill a precise gap before college, in mathematics or English for instance.

The calendar problem

A PG arriving in September applies to college during that same autumn. By the time applications go out, the college counselor has known the student for a few weeks, and the file rests mostly on the high school record plus a first semester at NMH. The year can strengthen a profile, through new courses, a better English score, a stronger athletic record, but it cannot erase the years before. Families should plan the college list and testing as soon as the place at NMH is confirmed, not after arrival.

What the application needs

PG applicants follow the same process as other applicants: the SAO or Gateway, two current teacher recommendations, a required interview, and the January 15 deadline. Testing is optional; for a student already preparing college admissions, a strong SAT or ACT can be the natural score to send. Non-native English speakers need the English test, with recommended minimums of 100 on the TOEFL iBT under the old scale, 7 on the IELTS or 125 on Duolingo. Student-athletes can add a short video by link, and the interview should make clear what the student wants from the year.
