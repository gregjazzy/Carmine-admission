Columbus Academy takes its youngest students at three, in the Explorers program, then in Pre-Kindergarten and Kindergarten. Admission at these ages is about getting to know the child and the family rather than testing what a three- or five-year-old knows: a conversation with the parents, a report from the current preschool and a play-based screening visit on campus.

Book a conversation

The four steps

After the inquiry form, the family opens a Blackbaud account and submits the application; a reduced $75 fee applies until December 15. Parents then book a conversation of about thirty minutes with the early childhood admissions director, to share what they are looking for and ask their questions. From December 1, they send the Teacher Report Form to the child's current preschool or daycare teacher; a child who attends neither can have it waived. In January or early February, the child comes to campus for a screening visit in a play-based setting, on one of the pre-selected dates. A birth certificate is uploaded for age verification.

Preparing a young child

A play-based visit cannot and should not be rehearsed. What helps is ordinary: a child used to being with other children and unfamiliar adults, comfortable following simple instructions, and told in advance what the morning will look like. Booking early matters more than any preparation, since applying by December 15 gives priority in choosing visit dates, and a well-rested child on a good day shows more of themselves.
