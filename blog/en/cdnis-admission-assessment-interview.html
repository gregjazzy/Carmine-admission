The CDNIS assessment and interview

CDNIS does not set one entrance test for everyone. Applicants who complete their file and meet the selection criteria are invited to an assessment, conducted by admissions and academic staff, whose format depends on the grade. The school can change formats at its discretion, but the pattern is stable.

By section

Grades 1 and 2November to December60 minutes: interview, individual and group work, and an in-house paper covering vocabulary, writing, reading and comprehension in English or Mandarin, maths and conversation
Grades 3 to 5February to April60 minutes on site: group activities and/or an in-house paper in English and maths, basic Mandarin literacy in Grades 3 and 4, and a one-to-one interview
Grades 6 to 12February to April1 to 1.5 hours on site: group activities and/or a short piece of writing on a set topic, and/or a one-to-one interview

Throughout, the school also reviews school reports and the Student Reference Form; from Grade 6, the Student Application Questionnaire; and from Grade 10, the official transcript.

From abroad

Families living outside Hong Kong are offered a video assessment for the youngest, or an online interview: 60 minutes for Grades 3 to 5, and 10 to 15 minutes for Grades 6 to 12. Whatever their place of residence, candidates for Grade 10 and above may be offered only an online interview, at the school's discretion. A short online interview carries a lot of weight, because reports, the reference and the questionnaire then do the rest of the work.

Ready for each format

I prepare CDNIS applicants for the format of their grade: the in-house paper, the timed writing on a set topic, and a one-to-one interview in which the child speaks naturally.

What the assessment feeds into

Results go into Stage 1 of the screening, together with school results and the reference form, and produce a ranking: Excellent, Good, Marginal or Not suitable. Only Excellent and Good move to Stage 2, where priorities such as debentures and siblings apply. The assessment is therefore the decisive moment for most families.

Preparing sensibly

  • Youngest children: nothing to drill. Familiar play, time with other children, and some exposure to English or Mandarin conversation help more than any exercise.
  • Grades 1 to 5: reading and writing in English at grade level, mental and written arithmetic, and, in the bilingual grades, basic Mandarin.
  • Grades 6 to 12: practice writing a short, organised piece on an unfamiliar topic in a limited time, and conversations with a new adult about school, interests and reasons for choosing CDNIS.
