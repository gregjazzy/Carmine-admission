The Davidson Academy publishes four conditions that every applicant must meet. They look simple, but each hides a rule that families often misread, and the school is explicit that meeting the minimum is not the same as presenting a strong application.

I go through these conditions with every family I work with on Davidson. Here they are.

The four conditions

ConditionWhat it means in practice
Minimum testing criteriaSAT, ACT, IQ or, for younger students, PSAT 8/9 or PreACT scores at the thresholds for the age-based grade
Placement requirementsAdvanced middle school curriculum or higher in all core subjects
ReadinessSocial and emotional maturity, overall readiness for a rigorous school, and the student's own commitment to accelerated education
ResidencyBeing, or intending to become, a Nevada resident

The grade rules

Eligibility is judged on the student's age-based grade, ignoring any grade skip, using Nevada's kindergarten cutoff of turning five by August 1. A grade-skipped eleven-year-old in seventh grade is measured against the sixth-grade thresholds if that is their age-based grade. Students below sixth grade must meet sixth-grade thresholds. Students in third grade or below cannot apply at all, and the school strongly recommends that applicants have at least fifth grade in progress.

Has your child skipped a grade? Tell me their age and grade: it changes which thresholds apply.

"All subjects" means all

Davidson students work at least three years ahead in every subject, not only in their strongest. The placement requirement is advanced middle school work in all core subjects. A mathematically exceptional child whose writing is at grade level fails that condition in practice, even if the test scores qualify, and the readiness assessment, which gives 25 points to written expression and 25 to critical reading and discussion, will show it.

Residency and the commitment it implies

Families can apply before moving, but must live in Reno before the first day of school in August. Since the school is free for Nevada residents, the decision is less about tuition than about relocating a household, which is why the Academy also asks about the student's own commitment: a child who does not want this kind of school will not thrive in it.
