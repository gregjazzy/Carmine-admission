Chinese or Spanish as a second language? Chinese in Paris middle schools

Languages October 2026 7 min read

In French collège (lower secondary, ages 11 to 15), almost everyone takes Spanish as a second language: according to the DEPP, the statistics department of the French Ministry of Education, 57.5% of collège pupils studied it at the start of the 2024 school year. Choosing Chinese in Paris nearly always means choosing a school, because the language is not taught everywhere. And for pupils who already speak it, there is a separate, highly selective route whose rules changed recently.

What follows is based on the pages of the Académie de Paris, the city's education authority. I have organised it by type of pupil, because the right door is not the same for a child discovering Chinese and for a child who speaks it at home.

Chinese as a second language in Paris middle schools

For most families, Chinese starts as a second language, in cinquième (age 12), or from sixième (age 11) in schools that offer a two-language class. This is the easiest option to reach, and the worst documented: the Académie does not publish a consolidated list of the collèges that teach Chinese as a second language on its website. The only reliable method is to ask the school you have in mind directly, and to do so before the sixième placement procedure.

Be wary of the lists circulating online. Some still mention “oriental language sections” in Chinese at collège level. The Académie's page on oriental sections, updated in January 2026, names only two at collège level, in Japanese and Vietnamese, both at Collège Jean-de-La-Fontaine.

In the private sector, École Jeannine Manuel says it teaches Mandarin to all pupils from CE2 (age 8), then as an option from cinquième. It is one of the few schools in Paris where Chinese starts that early for everyone.

The Chinese international section in Paris: for children who already speak Chinese

The international section is designed for pupils who already have a good command of the language, spoken and written, often because it is spoken at home or because they have lived in China, Taiwan or Singapore. It leads to the baccalauréat français international, the French school-leaving diploma with an international option.

The Chinese section has a feature I find remarkable. In the other international sections, pupils take four hours of language and literature and two hours of history and geography in the section's language. In the Chinese section, history and geography are replaced by mathematics. A pupil there does maths in Chinese, which gives the section a scientific flavour the others lack.

How to get in

Applications are made online, on an Académie platform, at the start of the calendar year for the following September. Families can apply to several schools and several languages, up to ten ranked choices across all specialist tracks. The file includes the previous year's school reports and the first-term report, plus a declaration of language practice.

For entry into sixième, admission is based on the file and on written and oral tests. For cinquième, quatrième and troisième, there are two stages: shortlisting on the file and a written test, then an oral in the section's language for those who pass. The Académie says it looks at three things: the level reached in the language, motivation, which the oral is used to assess, and the pupil's ability to follow the programme given their record so far.

Two points families often miss. Recruitment is open across education authorities: a child who does not live in Paris can be admitted. And a Parisian family applying for sixième must also file an exemption request in the Académie's campaign, even if the school requested is their catchment school.

These tests are prepared like a competitive exam: written level in the language, an oral on motivation, and school reports that hold up, in maths above all, since the section teaches mathematics in Chinese. That is the work we do for school entry, and, for maths, in one-to-one tutoring.

Example: the campaign for September 2026 entry. Platform open from 12 January to 10 March 2026; written tests on 18 and 19 March; orals between 7 April and 13 May; panel in early June; results on 15 June 2026. The calendar repeats from year to year, but the Académie changed it recently: check it every year on its page.

Paris high schools with Chinese: the oriental language sections

In lycée (upper secondary, ages 15 to 18), another route opens: the oriental language section, which puts the words “section de langue orientale” and the language on the baccalauréat diploma. In Chinese, the Académie lists three. Those at Janson-de-Sailly and Louis-le-Grand have their own selection. The one at Gabriel-Fauré, in the 13th arrondissement, is open to lycée pupils who study Chinese as a first or second language, with no other condition.

It is the natural next step for a pupil who has done Chinese throughout collège: they keep the language in lycée, in a reinforced setting, rather than relegating it to an option.

So, Chinese or Spanish?

Spanish is the easy choice, in the most neutral sense of the word: offered almost everywhere, close to French, quickly rewarding. Chinese demands daily work from the first week, and it often means looking for the right school. I recommend Chinese for the child who already enjoys precise work, or whose parents are ready to support them through the first months; that is the child for whom the language becomes a real asset, for the reasons I set out in the article on what Chinese gives a child.

If your local collège does not offer Chinese, a child can start at home, with a short session every day. That is the idea behind Xiaomo, our Chinese app, free to use in our resources.
