Harrow Houses and offers: A1, A2, the waiting list and the House Master

The Old Schools, Harrow School, Harrow on the Hill. Photo Bernard Burns, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0

At Harrow, a boy does not join a school so much as a House. The School has twelve boarding Houses on its 324 acres, and every boy lives in one from his first day in the Shell to his last day in the Upper Sixth. That is why the House runs through the whole admissions process: a House Master interviews every applicant at the Harrow Test, and the offer letter itself is framed around Houses.

Reading the result letter

ListWhat it meansWhat happens next
A1A place at Harrow in a named HouseA one-to-one meeting with the House Master and a tour of the House, from the summer of Year 6
A2A guaranteed place at Harrow, House to be confirmedHouse allocated later, usually in Year 8; a visit is then arranged with the House Master
BA place on the waiting listPossible offer later as places free up; eligible to be invited to scholarship assessments
CNo offerThe application goes no further

A1 and A2 are both firm offers, conditional in the same way on Common Entrance or the Academic Scholarship in Year 8. The difference is timing, not status: an A2 boy is as admitted as an A1 boy, he simply learns his House later. Families sometimes read A2 as second best; it is better read as "admitted, House to follow".

Should you state a House preference?

The registration form allows it, and many parents leave the decision to the Registrar. Those who do express a preference often have an existing tie with a House through an Old Harrovian father or relative; around 15 percent of boys are sons of Old Harrovians. For a family with no such link, a preference based on a single Open Morning visit is a weak basis. The Registrar, on the other hand, knows each House and its balance from the inside.

If you do want to choose, use the Open Mornings to talk to more than one House Master, and ask your son's current head, who may know Harrow well.

Making the House Master interview count

A House Master decides whether he wants a boy in his House for five years. We prepare boys to show, in English and in twenty minutes, who they really are and what they will bring.

Why the House Master matters so much

The House Master is responsible for some seventy boys, day and night, across five year groups. He sees their academic work, their friendships, their homesickness in the first term and their leadership in the last. He is the parents' main point of contact for five years. A boy who reaches his A1 meeting has, in effect, been chosen by the person who will know him best at Harrow.

That is also why the Harrow Test interview is not a formality. A House Master is weighing whether this ten-year-old will settle, contribute and grow in a House like his. Curiosity, openness and a sense of humour count; so does being able to talk about something he cares about, in his own words.

The waiting list

A B result is not a refusal. Places can free up between Year 6 and Year 8, and boys on the waiting list can still be invited to the scholarship assessments in Year 8. A strong scholarship performance can change a family's position considerably. The sensible course is to keep another school in play while remaining ready for Harrow.

Between the offer and the first day

Boys holding offers are invited to Experience Harrow days in the summer of Year 7 and to School events through Years 7 and 8. In June of Year 8, a welcome afternoon brings new boys together with their House Master, the House team, some current boys and the other new boys of their House. By September, the House is already familiar.
