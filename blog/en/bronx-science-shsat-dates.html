The SHSAT calendar is short and fixed. Between early October and late November, a family registers, ranks schools and sits the test; the answer comes in March. Bronx Science also serves as one of the central test sites, for students from districts 7 to 12.

Where students test

Central sites are assigned by district. Students from districts 7 to 12 test at Bronx Science itself; others test at Stuyvesant, Brooklyn Tech or other sites. All students use Department of Education computers, not their own. From fall 2026, the test is computer-adaptive. Before the test, the official NYC SHSAT Portal offers a Student Readiness Tool and practice tests in the new format, which every candidate should use.

The months before October

I prepare students over the spring and summer of 7th grade into 8th, so that registration in October is a formality and November a test they recognise.

Accommodations come earlier

Students with IEPs or 504 Plans, and current or recent English language learners, can receive testing accommodations, such as double time. These require documentation in place in advance; private school documents must reach the Department of Education at least three weeks before the registration deadline. Families should start in September, not October.

After the test

Offers come in March, with scores released after them. In spring, families can view the test through the Test View process. Those who narrowly missed and qualify may be invited to the Discovery summer programme.

How I plan it

I have students at Bronx Science, and for seven years I have worked with around fifteen families a year aiming at the most selective schools. For the SHSAT, I build the calendar backwards from November: practice tests in the spring, content work through the summer, timed sections in the autumn.
