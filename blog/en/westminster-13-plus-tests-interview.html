Westminster 13+: the Pre-Tests, the January papers and the interview

At 13+, Westminster decides in Year 6 who will join it three years later. The process has three filters: the ISEB Common Pre-Tests and the school report in the autumn, Westminster's own papers in January, and an interview. Around 500 children apply, about 250 reach the interview stage, and roughly 125 join the Fifth Form, as Westminster calls Year 9. Just under half of them come from Westminster Under School; the rest from a wide range of schools.

Autumn of Year 6: Pre-Tests and report

Candidates sit the ISEB Common Pre-Tests at their own prep school in October or November: online assessments in mathematics, English and reasoning. Westminster adjusts raw scores for age, so a child born in August is not penalised against one born in September, and states that no special preparation is needed. Children based overseas can sit them at their own school, although the 13+ route is open only to families living in the UK.

In November, Westminster requests a report from the current school for every candidate who sat the Pre-Tests, and it gives that report real weight. A strong score with a cautious report is not a strong file.

Mid-December to February: papers and interview

In mid-December of Year 6, parents of candidates who performed well hear that their child is invited to the next stage. It has two parts:

  • Further papers in early January: mathematics and English, 40 minutes each, taken at Westminster.
  • An interview in January or February, separately.

Westminster's papers are short and searching. In mathematics, a 40-minute paper set for able ten- and eleven-year-olds rewards the ability to reason through an unfamiliar problem far more than speed with routine methods. In English, it rewards close reading and precise, lively writing. Both are skills a child builds over years of reading and thinking, which is exactly why Westminster can say that cramming is pointless.

The interview

By the interview, Westminster has a Pre-Test score, a report, two papers and the registration details. The interview is where the school meets the child behind them. At this level, what impresses is rarely knowledge for its own sake: it is a child who can follow an idea, explain how they got to an answer, admit they are unsure and then reason it out, and talk with real enthusiasm about something they have read or made. Children who have only been coached for tests often struggle precisely there.

The offer, and what it asks of the next two years

By the end of February of Year 6, parents hear whether their child has an unconditional place. It means Westminster believes the child will comfortably meet the standard required at 13 while enjoying a broad education. The condition that remains is implicit but real: continued good conduct and academic progress at the prep school, and an unreserved reference from that school in March of Year 8. An entrance fee of £2,400 is paid on accepting the place, and a £4,000 deposit by 1 September of Year 8.

The waiting list

A child who is not offered an immediate place may be put on the waiting list. Westminster asks heads for updates on waiting-list pupils from time to time, and a child can be promoted to an unconditional place at any point up to the end of June in Year 8. Two years is a long time for an eleven-year-old: a child who keeps progressing has a real chance.

Day or boarding

The choice can wait until the February of the year of entry. Boarding is weekly and term-time only; boarders under 16 must have parents living in the UK to stay with at weekends. King's Scholars, chosen through The Challenge in Year 8, must board in College.
