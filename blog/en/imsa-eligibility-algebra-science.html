Before grades, scores or essays matter, IMSA applies a simple academic gate. An applicant must show that they have already started high school-level mathematics and science. For many 8th graders, this is the first real planning question, and it often needs to be solved a year ahead.

The conditions

ConditionDetail
Grade8th or 9th grade; students in 10th to 12th grade are not eligible
MathematicsAlgebra 1, at least one year
ScienceOne high school-level science course, at least one year
For 8th gradersEnrolled in both courses, with at least half of each completed by the application deadline
ResidenceIllinois resident, or US resident outside Illinois for the small tuition-paying intake

Eighth graders must document coursework comparable to ninth-grade work in mathematics and science.

When the school offers no high school science

Many middle schools do not teach a high school-level science course in 8th grade. IMSA allows applicants to meet the requirement through any accredited institution; families have used online programmes and community colleges. Any course taught at high school level counts, and IMSA recommends chemistry, biology or physics as the best preparation for its curriculum. Online algebra courses are accepted in the same way.

Planning backwards

  • In 7th grade: check whether the school will offer Algebra 1 and a high school science course in 8th grade.
  • If not: enrol in an accredited course starting in the summer or autumn, so that at least half is completed by early February.
  • In 8th grade: keep strong grades in both, since classroom performance is part of the holistic review.

8th grade or 9th grade?

Students can apply in the autumn of 8th grade, 9th grade, or both. Either way, they enter IMSA as sophomores. A strong 8th grader may apply early; a student who needs another year of algebra or science, or a stronger test score, can apply in 9th grade. The requirements are the same.

Eligibility is not admission

Meeting the conditions only opens the file. Selection then rests on grades, an SAT or ACT score, three teacher evaluations, essays, activities and a parent statement, with about 200 to 250 places a year.
