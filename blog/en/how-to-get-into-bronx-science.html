How to get into Bronx Science

The Bronx High School of Science, New York. Photo Bxsstudent, Wikimedia Commons, public domain

The Bronx High School of Science counts nine Nobel laureates among its alumni and has produced more Science Talent Search finalists and semifinalists than any other high school. Getting in, however, depends on a single morning: the Specialized High Schools Admissions Test, the SHSAT. No grades, essays, interviews or recommendations are considered.

The SHSAT serves eight specialized high schools at once. In the 2026 cutoffs, Bronx Science came fourth, behind Stuyvesant (561), the High School for Mathematics, Science and Engineering at City College (539) and Queens High School for the Sciences at York College (531).

Who may sit the test

Any New York City resident in 8th grade, or in 9th grade for the first time, whether at a public, private, parochial or charter school, or taught at home. Registration runs through MySchools in October, and the test follows in November. Everyone sits the same exam; private school students receive no separate route.

One test, prepared properly

I prepare students for both SHSAT sections, the math and the English, over the year before the test, with the adaptive format in mind.

Score, then list

Offers go out in order of score, from highest down. Each student, in turn, is offered the highest-ranked school on their list that still has seats. A student who does not list Bronx Science cannot receive an offer there, whatever the score. The ranking decision made in October therefore matters as much as the test itself.

Another door: Discovery

Some disadvantaged students who score just below the cutoff may be invited to a summer Discovery programme; completing it successfully leads to a place in September.

Is it the right school?

Most Bronx Science students come from Queens, the Bronx and Manhattan, and the average total commute is two hours a day. Every 9th grader does research, and the school has more than a hundred clubs, a debate team ranked first in the country and 43 athletic teams. For a curious student who wants science at a high level, the trip is part of the bargain.
