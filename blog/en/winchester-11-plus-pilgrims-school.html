Winchester at 11+: entering through The Pilgrims' School

Most boys who join Winchester at 13 come from preparatory schools that run to Year 8. Many families, in the UK and abroad, have children in schools that end at Year 6 or that follow other systems entirely. For them, Winchester now offers an 11+ route: entry to The Pilgrims' School in Year 7, with onward progression to Winchester College in Year 9.

What The Pilgrims' School is

Pilgrims' is an independent prep and pre-prep school for boys aged 4 to 13, just across the road from the College. It merged with Winchester College in September 2025, after a partnership of nearly a century, and shares many of the College's facilities. It is known for academic standards, music and a broad education. For a boy who has been accepted at 11+, the two years there are a preparation for the College in the most literal sense.

How boys are assessed

The 11+ route uses the same ingredients as Winchester's 13+ process, earlier: school references, General Ability Testing, a writing assessment and an interview. Winchester's stated aim is assessment that is accessible, challenging and aligned with the curriculum, without stress or extra tutoring. At interview, families meet the Headmaster of Pilgrims', and the boy is interviewed by a Winchester College Housemaster, who looks, as at 13+, for curiosity, kindness, integrity, resilience and a willingness to contribute.

The timing is tighter than at 13+. Assessment comes a few days after the registration deadline, and offers arrive in December rather than March. A family considering this route has little time between deciding and being assessed.

Who the route suits

It suits boys whose current school stops at Year 6, including British state primary schools and many international and national schools abroad, and families who want certainty about Winchester two years earlier. Interviews held in Hong Kong show that the route is also designed for families in Asia. For a boy from another system, the two years at Pilgrims' have an added value: English, the British curriculum and the habits of boarding, learned in the place where he will then spend five more years.

It suits less well a boy already happy in a strong prep school that runs to Year 8, for whom the standard 13+ route remains the natural one.

After Year 6

In Year 7, boys are placed in a Winchester boarding house and invited to taster events. In Year 8, like 13+ entrants, they sit Winchester Entrance, which helps set classes but does not decide admission, and the strongest may sit Election for a place in College.

How I prepare 11+ candidates

With ten-year-olds, the work is reading and conversation above all: a reading plan, short handwritten pieces in response to a text or an image, practice in talking about themselves to an unfamiliar adult, and enough familiarity with the GAT format that nothing on the day is new. For boys abroad, I also work on English, so that the October assessment and the interview show their real level.
