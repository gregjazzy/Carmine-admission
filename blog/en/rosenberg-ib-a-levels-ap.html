Institut auf dem Rosenberg: IB, A-levels or AP, which programme to choose

Institut auf dem Rosenberg, St. Gallen. Photo Rene Nueesch, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 3.0

Boarding schools November 2026 5 min read

Most boarding schools offer one route, sometimes two. Rosenberg offers almost all of them: IGCSEs, British A-levels, American Advanced Placement, the International Baccalaureate, the German International Baccalaureate, and the school's own high school diploma. With close to 500 courses and a timetable built for each student, it is an exceptional freedom, provided one knows how to use it.

An in-house curriculum, then chosen exams

The foundation is the Rosenberg International Curriculum, the school's own accredited programme, which runs from pre-school to the end of high school and allows students to join at different stages. On this foundation, each student prepares the external exams that serve their plans. It is not uncommon, the school says, for students to take some exams early, or follow classes well ahead of their age: learning is organised by level, not by age. Languages are many, from German to Mandarin, with preparation for Cambridge, Goethe or DELF certificates.

The right path to the right universities

I help your child choose between IB, A-levels and AP according to the universities they are aiming for, then support them through to their admissions: since 2021, my students have been admitted to Columbia, Stanford, Imperial, EPFL, Bocconi, McGill and King's College. My aim: that their freedom of choice becomes an advantage.

How to choose

The choice depends first on the target country. A-levels are the most direct route to British universities, which generally require three subjects at a high level. APs strengthen an American application, where they show the ability to follow university-level courses. The International Baccalaureate, broader, is read everywhere and keeps doors open when plans are not yet settled. The German International Baccalaureate is mainly of interest to German-speaking families.

IB, A-levels or AP for your child? Tell me what they are aiming for after school: I will tell you which path serves them best.

The flip side of freedom

Such a wide choice needs guidance. Rosenberg follows each student with an individual development plan and mentors. But the coherence of the path with the target universities remains decisive: a combination of subjects that looks brilliant on paper may not meet a specific university's requirements. That is often where an outside view, familiar with what universities expect, makes the difference.
