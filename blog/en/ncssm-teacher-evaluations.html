Teacher and counselor evaluations

Most selective schools ask for recommendation letters. NCSSM does something different: it asks for evaluations, completed on a structured online form, from three people who know the applicant's work in 9th or 10th grade. The format makes every file comparable, and it makes the choice of evaluators matter even more.

Who evaluates

EvaluatorRule
STEM teacherA science, technology, engineering or math teacher from 9th or 10th grade
English teacherAn English teacher from 9th or 10th grade
Guidance counselorMust be the current 10th grade counselor

How it works

The applicant provides each evaluator's contact information in the application. NCSSM then sends the evaluator a direct link to a standard online form. There is no free letter to upload, and no form for the family to forward. All three evaluations are due by 28 February.

Choosing the STEM and English teachers

Because 9th and 10th grade teachers are both allowed, a student can often choose. The best evaluator is not always the one who gave the highest grade, but the one who saw the student work hardest, ask the most questions, or go beyond the assignment. On a structured form, that teacher has concrete reasons to rate initiative, work ethic and curiosity highly. For the STEM evaluation, a teacher from a demanding course is worth more than one from an easy one.

A file the evaluators confirm

I help NCSSM applicants choose their evaluators and approach them early, so that the forms confirm what the essays and the record already show.

The counselor

The counselor must be the current 10th grade counselor. In a large high school, that person may know the student only slightly. A short meeting in the autumn, to explain the application to NCSSM and what the student has done, helps the counselor complete the form accurately.

Not sure which teachers to ask? Tell me about your child's courses.

What evaluations add

The rubric scores criteria such as work ethic and initiative, community involvement and enthusiasm for learning. Grades show results; essays show how a student thinks. Evaluations show how they behave in a classroom over months, which is hard to fake and hard to replace.

Practical points

  • Ask in person, in October or November, before the winter rush.
  • Enter contact details carefully; a mistyped email address delays the form.
  • Thank evaluators, and remind them politely before 28 February if the form is still pending.
