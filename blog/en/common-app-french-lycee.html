Common App: applying from a French lycée

Common App October 2026 13 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

Book a conversation

The Common App is the platform used to apply to most American universities: more than 1,200 members for the 2026-2027 cycle, over sixty of them outside the United States. Its own site explains well which boxes to fill in. It says little about how to fill them in when the applicant comes from a French lycée, a school that has never handled a School Report, where activities happen outside school hours and off the premises, and where a 14 out of 20 looks, to an American reader, like an ordinary grade.

Creating the account, and the order that saves you redoing it

An account takes a few minutes to open, at any time of year, and it rolls over from one cycle to the next. I advise opening it in the spring of première, the penultimate year (Year 12, or 11th grade). Everything in "My Common Application" (profile, family, education, testing, activities, essay) carries over to the new cycle on 1 August. What does not carry over is anything attached to a specific college: its own questions, the FERPA release and the invitations sent to the counsellor and teachers. An invitation sent to teachers in June has to be sent again in August.

On FERPA, I recommend waiving the right to see the letters. Admissions readers trust a letter the student has not read, and a deputy head writes more freely knowing it will stay confidential. One more thing that costs weeks: the Common App invitation arrives in English, from an unfamiliar address, and can land in the junk folder of a French school's email system. Warn the person before you invite them.

What the Common App contains

The student's part covers profile, family, education, tests, activities and the main essay. For 2026-2027 the essay prompts are unchanged from the previous year, and the activities section is now called "Activities and experiences", with the same character limits. Each college then adds its own questions and often supplemental essays. English tests (TOEFL iBT, PTE Academic, the Duolingo English Test) go in the Testing section when a college asks for one.

Two sections puzzle French applicants. Education asks for a GPA. The Franco-American Commission (Fulbright France) suggests a scale of 20 and the overall unweighted average; the Common App itself notes the field is optional when a school does not calculate a cumulative GPA. I would rather fill in the average out of 20, since it appears on every transcript anyway. Testing asks whether the education system uses national leaving examinations. For the baccalaureate the answer is yes, and a subsection opens for the papers already sat in première and those still to come.

Common App essay prompts and length

The main essay, often called the personal statement, runs from 250 to 650 words on one of seven prompts, and the same text goes to every college that requires it. It is a genre French schools do not teach: a first-person narrative showing who the student is rather than what they achieved. It has nothing to do with the British personal statement, which goes through UCAS, is counted in characters and answers three set questions. The prompts and why a French-style cover letter misses the point are covered in Cover letter for a US or UK university; college-specific writing in "Why us" and supplemental essays.

Two things I tell students from French schools. The dissertation structure they are trained in (thesis, qualification, conclusion) shows by the second paragraph and kills the story. And moving countries, the natural topic for expatriate children, only works if it tells something another expatriate child would not have lived. The activities list already says what the student did; the essay need not repeat it.

Common App activities section: ten lines when the school runs almost nothing

The section takes up to ten activities. For each: a type from a drop-down list, a role, the organisation's name, a description, the years of participation, hours per week and weeks per year. From 2026-2027 the role field is optional. Awards have their own place, the Honors section under Education.

An American high school hands students ready-made activities: varsity teams, a newspaper, clubs, student government. A French lycée offers few, and French applicants on US admissions forums have said so for years; the ones who managed counted what they did elsewhere, in an English-language section, a sports club or a theatre group. The mistake I see most is the opposite one. A student enters three lines because they feel they have "done nothing", when they have eight years at a music conservatoire, a club licence with regional competitions, a youth-leader certificate (the BAFA), a work-observation placement and afternoons spent looking after younger siblings.

All of that counts, and the Common App says so: jobs, family responsibilities, volunteering, sustained hobbies. The extra hours of an international section or the bilingual BFI diploma, on the other hand, are coursework; they belong under Education. Hours are reported per week, and French students nearly always undercount them: daily practice on an instrument plus a weekly lesson comes to four or five hours, not one. The 150 characters take verbs and numbers, not full sentences. A model line: "Cello, advanced cycle at conservatoire; youth orchestra, 4 concerts/yr; teaches beginners on Saturdays". Order the lines from most to least important. Six solid lines beat ten lines of padding.

Since 2025-2026 the section also contains a checklist, "Responsibilities and circumstances", covering family duties and life circumstances. Common App reports that 28% of applicants who tick seven items or more are international, against under 10% of all applicants. A student who has changed country and school language several times belongs on that list, without having to make it the subject of the essay.

Additional information: what goes in, what stays out

The Writing section has two optional boxes. "Challenges and circumstances", 250 words at most, is for hardship: war, disaster, displacement, illness. "Additional information" has been capped at 300 words since 2025-2026, down from 650.

For a student at a French lycée, I use it for what an American reader would misread without help. The specialty subject dropped at the end of première, for instance: the baccalaureate requires three in that year and two in the final one, but on a transcript it looks like giving up. Or a switch from another school system mid-way, the absence of class rank, a missing term after a move. One sentence per point is enough. I never put a second essay there, nor a list of prizes already declared, nor a complaint about a teacher's marking. The grading scale belongs in the school profile; a student explains it only if the school has none.

The school's role: the part the family does not fill in

The Common App includes several documents sent directly by the school:

  • The school report, required, with transcripts and the school profile.
  • The counsellor recommendation, a letter that places the student within the school.
  • The midyear report, sent as soon as first-term or first-semester grades of the final year are available.
  • The final report, sent after an offer has been accepted, with the latest results.

In the United States these are the job of the school counsellor, a dedicated professional. That role does not exist in a French lycée. The school has to designate someone, often a deputy head, an English-speaking CPE (the pastoral lead) or the student's professeur principal, willing to create an account, upload transcripts and write the letter. This needs organising from the spring of première.

What that person does not know can cost the student. The School Report asks them to rate the rigour of the student's course choices against other students at the school, on a scale from "most demanding" to "below average". A deputy head who reasons that "everyone follows the same national curriculum" ticks "average" for a student taking the advanced maths option (maths expertes) on top of two science specialties. Explain it before the form is filled in. The midyear report also goes out unprompted or not at all: the Common App sends no reminders, and the School Report must be submitted first. I ask for it straight after the first-term class council, before the December holidays.

American counsellors have every student fill in a "brag sheet", a summary of their record, activities and what they hope the letter will cover. A French school will not ask for one. The student should prepare it and hand it to the designated person, in English and French, with the list of colleges and deadlines.

Transcripts and the school profile

French school reports raise two issues. Language first: documents in a language other than English come with a translation, and Harvard specifies that applicants should not translate their own documents. The grading scale next: a 15 out of 20 needs explaining. That is the job of the school profile, a short document presenting the school, its tracks, its grading and the distribution of grades.

Many French lycées do not have one. The Common App nonetheless provides schools with resources to write one. A school that sends students to the United States every year usually has one; for the others, helping to build it is part of the application work. The advice French applicants get on US forums is to place the school against national baccalaureate results. The French education ministry publishes pass and honours rates school by school, and that is the first figure I reach for.

Teachers' letters

Most selective universities ask for one or two teacher letters, in addition to the counsellor's. An American letter tells a story about a student: concrete examples from class, how they reason, what they bring to others. A French teacher more readily writes a brief, general comment, and French applicants point out on forums that their teachers tend to understate their students in writing. Choose two teachers early who know the student well, and give them the material they need, without ever writing in their place. College-by-college requirements and translation rules are in Recommendation letters from French teachers.

Application fees and the fee waiver for international students

The Common App itself is free. Each college sets its own fee in US dollars, and some charge international applicants more. Over 600 members charge nothing. The most selective universities charge between $80 and $100.

A list of twelve selective universities therefore costs around $1,000 in fees, before sending test scores. The Common App fee waiver removes them, but its criteria are American: income below federal thresholds, a Pell Grant, public assistance, or a statement from a school official. The counsellor has to confirm the request. From 2026-2027 each college states whether it accepts the waiver from all applicants, from US applicants only, or from no one, and in the official requirements grid more than a hundred members restrict it to US students or refuse it. A college that declines the waiver may ask for the fee after submission. Fulbright France considers it very unlikely that an applicant without US citizenship qualifies. For a family paying French international school fees, I do not request it. A family in genuine difficulty should write to each admissions office instead, as many have their own waiver.

The same goes for Common App direct admissions, the unsolicited offers some member colleges send to applicants: more than 225 institutions took part in 2025-2026, but the scheme targets first-generation and lower-income students living in the United States. A student at a French lycée abroad will not receive one, and that says nothing about their file.

Example: the 2026-2027 calendar. The Common App opened on 1 August 2026. Early deadlines on 1 November 2026 at Harvard, Stanford, Princeton, Columbia, Brown, Cornell and Dartmouth, 2 November at Duke and Chicago. Regular deadlines between 1 and 5 January 2027 depending on the college (1 January at Harvard, Princeton, Columbia and Dartmouth, 5 January at Stanford and Brown). Midyear report once first-term grades of the final year are in, final report after an offer is accepted. The previous cycle closed on 28 July 2026 at 5 pm New York time. The Common App opens every year on 1 August, so the 2027-2028 cycle opens on 1 August 2027; its essay prompts had not been published by October 2026 (the 2026-2027 ones were announced on 27 February 2026).

Preparing the Common App from a French lycée

The Common App is prepared on two parallel fronts. The student's: activities pursued over several years, the essay, the tests. The school's: designating a contact, writing the school profile, preparing teachers for the letters. The second is the one most often neglected, and the one that sinks the most strong applications. The general mechanics are in Do US universities accept the French baccalaureate?, and strategy for the most selective universities in Getting into the Ivy League from France.
