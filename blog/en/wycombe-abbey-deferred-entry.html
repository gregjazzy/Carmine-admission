Deferred entry to Wycombe Abbey

Entrance to Wycombe Abbey, High Wycombe. Photo David Hillas, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 2.0

English boarding schools November 2026 4 min read

Book a conversation

Many girls' prep schools in England run to the end of Year 8, while Wycombe Abbey's main entry is at 11+. Deferred entry bridges the two. A girl secures her place through the 11+ process, then completes her prep school education in full before joining Wycombe Abbey in Year 9.

How it works
PlacesAbout 15 a year
Designed forGirls at UK-based prep schools that continue to the end of Year 8
RegistrationBy 1 June of Year 5, as for 11+
AssessmentThe 11+ process: ISEB Pre-Test, then the Assessment Day in November of Year 6
JoiningAt the beginning of Year 9
Between offer and entryThe girl remains at her current prep school

Why families choose it

Deferred entry offers certainty early: a family knows in December of Year 6 that the place is secured, two and a half years before the girl arrives. It also respects the prep school's own course, with its final years of leadership and responsibility, which many families value.

Ready in Year 6

Deferred places are won at 11+. I prepare girls for the Pre-Test and the Assessment Day in Year 5 and early Year 6.

Who it does not suit

The route is designed for girls at UK prep schools that run to Year 8. A girl at a primary or international school that ends in Year 6 would normally take the standard 11+ place. A girl who is not yet ready in Year 6 keeps the 13+ route, with registration by 1 June of Year 7 and the Assessment Day in January of Year 8.

Is your daughter at a prep school that runs to Year 8? Let's compare 11+, deferred and 13+.

Competition

With around 15 places, deferred entry is highly sought after. Girls are assessed on the same Pre-Test, papers, interview and group activities as other 11+ candidates, at the same moment in Year 6. A deferred offer is not a lesser place: it is the same decision, simply taken up two years later.
