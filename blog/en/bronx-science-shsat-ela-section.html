The SHSAT English section

Many Bronx Science students grow up with another language at home; more than half of the current student body does. For them, as for everyone, the English section of the SHSAT counts exactly as much as the math. It rewards precise reading and an ear for correct written English.

Two kinds of task

PartWhat students do
Revising and editingImprove a text: correct sentences, choose the clearest wording, organise ideas
Reading comprehensionRead passages and answer questions on meaning, structure, purpose and evidence

Together they make up 50 questions, scored and standardised, then added to the math score.

Inside a passage set

The section is computer-adaptive from fall 2026, but reading passages bring one important freedom: within a set of questions on the same passage, a student can go back and change earlier answers, until the set is submitted. Once submitted, the set closes for good. That calls for a habit: check the whole set before submitting, rather than answering in a rush and moving on.

Reading closely, at pace

I work with SHSAT candidates on close reading, evidence questions and the grammar behind editing items, and on managing passage sets under time.

Where points slip away

  • Editing by ear: what sounds natural in speech is not always correct in writing.
  • Answering from memory of the text instead of returning to the line that proves the answer.
  • Running out of time on long passages, with no way to skip ahead.
  • Overlooking structure questions, which ask why a paragraph or sentence is there, not just what it says.

English learners

Current English language learners, and former ELLs who reached proficiency on the NYSESLAT within the last two years, may receive double time, 360 minutes instead of 180 with two 15-minute breaks, and translated glossaries of the footnotes in ELA passages in several languages, including Chinese, French and Spanish. Paper bilingual glossaries are provided; students may not bring their own, and the questions themselves are never translated. Accommodations require a documented history, so families should check eligibility with the school well before registration.
