How to get into Winchester College

Winchester College, the Chapel and Chamber Court from above. Photo Tim Sheerman-Chase, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 4.0

Book a conversation

Winchester College has educated boys for nearly 650 years, and it has changed the way it chooses them. Entry at 13+ is now decided in Year 6 through a school reference, General Ability Testing, a handwritten writing assessment and an interview. The Year 8 entrance examination, which once decided a place, no longer does. For families used to the old pattern, or to other schools' Common Entrance, that shift moves the whole effort two years earlier.

What the registration asks for

Recent school reports, a CV, the contact details of a confidential referee, and identity documents including birth certificate and passport. Winchester describes these documents as a springboard for conversations about the boy. Read that literally: whatever goes on the CV may be raised at interview, so it should list real interests he can talk about, not an inventory of activities designed to impress.

A family that needs a means-tested bursary should register in the main round and tick the box on the form. Families with a previous connection to the College can request a particular House at this stage.

The two rounds

The first round is the file plus the GAT. Boys are invited to interview on the strength of both: reports, references and CV on one side, the General Ability Test on the other. The second round takes place in Winchester in January: an interview with a member of the pastoral team, usually a Housemaster or a senior leader, and, on the same day, the Winchester Writing Assessment, an extended handwritten response to stimulus material that looks for creativity and critical thinking.

Winchester says plainly what it values at interview: curiosity, kindness, integrity, resilience and a willingness to contribute to the life of a House, as much as academic potential. Houses hold 60 to 70 boarders, 10 to 14 boys per year group, who eat together in their own dining room. The interviewer is picturing your son at that table.

A process that claims not to need tutoring

Winchester states that good assessment should not be stressful or require additional tutoring, and recommends what any good teacher would: read widely and for pleasure, talk about ideas at home, pay attention in class, ask questions. That is sound advice, and it says a great deal about what is being measured. The GAT measures general ability, not a syllabus learned by heart. The writing task rewards a boy who has read and thought, not one who has memorised essay plans. The interview rewards curiosity that is real.

What preparation can sensibly do is therefore different from drilling: build the reading and writing habits the College describes, make the GAT format familiar so that nothing on the day is a surprise, and give a boy practice in talking about himself and his ideas to an adult he has never met.

After the offer

In Year 7, boys are placed in a House and invited to taster events. In Year 8 they sit Winchester Entrance, which now helps staff set classes but no longer decides admission, choose their electives, and attend an induction day. The most academically ambitious sit Election, the scholarship examination that leads to College. Families who missed 1 October can still use the Registrar's Route, processed as places become available.

How I prepare boys for Winchester
