The Godolphin 11+ interview

Godolphin and Latymer School, Hammersmith. Photo Des Blenkinsopp, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 2.0

At most London 11+ Consortium schools, every candidate is interviewed. Godolphin and Latymer is different: with South Hampstead High School, it selects from the assessment which girls it will invite. The interview is therefore both a second stage and a sign that the test went well enough.

The essentials

QuestionAnswer
Who is interviewed?Girls Godolphin selects on the basis of the Consortium assessment
When?Friday 15 January 2027, for entry in September 2027
Another date?No; the school states it cannot offer one
What follows?Offers on 12 February 2027

What the interview is for

The Consortium describes each school's interview as an imaginative experience, individual to the school, that explores a candidate's skills, aptitudes and intellectual acuity. The schools say they look for evidence of creative and independent thinking, and for suitability for the school. At Godolphin, that means a girl who will thrive among very able classmates and take up a wide range of activities, from more than 90 clubs and societies to music, sport and drama.

Ideas of her own

I do not rehearse answers. I help girls get used to thinking aloud about an unfamiliar problem or text, which is what an interview at this level brings out.

Why coaching works against her

The Consortium actively discourages interview preparation, and warns that its interviewers can easily identify a candidate who has been coached. A girl who recites a prepared answer about her favourite book shows memory, not thought. Interviewers are also experienced in putting nervous candidates at ease, so shyness is not the problem families fear.

Has your daughter been invited to interview? Let's talk about how she thinks, not what she says.

What genuinely helps

  • Reading that she has chosen and enjoyed, so that she has something real to talk about.
  • Conversation at home about ideas, the news, a problem, where her opinion is asked for and challenged.
  • Thinking aloud when she meets something she does not know, rather than going silent.
  • Honesty about her interests, including the unusual ones.

How I help

I have pupils at Godolphin, and for seven years I have worked with around fifteen families a year aiming at the most selective schools. Before an interview, I work on reasoning aloud through problems and texts, the skill that shows whatever the questions turn out to be.
