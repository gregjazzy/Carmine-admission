The GSIS primary assessment and CAT4

For primary entry to the English International Stream at German Swiss International School, the assessment depends on the year group. From Year 2, children sit written English and mathematics papers and a verbal English reading comprehension. From Year 3 to Year 6, a computer-based CAT4 comes first, and only children who succeed on it go on to the written papers. GSIS keeps every paper confidential and publishes no sample tests.

I prepare children for these assessments every year. Here is how they work.

The sequence by year group

Entry yearAssessment
KG1 and Year 1Assessed individually within a group of children
Year 2Written English and mathematics; verbal English reading comprehension
Years 3 to 6CAT4 on computer; if successful, written English and mathematics and verbal reading comprehension
All primary years from Year 2Confidential recommendation form from the current school

The school form is not a formality: GSIS does not offer a place to an applicant whose school fails to provide it. Families should tell the current school early that the form will come, and check that it has been sent.

What CAT4 measures

CAT4, the Cognitive Abilities Test published by GL Assessment, measures four kinds of reasoning: verbal, non-verbal, quantitative and spatial. It is widely used in British and international schools and is standardised on large samples of children. For children aged 8 and over it is taken in three sections of 45 minutes; for younger children, two of 30 minutes. Each section begins with untimed practice examples that show the style of the questions.

Because CAT4 is a filter for the rest of the primary assessment, a child who has never met non-verbal or spatial reasoning questions can stop at the first step without showing what they can do in English or mathematics. Children educated in systems that rarely use such questions are the most exposed.

The written papers and reading aloud

Children who pass the CAT4 sit written English and mathematics papers and a verbal reading comprehension: reading a text aloud and answering questions on it in English. In the EIS, the school expects English at the level of a native speaker of the same age, and every part of the assessment must be passed. Reading aloud reveals a great deal quickly: fluency, pronunciation, and whether the child understands what they read or only decodes it.

Mathematics is assessed in English too, which matters for a child taught maths in another language. Word problems and instructions have to be understood before they can be solved.
