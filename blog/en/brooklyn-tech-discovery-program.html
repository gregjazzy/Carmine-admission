Discovery at Brooklyn Tech

For a small number of students, missing the Brooklyn Tech cutoff by a few points is not the end of the story. Each specialized high school runs its own Discovery summer, and Brooklyn Tech's can turn a near miss into a September place.

A typical case

Imagine a student at a public middle school in a high-need district who lists Brooklyn Tech first and scores a few points under 506. In March, with the letter announcing the results, comes information about Discovery. If the student takes it up and meets the summer's requirements, they start 9th grade at Brooklyn Tech in September like any other student.

The conditions, in plain terms

  • The score must fall inside a band set below the school's cutoff, not anywhere below it.
  • The family's situation must meet the programme's economic criteria: for most public and charter pupils, a school Economic Need Index of at least 60 %; for private or home-schooled pupils, an address in a high-poverty area.
  • The list must include Brooklyn Tech, because invitations are only made for listed schools.
  • The summer runs three to five weeks, with requirements such as attending about nine days in ten.

A few points can change the route

For students near 506, a few more points can turn a summer programme into a direct offer. I prepare SHSAT candidates to find them.

Three misconceptions

"We can sign up for Discovery." There is no application; everything flows from the test, the list and eligibility. "It is a lesser place." Students who complete it join the same 9th grade, follow the same courses and, in 10th grade, compete for the same majors on the same Power Index. "Leaving Brooklyn Tech off the list keeps options open." It does the opposite: it removes both the direct offer and the Discovery invitation for that school.

Is your child near 506 on practice tests? Let's work on the margin.

Reading the official rules

The Department of Education publishes the Discovery criteria each year, and they can change. Families who think they may qualify should read them in the autumn, before ranking schools, rather than in March.

My part in it

I have students at Brooklyn Tech, and for seven years I have worked with around fifteen families a year aiming at the most selective schools. Nobody prepares for Discovery as such. What I work on is the score: enough points either to make the summer route unnecessary, or at least to bring a student within its band.
