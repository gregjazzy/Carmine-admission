Common Entrance for Harrow: which papers, and what score

A Harrow offer in Year 6 is real, but it is not the end of the process. Every place is conditional on a boy sitting Common Entrance, the 13+ examination of British prep schools, in June of Year 8, unless he has done well enough in Harrow's Academic Scholarship examinations to be excused. Families who relax for two years after the A1 letter sometimes find the June results decide more than they thought.

No pass mark, but a clear expectation

Harrow sets no formal pass mark for Common Entrance. It does, however, say that it hopes boys will achieve an average of at least 65 percent across their subjects, and it uses the results to place each new boy in his starting sets in September of Year 9. Those starting sets shape a boy's whole first year, in mathematics and Latin above all. Harrow also weights results to reward boys who attempt the harder papers.

Which papers, depending on where your son is at school

Current schoolPapers expected in June of Year 8
UK prep school teaching the full Common Entrance syllabusAll subjects: mathematics, English, science, French, Latin, history, geography, and possibly theology, philosophy and religion. Core papers in English, maths and science; Level 2 or 3 in Latin and French; the Additional maths paper for boys aiming at the top sets
School teaching some Common Entrance subjectsAt least English, mathematics and science (Core), plus the Additional maths paper for strong mathematicians, and French and Latin if studied
State school or school overseasAt least English, science and mathematics (Core, preferably with the Additional paper), with mental arithmetic; French and Latin only if studied

For boys at school in Singapore, mainland China or Hong Kong, the Additional mathematics paper is compulsory. Boys sitting French are asked, where possible, to take the speaking and listening papers in May as well as the written paper in June.

Boys from outside the British system

A boy at an international or national school abroad has never been taught for Common Entrance, and Harrow knows it. He does not need to have studied French or Latin, and he sits a limited range of core papers whose main purpose is setting. Harrow states plainly that boys who have not had specific Common Entrance preparation are not disadvantaged.

That said, two things trip such boys up. The first is format: Common Entrance papers ask for written working and structured answers in English, which a boy used to multiple-choice tests or to another language of instruction has rarely practised. The second is the Additional mathematics paper, compulsory for some and expected of any boy aiming at the top set, which goes further than the Core paper.

Your son holds a Harrow offer and is at school outside the UK? Let's plan Year 8.

Common Entrance or the Academic Scholarship

A boy invited to sit the Academic Scholarship examinations, in January and February of Year 8, and who performs well in them, is unlikely to be asked to sit Common Entrance at all. For a strong boy, the scholarship route can therefore replace June with February, with a 5 percent fee remission and a scholar's standing on top. The scholarship papers go wider, however: analytical reasoning, philosophy and applied ethics, linguistic skills and several humanities and languages.

One school only

A boy may apply to several schools, but he sits Common Entrance for one school only. By June of Year 8, the choice has to be made. Families holding offers from Harrow and from another school must decide before the examination, not after.

How I prepare boys for Common Entrance

For boys outside the British system, I build Year 7 and Year 8 around the Core papers in English, mathematics and science, with written answers in the expected format, and add the Additional mathematics paper for those who can reach it. For boys already at prep schools, I target the subjects that weigh on setting, usually mathematics and Latin. The objective is the same: a confirmed place, and a strong start in September.
