How to get into Blair Academy

Blair Academy, Blairstown, New Jersey. Photo Mcollins109, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

Blair Academy sits on 463 acres in Blairstown, New Jersey, 65 miles from New York City. Founded in 1848, it has 484 students, a student-to-faculty ratio of 5 to 1 and an acceptance rate of 15 %. Nine in ten teachers live on campus, which says much about the school's idea of education: knowing each student well.

I have students at Blair Academy. The process runs as follows.

Four steps

StepDetail
1. InquiryThe online inquiry form opens the process and the interview calendar
2. Visit and interviewRequired; student-led tour and interview on campus, about two hours, from 3 September to 15 January
3. ApplicationSAO or Gateway, from 1 August; $75 in the US, $140 abroad
4. Supporting documentsEnglish and math recommendations, transcripts for three years, optional test scores, by 15 January

Decisions are emailed on 10 March. Applications received after 15 January are considered on a rolling basis.

An interview that matters

Blair requires every applicant to interview, ideally on campus. The school says a personal interview is the most helpful element for the Admission Committee in making a decision that is truly in the applicant's interest. Families who cannot travel interview by Zoom; applicants from mainland China use Vericant.

What Blair looks for

Blair seeks applicants of high character who are kind, with strong academic ability, a real passion for learning, a strong work ethic and intellectual curiosity. It values students who are engaged and collaborative, who will take part in life "on the hilltop", and who understand and appreciate different viewpoints.

Is your child in 7th or 8th grade? Tell me about them; interviews run from September.

Testing and aid

Standardised tests are not required but preferred: the SSAT or ISEE for grades 9 and 10, other tests for older applicants. Blair meets 100 % of demonstrated need for admitted families, in grants rather than loans; more than a third of students receive aid, and international families can apply too. There are no merit scholarships.
