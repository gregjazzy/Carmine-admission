The Strength of Ecole Jeannine Manuel: Academic Excellence and International Outlook

Photo: École Jeannine Manuel

Ecole Jeannine Manuel January 2026 · updated November 2026 5 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. Has worked with students of École Jeannine Manuel for more than ten years.

Book a conversation

When bilingual families look for a school in Paris, one name keeps coming up: École Jeannine Manuel. Not for a place in a magazine ranking, but because it is one of the few schools in France where bilingualism is not an add-on to the curriculum: it is its structure. Having watched students I have worked with for more than ten years grow up in its classrooms, I will say it plainly: it is one of the strongest choices for a bilingual family, and one of the most demanding. Its strength lies precisely in that demand.

Bilingualism as structure, not as an option

In Paris, dozens of schools offer an international section or a bilingual track, added to a standard French curriculum. At Jeannine Manuel, it is the other way round. Each teacher teaches in their own language, French or English, and the school has written its own English, science and Chinese programmes. In middle school, every student is in the American international section. Students do not just "do English": they learn, reason and write in English, without letting go of French.

A French school, not a more relaxed one

A misconception often heard from families coming from the American system or the IB is that "it is an international school, so it must be more relaxed than the French system". It is not. The school is under contract with the French state and follows the national curriculum, in mathematics in particular, with the standards that come with it. A student arriving from the American system or the MYP meets a real gap: written proofs, justified geometry, structured French-style reasoning. It is not a question of intelligence, but of method.

Is your child coming from the American system or the IB? I measure the gap with three tests at the school's level, in French, English and mathematics. Ask for an assessment.

In lycée, a launchpad to higher education

In the last two years, the school offers two programmes: the French International Baccalaureate (BFI), in the American section with Advanced Placement courses, and the International Baccalaureate (IB). The BFI remains a French baccalaureate, opening access to Parcoursup and the classes préparatoires, while adding an international dimension that speaks to universities abroad. That double access is what makes it one of the most useful diplomas for a bilingual student. Destinations are covered in university outcomes after Jeannine Manuel.

A culture of high standards

The teaching style is demanding, and I see it every week with my students. Those who come from schools where grading is generous and feedback always positive are sometimes thrown: here, 12 out of 20 in French is not a bad grade, it is an honest one that shows what remains to be worked on. This culture is unsettling at first. Yet it produces students who approach the most selective programmes without unpleasant surprises.

What that demand asks of the student

The school is not looking for perfect students. What I see in those who thrive is ease in moving between languages and methods, a solid level in every subject, stamina under a heavy workload, and genuine curiosity beyond the classroom. The profile is covered in detail in the student profile Jeannine Manuel looks for.

Tests in French, English and mathematics, then an interview: tell me about your child and we will see how to prepare them for each step.

Why it is not for everyone

It would be dishonest to present Jeannine Manuel as a universal answer. The workload is intense, and expectations are high from sixième (age 11). A student uncomfortable in one of the two languages will struggle; a student looking for a relaxed setting will be frustrated; parents expecting straight 18s out of 20 will be disappointed.

The school suits a bilingual, curious student who can handle pressure, whose family knows that excellence is paid for in daily effort. If that is your child, it is probably one of the best decisions you will make for their schooling. If not, other schools, just as respectable, will suit them better.
