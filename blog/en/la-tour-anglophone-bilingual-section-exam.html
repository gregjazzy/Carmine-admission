La Tour: the entrance exam for the anglophone or bilingual sections

Paris schools November 2026 5 min read

At La Tour, students enter the school on a file, a letter and an appointment. But what makes the school's reputation, its anglophone section and language sections, is won by exam. It is the step bilingual families underestimate most: they think speaking the language is enough. I have students at La Tour, and every year I prepare children for these exams.

The sections, and what they ask

The anglophone section, created in 1989 for families returning from abroad, now has more than 900 students in collège and lycée, across three programmes. Anglophone Studies is for bilingual students who speak and write English fluently: two lessons of literature and history of the English-speaking world on top of everyone's English, with textbooks from English-speaking countries and native teachers. Advanced English is for the best students of academic English, with excellent results. Reinforced English, in sixième and cinquième, can lead to it. Add the Spanish, Italian, Arabic and Chinese bilingual sections, and advanced-bilingual sections. The most demanding sections open by exam.

Preparing for the section exam

I prepare each child for the exam of the section they are aiming for, written and spoken, with work timed to the date of their test. Let's start with a first conversation about your child.

What the school does not say

La Tour describes its sections. It says nothing about the content of the exam, its format, or the level of writing that separates an applicant admitted to Anglophone Studies from one steered towards Advanced English. Yet that is what decides, and it is what I know through my students.

Why bilingual children fail it

Perfect speaking, lagging writing

A child returning from an English-speaking country often speaks impeccable English. But if they left English-speaking school early, their writing, long reading, composition, academic vocabulary, has fallen behind. Anglophone Studies asks for both.

Is your child's written English at the section's level? Tell me about them: I will tell you which programme to aim for.

Aiming too high, or not high enough

Entering a child for Anglophone Studies without the writing it requires risks a refusal; entering them for Reinforced English when they could aim higher wastes years. The right programme is chosen on real level, not on ease at home.

Forgetting the rest of the file

The section is won by exam, but the place in the school is won by the file, the letter and the appointment. A very good exam does not make up for uneven reports.

When to start

Sixième applications open in mid-September of the CM2 year, other years in January, and section exams follow. A gap in written English takes several months to close: you need to know where the child stands from the start of the year.
