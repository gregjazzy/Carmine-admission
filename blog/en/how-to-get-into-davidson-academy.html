How to get into the Davidson Academy

The University of Nevada, Reno campus, home of the Davidson Academy. Photo NevadaMarCom, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

US schools November 2026 7 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

The Davidson Academy, on the University of Nevada, Reno campus, is a free public middle and high school for profoundly gifted students, those who score at the 99.9th percentile on IQ and achievement tests. Classes group students by ability, not age, and students work at least three years ahead in every subject. Families come from across the country and abroad, and every admitted student must live in Reno.

I work with families of very advanced students, often in mathematics. Here is how Davidson admission works for 2027-28.

Three filters

StageWhat it checks
Test scoresSAT, ACT or IQ results meeting the thresholds for the student's age-based grade
ApplicationReports, three recommendations (math teacher, English teacher, one other), parent agreement forms
Academic Readiness AssessmentA day of curriculum-based work in math, science reasoning, reading, discussion and writing, plus observed work habits

The 2027-28 application opened on August 17, 2026 and closes on January 20, 2027 at 4 p.m. Pacific. Assessments run monthly from September to January, with limited places each month, so applying early brings an earlier assessment date.

Scores are a floor, not a ticket

The published thresholds are high: for a sixth grader, for example, at least two of a 630 SAT Reading and Writing, a 650 SAT Math and a 1280 total, or an IQ of 145 or more on an accepted individual test. Yet the school states plainly that all scores are considered, and that students whose scores only just meet the minimum may not be invited to assess. The strongest applications go beyond the scores.

The readiness assessment decides

The Academic Readiness Assessment is built from the Academy's own curriculum and scored by several raters. The school looks for 80 percent or more, and accepts 70 percent when the rest of the file is strong. Writing carries real weight, since the Academy teaches writing across the curriculum, and so does mathematics: a seventh grader must be ready for Algebra I, an eighth grader for Geometry.

Residency and cost

Applicants must be, or intend to become, Nevada residents and live in Reno before school starts in August. For Nevada residents there is no tuition. The cost for most families is the move itself.
