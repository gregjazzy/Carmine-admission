International students and the AEIS

Families moving to Singapore often hope to apply straight to Raffles Institution. That is not how it works. The school sends international students to the Ministry of Education first, because entry into Singapore's schools from abroad goes through a national exercise.

The AEIS
NameAdmissions Exercise for International Students
Run byThe Ministry of Education, centrally
LevelsPrimary 2 to 5, and Secondary 1 to 3, for the following year
TimingExercise typically starts in July; in 2026, tests in Singapore from 1 to 3 September
StartJanuary of the following year
OutcomeNot guaranteed; depends on test performance, then on vacancies and the declared residential area

From a mainstream school to Raffles

Raffles' own admission pages list international students among those eligible for several routes, once they are in Singapore's mainstream system. A boy in Primary 6 at a mainstream primary school can enter Year 1 through the S1 Posting on his PSLE results, or the appeal that follows. A boy in Secondary 2 at a mainstream school can apply for the Year 3 intake. Students in Secondary 4 can apply for Year 5 through the JAE.

Planning the timing

Because the AEIS places students for January, a family arriving mid-year should plan around the September tests. A boy who enters a mainstream primary school through the AEIS in, say, Primary 4 or 5 then has time to adapt to the Singapore curriculum before the PSLE, which is where the route to Raffles Year 1 is decided.

Moving to Singapore with a school-age son? Let's plan the steps.

Languages and curriculum

The AEIS consists of English and mathematics tests. At secondary level in 2026, families applied in July through the Ministry's Candidates Portal; Secondary 1 candidates sat the tests on 1 September, Secondary 2 and 3 candidates on 2 September. Children from other systems often meet a different pace in mathematics and a more demanding standard of written English; preparation before the September tests helps them start well.
