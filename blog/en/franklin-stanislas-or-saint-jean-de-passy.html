Franklin, Stanislas or Saint-Jean-de-Passy: how to choose

Saint-Louis-de-Gonzague, Paris 16th. Photo Ornd, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

Franklin, Stanislas and Saint-Jean-de-Passy are three of the most sought-after Catholic schools in Paris, and many families apply to all three. They then discover that these are not three similar applications: each has its own procedure, calendar and way of looking at a child. Getting one wrong often means losing a year.

Three calendars that look nothing alike

Franklin opens sixième applications only from 15 to 30 September of the CM2 year, then closes. Stanislas publishes no dates: it reads files as they arrive, and places go one by one; so you must apply in the autumn before entry. Saint-Jean-de-Passy publishes opening dates by year group, sixième in mid-September, but no closing date. A family that follows the same rhythm for all three almost always misses one.

Three applications, one strategy

I keep all three calendars with you, prepare applications of excellence tailored to each school, and bring your child to the appointments and interviews at the top of their level. My aim: that they get into the one that suits them best.

Three ways of selecting

At Franklin, no written exam: an online file reviewed by the director or the préfet, then an appointment that does not guarantee enrolment. At Saint-Jean-de-Passy, a paper file with a handwritten letter from the parents, and an interview that both parents must attend. At Stanislas, an online pre-registration then a file that varies by year group. In all three, the Catholic educational project matters, and the family must show it embraces it.

Aiming for two or three of these schools? Write to me: I will tell you in what order to apply, and how to prepare each appointment.

For which child?

All three share high standards and a clear framework. Franklin, a Jesuit school, emphasises human and spiritual formation and extends its lycée with an economics prépa that sends students to HEC, ESSEC and ESCP every year. Stanislas, with its classes préparatoires and boarding house, suits the solid, hard-working student aiming for prépa. Saint-Jean-de-Passy is known for its demands from collège onwards and expects genuine family commitment to its framework. The right choice depends on the child's temperament: not everyone lives with high standards in the same way.
