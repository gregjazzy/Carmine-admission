Music awards at Wycombe Abbey

Entrance to Wycombe Abbey, High Wycombe. Photo David Hillas, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 2.0

English boarding schools November 2026 4 min read

Music awards at Wycombe Abbey follow the school's general rule for scholarships: prestige first, limited financial value. What they provide is not a reduction in fees but free specialist tuition, which for a serious young musician is worth a great deal over five or seven years.

Three kinds of award

AwardWhat it provides
Music ScholarshipFree tuition on two instruments, or one instrument and voice
Music ExhibitionFree tuition on one instrument (not voice)
Choral AwardFree voice tuition

The awards exist at 11+, 13+ and 16+, and at 16+ they are open to internal and external candidates alike.

The level expected

EntryFirst instrument, as a guide
11+At least ABRSM Grade 5 or equivalent
13+At least ABRSM Grade 6 or equivalent
16+About Grade 8, with technical and expressive mastery and ensemble experience

Grades are a guide, not a threshold: the school looks above all for a girl who loves music and shows unusual flair at whatever level she has reached. Choral candidates need a strong background of singing in parts; at 16+, Grade 7 or above in singing is the reference, with excellent aural perception and sight-reading.

The academic bar comes first

A music award only counts if the girl meets the academic criteria for entry. I prepare young musicians for the Pre-Test and entrance papers, so their talent has its chance.

Dates for 2027 entry

  • 11+: auditions on 27 January 2027, the day before the academic scholarship papers.
  • 13+: auditions on 13 January 2027, the day before the Assessment Day.
  • 16+: auditions on 13 October 2026, the day after the entrance exams.

Music scholarship forms are sent to registered candidates before the assessment.

Music and academic entry together

Every music candidate must be registered by 1 June of the year before entry and must satisfy the academic criteria. In practice, a gifted musician prepares on two fronts in the same months: her instrument for the audition, and the Pre-Test and entrance papers for the place.

What an audition shows

The school's own criteria point to what matters on the day: love of music and flair at every age, then, at 16+, technical and expressive mastery, commitment through ensembles, aural perception and sight-reading. A girl is better served by pieces she plays with conviction than by the hardest pieces she can just manage, and by regular sight-reading practice in the months before.
