École des Roches: the IB Diploma and the foundation year

Les Roches is first a French school. But in recent years the International Baccalaureate Diploma has become a path in its own right, with an asset few French boarding schools offer: a foundation year for students not yet ready to go straight into the programme.

Three years, or two

The IB Diploma takes two years: six subjects chosen across the subject groups, three at Higher Level and three at Standard Level, the Extended Essay, Theory of Knowledge and the CAS programme (creativity, activity, service). At Les Roches, a foundation year can come first. It is recommended for students new to the IB, who need to strengthen their English or consolidate their foundations. It is not compulsory: a student already at ease goes straight into the first year.

The English level expected is that of a good bilingual student: reading, writing, speaking and understanding well enough to follow a demanding programme entirely in English. Students usually join at 14 or 15, in the foundation year or in IB year one.

The right path to university

The IB is not the right path for every student. I know which diploma serves which ambition, and I prepare applications to the standard of the most demanding universities: since 2021, my students have been admitted to Stanford, Columbia, EPFL, Bocconi and King's College.

Jeannine Manuel's support

The programme benefits from the partnership with École Jeannine Manuel, which brings its experience of the IB and of guidance towards leading universities in Europe, the UK and North America. Classes are small and run as seminars, with two supervised study periods a day and weekly progress checks. Students can choose five-day boarding, for families nearby, or seven-day boarding.

The fees

In 2026-2027, the Diploma Programme costs €47,520 a year with five-day boarding and €57,420 with seven-day boarding, plus a one-off registration fee of €5,900 in the first year. That is markedly less than the leading Swiss boarding schools preparing the same diploma.

Choosing the right diploma

I have students at École des Roches and have followed students of Jeannine Manuel, its partner, for more than ten years. What I look at before recommending the IB is the student's real written English, not their spoken ease, and the universities they are aiming for: for British or North American universities, the IB is often the best path; for a mainly French plan, the bac or the BFI serve better. When the IB is the answer, the question of the foundation year comes up at once, and it often makes the difference on arrival.
