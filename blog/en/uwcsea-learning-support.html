UWCSEA welcomes applicants with mild learning difficulties. A small team of experienced learning support teachers works across all grades to help students access the curriculum, and places in the programme are limited in each grade. The school is equally clear about its limits: with the resources on its campuses, it is not an appropriate school for students with moderate to severe learning or behavioural support needs.

Before applying

If a child has received individual or small-group support in the past, the family should contact the admissions team for guidance before submitting an application. The application itself must include current and past school reports, full details of any learning support received, and copies of every specialist report, from an educational psychologist, occupational therapist or speech and language therapist. UWCSEA may ask for more information about a child's learning profile even when the family has not asked for support.

How the child is assessed

SectionAssessment
Infant SchoolA 30 to 60-minute session of play-based activities; a comforting toy is welcome. No virtual assessment for applicants abroad: the school gathers information from the current school instead.
Primary SchoolUp to 60 minutes on reading, writing, language, spelling or maths, in conversation with a learning support teacher.
Middle School60 to 90 minutes, starting with a conversation about the child as a person and a learner, then academic and self-management tasks.
High SchoolA session with a High School learning support teacher, alongside the general High School process.

The school prefers to assess in person. It may also, with the family's permission, contact the current school, observe the child in their own classroom, or invite them to spend time with UWCSEA students. Parents are not present during observations.

Why the answer comes later

UWCSEA confirms its learning support capacity only after reviewing the ongoing needs of current students, a process completed by February. As a result, applicants who may need support are usually not offered a place in the first round, even if they applied before the first deadline. Their application rolls into the second round, with offers in March.

Does your child have a support plan at school? Tell me about it before you apply.

The decision

The decision rests on whether UWCSEA can support the child effectively within its programme. The learning support coordinator and members of the leadership team review the information together. Families do not receive detailed individual feedback.

Applying again

A family reapplying after an unsuccessful application may be advised not to apply too early in the cycle, so that a complete new set of school or specialist reports can be included.
