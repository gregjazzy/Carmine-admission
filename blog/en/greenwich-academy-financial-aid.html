Greenwich Academy awarded more than $9.8 million in financial aid last year. Almost a quarter of its students, 23 percent, receive help, with an average award of $43,015, and half of the families who receive aid earn more than $150,000 a year. Admission is need-blind: the decision is not influenced by a family's ability to pay.

I help families plan aid alongside admission. Here is how it works, and the rules that most often catch families out.

How need is assessed

The school's method is modelled on the College Scholarship Service. Clarity's estimate is the baseline, but Greenwich Academy's committee adjusts it in light of its total budget, its policies and the whole applicant pool, and may ask a family to contribute more or less than Clarity suggests. Two-parent families are expected to work full time where health and home allow; if one parent chooses not to work while the children are in school, the committee imputes a minimum income. Families are expected to have cut non-essential spending such as club memberships or vacation homes. Divorced or separated parents both complete Clarity, and a step-parent's assets may be considered.

The December 15 trap

The aid deadline falls a month before the admission deadline of January 15, and on the same day as Part I. A family that treats aid as something to look at after the application is complete will miss it. No grant is made and no contract issued until all forms are in, so the safest plan is to start Clarity when it opens on September 1.
