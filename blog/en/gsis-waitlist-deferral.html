The GSIS waitlist and deferral

At German Swiss International School, applying does not lead straight to an assessment. Every application, once acknowledged within one or two working days, places the child on the waitlist for their year group. From there, invitations to assess are sent according to admissions priority, and in the English International Stream they are not guaranteed. Understanding what happens on that list avoids most of the anxiety families feel while waiting.

I explain these mechanics to every family I accompany to GSIS. Here they are.

Four situations

SituationWhat happens
No invitation because of a long waitlistThe application is deferred to the next available year group, at no extra cost, keeping the original date
Deferral from Kindergarten to Primary or SecondaryA one-time fee of HKD 1,350, the difference in application fees
Assessment not passedDeferral for at least two years, without a new application
A place opens during the yearThe next applicant in line is contacted; a new test or interview may be arranged

The school does not disclose an applicant's position on the waitlist, which changes daily, nor other applicants' priority. A family therefore cannot know exactly how close it is; it can only keep the application alive and the child ready.

The two-year rule

A child who does not pass the assessment for the year group applied for is offered a deferral of at least two years; the reasoning is that a child benefits from consolidating at least a year of education before sitting the entrance assessment again. For a family, that means a failed assessment in Year 2 leads to the next attempt for Year 4 at the earliest. The cost of arriving unprepared is therefore not one year but two.

Places that open mid-year

When a vacancy arises during the school year, the admissions office contacts the next applicant in line to ask if they are still interested. If so, a new entrance test or interview may be arranged before the place is confirmed. These calls can come at short notice, months after the original assessment, and a child whose English or mathematics has slipped since then can lose a place that was within reach.

Declining an offer

A family that declines an offer has its application archived; to be considered for a later year, it must reapply, losing the original date. With priority partly based on that date, declining is a decision worth weighing carefully.
