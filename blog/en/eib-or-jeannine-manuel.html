EIB or Jeannine Manuel: how to choose

Paris schools November 2026 5 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

Book a conversation

For a family wanting a bilingual school in central Paris, EIB and Jeannine Manuel are often the first two names. From afar they look alike: two private bilingual schools leading to the bac, the BFI and the IB. Up close, they neither recruit the same way nor the same child.

Two ways of recruiting

EIB is a network of eight schools, from primary to lycée, which admits students all year, subject to places, after a meeting and an assessment. Jeannine Manuel is a single school that opens mainly five entry years, from the second year of nursery to seconde, with applications closing in mid-January for families living in France; elsewhere, a place only exists if a student leaves. Every year, the great majority of its applicants leave without a place.

Two applications, one strategy

English, and what is required

At Jeannine Manuel, English is a condition: from CE1, the school turns away beginners in English. At EIB, requirements depend on the school: the half-English schools require both languages from CE1, but offer immersion classes and reinforcement, while Lamartine and Monceau follow the French curriculum with daily English in level groups. A child with school English therefore has a real door at EIB, and very little at Jeannine Manuel outside the entry years.

EIB, Jeannine Manuel, or both? Tell me about your child: I will tell you where their chances are most real.

In lycée

Both prepare the BFI and the IB. At EIB Lycée Étoile, the IB is outside the state contract, with its own fees, and a student can move from IB seconde to the French première by tests. For a plan aimed at English-speaking universities, Jeannine Manuel keeps an edge through the density of written English. For an open plan, EIB offers more flexibility.
