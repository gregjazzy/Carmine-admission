At German Swiss International School, the fees a family sees first are the annual tuition. The larger decision usually comes with the offer of a place: every student must be covered by a debenture or by the Capital Levy, and the choice between them affects cost, refundability and even admissions priority.

I go through these numbers with each family I accompany to GSIS. Here they are, in Hong Kong dollars.

Fees are paid in two instalments, at the start of September and of March, and are subject to Education Bureau approval. Bus fees, PE uniform, afternoon activities, exam fees and overseas trips are extra; the school bus is compulsory for students at the Pok Fu Lam campus. On acceptance of a place, a registration fee equal to one month's fees is invoiced in March and offset against the first instalment, but forfeited if the student withdraws before term starts.

The four debenture options

OptionAmountKey point
Standard Debenture800,000Refunded on written application when the student leaves, subject to conditions
Development Debenture350,000Non-refundable; keeps a nominal value of HKD 1 and future nomination priority after upgrade
Infrastructure Debenture6,000,000Non-refundable, non-transferable; first admissions priority; bought before the admissions process
Capital Levy40,000 a yearNon-refundable; new students only; limited number; lower priority than any debenture

The comparison is less obvious than the amounts suggest. A Standard Debenture ties up HKD 800,000 but comes back; a Capital Levy costs nothing up front but HKD 40,000 every year, which adds up to HKD 400,000 over ten years of schooling and is never returned. Families planning a short stay in Hong Kong and families planning a whole school career reach different answers. A further rule applies: if a student does not start, or leaves within six months of starting, a levy of 20 percent of the Standard Debenture's nominal value is charged.

When the decision is made

The debenture or levy must be finalised by the deadline in the offer letter. The Infrastructure Debenture is the exception: it must be bought before taking part in the annual admissions process, since it is what gives first priority. Its holder must still meet the standard entrance criteria, and a place must be available.
