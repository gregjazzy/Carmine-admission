The ISEB Common Pre-Test for Harrow: one attempt in Year 6

Every boy applying to Harrow for Year 9 sits the ISEB Common Pre-Test in the autumn of Year 6. It is the first academic filter, and the only one a boy faces before Harrow decides whether to invite him to the School. Two rules make it different from an ordinary practice test: it can be taken only once in an academic year, and only once after registering for Harrow. There is no second go in the spring.

What the test contains

The Pre-Test is an online, multiple-choice assessment in four areas: verbal reasoning, non-verbal reasoning, English and mathematics. Together they take about two and a half hours, and the sections can be sat in one go or spread over separate sessions. It is age-adaptive and standardised, so a boy sitting it early in Year 6, or one who is young for his year, is not penalised for that.

Two of the four sections are taught at school, and two are not. English and mathematics reward a good curriculum. Verbal and non-verbal reasoning, on the other hand, are question types most children meet only through specific practice: series, analogies, codes, hidden shapes, spatial rotation. A capable boy who has never seen them loses marks on format, not on ability.

When and where

Standard applicants sit it between 1 October and the end of November of Year 6. A boy at a UK prep school usually takes it at school, which knows the routine. A boy at a state school, or at a school overseas, needs his school to provide a room, a computer with internet access and an adult to supervise; if the school cannot, a British Council office or another educational institution can host it. Families abroad should arrange this in September, not discover in November that their school has never run it.

If a boy has already sat the Pre-Test for another school in the same year, Harrow can read the same result through his ISEB applicant ID. He does not sit it again, and cannot: the result he has is the one every school will see.

Why a single attempt changes the strategy

Because the result is shared across schools, the Pre-Test is not a Harrow test so much as a boy's one card for the year, played for every school on his list that uses it. That argues for two things. First, preparation should be finished before the first section is sat, not continued alongside it. Second, the order of schools matters less than the date: a boy should sit when he is ready, within the window, not on the first available day because a friend did.

It also explains why families sometimes misread their result. A good score is necessary, not sufficient: Harrow combines it with the confidential reference from the current school, and a small number of boys are de-selected at this stage, while most go on to the Harrow Test. For late applicants the bar is higher, since they are invited only with a very strong reference and an equally strong Pre-Test.

How much preparation is sensible

Harrow itself suggests looking at the ISEB walkthrough and practising questions in all four areas. In practice, a boy who is strong at school usually needs a few weeks of regular, short sessions on reasoning formats and on working at speed on screen, then two or three full timed runs. Drilling for months is counter-productive: the test adapts to the child, and a tired boy does worse than a fresh one.

For boys educated in another language, reading speed in English is the hidden variable. Verbal reasoning in particular assumes a vocabulary that a bilingual child may not have built in English, and that is worth working on from the summer before Year 6.

How I prepare boys for the Pre-Test

I begin with a diagnostic across the four sections to find where marks are lost, then concentrate on the reasoning formats and on timing, using adaptive online practice similar to the real thing. We finish with full timed runs, and I make sure the practical side is settled: date, place, supervisor, and a boy who knows the screen as well as the questions.
