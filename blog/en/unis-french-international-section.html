The UNIS French International Section: for which child?

United Nations International School, on the East River, Manhattan. Photo Transpoman, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

Book a conversation

French was the first modern language taught at UNIS, and the school still runs a full French International Section, from Kindergarten to 12th grade, with around twenty French teachers from across the French-speaking world. For a French or francophone family arriving in New York, it offers a particular combination: a large international school taught in English, the IB, and French kept at native level along the way. It also has limits that are easy to miss in a first conversation, and they decide whether it suits your child.

Not a bilingual program

The section is designed for children who already speak French fluently and, depending on age, read and write it. They are taught as they would be in a French school, in their own classes, for part of the week. It is not an immersion track for children who are learning French; those children follow the French as a foreign language path (French B), with four weekly sessions in the Junior School and three in Middle School and Tutorial House. Entry into the section itself, French A, is by placement test.

The accredited years, CE1 to CM2, follow the official French program with national assessments during the year, and they allow French families who meet the criteria to apply for consular scholarships. The section is affiliated with the Mission laïque française.

The three limits to know

No French baccalauréat. UNIS does not prepare for the bac. The section leads to the IB Diploma, with French literature at the highest level. For a family that wants the bac, the answer is a French lycée.

Mathematics leaves French at 6e. From 6e (M2), the French section covers French and humanities; mathematics and sciences are taught in English with the rest of the school. A child who will return to a French collège or lycée will know the mathematics but not always its French vocabulary and its way of writing proofs, and that gap is worth working on before the return.

Accreditation stops at CM2. From 6e, the French courses mirror those in France and are taught by teachers trained in the French national system, but the years are not accredited. A family who expects to return to France during collège should plan that return with the school it will enter, not assume a seamless transfer.

For which child, then?

The section suits a francophone child whose family expects to stay in the international system, to aim for universities in the United States, the United Kingdom, Canada or Europe through the IB, and who wants to keep French as a true first language rather than let it fade. It suits less well a family that will go back to France within a few years and needs the French system's continuity, especially for mathematics.

Inside UNIS, the benefit is concrete: a child who keeps French at native level and adds English at the same level can take IB French literature and aim for the bilingual diploma. For admissions to McGill, to Sciences Po's joint programs or to European universities, that profile is a strength.

Admission to the section

The child applies to UNIS through the normal process for their grade: observation for the youngest, a test score and a meeting from 5th grade. The placement test for French A is added. From 2nd grade, children in the section enter the grade recommended by their previous school, which matters for families coming from the French calendar.
