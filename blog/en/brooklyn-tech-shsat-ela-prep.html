Preparing the SHSAT English section

Brooklyn Tech has a technical reputation, and candidates sometimes put their energy into math alone. The English section, however, weighs exactly as much in the composite. It is also the section where steady habits, built over months, make the biggest difference.

What the section asks

TaskSkill behind it
Revising and editingKnowing the rules of standard written English and choosing the clearest version of a sentence or paragraph
Reading comprehensionUnderstanding meaning, structure and purpose, and pointing to the evidence for an answer

The section has 50 questions. Reading questions come in groups attached to one text; inside a group, nothing is final until the student submits it, and once submitted the group cannot be reopened.

Building the habit

  • Read daily outside school: fiction, non-fiction, long articles. Reading volume is the base of comprehension.
  • Read actively: after a chapter or article, state its main idea in one sentence and find the line that proves it.
  • Study grammar explicitly: punctuation, agreement, modifiers, transitions. Editing questions reward rules, not instinct.
  • Practise passage sets on screen, checking the whole set before submitting.

The section many candidates neglect

I prepare Brooklyn Tech candidates for the English section with the same care as math: reading strategy, grammar, and timed passage sets.

Grammar worth mastering

Among the rules of standard written English, a handful deserve particular attention when preparing editing questions: comma use and run-on sentences, subject-verb agreement, pronoun clarity, misplaced modifiers, parallel structure, and the transition word that links two ideas correctly. A student who knows these rules explicitly answers quickly and confidently, leaving more time for long passages.

A year, in three stages

In the spring of 7th grade, a diagnostic on the official practice test shows whether the weakness is editing, reading or time. Over the summer, reading volume and grammar rules carry most of the work. In the autumn of 8th grade, timed passage sets on computer bring the section to test conditions.

For English learners

A student still classified as an English language learner, or who left that status within the past two years, can be granted twice the standard time. Footnotes in the reading passages are offered in translation, but never the questions. For these students, a year of steady reading in English matters even more than for others.
