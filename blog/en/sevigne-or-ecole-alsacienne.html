Sévigné or the École alsacienne: how to choose

Collège Sévigné, rue Pierre-Nicole, Paris 5th. Photo LPLT, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0

Paris schools November 2026 5 min read

The two schools are so alike that many families apply to both. Sévigné and the École alsacienne are secular, private under state contract, a few streets apart, and both claim an excellence that does not come through competition. Yet they do not suit exactly the same child.

What brings them together

The same philosophy, first. Sévigné speaks of "excellence without competition" and says it rejects elitism; the Alsacienne excludes competition and elitism from its education. Both run from nursery to terminale, give real weight to the humanities, classical languages and the arts, and achieve excellent bac results: 99% honours at Sévigné in 2026, 93% at the Alsacienne.

Choosing, then getting in

I help you see which of the two schools truly suits your child, then prepare their application to one, the other or both, with their own tests and calendars. My aim: that they get into the one where they will thrive.

What sets them apart

Size. Sévigné claims a small size where everyone is known; the Alsacienne has about 1,850 students. Languages. Sévigné is bilingual from nursery to terminale, with a bilingual track and the BFI in lycée; the Alsacienne offers European and Oriental sections, without the BFI. Entry. At Sévigné, the sixième test depends on the chosen track, with or without English; at the Alsacienne, a French and maths test in CM1 and sixième, then an interview with the child alone and a teacher. Selection. The Alsacienne receives about seven applicants per place; Sévigné welcomes about 35 new students in seconde each year.

Sévigné or the Alsacienne for your child? Tell me about them: I will tell you which one fits them.

For which child?

Sévigné particularly suits a bilingual child, or a family wanting a genuine bilingual path through to the BFI, in a very human-scale school. The Alsacienne suits a curious child who will thrive in a larger school with a very rich community life, whose horizon is French first, with international openness through exchanges. For many families, the right strategy is to apply to both.
