École des Roches or a Swiss boarding school: how to choose

École des Roches, Verneuil-sur-Avre. Photo Djampa, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

Families considering a boarding school for their child often look at Switzerland first. The École des Roches, in Normandy, nonetheless deserves to be part of the same comparison: it is an international boarding school more than 125 years old, just over an hour from Paris, in partnership with the École Jeannine Manuel. The two choices are not for the same child.

What sets them apart

Distance, first: Les Roches is an hour and a quarter from Paris by direct train, while the leading Swiss boarding schools require a journey. Size, next: about 240 students at Les Roches, around 460 at Le Rosey and 500 at Aiglon. Programmes: Les Roches offers the French bac, the BFI and the IB, like an extended French curriculum; Le Rosey is bilingual with IB or bac; Aiglon and Rosenberg are English-speaking. Selection, finally: the leading Swiss boarding schools turn away excellent applicants every year and, like Le Rosey, apply strict quotas; Les Roches guides students to the right programme more than it sorts them.

The right boarding school, then the right application

I help you choose the boarding school that suits your child, in France or Switzerland, then prepare their application according to each school's rules: fifteen of my students have been admitted to Le Rosey, and I have students at Les Roches as at Aiglon and Rosenberg.

For which child?

Les Roches suits a child who keeps a French horizon, or wants to become fully French-speaking, and a family that wants a boarding school nearby. The leading Swiss boarding schools suit a child ready to live far away, in a very international community, aiming first for English-speaking universities. Between the two, it is not prestige that should decide, but the child: their language, age, independence, and what they are aiming for after school.
