Institut auf dem Rosenberg: admission, application and entry exams

Institut auf dem Rosenberg, St. Gallen. Photo Rene Nueesch, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 3.0

Getting into Institut auf dem Rosenberg is unlike any other admission in Switzerland. The St. Gallen school does not fill year groups: it builds a community of students from all over the world, and each application is read as a portrait. I have helped students win places at Rosenberg and Aiglon, and fifteen at Le Rosey, and what makes the difference is almost never where families expect it.

What Rosenberg wants from an application

The school sketches the student it wants in a few strokes: a creator who means to make a mark in their chosen field, someone who aims high and accepts the work that takes, who appreciates the privilege of being there, who builds success on their own choices rather than on family legacy, and who is curious about the world, from technology and the arts to science and the economy.

Read quickly, the list sounds like every boarding school brochure. Read closely, it rules out one precise profile: the bright but passive child who follows the programme without doing anything with it. At Rosenberg, every student builds their own timetable from close to 500 courses; a school that works this way needs students who can choose. That is what the file must show before anything else.

The file, piece by piece

The application is made online. It brings together report cards for the last three years, a personal statement, a scan of the passport, any document that sheds light on the profile, and a non-refundable fee of CHF 1,000 per candidate.

Every piece can be worked on. Three years of reports tell a trajectory: an unexplained dip worries readers more than a single average grade. The personal statement can take any form, video, collage, digital presentation: a rare freedom, which most candidates waste on a conventional letter. As for supporting documents, a competition, a creation, a sustained commitment, they count as soon as they prove one of the qualities the school is looking for.

Arriving with a direction

In high school, Rosenberg prepares students for IGCSEs, the IB, APs, A-levels and its own diploma, and from Grade 9 each student follows an individual development plan that links courses to university ambitions. A candidate who already knows, even roughly, which universities and subjects they lean towards fits naturally into this system. One who has no idea is not ruled out, but must show they will be able to choose. I work on this question with the family before the file goes in, because it comes back at the interview. For the tracks in detail, see IB, A-levels or AP at Rosenberg.

After the first reading

Candidates shortlisted on paper are invited to an interview and to the entrance exams. Both can be prepared, and differently: the exams also serve to place students in the right levels, while the interview checks that the child matches the portrait drawn by the file. I cover them in two articles, on the entrance exams and on the interview.

The visit, then the decision

Rosenberg only opens its campus to families whose application is complete, out of respect for its students' privacy and to plan a personal visit. The visit therefore comes after the application, not before as at many boarding schools: you have to choose the school on paper first.

At the end of the process, the school makes either a direct offer or a conditional one. The family then has only a few days to accept and secure the place by settling the first invoice. That is short if you are still weighing Le Rosey or Aiglon: the comparison has to be done beforehand, not when the offer arrives. To place the three schools side by side, see Rosenberg, Le Rosey or Aiglon.
