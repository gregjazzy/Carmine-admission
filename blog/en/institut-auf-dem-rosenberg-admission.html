Institut auf dem Rosenberg: admission, application and entry exams

Institut auf dem Rosenberg, St. Gallen. Photo Rene Nueesch, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 3.0

Institut auf dem Rosenberg in St. Gallen was founded in 1889, and is probably the most unusual of the Swiss boarding schools. It welcomes students aged 6 to 18 from some fifty countries, in classes of eight on average, with one adult for every two students. Its admission reflects that: personalised, and centred on what the applicant will bring to the school.

The application

Applications are made online. The school asks for school reports from the last three years, a personal statement, a passport scan, any document that may support the application, and a non-refundable application fee. The personal statement can take any form: writing, a video, a collage, a digital presentation. It is a rare freedom, and a trap for those who take it lightly.

An application that stands out

I prepare with your child an application and personal statement that show what Rosenberg looks for: a creative mind, real potential, the wish to contribute. Then I prepare them for the interview and the entry exams. My aim: that they stand out from the first reading.

The interview and the exams

Applicants who pass the first reading of their file may be invited to a personal interview and to sit the school's entry exams. Rosenberg then reviews the results in light of the whole profile, and makes either a direct offer or a conditional one. The family then has a few days to accept and secure the place by paying the first invoice. The school also encourages families to visit the campus, at a time that suits them.

What the school looks for

Rosenberg says it expects internationally minded students with a creative mindset, strong academic potential and solid social skills, able to show how their personality and talents will enrich the school community and the world beyond. It wants them ambitious, ready to work to develop their talents, aware of their privilege, and focused on their own achievements rather than their family's legacy. That is the whole point of the personal statement and the interview: to show a project, not a status.
