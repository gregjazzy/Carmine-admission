The École alsacienne: where its students go after the bac

The École alsacienne, Paris 6th. Photo Celette, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

Paris schools November 2026 7 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

Book a conversation

Families aiming for the École alsacienne almost all ask me the same question, often without quite saying it: does a school that refuses competition and does not rank its students open the same doors as the leading lycées that do sort them? I have followed Alsacienne students for years, all the way to their admissions after the bac. My answer is yes, on one condition that few families see coming.

A school that opens every door

What strikes you, following its students, is that they do not all go to the same place. The class of 2025 split almost evenly between university, the classes préparatoires, post-bac schools and study abroad, and every student received at least one offer. Among them are students admitted to Louis-le-Grand and Henri-IV, Sciences Po, ESSEC, Polytechnique, McGill, King's College and the LSE.

That is the Alsacienne's real strength, and it comes from its project: a broad education, as literary and artistic as it is scientific, which lets each student find their own path instead of lining them up behind everyone else. A student who discovers in première that they want to study architecture, French and English law, or go to Montreal is not an exception there.

An application read at its true value

Since 2021, my students have been admitted to Columbia, Stanford, Harvard, EPFL, Bocconi, McGill, Imperial and King's College. I work with Alsacienne students from seconde onwards: specialities that serve their plans, an application that gives their grades the reference point they lack, and letters, essays and interviews at the level of the most selective programmes. My aim: the programme they are aiming for, not just an offer.

The condition: what families see too late

Opening every door is not walking through it. At the most sought-after programmes, the gap widens sharply. In 2025, 5 of its 46 applicants got a prépa place at Louis-le-Grand, 10 of 44 at Sciences Po Paris, 6 of 28 at PSL's CPES. Brilliant students, at an excellent school, and yet most came away without the programme they wanted.

What I see year after year is that this gap is not only about grades. It comes from a feature of the school that families underestimate: the Alsacienne does not rank its students or display weighted averages. Its school reports describe a student; they do not place them. A prépa or Sciences Po committee comparing hundreds of files from lycées that do rank has to read these grades without a reference point. The application that succeeds is the one that supplies that reference: consistent results in the subjects that matter for the chosen programme, specialities chosen accordingly from the end of seconde, teachers' comments that say what a ranking cannot, and a letter that does the rest.

The top prépas and Sciences Po read the reports from première and terminale. Tell me about your child's plans: I will tell you what their application needs to show, and from when.

Abroad: a real opening, to be prepared

The Alsacienne sends nearly a quarter of its graduates outside France, without the IB or a full international section: that comes from its exchanges, its English course for native speakers and an office that supports applications. But a British or North American application is built differently from a Parcoursup one, with predicted grades, essays and letters. Those of its students who get into McGill, King's College or the LSE did not start in terminale.

What I bring to Alsacienne students

I know the school from the inside: how it grades, what its school reports say to a committee in France or abroad, and what it takes for a good Alsacienne student to become a successful applicant. With each student I build a complete strategy: the programmes to aim for, in France and abroad, and in what order; the specialities and options that serve them; an application in which every piece, reports, teachers' comments, letter, essay, points in the same direction; and preparation for the interviews and orals. My students admitted since 2021 to Columbia (7), EPFL (12), McGill (15) or King's College (5) went through exactly this.
