Math Private Lessons for Henri IV Students

Private Tutoring February 2026 11 min read

Henri IV is not a school. It is an institution. The most prestigious public lycee in France, situated in the heart of Paris's 5th arrondissement on the Montagne Sainte-Genevieve, steps from the Pantheon. Every year, the best students in France converge here, selected on academic record, to follow a program that bears no resemblance to what 99% of French high schoolers experience. In mathematics, the level is stratospheric. That is not hyperbole. It is a measurable fact: Henri IV's math assessments in the specialization track regularly reach the difficulty of the first months of preparatory school. And this is precisely why a generic math tutor is powerless for a Henri IV student. What is needed is a specialized tutor who understands what studying at this institution truly demands.

Having worked with over 1,600 students, including a significant number from Henri IV, I know exactly where the breaking points lie. The problem is almost never intelligence. It is the gap between being "excellent at math" and being "excellent at math at Henri IV." These are two radically different things.

The mathematical level at Henri IV: already in prepa before prepa

To grasp the scale of the challenge, one must first understand what Henri IV is. The school admits the most brilliant students in the French education system. Selection for entry in Seconde is fierce: only flawless academic records, with overall averages above 18/20 and exceptional teacher comments, stand a chance. In Premiere and Terminale, the level climbs higher still. Henri IV's mathematics teachers are not preparing students for the baccalaureat. They are preparing them for entrance exams. The mathematics specialization curriculum is covered with a depth and speed that effectively places it at the level of the first chapters of MPSI preparatory classes.

Proofs are demanded with absolute rigor. Open-ended problem exercises are the norm, not the exception. Grading is calibrated for an elite audience: a 12/20 at Henri IV easily corresponds to a 16/20 or 17/20 at a standard lycee, even a good Parisian one. This reality is fundamental to understanding student struggles. A student scoring 10/20 in math at Henri IV is not a struggling student in the conventional sense. Placed anywhere else, they would be at the top of their class. But at Henri IV, 10/20 means being in the bottom half. And for a student who has always been first, this realization is devastating.

The psychological dimension: when the best feel average

This is perhaps the most underestimated aspect of life at Henri IV. These students have spent their entire academic career being the best in their class, their school, their district. They arrive at Henri IV with the certainty that they are exceptional. And within weeks, they discover that they are surrounded by 35 students who were also the best in their respective classes. The competition is no longer between a strong student and average peers. It is a competition among students who were all first. Every single one of them.

The psychological impact is brutal. Brilliant students, accustomed to scores of 18/20, find themselves with 9 or 10 on their first math assessment. The shock is real. Some respond by working harder. Others freeze. Still others begin to doubt their abilities, and this spiral of doubt is the worst enemy of mathematical performance. A student who doubts themselves in math loses the capacity for initiative in open-ended problems. They become hesitant, triple-check every step, waste precious time, and underperform relative to their actual level.

A math tutor who does not understand this dynamic cannot help. Telling a Henri IV student to "review the basics" is not only useless but insulting. Their foundations are excellent. What they need is to regain the confidence to take initiative in an exercise, to attempt a path without guarantees, to venture a proof without a safety net. This is not learned through revision sheets. It is learned with a specialized tutor who knows the actual level of the assessments and who can recalibrate the student's expectations.

Private math lessons for Henri IV: the trap of generic support

Paris has no shortage of math tutors. The Latin Quarter is full of prepa students, normaliens, and polytechniciens available for private lessons. Their mathematical level is often excellent. But their ability to help a Henri IV student is, in most cases, insufficient. Why? Because they do not know the reference frame.

Effective private lessons for a Henri IV student do not involve revisiting the Terminale curriculum and working through bac exercises. A Henri IV student can dispatch bac exercises in fifteen minutes. Their problem is Henri IV's own assessment papers, which are of an entirely different nature. These are long, demanding problems that require a global vision, the ability to articulate multiple concepts within a single line of reasoning, and writing of surgical precision. A tutor who does not know these papers is preparing the student for an exam that is not theirs.

Private lessons at Carmine are different. Our math tutors know Henri IV's assessment papers. They know that the school's teachers expect complete proofs, that logical connectives must be used precisely, that intermediate justifications are not optional. They prepare the student for the actual level, not the assumed one.

Physics and chemistry: same altitude, same turbulence

What holds true for math holds equally true for physics and chemistry at Henri IV. The program is taught with the same ambition, the same depth, and the same demands for formalism. Students targeting scientific preparatory classes -- and at Henri IV, that is the majority -- must excel in both subjects. A weakness in physics-chemistry can compromise a prepa application just as much as a weakness in mathematics.

The difficulties in physics-chemistry at Henri IV are specific. Problem equation setup is demanding, dimensional rigor is non-negotiable, and direct application exercises are rare. Teachers favor synthesis problems that require mobilizing multiple chapters simultaneously. Effective physics-chemistry support for Henri IV must replicate this demand, not sidestep it.

The post-bac stakes: elite prepas expect elite performance

At Henri IV, post-bac is not a Terminale topic. It is a permanent topic. From Seconde onward, students and their families know that the target destination is an elite preparatory class: MPSI or MP2I at Louis-le-Grand or Henri IV itself, PCSI at Sainte-Genevieve, ECG at Stanislas or Lycee du Parc. The most ambitious aim directly for Polytechnique, ENS Ulm, Centrale Paris. Others, in growing numbers, target top international universities -- MIT, Stanford, Cambridge, EPFL.

In every case, the mathematical record is the cornerstone of the application. Elite scientific prepas do not simply look at the average: they look at class rank, teacher evaluations, and trajectory. A Henri IV student whose score drops from 14 to 11 between Premiere and Terminale sends a negative signal, even though 11/20 at Henri IV is an excellent level in absolute terms. Trajectory matters as much as level.

This is where Carmine's support takes its full meaning. We do not work to "raise the average." We work to stabilize and optimize a trajectory. Every point gained at Henri IV carries disproportionate value in a prepa application. Our math tutors know this, and they calibrate their work accordingly.
