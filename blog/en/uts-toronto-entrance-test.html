The UTS Entrance Test

Every applicant to University of Toronto Schools writes the UTS Entrance Test. There is no exemption and no substitute: SSAT scores are no longer accepted. For entry in September 2027, the test takes place on Saturday, December 5, 2026, and it decides who goes on to the interviews in January. With about 96 places in Grade 7 and about 22 in Grade 9, it is the narrowest point of the process.

I work every year with families applying to the most selective schools, UTS among them. Here is what the test contains, and what preparation is worth doing.

The test is written with paper and pencil. Calculators, rulers, dictionaries and devices are not allowed, and pencils are provided. Tests for Grade 10 and 11 applicants follow the expectations of the high school courses completed and in progress, and are slightly longer. The test is revised every year, sometimes slightly, sometimes completely.

What the test is really measuring

The content is grade-level, but the test is not a grade-level test. It is meant to find the children who have mastered that curriculum deeply enough to explain it, apply it to an unfamiliar problem and write about it with precision. "Full solutions" is the key phrase on the math side: a right answer with no reasoning shown earns less than a well-argued method. On the English side, the writing task rewards a structured, specific argument far more than length or vocabulary.

For Grade 7, the 2021 papers and sample questions from past tests give a feel for the format, without solutions. Nothing is published for Grades 9 to 11, so older applicants prepare on the curriculum itself.

Preparation that actually helps

Drilling past papers is of limited use for a test that changes every year. What helps is building the skills the test samples: reading demanding texts and saying precisely what the author does; writing a short argument with a clear claim and evidence; solving multi-step problems and writing every step so that another person can follow. Children who already do this well at school need practice under timed conditions and without a calculator. Children who rely on speed or memorized methods need more time, and should start in the autumn rather than the week before.

The school's own advice points the same way: read widely, write every day, discuss current events at home and practise forming and defending an opinion. Those habits also feed the interview stations in Stage 2.

On the day

The test is written not at UTS but at the University of Toronto Exam Centre, 255 McCaul Street, near Queen's Park station. Grade 7 applicants write in the morning, in two groups by last name; Grade 9, 10 and 11 applicants write from 1:30 to 5 p.m. About 200 to 250 students arrive for each start time. There is no snack break, nobody leaves early, and parents must leave the building. Each child brings a photocopy of photo ID and the printed application confirmation. Only applicants whose application is complete and paid by December 1 can write.
