How to get into Loomis Chaffee

Grubbs Quadrangle, Loomis Chaffee, Windsor, Connecticut. Photo Daderot, Wikimedia Commons, CC0

Loomis Chaffee, in Windsor, Connecticut, opened in 1914 as a school for girls and boys regardless of political beliefs, national origin or financial resources, and operated tuition-free until the 1960s. That history still shapes its admission: a holistic reading in which no single element, and certainly not a test score, decides on its own.

I have students at Loomis Chaffee. The process runs as follows.

Choices within a structure

Loomis describes its process as valuing both structure and flexibility. Applicants choose their platform, decide whether testing represents them well, and may add a talent portfolio through Vimeo, Hudl or YouTube links, or a further recommendation from someone who knows them well. What the school asks in return is completeness: an application is not reviewed until every required piece, including the Test Optional Form, is in.

A file that holds together

I help students decide what to submit, prepare the SSAT when it helps, and work on the essays and the interview so the whole file tells one story.

The calendar

For fall 2027, applications close on 15 January 2027, financial aid applications on 31 January, decisions arrive on 10 March and families reply by 10 April. Test scores must arrive by 1 February. Loomis does not admit students mid-year.

Money and admission

For 2026-27, Loomis awarded $14.2 million in need-based aid to a third of its students, with an average grant covering 80 % of tuition. The school is candid that aid is extremely limited at present, and that in most cases it admits only the aid applicants it can fund. Ticking the financial aid box on the application is essential: without it, a student is not considered for aid.

Entry points

Students apply for 9th, 10th or 11th grade, or for a postgraduate year, with 20 to 25 postgraduates enrolled each year. The LC Scholars programme chooses only among 9th grade applicants. International students, from more than 40 countries, make up about 14 % of the school.
