Boarding life at Gordonstoun

Gordonstoun House, Moray, Scotland. Photo Nibaba, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0

Gordonstoun is one of the last full boarding schools in the United Kingdom, in the literal sense. The school runs seven days a week. Life happens on the campus, a 200-acre woodland site on the Moray coast, and even day pupils are given a room in a boarding house. Families considering the school should picture a life, more than a timetable.

Seven houses, mixed on purpose

The Senior School has seven boarding houses, the Prep one more. Each is led by a housemaster or housemistress who lives in with their family, an assistant who runs the house on some evenings, and matrons, the people pupils turn to first. Unless a pupil has a family tie to a house, the school places them so that each house mixes nationalities, abilities and personalities. Friendships still cross houses and years.

A weekend at school

Saturday starts with lessons. The afternoon is for activities, and the evening for a social event: a bonfire on the beach, a quiz, pizza, a film. Sunday begins with chapel and prep, then leaves time for rest, drama, music or sport. Some weekends bring mountaineering, sailing expeditions or, in winter, skiing. Twice a term, as a rule, there is a leave-out weekend; pupils who live far away go to a guardian or stay on at school.

The sea

Kurt Hahn called the Moray Firth his best schoolmaster. Every Senior pupil takes part in sail training, in dinghies, cutters and the school's own Ocean Spirit of Moray, an 80 ft ketch that sails from Hopeman Harbour, three miles away. In summer, student crews take her to the Arctic or the Azores, and into Tall Ships Races.

The services

Every Senior pupil joins one of the school's rescue and community services, among them the fire service, the coastguard, conservation and community work. There is always a fire crew on campus, staffed by pupils. Since the school became co-educational in 1972, girls have been part of that crew. Service projects also take pupils to Kenya, Romania and Thailand.
