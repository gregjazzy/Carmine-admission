Capital Notes and priority at ISF

At The ISF Academy, every new student contributes to the school's capital, either through an Annual Capital Levy or by being the nominee of a Capital Note. The Capital Note is also the strongest admissions advantage the school offers, which is why families considering ISF often ask about it first.

I explain this system to every family I accompany to ISF. Here is how it works.

Two ways to meet the capital contribution

Annual Capital LevyCapital Note
AmountHK$40,000 a year, subject to annual adjustmentBought from a current holder on the secondary market
Refundable?NoNo, but transferable
TransferNot transferableFrom one year after issue, or three months after the nominee leaves, whichever is later
Admissions effectNoneNominee priority

A nominee of a Capital Note holder does not pay the Annual Capital Levy. The note itself is not sold by the school: interested families buy one from a current holder, and the school's Foundation Secretariat answers questions about the process.

What priority means

For Capital Note nominees, ISF provides an early admissions assessment for Foundation Year, an additional assessment opportunity and admissions priority. That additional opportunity matters: other applicants, apart from alumni children, siblings and ISF Pre-School children, are not reassessed in the same year. Families buying a note for a Foundation Year child should submit their application by the relationship deadline of August 31.

Priority is not a place. Every applicant, nominee included, must pass the assessments, complete the whole process and meet all requirements. A Capital Note shortens the queue and widens the margin for error; it does not replace Putonghua and English at the expected level.

Doing the arithmetic

A family comparing the two routes should set the purchase price of a note, which depends on the secondary market, against the levy it replaces: HK$40,000 a year, or HK$520,000 over thirteen years from Foundation Year to Grade 12 at today's rate, and the note's resale value when the child leaves. Families who are confident their child will pass the assessment and who plan a short stay may reach a different answer from families planning the whole school career.
