How to get into Godolphin and Latymer

Godolphin and Latymer School, Hammersmith. Photo Des Blenkinsopp, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 2.0

Godolphin and Latymer is an academically selective day school for girls in Hammersmith, in west London. It looks for intellectual potential, and for girls who will make the most of a very broad range of curricular and extra-curricular opportunities. Entry is by examination and interview, at two main points.

I have pupils at Godolphin. This is how the process works.

The 11+ in three stages

The test. Godolphin belongs to the London 11+ Consortium, thirteen girls' schools that share one online assessment. A girl sits it once, in Year 6, usually at her own school, and the result goes to every Consortium school she has applied to. It lasts 100 minutes, with a break, and covers maths, English, verbal and non-verbal reasoning, problem solving and a creative comprehension.

The reference. Each Consortium school asks the head of the girl's current school for a reference before the test, with commentary on her academic performance and character.

The interview. Godolphin is one of the Consortium schools that select for interview from the assessment. Interviews are held on a single day in January, and offers arrive in mid-February.

Reasoning, not recitation

The Consortium test rewards girls who think, not girls who have drilled. I build the mathematical depth, reading and reasoning that show on the day.

What the school is looking for

The Consortium designed its test to go beyond cognitive ability towards creative and critical thinking, analysis and problem solving, and it openly discourages relentless teaching to the test. Godolphin adds its own interview. The girls who do well are those who read widely, enjoy a hard problem, and can talk about what interests them with real curiosity.

At 16+

Girls join the Sixth Form from many schools, choosing A levels or the IB Diploma. They sit Godolphin's exam in early November of Year 11, with an extra maths assessment for those who want to study mathematics, and are interviewed a fortnight later. Grades 8 or 9 at GCSE are expected in the subjects they plan to take.

Money should not decide

Godolphin offers means-tested bursaries of up to 110 % of the fees; around ten girls in each year receive help, most of them with fully funded places. Bursary applicants sit exactly the same assessment as everyone else.
