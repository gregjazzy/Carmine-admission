Applying to Gordonstoun from abroad

Gordonstoun keeps its community in thirds: a third of its pupils are Scottish, a third come from the rest of the United Kingdom, and a third from overseas, from more than forty countries. That balance is deliberate and goes back to the founder, Kurt Hahn, who arrived in Moray in 1934 as a refugee from Nazi Germany. An international applicant is therefore not an exception here, and the process makes room for families who cannot easily travel.

From a distance, step by step

  1. First contact. A call or Zoom meeting with the admissions team, and the online "Virtual Taste of Gordonstoun" webinar, where families hear the Principal, pupils and teachers.
  2. Registration by form, once the family has decided.
  3. The interview with the child.
  4. Documents: the current school report, a referee and a copy of the child's passport.
  5. Assessments if the school requires them, among which, for a non-native speaker, an assessment of English.
  6. Review by the Principal's Office, then a deposit and a signed contract.

A visit before entry is still worth the journey. The school welcomes families any term-time morning except Sunday, with a tour led by a pupil and, often, lunch.

From abroad, at the right level

I prepare international children online for Gordonstoun: written and spoken English, maths in the British order, reasoning, and an interview in English with an adult they do not know.

The English question

Lessons, house life and services run in English from the first day. Gordonstoun has an English as an Additional Language department that continues to support pupils after arrival, but the admission still rests on whether the child can follow a science lesson or a history essay in English now. Children educated in another language usually speak better than they write; the written side deserves months of work, not weeks.

At 15 or 16, a pupil from another system with good English can join a one-year GCSE course in Year 11, in place of the usual two years, before moving into the Sixth Form.

Getting there

The campus lies 31 miles from Inverness and 65 miles from Aberdeen airport, with trains to Elgin.

Leave-out weekends and guardians

Gordonstoun is a full boarding school, so most weekends happen on campus, with Saturday lessons, activities and expeditions. There are usually two leave-out weekends each term. A pupil who lives far away can go to a local guardian, and many choose to stay at school instead. Families should decide early which they prefer.
