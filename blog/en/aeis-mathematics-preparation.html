Mathematics is the one subject present at every level of the AEIS. At primary level it is the entire test; at secondary level it shares the day with English. In both cases, the rule is the same: the child is tested on the content taught in Singapore's mainstream schools at the level before the one they are applying for.

I prepare children from many school systems for AEIS mathematics. Here is how to approach it.

Starting from the syllabus

The ministry points families to its own primary syllabus and, at secondary level, to the O-Level syllabus. These documents list topics level by level, and they are the right starting point: not a textbook from the child's home country, and not a generic workbook. Mapping what the child has already studied against the Singapore list, topic by topic, shows quickly where the gaps are. The preparation then follows that map: each missing topic learned, then practised on problems of the level until it is secure.

Working in English

All papers are in English, including the mathematics. A child who calculates well in another language still has to read a word problem in English and understand exactly what it asks. Practising problems in English from the start, rather than translating, removes a source of errors that has nothing to do with mathematics. For secondary applicants, the English paper adds its own preparation on comprehension and writing.
