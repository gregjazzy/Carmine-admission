French Curriculum Math Tutoring in Hong Kong: Support for FIS Students

Private Tutoring January 2026 8 min read

Hong Kong is one of the most academically competitive cities on earth. Students are pushed toward excellence from early childhood. Families invest heavily in supplementary education. The pressure to perform is relentless and pervasive. For French expatriate families in Hong Kong, this intensity creates a painful paradox: despite living in an environment of extreme academic ambition, finding a tutor capable of helping their child with French-curriculum mathematics is virtually impossible.

The Lycee Francais International de Hong Kong -- the FIS Victor Segalen -- serves a relatively small Francophone community within a metropolis dominated by English and Cantonese. This demographic reality has a direct consequence for academic support: there is essentially no local tutor who understands the French mathematics program.

FIS Victor Segalen: rigorous standards in an isolated market

The Lycee Francais International de Hong Kong, commonly known as FIS or Victor Segalen, is an AEFE-accredited institution covering the full French curriculum from kindergarten through Terminale. The school follows the French Education nationale programs in their entirety, with all the rigor that implies in mathematics: formal proofs, structured written reasoning, systematic function studies, deductive geometry.

The French community in Hong Kong is small compared to Singapore or Dubai. The number of Francophone families is limited, and consequently, the pool of potential tutors who know the French program is nonexistent. Hong Kong's tutoring market is dominated by professionals trained in the British system (GCSE, A-Levels, IB) or the local Hong Kong system (DSE). These tutors are often excellent mathematicians. But they do not know what a French-format proof looks like.

A parent searching for a math tutor in Hong Kong will find dozens of centers specializing in the IB program, A-Levels, or preparation for British and American universities. They will find nobody who can explain to their child how to write a proof by mathematical induction, how to structure a function study with a variation table, or how to manipulate algebraic identities in the format required by the French program.

The trap of Hong Kong's competitive culture

Hong Kong is a city where academic performance is an obsession. Local families spend extraordinary sums on tutoring. Children stack supplementary classes after school. The culture of "tutoring" is deeply embedded in Hong Kong society.

For French families, this culture creates additional pressure. When you see your neighbors' children attending three hours of tutoring daily, when your Hong Kong colleagues explain that their son has had a math tutor since age six, the temptation to follow suit is powerful. The problem is that the tutoring available in Hong Kong is calibrated for the Anglo-Saxon and local systems. It is excellent for what it does. But what it does has nothing to do with what your child needs at the FIS.

French mathematics demands demonstration. Not fast calculation. Not formula memorization. Not efficient solving of standardized problem types. Demonstration: a logical, structured argument written with precise vocabulary, where every step is justified and the conclusion follows necessarily from the premises. This is a specifically French intellectual exercise, and no Hong Kong tutor, however competent in their own system, can teach it.

The challenges at each level

College (5eme - 3eme): where everything is decided

The college years are decisive in the French mathematics trajectory. Between 5eme and 3eme, students acquire the fundamentals that will condition everything that follows: writing geometric proofs, mastering algebraic manipulation and factoring, working with algebraic identities (identites remarquables), introduction to functions and equations. The Brevet des colleges at the end of 3eme constitutes the first serious milestone.

An FIS student who falls behind at this stage accumulates a deficit that becomes exponentially harder to address at the lycee level. The French program is designed as a pyramid: each level rests on the one below. If the foundations of mathematical rigor are not solid in 3eme, the structure collapses in Seconde.

Lycee (2nde - Terminale): specializations and post-bac stakes

At the lycee level, mathematical expectations intensify dramatically. Seconde is a pivotal year where mathematics becomes significantly more abstract. In Premiere and Terminale, students who choose the mathematics specialization -- an essentially mandatory choice for anyone targeting scientific or engineering studies -- face a program of considerable density: derivatives, continuity, limits, sequences, integration, probability, three-dimensional geometry.

The Baccalaureat specialite mathematiques exam is a moment of truth. The grade carries major weight in Parcoursup for formations in France and constitutes a critical criterion for international universities. An FIS student targeting a prepa scientifique, EPFL, or a top-tier Anglo-Saxon university cannot afford a mediocre math grade.

Physics and chemistry: a parallel challenge

The problem we describe for mathematics reproduces identically in physics and chemistry. The French science curriculum imposes a methodological rigor that local Hong Kong tutors do not possess: systematic use of symbolic (literal) expressions before any numerical application, dimensional analysis, justification of every step, precision in presenting results.

A local tutor teaches physics through the lens of A-Levels or the Hong Kong DSE: direct formula application, numerical resolution, final answer. The French program expects far more than that. Physics and chemistry are taught as disciplines of reasoning, not exercises in calculation. And this methodological difference is one that local tutors simply cannot bridge.

Online tutoring: the natural solution for Hong Kong

The near-total absence of French-curriculum math tutors in Hong Kong makes online tutoring the only credible option. Our tutors are specialists in the French program. They know the textbooks used at the FIS, the requirements of the exams, the writing methodology expected. They teach mathematics exactly the way it is taught in the best classrooms in France.

Hong Kong's timezone (GMT+8) lends itself perfectly to online sessions. Lessons scheduled in the late afternoon or evening Hong Kong time -- typically between 5pm and 8pm local -- correspond to mid-morning in Europe (10am-1pm), an optimal slot for our tutors. The student returns from the FIS, settles into a familiar environment, and works one-on-one with a tutor who understands their difficulties precisely because they know precisely the program the student follows.

Every session is individualized. The initial diagnostic identifies the student's specific gaps -- not vague weaknesses in "math," but precise deficiencies relative to the French program at their level. The progression plan is then built to measure: addressing identified gaps, reinforcing proof-writing methodology, training on the exact types of exercises the student will encounter at the FIS.
