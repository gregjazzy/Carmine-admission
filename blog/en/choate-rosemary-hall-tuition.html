Choate Rosemary Hall tuition and financial aid

For 2026-2027, a boarding year at Choate costs $74,530 and a day year $57,610. Those totals include a $1,000 technology fee charged to every student. The figures are clear; what families tend to misjudge is everything around them: the deposit, the costs that are not on the bill, the way aid is calculated, and one sentence in the aid policy that changes how the application should be thought about.

The bill

2026-2027BoardingDay
Tuition$73,530$56,610
Technology fee$1,000$1,000
Total$74,530$57,610

A 10% deposit is due on the date set in the enrollment contract, and it is not refundable. On a boarding place, that is $7,453 committed the day the contract is signed.

Costs outside the bill

  • Books, supplies and incidentals: budget $1,000 a year.
  • Health insurance: every student must be covered.
  • A cell phone with a US data plan: required, including for students from abroad.
  • Travel: to and from campus, and during the major breaks, when the school is closed and boarders must leave.
  • Music lessons: billed according to how often and how long.

For a family overseas, travel is the line that grows fastest. Four journeys home a year, plus the start and end of the year, are a real budget item, and they should be counted before the decision, not discovered after it.

The fee is not the hard part

Many families can pay Choate. Far fewer win a place. I prepare children for the most selective schools at the highest level of their ability: interview, essays, recommendations, and testing when it helps.

Three ways to pay

A single payment by June 1 earns a 1% discount on net tuition and fees, after the deposit. On a full boarding bill that is a little under $700. Two payments split the year: half by July 15, the rest by November 15. The nine-month plan runs from July to March, by automatic debit on the 1st or the 15th of each month; enrollment opens in April and closes on June 15. It is not a loan, so there is no credit check and no interest.

What aided families pay

Choate meets 100% of demonstrated need, in grants. There are no merit scholarships and no loans. The average family contribution for 2026-2027, by income:

Under $75,000$570

$250,001 to $400,000$24,230
Over $400,000$45,320

The last line is the one families overlook. A household earning more than $400,000 still contributes, on average, well under the $74,530 boarding bill. Need is calculated on discretionary income through the Clarity application, and it takes into account books, supplies, laundry and travel, not only tuition. A family that assumes it is "too well off" should run the numbers before deciding not to apply.

Aid is not invisible to admission

Every file is first read without regard to aid. But when the total need of the applicant pool exceeds the aid budget, financial need may weigh, among other factors, in the admission decision. Choate is not need-blind in the strict sense. That does not mean a family in need should hesitate; it means the rest of the file has to be strong enough that the committee wants the child whatever the cost.

Apply in the first year, or accept the consequences

A family that does not apply for aid at admission is not eligible later, unless its circumstances worsen materially: a job loss, or another child entering a fee-paying school. A family that is aided must reapply every year, and the grant is recalculated each time. The application opens on Clarity after September 1 and closes on January 15; the aid decision arrives on March 10, together with the admission decision.

Two practical points. Self-employed parents submit their business returns, and owners of partnerships or S-corporations add forms 1065 or 1120-S with the K-1s. Parents who are divorced or separated should expect both birth parents to be asked to contribute. These files take longer than families expect.

Help beyond tuition

The Beyond the Classroom Fund is open to every student, aided or not, for the costs of opportunities linked to their academic, athletic or extracurricular life. Students with very high need receive extra help with books and travel. And a few named programs, such as the Icahn Scholars, cover more than tuition; there is no separate application, as recipients are identified during the admission review.

The place cannot be bought

I have students at Choate, and for seven years I have worked with around fifteen families a year aiming at the most selective schools in the United States, England and Switzerland. At this level, money separates nobody: the families in the pool can almost all pay, or will be helped to. What separates them is the interview, the essays, the recommendations and, for some, a well-chosen score. That is where I work with the child, so that the file reaches the level the school reads at.
