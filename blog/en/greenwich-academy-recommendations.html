Part II of a Greenwich Academy application, due January 15, brings together the pieces that come from the girl's current school: recommendations and, from Group I, transcripts. Who writes depends on the Group, and the school sets a precise start date: forms go to the current school only after November 15.

I help families organise these requests. Here is how they work.

An optional extracurricular recommendation, from a coach, music teacher or leader of an activity, is also available. Requests go out electronically through Veracross or the SAO.

Transcripts

From Group I, the school transcript must cover the current year to date and the previous full year, including any standardized testing. The current school sends it, and school staff can email it directly to the admission office. For a girl coming from a school abroad or a different grading system, a short explanation of the scale helps the committee read it. If the school also gives standardized tests every year, those results come with the transcript and are read with it.

The ISEE or SSAT, for the older groups, is sent separately by the testing organisation and must also arrive by January 15.

Not sure which teachers to ask? Tell me about your daughter's school.

Timing

The window between November 15 and January 15 includes the holidays, and teachers write many letters at that time of year. Families do better to speak to the teachers in early November, explain the application, and make sure they expect the portal's email on November 15, than to discover a missing letter in January. The optional extracurricular recommendation is worth adding only if it shows something the teachers' letters cannot, a sustained commitment outside school.

Recommendations are read alongside test scores, report cards and interviews; the school states that it weighs scores as only one piece among these.
