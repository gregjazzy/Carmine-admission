How to get into James Ruse

James Ruse Agricultural High School in Carlingford is a fully selective government school with no tuition fees and 120 places in Year 7: 60 for boys and 60 for girls. It is one of four agricultural high schools in New South Wales and the only one that takes day students alone. Entry to Year 7 depends on one thing above all, the Selective High School Placement Test, sat in May of Year 6 and run by the NSW Department of Education, not by the school.

I work with families aiming for the most selective schools, including the NSW selective system. Here is how James Ruse entry works for Year 7 in 2028, and what the later entries look like.

Year 7: one test, three choices

Parents apply online between November 5, 2026 and February 15, 2027, when the child is at the end of Year 5 or the start of Year 6. They list up to three selective schools in order of preference. In May 2027 the child sits the placement test, on computer, at a test centre in NSW. Offers are made on test performance and the order of choices: a child who reaches the level for several schools receives only one offer, from the highest choice they qualify for, and may sit on reserve lists for the schools above it.

Each school's threshold depends on how many students apply, how they perform and how many places there are. No fixed pass mark is published: the level needed for James Ruse comes from that balance each year. Families should only list schools they genuinely want, in the order they genuinely prefer.

Questions draw on the NSW curriculum up to Year 6, and calculators are not allowed. Free official practice tests reproduce the format. What makes the difference is a child who reads widely, writes regularly, reasons through problems rather than guessing, and has seen the format before test day.

Places held for equity

Up to 20 percent of places at each selective school are held for students from low socio-educational advantage backgrounds, Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander students, students from rural and remote areas and students with disability. They are still placed on test performance, under the Equity Placement Model. Families who belong to one of these groups should make sure the application says so.

After the offer

Outcomes arrive in August of Year 6, with a performance report showing the child's band in each section. An offer must be accepted by its due date; declining is final. Reserve-list offers can come until the end of Term 1 of Year 7.

Years 8 to 11

Later entry works differently. James Ruse runs its own three-stage process for Years 8 to 11: an Edutest exam in August, an invited application with reports and achievements, and an interview with the principal or senior staff. There is no entry in Year 12. From 2028, Year 8 places across NSW selective schools are filled from the Department's reserve list rather than by direct application.
