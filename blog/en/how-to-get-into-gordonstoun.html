How to get into Gordonstoun

Gordonstoun House, Moray, Scotland. Photo Nibaba, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0

UK boarding schools November 2026 6 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

Book a conversation

Gordonstoun has no open days. Families visit one at a time, on a weekday or Saturday morning in term, meet the admissions team and the Headmaster, walk the campus with a current pupil and see a boarding house. That choice says a lot about how the school admits: it wants to know a family before it judges a child, and it states plainly that not every school is right for every child.

The school takes pupils from 4½ to 18, in a Prep School of around 120 and a Senior School of around 460, on a woodland campus in Moray, in the north of Scotland. The main doors are Year 7 or 8 in the Prep, Year 9 at the start of the Senior School, and Year 12 for the Sixth Form.

Stage one: getting to know Gordonstoun

Before any form, the school offers several ways in: the private visit, a call or Zoom meeting with admissions, an online webinar where families hear the Principal, pupils and teachers, and Taster Days for the Prep and for Year 9, free and without obligation. Children who come to a Taster Day often meet their future classmates there. For a family abroad, the call and the webinar replace the visit until a trip is possible.

Stage two: registration and the interview

When the family is ready, it completes the registration form. The child is then invited to an interview. The family provides the current school report, the name of a referee, and a copy of the child's passport.

An application that shows the child

At Gordonstoun, the interview and the reference carry the application. I prepare children to speak about themselves with ease and substance, and to sit any assessment the school requests.

Stage three: assessments, if the school asks

There is no single entrance exam imposed on every candidate. Depending on the file, Gordonstoun may ask for additional assessments in maths, English, verbal reasoning, non-verbal reasoning, or English for non-native speakers. A child whose report already answers the school's questions may sit few; a child arriving from another system, or with uneven results, may sit several.

Stage four: the decision

The application ends with a review by the Principal's Office. If it succeeds, the school asks for a deposit and a signed contract to secure the place. Pupils are then allocated to a boarding house, unless they have a family tie to one, so that each house mixes nationalities, abilities and personalities.

What the school is reading

Gordonstoun was founded in 1934 by Kurt Hahn, and his motto, Plus Est En Vous, "there is more in you", still runs the school. Every pupil sails, joins a rescue or community service and lives a full boarding week, seven days out of seven. The admissions team is therefore reading two things at once: whether a child can follow a demanding academic programme in English, and whether that child will throw themselves into a life of expeditions, responsibilities and shared rooms. A strong report with a reluctant interview is a weaker file than it looks.

My work with Gordonstoun families

I have pupils at Gordonstoun. Each year I take on around fifteen families, and have done for seven years, all aiming at highly selective schools in Britain, the United States and Switzerland. For Gordonstoun, my work sits where the decision is made: the child's account of themselves in the interview, the reference, the subject and English assessments when they come, and, for a scholarship candidate, the weekend of assessments the year before entry.
