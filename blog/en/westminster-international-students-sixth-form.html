Westminster School for international students: the Sixth Form route

For a family living abroad, Westminster has one door: the Sixth Form. The school considers itself unsuitable as a boarding environment for children under 16 whose parents live outside the UK, so it does not take international pupils from Years 9 to 11 and does not sponsor Child Student visas at 13+. From 16, the picture reverses: Westminster welcomes international candidates as boarders, and they apply through essentially the same process as UK candidates, with a handful of additional rules.

Who is eligible, for 2027 entry

Candidates for September 2027 must be born between 1 September 2010 and 31 August 2011. They apply between 1 June and 30 September of the year before entry, choose four A level subjects, and sit Westminster's entrance exams in November. Overseas candidates who are selected are offered boarding places.

Families with children under 16 who are moving to the UK are a different case: with at least one parent resident in the UK, a child can apply at 13+ like any other.

English: a test before the test

Westminster expects fluency in written and spoken English; lessons move quickly, and dictionaries are not allowed in the entrance exams. Every applicant with English as an additional language must submit a UKiset or IELTS Academic profile with the application, completed before the registration deadline (30 September 2026 for 2027 entry). The same report is needed for visa sponsorship later.

This is the first filter many international candidates underestimate. A candidate strong in mathematics but slow in reading English loses time on every paper, including the sciences, and the interview day is conducted entirely in English.

No GCSEs? No problem, with conditions

Candidates at schools that do not prepare for (I)GCSE are not required to have them. They are assessed on their performance in Westminster's entrance exams and on a transcript from their current school, and any offer is conditional on satisfactory academic progress, with copies of further public exam results. For a candidate from the French, American, IB or another system, the transcript should be easy to read: a short note from the school explaining its grading helps the admissions team place the results.

Exams at the British Council, interviews in London

Overseas candidates may sit the four entrance papers at their local British Council, arranged and paid for by the family, with supervision approved in advance by Westminster. The papers must be taken on the examination day (7 November 2026 for 2027 entry), or, with permission, on 6 or 8 November. Candidates offering Music must sit at Westminster.

Candidates invited to interview must come to Westminster in person on the interview day (28 November 2026). The school offers no alternative dates and no remote option, and warns that last-minute travel can be difficult: families should hold that Saturday free from the moment they register.

Guardian, visa and boarding

  • Guardian: every pupil whose parents live abroad must have a guardian acting in their place during term, available to staff, able to take the pupil home if unwell. Westminster closes at half-term and for two exeat weekends each term, when pupils must stay with their guardian. The guardian's name, address and relationship are required before an application is processed.
  • Visa: Westminster issues Confirmations of Acceptance for Studies for Child Student visas at 16+; a charge of £1,200 applies.
  • Boarding: weekly, term-time, with Saturday morning school; international boarders have access to the sports centre, library and supervised activities at weekends, and the city on their doorstep.

Costs

For 2026-27, boarding fees are £23,094 a term. Applicants pay a £360 registration fee; on accepting an offer, a non-refundable £2,400 entrance fee and a £5,000 deposit for international boarders. Means-tested bursaries require UK residence conditions that families abroad do not usually meet; King's Scholarships and Music Scholarships are open to all.

How I prepare international candidates

I start with English: reading speed and written precision at the level of a 45-minute paper, and the oral ease needed for five interviews in a day. Then the four subjects, beyond the syllabus of whatever system the candidate comes from, with timed papers by hand. Before the interview day, mock interviews in each subject.
