Joining the Wycombe Abbey Sixth Form

Most Wycombe Abbey Sixth Formers have been at the school since 11 or 13, but each year a limited number of places go to girls from other schools. Entry is highly competitive, and the process starts more than a year before arrival.

The academic bar

Candidates should expect to achieve at least six GCSE or IGCSE passes at A*, A, 9, 8 or 7, or their equivalent, with A*, 9 or 8 in each subject they intend to study at A level. Most candidates exceed it.

Four stages

WhenStep
By 1 June of Year 10Online registration, no fee at this stage
January to 31 July of Year 10UKiset, sat at an approved centre in the UK or abroad; summer term report and summary of co-curricular interests by 31 July
SeptemberDecision on who goes forward; the £420 registration fee is then charged
October of Year 11Entrance papers and a 20-minute interview at Wycombe Abbey; confidential reference and predicted grades requested
Late NovemberConditional offers; acceptance and deposit by the second week of December

For 2027 entry, the papers and interview were on 12 October 2026 and offers were made by 1 December. UKiset results arriving after noon on 31 July cannot be considered.

Four papers, four subjects

I prepare 16+ candidates for UKiset in Year 10, then for the subject papers in the autumn of Year 11, especially mathematics and further mathematics.

UKiset

UKiset is an adaptive online test taken in English, lasting two and a half hours. It measures underlying academic ability through verbal, non-verbal and numerical tests, with a creative writing exercise and an English placement test.

The subject papers

Each girl sits a paper in each of the four subjects she proposes to study at A level, based on the GCSE or IGCSE syllabus. Mathematics and further mathematics count as one subject and one paper, so a girl taking further maths chooses an additional fourth subject. Art is a practical assessment, with a portfolio. A girl choosing a subject she has not studied before still sits its paper, which is designed to test aptitude. No specimen papers are available.

After entry

Girls usually take four A levels in the Lower Sixth and continue with three or four. Alongside them come the Carrington enrichment programme and either the Extended Project Qualification or a University Preparation Project. Each year, around a fifth of the year group gains places at Oxford or Cambridge, and more than 20 girls apply to US universities.
