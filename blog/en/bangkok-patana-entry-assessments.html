Bangkok Patana does not publish a single entrance exam. Instead, once a complete file has been reviewed, the school arranges the assessments each applicant needs, subject to places. Three groups are assessed.

Who is assessed

Non-native and multilingual English speakers. Applicants whose first language is not English, or who are multilingual, are assessed by an English as an Additional Language specialist. The assessment takes place at the school, by appointment, before a place is confirmed.

The youngest children. All Nursery, FS1 and FS2 applicants are assessed in person to check their readiness for school and the school's ability to meet their needs. For families overseas, a preliminary online meeting may be arranged to reserve a place.

Students with learning support needs. Any potential or confirmed need should be disclosed with the application, and further assessments may be required.

Why the English assessment weighs so much

The first admission criterion is the student's ability to access the curriculum with the support available in the year group, and the waiting pool weighs the availability of EAL support. When those places are full, a child who needs support has to wait. English therefore shapes not only the decision but also the timing.

What families often underestimate

A child can speak fluent everyday English and still need support with academic English: reading dense texts, writing structured paragraphs, using subject vocabulary. The EAL specialist looks at that academic level. Preparing for it means reading and writing regularly, not only conversation.

The decision that follows

The admissions decision takes into account the results of any assessments, school reports, the confidential recommendation from the previous school and the availability of places. The school contacts families as soon as possible; a place is guaranteed only by the formal offer letter from the Head of School. An assessment is therefore one piece of evidence among several, read alongside the file.
