IMSA asks something unusual of 13 and 14 year olds: a real SAT or ACT, the same tests that high school juniors take for college. The score is required, but it is one piece of a holistic review, and there is no minimum.

  • Scores must be official, sent directly by the testing company, and received by the application deadline; later scores cannot be considered.
  • The PSAT is not accepted.
  • There is no minimum SAT or ACT score for admission.

Why IMSA uses it

Applicants come from schools of every kind across Illinois and beyond. A standardised score gives a common baseline for comparing students from different schools and backgrounds, read alongside grades, teacher evaluations, essays and activities.

The particular challenge at 13 or 14

The SAT and ACT are designed for older students. An 8th grader will meet math beyond Algebra 1 and reading passages written for high school juniors. Preparation therefore matters: familiarity with the format, the content that comes up most often, and the stamina to sit a long test calmly.

Help and alternatives

  • Fee waivers: families who qualify for free or reduced-price lunch, or face financial hardship, can request a waiver code through IMSA before registering.
  • Accommodations: must already be in the student's IEP or 504 Plan and be processed by the home school's case manager; IMSA cannot authorise them.
  • Virtual practice SAT: eligible applicants can take an official practice SAT online through Northwestern's Center for Talent Development, for example if they started the application after 1 November, if their exam was cancelled, or if no test centre is within 45 minutes.

Choosing a date

Because scores must arrive by the deadline, 15 January for out-of-state applicants and 5 February for Illinois, an autumn test date leaves room for a second sitting if needed. A December test is the latest sensible option.
