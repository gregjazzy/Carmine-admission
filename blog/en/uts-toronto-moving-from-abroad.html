Selective schools in Canada

Applying to UTS from abroad

University of Toronto Schools does not admit students who would live away from their families. In all cases, the student and their parent or parents must live full-time in the Greater Toronto Area for the whole of the student's enrolment. For families outside Canada, that rule shapes everything: whether they can apply, when, and how.

I work with families relocating to Toronto, or returning to it, who are considering UTS. Here is how the rules apply to them.

Who can apply from abroad

Family situationWhat happens
Canadian citizen or permanent resident, studying abroadCan apply; if admitted, must live in the GTA with a parent or legal guardian by the start of school. UTS asks to be contacted first, as additional documentation is required.
Not a citizen or PR, family already living in or relocating to the GTAContact the Office of Admissions before applying; the team reviews the circumstances and advises on documents.
Not a citizen or PR, family not living in or relocating to the GTANot eligible.

The practical lesson is to write to UTS admissions early, well before the portal closes on December 1, with a clear account of the family's status and moving plans. A family that discovers in late November that its file needs extra documents has little time to gather them.

Two dates in Toronto

The UTS Entrance Test is written in person on December 5, 2026, at the University of Toronto Exam Centre on McCaul Street. Applicants invited to Stage 2 come to UTS on January 23, 2027, for the interview stations and the group task. Families still abroad should plan for two trips seven weeks apart, around the end-of-year holidays, and for the possibility that the child is invited to the second only on January 8.

A child who is ill on test day should not come; the family emails admissions and waits for make-up test details. That safety net is meant for illness, not for travel plans.

A test built on the Ontario curriculum

The test follows Ontario curriculum expectations: Grade 6 and below for Grade 7 applicants, Grade 8 and below for Grade 9, and the high school courses taken for Grades 10 and 11. A child educated in another system may have covered the same mathematics in a different order, with financial literacy or coding barely touched, and will have learned to write in English according to other conventions. Comparing what the child has studied with the Ontario expectations for their grade is the first step, and it usually reveals a few specific gaps that are easy to close with time and hard to close in a week.

The test is written on paper without a calculator. Children used to calculators or to screen-based tests need practice in that format too.

Bursaries for families new to Canada

UTS bursaries are need-based, from 5 to 100 percent, and are assessed by Apple Financial Services on full financial disclosure, including a Canada Revenue Agency Notice of Assessment. A family that has just arrived may not yet have one. In that case, it is worth contacting UTS's finance office early to understand what will be accepted before the January 11 deadline.
