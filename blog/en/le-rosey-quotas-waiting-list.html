Le Rosey: nationality quotas, deferral and the waiting list

The Château du Rosey, Rolle. Photo Eric Bajart, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0

Boarding schools November 2026 5 min read

It is the Le Rosey rule families discover last, and the one that weighs most. The school welcomes about 460 students from around seventy countries, and it holds on to that balance: it applies strict quotas by nationality and language group, at every level. A perfectly admissible child may therefore not get a place, simply because the quota for their nationality, in their year group, has already been reached.

For many of the families I support towards Swiss boarding schools, that is where the application is decided, far more than on grades.

Four possible decisions

The admissions committee can reach four decisions. Acceptance, sometimes conditional on results in the current year. Refusal, which does not prevent applying again the following year. Deferral, when competition is strong, often because of language quotas, or when reports are missing: the file is then reviewed later with the others. And the waiting list, for applicants who have the level but for whom no place remains.

Not letting the quota decide for you

I help you apply while the quota for your nationality is still open, and prepare your child so that, against other applicants from their country, their file and their day on campus make the difference. My aim: that they get a place, not a wait.

What the waiting list really means

A place on the waiting list is not a polite refusal: it means the child has the level. But it depends on departures the school does not control, which may come in spring, or only in the middle of summer. For a family, it is a lasting uncertainty that requires another school to be ready. At Le Rosey, the older classes fill in rounds from the autumn; a file completed after the last February round can only lead to the waiting list or a refusal.

Is your child on the waiting list, or at risk of it? Write to me: I will tell you which options to prepare in parallel.

Le Régent, the door next door

Le Rosey has a sister school in Crans-Montana, Le Régent International School, whose quotas are under less pressure. An excellent applicant wait-listed, deferred or even refused at Le Rosey can extend their application there, at no extra cost and without new forms. For wait-listed applicants it is automatic if there is room, and a child enrolled at Le Régent can stay on Le Rosey's list and transfer if a place opens before the end of September. Le Régent has its own character, though, and most students who enrol there become attached to it.

How I approach quotas with families

Fifteen of my students have been admitted to Le Rosey, and every year I support around fifteen families towards the most selective schools and boarding schools. For Le Rosey, the question of quotas comes up from the first conversation: it decides when to apply, and sometimes which year group to aim for. I assess your child in maths, English and French at the level of the school's exams, prepare them for the tests and the campus day, and build with you the list of boarding schools to aim for in parallel, Le Régent included.
