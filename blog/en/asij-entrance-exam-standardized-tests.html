Tests and screening at ASIJ

ASIJ has no general entrance examination. That surprises families used to the entrance tests of Japanese private schools, and it is sometimes misread as meaning there is no testing at all. In fact, the school asks for a recent standardized test result, may require one if the child has none, and can ask any applicant to sit an academic, English or other screening before it decides.

I prepare children for these tests and screenings. Here is how they work.

When the child has no test

Many children in Japanese schools, and some in international schools, have no recent standardized result. In that case ASIJ may require testing to be completed before it can decide. For a Middle or High School applicant, the SSAT is a common choice: it is widely available, scores go directly to the school with code 1253, and it is recognised by the other international and American schools a family may be considering. Sitting it early keeps the file moving; waiting until the school asks delays the decision.

Screening

The school may also ask a child to take part in an academic, English or other screening before deciding. For local applicants to the Early Learning Center, screening sessions usually take place in December, January and February. For older children, the purpose is to check readiness for a demanding program taught entirely in English. A child who reads widely in English, writes regularly and is used to answering questions about what they have read is far better placed than one whose English is mostly conversational.
