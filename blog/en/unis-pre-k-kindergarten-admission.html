UNIS Pre-K and Kindergarten admission: the classroom evaluation

At UNIS, the youngest applicants are judged on the only thing a four-year-old can reliably show: how they behave in a room full of other children. There is no test before 5th grade and, unusually for Manhattan, no parent interview at all. The whole decision rests on a classroom evaluation, a form filled in by the child's current teacher and, from 2nd grade, school reports. For parents used to the anxiety of New York kindergarten admissions, that sounds like good news, and it partly is. It also means there is very little else in the file to lean on.

Is my child the right age?

UNIS counts ages on September 1 of the year of entry. A Pre-K applicant must be four by then, a Kindergarten applicant (called JA at UNIS) five. A child born in the autumn therefore waits a year, whatever their maturity, and there is no flexibility to argue for. Families arriving from countries where school starts by calendar year should check this before anything else, because the grade a child was in at home does not settle the question.

From 2nd grade, children joining the French section enter the grade recommended by their previous school, which can differ from the grade suggested by age alone.

What happens in the classroom

The child joins a group of other applicants in a classroom and takes part in ordinary activities, with UNIS staff present to watch. Nothing is set up as an exam. What the observers can see in that time is fairly predictable: whether the child separates from the parent and settles; whether they follow a simple instruction given to the group, not to them alone; how they handle sharing, waiting and a small frustration; whether they join in, even quietly; and how they use language, in English or in another language, to ask, explain or play.

Meanwhile, parents attend a live discussion with UNIS teachers. It is not an interview and nobody is scoring you, but it is the moment to understand how the Junior School works: two full-time teachers in most classes of 12 to 23 children, a teaching assistant per grade, specialist teachers for music, art and languages, and French or Spanish from the start.

A calm hour at UNIS

For the youngest, preparation is about familiarity, not drilling: group routines, simple instructions, separation from a parent. We guide families so that the child arrives relaxed and shows who they really are.

The teacher form carries more weight than parents expect

Every Junior School applicant needs the ISAAGNY Confidential Information Sheet, completed by the current teacher and sent directly to UNIS. At this age it is the only account of the child written by a professional who has watched them for months, so it often counts as much as the hour on campus. A shy child who freezes on the day can be rescued by a teacher who describes a confident pupil; a lively child who charmed the observers will be read again in the light of a teacher who mentions difficulty following rules.

Two practical consequences. Request the form early in the autumn, so the teacher writes it calmly rather than in a December rush. And if your child has only just started at a new nursery, tell the admissions office: a teacher who has known a child for six weeks writes a thin form.

For entry to 2nd, 3rd and 4th grade, report cards are added.

Preparing a four-year-old, sensibly

No one should coach a child of four for an admissions hour, and it would show. What helps is practical: a few group activities where the child takes instructions from an adult who is not a parent; practice in saying goodbye at a door; books read aloud and talked about, in whatever language the family speaks; and a relaxed morning on the day itself. For a child who speaks little English, nothing is lost: UNIS teaches many children who arrive with another language, and observers are watching how the child communicates, not the vocabulary.

What I do for families of young applicants

With the youngest, my role is mostly with the parents: choosing the right entry year against the September 1 cut-off, organising the timeline and the teacher form, preparing the parents' discussion, and, where it helps, a few short sessions to make group instructions and separation familiar. If UNIS is one of several New York schools on your list, I help you sequence the applications so the child is not tested to exhaustion in the same month.
