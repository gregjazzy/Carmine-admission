The Andover interview

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

Book a conversation

Andover offers three ways to do its interview: on campus, virtually, or as a recording. The family chooses. That freedom is useful, and it is also the first decision of the interview, because the three formats are not the same exercise and do not suit the same child.

Booking it

The interview scheduler sits in the Andover application portal. The portal opens twenty-four hours after the Candidate Profile is submitted on Gateway, so nothing can be booked before that step. Appointments run through January; new ones appear on a rolling basis, and a booking can be cancelled or moved in the portal. Andover also encourages a tour, on campus or virtual, if the family can manage one.

Which format for which child

The child who warms up in conversation. Some children start slowly and improve as they find their footing with the adult opposite. For them, a live format, on campus or virtual, is almost always better: the interviewer's follow-up questions give them the chance to go further.

The child who needs to see the place. For a family that can travel, the campus interview combines the conversation with a visit. The child walks into the interview having seen the classrooms and the dining hall, which often makes the conversation more concrete.

The child far away, or with an impossible timetable. The recorded interview solves the logistics. It is also the hardest of the three for most children: there is no one to respond to, no follow-up to rescue a thin answer, and a strong pull towards reciting. It should be chosen knowingly and prepared specifically.

The right format, well prepared

I help families choose the interview format that suits their child, then prepare the child in that format, in English, until they speak about what they care about with ease and substance.

What the conversation is for

What Andover wants to know is the applicant, their talents and their aspirations. Most children can state a talent. Fewer can say what they are trying to get better at, and why it matters to them. That second answer is the one an interviewer remembers. A child who plays chess and can explain the opening they are learning, and what went wrong in their last game, is having a conversation; a child who lists tournaments is reading a résumé aloud.

The interview and the video are read together

Andover also asks for a short video response, submitted in its portal as part of the written file. So the committee may see the child on screen twice: in the interview, and in the video. Families sometimes prepare the two separately and end up with two different children, one relaxed, one rehearsed. The two should sound like the same person, which is a strong argument for preparing them in the same period and with the same approach.

In English, at fourteen

For a child schooled in another language, the interview is the moment when English is heard rather than read. Andover already asks such children for a TOEFL, IELTS or Duolingo, so the interview is not an English test. But it is a conversation, and a child who translates in their head answers in short, stiff sentences. Practice has to happen in English from the first session.

Preparing without a script

A child who has memorised answers sounds it within a minute, and the recorded format makes it worse, because there is no interruption to break the script. I work the other way round: finding with the child two or three subjects they can talk about at length, then practising in the chosen format, with follow-up questions they have not heard before. The aim is a child who knows what they want to say and is happy to be taken somewhere else.

Who prepares the children

I do, personally. I have students at Andover, and for seven years I have worked with around fifteen families a year aiming at the most selective schools in the United States, England and Switzerland. For Andover, I usually prepare the interview and the video together, then check them against the essay, so that the committee meets one coherent child.
