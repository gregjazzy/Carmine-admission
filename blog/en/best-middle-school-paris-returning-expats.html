The Best Middle Schools in Paris, and the One That Suits Your Child

Parents looking for the best collège in Paris are right to aim high: the collège (middle school, ages 11 to 14) largely decides the lycée that follows. But a leading collège is only the best for a child if it suits them, their level, their pace and their language. I have students in most of the leading Paris collèges, and every year I prepare children to get in. Here are the leading collèges in Paris, what sets them apart, how to get in, and how to choose the one that suits yours.

The leading collèges in Paris, and what they are really worth

Families aim first for Stanislas, École alsacienne, Franklin, Saint-Jean-de-Passy, Sévigné or Jeannine Manuel: private schools under contract with the French state, almost all running through to the baccalaureate, where you apply directly, each with its own procedure.

A collège is chosen for what children learn there, and for what it opens up afterwards. Yet behind almost identical results, these schools do not shape the same students. One will help a child who doubts themselves to grow, carrying them to a strong record; another, just as renowned, will already have started sorting students by the end of sixième. Some have eyes only for the French classes préparatoires, while others also know how to prepare a student to be read in London or Boston. The bilingualism on display does not mean the same thing everywhere. And the same average does not carry the same weight from one school to another: a 15 out of 20 earned here can be worth more than a 17 earned elsewhere, and the lycées recruiting in seconde know it.

None of this is in the brochures, which all promise excellence and openness. I know it because I know these collèges from the inside: I always have students there, I often know their teachers and leadership teams, and I see year after year which lycées their students get into. That is what I rely on to help each family find, among the best, the one that suits their child.

Example: the application windows for September 2027. Franklin, sixième from 15 to 30 September 2026. Saint-Jean-de-Passy, sixième from 15 September 2026. École alsacienne, from early November to early January. Jeannine Manuel, closing on 12 January 2027 for families living in France. These dates change every year: check each school's website from the summer before.

Applications to the leading collèges open a year ahead, sometimes for only two weeks. Tell me which year you are aiming for and I will help you keep the dates and prepare the tests and interview.

Entering in sixième, or later in collège

Sixième (age 11) is the main door: the most sought-after collèges open most of their places there, and a child admitted in sixième at a school that runs to the baccalaureate does not have to apply again for seconde. A family that waits for lycée faces much stiffer competition for far fewer places.

In cinquième, quatrième or troisième, a place only opens if a student leaves, and some collèges take no one in troisième. An application at these levels has to be prepared for that level, with other collèges lined up in parallel. And a child who arrives late has little time: it is their troisième marks, in French and maths above all, that open the lycée.

Sixième is prepared from the start of CM2. Have your child assessed: three tests at the level of the leading collèges, in French, English and mathematics.

Other routes: state schools, international sections, the IB

State collèges depend on your address: a child is assigned to the catchment collège of their main residence in Paris, with waivers possible subject to places. A child who does not speak French yet is assessed first before being assigned.

State international sections add four hours of language and literature and two hours of history-geography in the section's language to the French curriculum, and lead to the BFI in lycée. In English: Montaigne (6th arrondissement), Voltaire (11th), Camille Sée (15th), Honoré de Balzac (17th) and Maurice Ravel (20th). Entry on a file, written tests and an oral in the section's language, in a single winter window.

The IB in collège (Middle Years Programme) exists only in a few private international schools. It is the gentlest transition for a child coming from an international school; but the longer they stay outside the French system, the wider the gap in written French and mathematics, precisely the subjects tested for entry to the leading lycées.
