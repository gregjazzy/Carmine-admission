Entering UTS in Grade 9, 10 or 11

University of Toronto Schools, Bloor Street, Toronto. Photo Tom Arban for UTS, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

After Grade 7, University of Toronto Schools still admits students, but far fewer: around 22 in Grade 9, and up to four in each of Grades 10 and 11, depending on space. There is no entry in Grade 8 or Grade 12. A family considering these later doors should know from the start that the process is the same in outline as for Grade 7, but heavier in its requirements and much narrower in its numbers.

I prepare candidates for these entries every year. Here is what changes after Grade 7.

"Based on space" is meant literally. The number of places in Grades 10 and 11 depends on room in the year group, and up to four can also mean fewer. A family aiming at these grades should treat UTS as one option among several rather than a single plan.

Two teacher references

The main difference in the Stage 1 file is two teacher references, required only for Grades 9, 10 and 11. They are submitted confidentially by the teachers themselves, through the Teacher Reference Form in the UTS Application Portal. The rest of the file is the same as for Grade 7: report cards from the last three years, a list of interests and activities, and optional documents such as an Individual Education Plan or a circumstances letter.

Since the portal closes on December 1, references have to be requested in the autumn, and the teachers chosen should be ones who know the student's academic work well this year or last.

A longer test, without practice papers

Grade 9, 10 and 11 applicants write the UTS Entrance Test on December 5 in the afternoon, from 1:30 to 5 p.m., at the University of Toronto Exam Centre. The test is somewhat longer than the Grade 7 version. For Grade 9, it covers the Ontario curriculum up to Grade 8. For Grades 10 and 11, it follows the expectations of the high school courses already completed and in progress. English combines reading and response with a writing task; math combines short answers with multi-step problems requiring full solutions, without a calculator.

UTS publishes no past questions for these grades. Applicants cannot familiarize themselves with the format from real papers, which makes solid command of the curriculum and practice in writing complete solutions under time pressure all the more important.

Joining an established class

A student who enters in Grade 9 joins about 96 classmates who have been together since Grade 7; one entering in Grade 10 or 11 joins a class where almost everyone has. UTS's program is enriched, with course acceleration at every level, so a new student also arrives in courses that may already be moving faster than at their previous school. Stage 2, with its interview stations and group task on January 23, is partly where the school judges whether a student will find their place quickly.
