Families often ask what is in Aditya Birla World Academy's entrance test. The honest answer is that the school does not publish its content. What it does say is where the test sits in the process, and that tells a family a good deal about how to prepare.

Stage three of six

The third of ABWA's six admission stages is called assessment and interaction. Depending on the grade, it may include an entrance test, a parent group discussion and/or an interaction with the child. It comes after the application and documents, and before the family meeting with shortlisted families.

Foundations rather than guesses

Without a published paper, the best preparation is real mastery. I strengthen mathematics and English at the level of the grade a student will join.

Four sensible habits

  • Consolidate the current year: gaps in mathematics in Grade 7 or Grade 10 are what a test is most likely to expose.
  • Read and write in English regularly: the curriculum that follows asks for analysis and argument.
  • Practise explaining: the interaction looks at how a child thinks and communicates, not only at answers.
  • Avoid guessing games: rumours about "the ABWA paper" are no substitute for solid knowledge.

What the next programmes expect

The programmes a new student joins give the best clue to what readiness means. The Cambridge IGCSE asks for critical thinking, inquiry and the application of knowledge across a flexible range of subjects. A Levels ask for subject mastery and independent learning; the IB Diploma for breadth, critical thinking and sustained written work. A student who already works this way will meet the test with confidence.

One element among several

ABWA hopes to shape curious, persevering and adaptable students with compassion and integrity. The test measures readiness; the interaction and the family meeting show the rest. A strong result with no curiosity behind it, or a family whose values do not match the school's, may not be enough.
