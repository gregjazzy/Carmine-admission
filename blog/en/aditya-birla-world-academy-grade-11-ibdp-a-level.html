Grade 11 is one of Aditya Birla World Academy's three main entry points, and the moment students choose between two very different routes to university: the International Baccalaureate Diploma Programme and Cambridge A Levels. Both last two years.

Two pathways compared

IB Diploma ProgrammeA Levels
ApproachBroad: several subject groups plus a coreDeep: subject mastery and independent learning
Core elementsCreativity, Activity, Service, documented over 18 monthsSubject choices guided by orientation sessions
FlexibilityA fixed structure across disciplinesStudents may add subjects beyond the school's combinations
RecognitionTaught in over 3,600 schools in 146 countriesTaken by more than 175,000 students in over 125 countries each year

Choosing between them

The IB suits students who enjoy many subjects, writing and reflection, and who want a programme built around international-mindedness and community service. A Levels suit students who already know where their strengths lie and want to go further in a few subjects, for example mathematics and the sciences. ABWA organises orientation sessions and family interactions to help each student choose subjects that match their strengths and aspirations.

Entering in Grade 11

Admission to Grade 11 follows the school's process: enquiry, application and documents, assessment and interaction depending on the grade, a family meeting for shortlisted families, the decision, then fee payment. The cycle usually starts at the end of May or early June for the following year. For 2027-28 it has concluded.

Beyond the classroom

Both pathways come with ABWA's internship opportunities, a career exploration programme and community projects. University counselling, which starts in Grade 8, becomes concrete in these two years: shortlisting universities, preparing applications and essays, and taking tests such as the SAT, which the school facilitates.

A sports route too

Students with sporting excellence at state, national or international level can apply for the Sports Scholarship on entry to Grade 11, which covers part or all of tuition for Grades 11 and 12.

How I help

I have students at ABWA, and for seven years I have worked with around fifteen families a year aiming at the most selective schools and universities. For Grade 11 entrants, I prepare the entrance assessment and the subjects that will matter most for their university plans.
