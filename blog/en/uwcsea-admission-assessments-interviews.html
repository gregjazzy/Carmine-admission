UWCSEA: assessments and interviews, what you need to know

Singapore, Marina Bay at dusk. Photo Basile Morin, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

International schools in Asia November 2026 4 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

Book a conversation

UWCSEA does not set an exam for everyone. But when the file is not enough, the school invites the child to assessments or an interview. I follow students at UWCSEA and at the leading schools in Singapore and Asia, and every year I support around fifteen families with their admissions.

What the school says

Assessments serve to confirm the child meets entry criteria, to place them against the holistic profile, and to understand their needs: English as an Additional Language, or learning support. The youngest, in K1 and K2, may be invited to a 45-minute 'Meet and Play' session with Infant School teachers. The school says it aims to put the child at ease, so they naturally show what they can do.

Preparing for the assessments

I prepare your child for the English assessment and the interview, so they show their real level, with ease.

What the school does not say

If the school judges an applicant will need English support it cannot offer, it closes the application without further assessment, and the application fee is not refunded. The English assessment is therefore no formality: it can end the application.

Why good students fail

Academic English

In Middle and High School, students need English that lets them follow lessons, not just converse.

Support capacity

Places for students needing English support depend on current students' needs; they are often allocated in the second round.
