International schools in Asia

UWCSEA assessments and interviews

UWCSEA does not set a common entrance exam. It reviews each application on its documents first, and uses assessments and interviews when it needs more: to confirm eligibility, to choose within the eligible pool, and to make sure it can support a child's English or learning needs. What a child meets depends on their age.

Infant School, K1 to Grade 1

Every K1 and K2 applicant living in Singapore is invited to a 45-minute "Meet and Play" in an Infant School classroom, supervised by teachers. For Grade 1, the session is not compulsory; only children the team wants to meet are invited. Applicants living abroad are not assessed online; the school gathers information from their current pre-school instead. Most young English learners take no English test: their level is judged during Meet and Play or through school reports.

Primary School, Grades 2 to 5

Applicants are assessed on their application and recent reports. The school may contact the current school about English and literacy. Where learning support may be needed, a session of up to 60 minutes looks at reading, writing, spelling, language or maths, in conversation with a teacher.

Middle School, Grades 6 to 8

From Grade 6, the handwritten student statement joins the file. Applicants who may need English support take a proctored online EAL assessment; if they pass, they enter the pool, sometimes after a short online conversation with the Middle School EAL Coordinator. If they do not, the application is closed and the fee is not refunded. Learning support sessions here last 60 to 90 minutes.

High School, Grades 9 to 11

  • First stage. The application, reports and statement, plus any proctored EAL test, which must be passed before going further. For Grade 11, some subjects require extra material: marked writing in a second language, a music or visual art portfolio, or a design technology questionnaire.
  • Second stage. Every eligible applicant joins an online Virtual Immersion Programme with 16 to 20 others: a discussion forum and activities led by admissions staff and current students, a question-and-answer session, and an individual interview with a member of the High School or boarding team. Sessions run from October to December for the first round and January to March for the second, with at least two weeks' notice. Interview times cannot be changed once set.

Boarding

Boarding applicants, from Grade 9, add a boarding suitability interview: 30 to 45 minutes online, with the Director of Residential Life or a houseparent, to check that the student is socially and emotionally suited to residential life.

On the day

UWCSEA prefers in-person assessments where possible. For online sessions, a quiet room, a computer or iPad with headphones, a stable connection and pen and paper are needed. The school suggests presenting the assessment to a child as a low-pressure chance to meet teachers, which is good advice at any age.
