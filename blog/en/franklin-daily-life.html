Franklin day to day: collège, lycée and the Jesuit project

Saint-Louis-de-Gonzague, Paris 16th. Photo Ornd, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

Saint-Louis-de-Gonzague, which all of Paris calls Franklin, opened on 28 September 1894 on the rue Franklin, run by Jesuit fathers. Today it has around 1,870 students, from nursery to classes préparatoires, and remains under Jesuit trusteeship. To know whether a child will be happy there, look at their week more than at the school's reputation.

A demanding framework, and proud of it

In collège, classes are held on Saturday mornings from cinquième, except one Saturday a month; in lycée, Saturday mornings are for supervised tests, with oral exercises after class or on Wednesdays. Each year group has its préfet, who supports students, families and teachers and ensures the educational charter is lived. Phones are strictly banned on the premises, and a dress code applies. Latin is compulsory for everyone in cinquième, and classical languages continue until troisième.

An education beyond academics

Franklin's project is to educate the student in every dimension: intellectual, human, spiritual, artistic and sporting. Human and spiritual formation is part of the timetable, in lectures or small groups. In lycée, students have an integration day in seconde and première, carry out a social action project in première and go on a four-day retreat at the start of terminale. The school also prepares students for language certificates, the Concours général and public speaking competitions, and its choir, some sixty singers, performs in concerts and festivals.

For which child?

Franklin suits a hard-working child who likes a clear framework and feels carried by high standards, and a family that embraces a Catholic, Jesuit educational project. It has had a 100% bac pass rate for eight years. It suits less a child who needs a lot of freedom, or a family that would come only for the results. Compared with its neighbours, it is often the room given to human and spiritual formation that makes the difference.
