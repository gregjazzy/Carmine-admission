Applying to Millfield from abroad

The entrance to Millfield, Street, Somerset. Photo Colin Smith, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 2.0

UK boarding schools November 2026 5 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

Millfield has been international since its first day: its founder, Jack "Boss" Meyer, opened the school in 1935 after returning from India with seven Indian boys, six of them princes. Today about a fifth of its pupils are international, from more than seventy countries, and another fifth are British families living overseas. A family abroad is therefore applying to a school organised for it.

Weekends: the question families ask most

A family far away often worries about weekends. At Millfield, most pupils board seven days a week, and on an average weekend at least 750 are on campus across nineteen boarding houses, with a full programme of activities. There is only one compulsory exeat a year, from Friday lunchtime to Sunday evening, when pupils go home, to a UK guardian or to a friend. For the Long Exeat Weekend, staff escort pupils on the train between Castle Cary and London and back.

Getting there

Millfield is in Street, Somerset, about 45 minutes from Bristol airport and two hours from London; Castle Cary station is twenty minutes away. At the end of each term, transfers by taxi, minibus or coach can be booked through the parent portal to Heathrow, Gatwick and Bristol airports and to Bristol Temple Meads, and an airport coordinator is present at Heathrow at the end of each holiday. There is no unaccompanied minor service, so parents of younger children must make their own arrangements.

Settling in

International pupils start the year at a welcome event with their houseparents and peers, and can join the International Students' Association, with dinners, talks and events through the year. National holidays are marked across the school.

The English question

UKiset measures English separately and gives a CEFR level, and the Zoom interview is held in English. For a child educated in another language, those two moments decide a great deal. Spoken English usually comes first; writing takes longer.

Working with families abroad

Many of the fifteen or so families who trust me each year, as for the past seven years, live outside the country of the school they want. For Millfield, I prepare UKiset and the Zoom interview online and help the family set a calendar that fits the visa timeline.
