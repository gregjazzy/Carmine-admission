Montgomery Bell: the ISEE, what you need to know

The Montgomery Bell Academy campus, Nashville. Photo Wikimachine, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0

Montgomery Bell requires the ISEE, the entrance test of many American independent schools. Registration is separate: it is the fourth step of the application. I follow students at Montgomery Bell and at the leading schools in the United States, and every year I support around fifteen families with their admissions.

What the test contains

The ISEE has five parts, in this order: verbal reasoning, quantitative reasoning, reading comprehension, mathematics achievement, then an essay. The first four are multiple choice; the essay is not scored, but it is sent to schools. The Middle Level is for applicants to grades 7 and 8, the Upper Level for grades 9 to 12.

Preparing for the ISEE

I prepare your son for all five parts of the ISEE, essay included, with a timetable set to his test date.

What the school does not say

Montgomery Bell publishes no expected score. The test is read alongside the shadow visit, the teacher evaluation and the interview.

Why good students fail

Reasoning

Quantitative comparisons and verbal analogies look like nothing done in class.

Vocabulary

The verbal section assumes a wide vocabulary, especially for a student coming from another system.

The neglected essay

Unscored, it is still read. A rushed text casts doubt on the rest.
