The Charterhouse assessment day

Charterhouse, Godalming, from the air. Photo Tim Sheerman-Chase, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 4.0

UK boarding schools November 2026 5 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

Book a conversation

The Pre-Test decides who is invited. The Interview and Activities Assessment decides much of the rest. Candidates selected after the Pre-Test spend a day at Charterhouse in the spring term of Year 6, or Year 7 for those on the later route, and the school designed it with one goal: to get to know each prospective pupil and uncover the talents they have.

Three parts, three windows on a child

PartWhat happensWhat it shows
Group activitiesTasks done with other candidatesHow the child works with others, listens and contributes
InterviewsFriendly conversations with staffHow the child talks about what they think and enjoy
Written assessmentA piece of creative writingImagination, and the ability to write freely under time

Offers follow during the same spring term, usually by the end of March.

The group activities

Children who have only been prepared for tests often struggle here. A group task is not a race to the answer. The child who listens to the others, builds on a suggestion and makes sure the quieter member of the group is heard shows the kindness and moral courage Charterhouse names among its criteria, just as clearly as the child who has the best idea. The only real mistake is to stay silent all the way through.

The interviews

Charterhouse calls them friendly, and they are meant to be. The questions are simple: what the child enjoys, what they have read, what they would like to try. What separates candidates is whether the answer goes beyond a sentence. A child who can say why they love a book, or what went wrong the last time they tried something, is having a conversation, and that is what the interviewer remembers.

Does your child enjoy talking with adults they do not know? Tell me about them.

The creative writing

Many able children rarely write fiction at school, and a timed piece of creative writing can surprise them. The answer is not to memorise stories, which reads as exactly that, but to practise starting quickly, choosing one strong idea, and finishing in time. A short, vivid piece with a clear ending is better than a long one that stops in the middle.

What not to do

Do not script the child. Charterhouse wants to see the complete child, and a rehearsed one hides it. Do not tell the child the day decides everything; anxious children close up. Explain what will happen, practise each part a few times, and let them be themselves.

How I prepare for the day

Between the Pre-Test and the assessment day, I work with the child on short group exercises, mock interviews with follow-up questions and timed creative writing, so that nothing on the day is unfamiliar.
