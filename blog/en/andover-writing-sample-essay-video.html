The graded writing sample, essay and video

Most boarding schools ask for an essay. Andover asks for three pieces of self-expression, each made under different conditions: a graded writing sample, chosen from the child's schoolwork; an essay response, written for the application; and a short video response, recorded for the Andover portal. The parents add their own statement. Read together, these pieces are where the committee learns how the child thinks, and the place where many files lose their coherence.

The graded writing sample

What it is. A piece of writing the child has already handed in at school, with the teacher's grade and comments, uploaded on Gateway.

What it shows. The child's real classroom writing, in normal conditions, judged by someone who is not trying to impress Andover. It is the most honest of the three pieces, which is precisely why it carries weight.

How to choose it. The paper with the highest grade is not always the best choice. The best one shows the child arguing something, taking a position, handling evidence. A short exercise with a perfect mark often says less than a longer essay with a good mark and a teacher's comment that engages with the ideas. It is also worth checking that the comments do not undercut the paper.

The language question. For a child at a school that does not teach in English, the graded paper may be in another language. That is a choice to think through with the family: the paper should show the child's thinking, and the reader must be able to follow it.

Three pieces, one voice

I help choose the graded paper, work on the essay with the child through several drafts without ever writing it, and prepare the video in real conditions. The result is a written file of excellence that sounds like the child throughout.

The essay response

What it is. The essay written for the application, on Gateway.

What it shows. The child choosing what to say about themselves, and saying it in their own words.

The trap. The committee reads it next to the graded paper. If the essay is written at a level the graded paper never approaches, the reader notices at once, and the doubt spreads to the whole file. An essay helped too much by an adult does more harm here than at a school that only asks for one piece of writing.

What works. A precise subject: one moment, one question, one thing the child changed their mind about. The child's own vocabulary, a little rough if need be. An ending that does not draw a moral.

Has your child ever written about themselves, in English, for a reader they do not know? Tell me about them.

The short video response

What it is. A brief recorded answer, submitted in the Andover application portal rather than on Gateway.

What it shows. How the child expresses themselves aloud, without the help of an interviewer.

The trap. The script. Children who write out their answer and read it, or learn it by heart, look exactly like that on screen: eyes moving, voice flat, no hesitation anywhere. The committee may also see the child in a recorded or virtual interview, and the two should not show two different people.

What works. Knowing the two or three points to make, then speaking, several times, until it sounds natural. A plain background, good sound, eyes on the camera. The rest is the child.

The parent statement

The Parent/Guardian Statement, on Gateway, is the family's piece. It is most useful when it describes what the school cannot see elsewhere: how the child behaves at home, how they deal with difficulty, what has changed in them recently. A list of achievements repeats the file.

Extra material

Multimedia links, such as an art portfolio or a performance video, can be added in the Candidate Profile or in part two of Gateway; families often use a private YouTube or Google link. An optional arts or athletics supplement also exists. The "Optional Additional Documents" section accepts a résumé, an award, another writing sample or news of activities that started after the profile was sent. Sending nothing extra is perfectly fine, and extra pieces only help when they show something new.
