LC Scholars and Kravis Scholars

Loomis Chaffee has two named programmes for exceptional entrants. The LC Scholars programme recognises a small group of 9th graders every year. The Kravis Scholars programme, created by the alumnus Henry R. Kravis, supports students with significant financial need. They work differently, and families often confuse them.

LC Scholars in detail

There is no separate application, and Loomis advises against sending extra material: the decision rests on the admission file alone. LC Scholars are told with their admission decision on 10 March. Before 9th grade, they attend the Loomis Summer Program's enrichment course for four weeks, tuition-free. Over four years they meet with each other and with the head of school, and between sophomore and junior year they travel abroad on a funded programme. Recent destinations have included Ghana, Morocco, Fiji, Cuba and Thailand.

The file is the application

Because LC Scholars are chosen from the admission file, I help 8th graders build a file that shows curiosity, engagement and leadership, not just grades.

The money question

Being named an LC Scholar does not reduce tuition. Every Scholar receives a laptop and a stipend for books and fees; a family that also qualifies for need-based aid receives help with tuition through the normal aid process. Families who can afford full tuition are equally eligible.

Only in 9th grade

LC Scholars are selected only from the 9th grade applicant pool, and enter together as freshmen. Students applying for 10th or 11th grade are not considered. Since 2012, LC Scholars have gone on to universities such as Princeton, Stanford, Yale, Dartmouth, Brown, Penn and the University of Chicago.
