Millfield scholarships and bursaries

Millfield awards scholarships each year to candidates who are exceptionally talented in one or two disciplines, for entry into Year 9 or the Lower Sixth. The award is modest in money, a fee reduction of up to 10%, and families who do not need help are encouraged to take it as an honour. Its importance lies elsewhere: a scholarship is the main route to a means-tested bursary.

The disciplines

Academic, Art, Chess, Dance, Drama, Music, Product Design and Sport. A candidate can be assessed in one or two of them. Senior scholarship forms are sent once the registration form has been completed, and candidates need an excellent school report and a recommendation from their headteacher.

From scholarship to bursary

Once a scholarship has been awarded, parents in genuine financial need can apply to have it augmented by a bursary, through a confidential financial circumstances form. The bursary is a further fee reduction, up to 100% in exceptional cases, set according to the family's means. A limited number of bursaries may also go to pupils who do not reach scholarship level, but the main path runs through the award.

How need is assessed

  • the family's capacity to improve its income or earning power;
  • capital that could be released, including investments and equity in property;
  • the cost of siblings at the school;
  • after a separation, the contribution of both parents;
  • contributions from relatives or other outside sources.

All awards depend on the school's budget and are reviewed each year. High-value awards are rare, and go to children with exceptional talent or potential and a genuine financial need.

At the Prep

Millfield Prep awards academic, art, drama, music and sport scholarships for entry into Years 6, 7 and 8, usually worth 5% and up to 10% for pupils outstanding in several fields, often raised to 10% on moving up to Millfield. For September 2027, external applications close on 1 December 2026, with assessments on 7 December 2026 and 14 and 15 January 2027.
