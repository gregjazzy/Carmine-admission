Applying to Harrow from overseas

Around 20 percent of Harrow's boys live overseas, and families abroad follow the same admissions process as everyone else, with a handful of rules that apply to them alone. Most of these rules are about time. A family in Dubai, Singapore or Paris that learns them late can lose a year, or the chance altogether.

Rule one: the September deadline is absolute

Registration for Year 9 closes on 30 September of Year 6. For boys at UK schools, there is a late route until 1 January of Year 7, for fewer places and only with an outstanding reference and Pre-Test. For boys at school overseas, there is none: Harrow does not assess late applicants from overseas schools. A family abroad who misses September of Year 6 is left with the scholarship-only route, for a narrow group of exceptional candidates, or with Year 12.

The practical consequence: an overseas family should know by the summer of Year 5 whether Harrow is on the list, and register in the first weeks of Year 6.

Rule two: Hong Kong and mainland China go through agents

Boys at school in Hong Kong register through Academic Asia, and boys at school in mainland China through UK Boarding Schools Admissions Service, before applying. Everywhere else, families apply directly online. For Year 12, applicants in Hong Kong are tested and interviewed in Hong Kong.

Rule three: the Pre-Test is organised by your school

The ISEB Common Pre-Test is online, and a boy abroad sits it at his own school, which provides a room, a computer and a supervisor. If the school cannot, a British Council office or another educational institution can host it. Schools that have never run it need time to understand the ISEB requirements, so the request should go to them as soon as the boy is registered.

The Harrow Test means coming to Harrow

The second round takes place at the School, in one of ten sessions between early January and mid-February of Year 6. For a family abroad, that is a trip to north-west London in the middle of the school year, around thirty minutes from Heathrow, for a morning that decides most offers. Booking flights before the invitation arrives is a gamble; planning for a January or February week is not.

English

Every boy must speak English well enough to take a full part in lessons. Harrow assesses boys with English as an additional language at no cost, and charges for any extra English lessons they need afterwards. The more important point is earlier in the process: the House Master interview and the group activity are in English, and a boy who is fluent but has never spoken at length about himself in English is at a disadvantage against boys from English prep schools.

About half of the boys from overseas who take up a Year 9 place spend Years 7 and 8 at a prep school in the UK. It is not required. It helps with English, with Common Entrance and with the rhythms of boarding, and families should weigh it against keeping a boy at home two more years.

Common Entrance from an overseas school

A boy who has not followed the Common Entrance syllabus sits a limited set of core papers in June of Year 8, mainly to set him: English, science and mathematics, preferably with the Additional mathematics paper, which is compulsory for boys at school in Singapore, mainland China and Hong Kong. He does not need to have studied French or Latin.

Money and papers

  • Visa: a boy who needs a visa to study in the UK applies for a Child Student visa, with Harrow as sponsor; Harrow guides families once a place is offered, and charges £3,000 a year for the administration.
  • Overseas deposit: when at least one parent normally lives abroad, a full term's fees is paid in advance and credited on the final bill.
  • Bursaries: Harrow cannot consider bursary applications from overseas.

How I help families abroad

I work with families from Year 5: registering on time, finding the right venue for the Pre-Test, preparing the boy for the reasoning sections and the on-screen assessment, and above all training him for the House Master interview and the group activity in English, where boys from abroad are most often underestimated. Later, I prepare the core Common Entrance papers that confirm the offer.
