Rejected by Le Rosey: the alternatives, and how to reapply

The Château du Rosey, Rolle. Photo Eric Bajart, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0

Boarding schools November 2026 4 min read

Book a conversation

A refusal from Le Rosey is hard on a family, and harder still on a child who has spent a day on the Rolle campus imagining their future there. Yet it is rarely the end of the road. Fifteen of my students have been admitted to Le Rosey, and I have helped others into Aiglon and Rosenberg. What matters is understanding why the answer was no, and choosing the next step with a clear head.

First, read the answer correctly

Le Rosey has four possible decisions, and only one is a real no. A deferral means the committee wants to compare your file with others for the same class or language group at a later date; the school tells you when. A place on the waiting list means your child has the level, but the class is full or the nationality quota reached; places can open up late, sometimes in the middle of summer, and parents are then asked whether they accept. Only a refusal closes the door for the following September, and even then not for good.

Why strong candidates are turned down

The reasons are not always academic. Le Rosey applies strict quotas by nationality and language, has a limited number of beds, and decides in rounds for the upper classes, where competition is fiercest. An excellent candidate from a well-represented nationality, applying late in the year, can be refused for lack of space rather than lack of merit. Others fall short on the papers, the mother-tongue test included, or at the interview, where the school looks for active, curious and ambitious children who share its values.

Option one: reapply the following year

Le Rosey says it plainly: children develop over a year, and a candidate who has been refused can always reapply for the following year. A second application should not be a copy of the first. It needs a real change: stronger results, a language brought up to the level required for the child's age, better preparation for the campus day, and above all an earlier submission, ideally between June and September of the year before entry, before quotas fill.

Thinking of reapplying? Write to me: we will work out what has to change.

Meanwhile, the current school year counts

Whatever the next step, the year at the child's current school matters more than ever. Le Rosey sometimes links an offer to results obtained during the year, and any new application will be read against the latest reports. A child who comes out of a refusal with a strong second term, a clear improvement in the weaker language and a personal project pursued seriously arrives at the next application with a very different file. It is also the best answer to a disappointment: turning it into visible progress.

Option two: Le Régent

When a refusal is due to lack of space, or to nationality or language quotas, Le Rosey indicates that Le Régent International School, its sister school, may be able to offer a place. For some families it is a sensible solution, close to the Rosey world without depending on the same quotas.

Option three: another leading boarding school

Le Rosey is not the only excellent Swiss boarding school, and it is not right for every child. Aiglon, in English and in the mountains, or Rosenberg, with its teaching by ability and tailored timetables, may suit a child better. The worst choice is the one made in a hurry, in July, for want of a plan. To compare, see Aiglon College and Swiss boarding schools compared.
