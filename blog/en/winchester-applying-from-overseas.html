Applying to Winchester College from overseas

Winchester College, the Chapel and Chamber Court from above. Photo Tim Sheerman-Chase, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 4.0

Book a conversation

Winchester's process is the same for every family, wherever it lives, and it is decided almost entirely in Year 6. For a family abroad, three things change: the logistics of being tested and interviewed, the money paid before the boy arrives, and the visa. One extra door, the 11+ route through The Pilgrims' School, is particularly relevant to families outside the British system.

The deadline is the same, and it is early

For 13+ entry, registration closes on 1 October of Year 6, nearly three years before the boy arrives. After that, families can still use the Registrar's Route, which is processed only as places become available. A family abroad that wants Winchester in Year 9 should therefore decide during Year 5, ideally after visiting, in person or through a virtual open event.

The registration asks for school reports, a CV, a confidential referee and identity documents. Reports from another system should be easy to read: if grades are on a scale Winchester will not know, a short explanation from the school helps.

Testing and the January interview

The General Ability Test is taken online in November of Year 6 and administered by Winchester staff. Shortlisted boys then come to Winchester for their interview and the handwritten writing assessment, by appointment, in January (11 to 15 January 2027 for 2029 entry). For a family abroad, that means a trip to Hampshire in mid-winter, booked once the invitation arrives.

The 11+ route, and Hong Kong

For a boy whose school ends in Year 6, as many international and national schools do, the 11+ route offers entry to The Pilgrims' School in Year 7 and progression to Winchester in Year 9. Assessment takes place in October of Year 6 at the College, and interviews are held in Winchester or, for some families, in Hong Kong. Two years at Pilgrims', across the road from the College, give a boy from another system time to settle into English, the British curriculum and boarding before Year 9.

Living abroad with a son in Year 4 or 5? Tell me where; we will choose the route together.

English, and the interview

Every part of the process is in English: the GAT's vocabulary section, the extended writing, and the interview with a Housemaster looking for curiosity, kindness, integrity, resilience and a willingness to contribute. A boy fluent in conversation but schooled in another language may be underestimated, not because of his ideas but because he has rarely written at length by hand in English or talked about himself to an English-speaking adult. Both are easy to practise, and worth starting early.

Money and visas

  • Deposit: parents living outside the UK pay a deposit of £20,700, one term's boarding fee, when signing the parent contract in Year 8, against £4,000 for UK residents. It is refunded when the boy leaves.
  • Visa: pupils who are not British citizens need a visa. Winchester supports a limited number of Child Student visa applications, does not sponsor visas for day boys, contacts families who ticked the visa box after Easter of Year 8, and works with a legal partner through each step. An annual charge of £1,440 applies.
  • Bursaries: requested at registration by ticking the box; the College uses an outside company to assess circumstances, including a telephone meeting.

How I help families abroad

I work with families from Year 5: choosing between 11+ and 13+, registering before 1 October, preparing the boy at a distance for the GAT format, the handwritten writing assessment and a Housemaster's interview in English, then planning the January trip.
