The AEIS, complete guide

An international child cannot simply enrol in a Singapore government school. Entry to mainstream primary and secondary schools goes through the Admissions Exercise for International Students, the AEIS, a national test run by the Ministry of Education with the Singapore Examinations and Assessment Board. The child sits the test in Singapore; admission depends on the result; and if successful, the ministry places the child in a school with a vacancy, near the declared home address where possible.

What is tested

At primary level, the AEIS tests mathematics, on the content of the level below the one applied for; but before a family can even apply, the child must have a Cambridge English Qualifications result at the minimum score set for their age. At secondary level, the test covers English and mathematics, again on the previous level's syllabus. Primary applicants can be offered the level they applied for or a lower one; secondary applicants are offered the level applied for or nothing.

Which level

The level depends on the age the child reaches in the year of entry, not on their current class abroad. The ministry's online checker gives the tests a child can sit and the possible outcomes. A child turning nine in the year of entry, for instance, sits the Primary 3 test.

After the result

Results come out in December. The school posting is final: no transfer, and no release of marks. Successful children must report to their school within a set period or lose the place. Children holding a Dependant's Pass, Long-Term Visit Pass or Immigration Exemption Order are confirmed on reporting; others apply for a Student's Pass.
