Entering Exeter after ninth grade

One of the academic buildings on the Exeter campus, New Hampshire. Photo Transmarinus, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0

Ninth grade is Exeter's main entry, but it is not the only one. The school also admits students into tenth and eleventh grade, into the senior year for a single year, and as postgraduates who have already finished high school. Exeter enrolled 355 new students in 2026, and a real share of them arrived after ninth grade.

These later entries interest families who discover American boarding schools late, who move countries, or who want a year in the United States before university. I prepare candidates for each of them. They do not work the same way.

The rest of the file is the same at every grade: interview, two essays, parent statement, four letters, two years of reports.

Tenth and eleventh grade: joining a class already formed

A student who enters in tenth or eleventh grade joins classmates who have already spent one or two years at the Harkness table, around which every class is taught. Ninth graders have a cushion: in their first autumn term, all their courses are graded pass or fail. Later entrants have no such term. They are graded from the first week, in classes built on discussion, where a student who has not prepared has nothing to bring to the table.

Every new student also sits placement tests in mathematics and languages before arriving, which decide the level they start at. Dormitories mix all four years and students usually keep the same one, so a new eleventh grader moves into a house where friendships are already made, with older students who know the place and can open it up to them.

A later entry, prepared properly

After ninth grade, Exeter admits fewer students and expects them to be ready from the first day. I prepare candidates for the right test, the essays and the interview, and for the pace of Harkness itself.

One year at Exeter: seniors and postgraduates

Each autumn, about 10 to 15 percent of the senior class is new to Exeter, and most of these one-year students are postgraduates. In the class of 2026, 35 of the 312 seniors had arrived for their final year. They take five courses in each of the three terms, take a full part in the life of the school, work closely with Exeter's college counselors on their university choices, and receive an Exeter diploma in June.

The calendar is tight. A student who arrives in September applies to university during the autumn of that same year, so the counselors have a few weeks to know them, and the university file rests mostly on what came before Exeter and on a first term there. The year is valuable; it is not a way to rewrite a weak record.

Who the postgraduate year is for

Exeter looks for postgraduates who have been earning A's and B's in a demanding programme in the last years of high school, and who have achieved considerable success outside the classroom, in sport, the arts or leadership. That success is what helps them settle quickly into a community they join for a single year. The year suits students who want to strengthen their academic record before university, and also those who finish school young and do not want to go straight on.

For a student finishing high school abroad, under the IB, A levels or a national diploma, the year offers a full American senior year with its college counseling, and an Exeter diploma. Exeter's recent university list also includes Oxford, Imperial, St Andrews, McGill and Sciences Po, so the year does not close the European route.

The test at sixteen and over

From the senior year, the SSAT and the ISEE are no longer accepted, and the score must come from the PSAT, SAT, PreACT or ACT, taken no earlier than one year before January 15. For a student outside the American system, that means an SAT or ACT prepared during the last two years of school, alongside a curriculum that does not prepare for it. It is the piece of the file most often left too late at this level.
