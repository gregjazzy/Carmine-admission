Financial aid at Loomis Chaffee

Loomis Chaffee was tuition-free until the 1960s, and it still describes its aim as enrolling the most outstanding applicants regardless of their ability to pay. The figures are substantial. But the school is also unusually frank about the limits of its budget, and families applying for aid should understand both sides.

The school's published distribution shows that many aided families contribute less than $5,000 a year, while a few contribute more than $60,000 and still receive help.

How to apply

  1. Tick the financial aid box on the admission application. Without it, the student is not considered for aid in the admission process.
  2. Complete a Clarity application by 31 January, with two years of tax documents sent directly to Clarity, never to Loomis.
  3. Each parent or guardian applies, whatever the custody arrangements.
  4. Explain special circumstances in the notes section of the Clarity application.

Clarity estimates what a family can contribute; the Financial Aid Committee uses that estimate as a starting point. Tuition paid for other children, business ownership and other factors are taken into account.

Need-aware admission

Loomis states that in most cases it admits only students it can fund through its aid budget. Each year, a small number of admitted students go onto a financial aid waiting list, and a second group is denied admission because aid cannot be provided. It also describes aid as extremely limited at present. For a family that needs aid, applying on time and presenting the strongest possible file both matter.

Wondering whether your family would qualify? Let's talk before 31 January.

After the first year

Aid is renewed each year on a new application, adjusted to changes in the family's situation. A family that enrolls without aid will find it hard to obtain later, unless its circumstances change. Students on aid also have access to a lending library for textbooks and supplies.
