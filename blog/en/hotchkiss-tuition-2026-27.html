Hotchkiss tuition 2026-27: what boarding really costs

The main building of The Hotchkiss School, Lakeville, Connecticut. Photo Adrien Delessert, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 2.5

US boarding schools November 2026 6 min read

Hotchkiss publishes its sticker price in full. For 2026-27, a boarding student pays $79,350 in tuition, plus an $850 technology fee and a $600 health services fee, for a total of $80,800. A day student pays $67,450, plus $850 and $400, for $68,700. Those figures are only the start of a realistic budget, and for a third of the school, they are not what the family actually pays.

What comes on top

  • Books and supplies: about $850 a year on average.
  • Health insurance: $4,495 for domestic students, $2,895 for international students.
  • Laundry: machines are available in dorms; an outside service runs $740 to $920 a year.
  • Spending money: snacks, haircuts, meals out; typically $50 to $75 a month.

Add travel, which for a family abroad can be the largest item after tuition. A realistic first-year budget for a domestic boarder is therefore close to $88,000; for an international boarder, similar, with flights instead of drives.

Who actually pays the sticker price

Hotchkiss meets 100 percent of each family's demonstrated need, entirely with grants and no loans, and spends more than $13.9 million a year on aid. A third of students receive aid; more than a quarter of them attend tuition-free; the average family contribution among aided families is $15,050, and the average grant is $67,413. The school also spends over half a million dollars on non-tuition support, including books, travel and health insurance.

The flip side: Hotchkiss offers no merit scholarships. A family that does not qualify for need-based aid pays the full price, whatever the student's achievements.

Timing

Financial aid applications for new students are due on January 15, the same day as the admission application, through Clarity. Funds are not reserved for late applicants. Hotchkiss provides an online estimator for families who want an early sense of their likely contribution, and the Clarity application can cover several children at once.

What "100 percent of need" means in practice

Need is the gap between the full cost and what the family can contribute, as Hotchkiss calculates it from income, assets, family size, other children in tuition-charging schools and living costs. Take a family whose contribution is assessed at $25,000: the grant covers the remaining $55,800 of tuition and fees, with no loan to repay, and non-tuition support may help with books, travel or insurance on top. A family assessed at $90,000 receives nothing, because its contribution exceeds the cost. Between the two, the result depends entirely on the calculation, which is why the online estimator is worth ten minutes before deciding whether to apply.

Two consequences follow. A family should never assume it is too well off to qualify without running the numbers, especially with several children in fee-paying schools. And because there is no merit aid, a strong student from a family above the threshold pays the full price at Hotchkiss as at any school with the same policy.
