École des Roches: arriving without French, the intensive French year

École des Roches, Verneuil-sur-Avre. Photo Djampa, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

A family settling in France, or wanting real command of French for their child, soon hits a wall: the leading French schools assume working French from day one. Les Roches has a rare answer, a French as a foreign language programme in a boarding school, open with no prior level at all.

The programme

Students take around 25 hours of French a week, from beginner to advanced level. They are not isolated: they share houses, meals, activities and daily life with French and international students, so French is practised well beyond the classroom, in the boarding house, on outings, every day. Students staying a full year, or a semester from January, can prepare and sit the DELF, the international French certification.

From language to the right school

After a French year, the choice between French bac, BFI and IB decides what follows. I know these paths and the schools that carry them, and I prepare applications to the level of the most selective.

What comes next

After the programme, the student joins either the French baccalaureate or the IB Diploma, depending on their progress in French and their goals. The move can be gradual: some students start with a few months of French, then join classes in their future programme step by step. The pace depends on the starting level and the student's commitment, but total immersion in boarding life speeds it up considerably.

Choosing the next path well

I have students at École des Roches, and every year I support families settling in or returning to France. For a student learning French, the real decision comes at the end of the year: the French bac if they are aiming at France and their French has progressed enough; the IB if they are at ease in English and aiming abroad. Getting it wrong at that point is costly. That is the choice I help families make, with an honest reading of the level reached.

For which student

The programme suits an international collège or lycée student who must join the French system quickly, or an international family wanting a full year of immersion before schooling in France. It suits less a student who only needs occasional reinforcement: the summer camp, with its 22 hours of language a week, may then be enough.
