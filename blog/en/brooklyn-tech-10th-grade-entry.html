Entering Brooklyn Tech in 10th grade

The SHSAT can be taken a second time, in a student's first year of 9th grade, for entry into 10th grade. For Brooklyn Tech, that late entry has a particular consequence: the major selection takes place in 10th grade, so a newcomer chooses a major within months of arriving.

Applying from 9th grade

The rules mirror those of 8th grade, with three differences in practice. Only students in their first year of 9th grade qualify; repeating students do not. The test has its own date, Saturday 21 November 2026, at a central site, after registration and ranking in MySchools between 6 and 30 October. And the number of places is far smaller than in 9th grade. The test itself is identical: the adaptive SHSAT, English and math, 180 minutes, with offers in March.

The major question

Brooklyn Tech students rank the majors during 10th grade, after presentations, and are placed by preference and Power Index, built from their grades. A student joining in 10th grade enters that process with grades from a different school in 9th grade and only a few months at Tech. The family should ask the school early how a newcomer's Power Index is calculated, and plan the first semester's courses with the desired major in mind.

When it makes sense

A family newly settled in New York after the 8th grade test, a student who fell short in 8th grade but has clearly progressed since, a teenager whose taste for engineering or architecture appeared during 9th grade: these are the profiles for whom a second attempt is worth the effort. For a student already happy at a strong high school, the case is weaker, because the move comes with a major to choose almost immediately.

Balancing the year

A 9th grader preparing for the SHSAT is also building a high school transcript that will matter for college. The best approach protects both: preparation over the summer before 9th grade, focused timed practice in October and November, and no neglect of current classes.
