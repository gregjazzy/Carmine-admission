Deerfield becomes fully boarding in 2027

The Main School Building, Deerfield Academy, Massachusetts. Photo John Phelan, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 3.0

US boarding schools November 2026 5 min read

From fall 2027, Deerfield will be a fully boarding school. A new building, the West Campus Dorm, with 38 student rooms, will let it expand its boarding population and bring local students, who until now have attended as day students, into residential life. For most families applying from afar, this changes little. For families living near Deerfield, it changes the decision entirely.

Why Deerfield is doing it

The school frames it as a natural next step for its mission: the friendships, joy and spontaneity of its dorms should be shared by every student. It fits everything else Deerfield says about itself. The school calls itself fully residential, a place where students "meet as equals, united by the shared experience of communal living", and builds its days around family-style sit-down meals, seven a week. Today, day students attend the sit-down lunches and are encouraged to stay for dinner; from 2027, the question will no longer arise.

What changes, concretely

  • No more day option for students entering from fall 2027: local children will board.
  • A single, truly comprehensive tuition rate, with no additional fees or hidden costs.
  • A financial aid initiative for local families, designed so that expected family contributions are met and cost does not prevent any local student from taking up the newly expanded experience.
  • A new dorm on the west side of campus, joining a residential system that already houses ninth graders together and, for instance, tenth-grade boys in Scaife.

The cost question for local families

In 2026-2027, a day year costs $57,850 and a boarding year $79,940. A local family that had planned on a day place now faces the boarding figure. Two things soften that. Deerfield's general aid policy already means that US families with W-2 income up to $150,000 pay nothing, and those above pay no more than 10% of total income per student. And the specific initiative for local families is meant to cover the transition. The right step is to run the numbers with the financial aid office rather than to rule the school out.

What it means for the application

For a local family, the application now asks an implicit question: is this child ready to live away from home at fourteen, in a community, at a distance from urban life? Deerfield's interview is one-on-one with an admission officer, and the officer will be thinking about that. A child who can talk about living with others, about independence, about what they expect from dorm life, gives a more convincing answer than one who has never imagined it.

It also changes the family conversation. Parents who planned on a child at home every evening should talk it through honestly before applying, not after an offer arrives on March 10.
