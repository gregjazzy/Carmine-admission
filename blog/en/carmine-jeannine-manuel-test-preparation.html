Our Preparation Program for Jeannine Manuel Entry Tests

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. Has worked with students of École Jeannine Manuel for more than ten years.

Book a conversation

Every year, I see the vast majority of applicants to École Jeannine Manuel leave without a place. For more than ten years, I have worked with students of the school, from their admission all the way to the university or grande école they are aiming for, and every year I prepare children to get in. Here is how I work with a family, step by step.

Why so many applications fail

Too late. The family discovers the procedure in October, really starts in January, and the file closes in mid-January for families living in France. There is no time left to close a gap.

In the wrong place. Some families start early but spend months on the letter, when the gap is in mathematics or in written French.

Conversational bilingualism. A child who speaks two languages fluently does not necessarily write at the expected level in either. That is what the school's assessments bring to light.

Preparing for entry to Jeannine Manuel

The right year group, a complete file on time, a child prepared for the tests and the interview: that is the work I do with every family. Let's start with a first conversation about your child.

My method, in five steps

1. The assessment

I do not ask parents how they see their child's level: I measure it. Your child takes three tests at the school's level, in French, English and mathematics, then an interview with me. You receive a written report and we discuss it together. You then know where your child stands, which year to aim for, and what remains to be worked on.

This assessment almost always reveals a gap the school reports did not show: a student from the American system or the IB who calculates very well but has never written a French-style proof, or a child back from years abroad whose written French has fallen behind.

It all starts with measuring where your child stands: I give them three tests at the school's level, in French, English and mathematics. Ask for an assessment.

2. Targeted catch-up

The work then focuses on the gaps found, and only on them. For a student from the British or American system, it is often the French method in mathematics. For a French-speaking student, written English. For a child back from abroad, written French. I teach the mathematics myself.

3. Practice under real conditions

Knowing the material is not enough: it has to come through on the day, in the time allowed. I prepare your child on tests built to the school's level, based on what I know of its assessments, with detailed feedback every time.

4. Interview preparation

An interview is not recited. I help the child talk simply about themselves, what they enjoy and their background, and I prepare the parents for their own interview. I know how these interviews go and what the school looks for.

5. The file and the backup schools

I follow the application with you until the school's answer: school reports put in context if the child has changed systems, the letter about the family, the motivation letter for lycée. And in parallel we prepare the backup schools, the ones that genuinely suit your child and that I know from the inside.

What the school looks for

The school says it admits students who it feels can benefit from its bilingual curriculum. From parents, it expects a strong international outlook, commitment to its mission and close involvement in their children's education and well-being. Between two equivalent applications, it gives priority to students of its sister schools in London and Lille and their siblings, to children of staff and of alumni, then to children with interests and talents outside school.

What it does not publish is how it measures all this during the assessments and the interview. That is exactly what I know: ten years working every week with its students and preparing children for admission to Jeannine Manuel. My job is to do everything I can so that your child is not among the majority who leave without a place.
