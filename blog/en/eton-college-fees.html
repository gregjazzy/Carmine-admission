Eton fees in 2026/27

Eton College Chapel. Photo David P Howard, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 2.0

UK boarding schools November 2026 6 min read

For 2026/27, the Eton school fee is £21,891 per Half, including VAT. With three Halves in the year (Michaelmas, Lent and Summer), that is £65,673 a year. The fee is set each May by the Provost and Fellows for the following year, and VAT at 20% has applied since January 2025.

The headline figure is only part of the bill, and it is the part families understand best. The rest needs a closer look.

The fee covers tuition, board and lodging, most games and most teaching materials. Music lessons, optional insurance, Boat Club membership and school trips are billed as extras, on the account sent home during each holiday together with the next Half's fee. Bills are paid by direct debit on the first day of the Half, or in three instalments during it for an administration charge.

A place earned, not bought

At Eton, a full-fee family and a family on a full bursary go through exactly the same assessment. I prepare boys for that assessment, to the standard of a school that turns away excellent candidates every year, so that the place is won on merit.

Five years, not one

A boy who joins in F Block normally stays five years. At today's fee, that is £328,365 before extras, and fees are reset every year. A family living overseas also advances one Half's fees as a deposit, refunded against the last bill, and pays the visa charge each year if their son needs one, £15,000 over five years at the current rate. Eton offers a pre-paid fees scheme for families who want to fix a capital sum against future bills.

Bursaries: generous, means-tested, early

In 2024/25, about 300 boys, 22% of the school, received means-tested help with fees; 89 paid nothing at all. Eton budgeted £10.6 million for scholarships and bursaries that year, and still cannot meet every request. Help can reach 100% of fees.

The timing catches families out. Every parent, including those who will pay in full, returns a financial declaration by 25 July of the summer after Year 5, and families who need help submit the full bursary application during Year 6. A family that went through the 13+ process without applying in Year 6 cannot ask for a bursary later on the strength of a King's Scholarship or a Music Award. And neither of those scholarships reduces the fee by itself: at Eton, a scholarship is an honour, and money comes only through the means test.

What money does not do

No amount changes the route in. A boy is registered, sits the Pre-Tests, comes to Stage 2 and is interviewed exactly like every other candidate, and his place is then confirmed by an examination in Year 8. Bursary decisions are confidential: boys are not told who receives help unless their parents choose to tell them. The question for a family is therefore not whether it can afford Eton, but whether its son will be ready for the day Eton decides.
