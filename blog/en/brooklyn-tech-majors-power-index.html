At most high schools, admission is the last selection. At Brooklyn Tech, it is followed by another. During their last two years, students follow a major, a set of selective courses in one field taken on top of the requirements of the New York City diploma. Which major they get depends on how they did in 9th and 10th grade.

The majors

The school currently offers 18 majors. Among those listed are Aerospace, Advanced Health Professions, Architecture, Bio Science, Chemistry, Civil Engineering, Computer Engineering, Electrical Engineering, Environmental Science, Finance, Industrial Design, Law and Society, the LIU PharmD track, Mathematics, Mechatronics and Robotics, Media, Neuroscience, Physics and Social Science.

How placement works

StepWhat happens
PresentationsAll sophomores attend sessions on each major's course sequence
RankingOver a few days, each student ranks every major on the Daedalus system
SortingStudents are sorted by preference and by their Power Index for each major
Full majorsWhen a major fills, remaining applicants move to their next choice
BumpingA student with a higher index for a second choice displaces the lowest-ranked student who had it first
ResultStudents are notified; the major cannot be changed

Two years that decide the last two

Once a student is admitted, I work with them in mathematics and science through 9th and 10th grade, so that the Power Index opens the major they want.

The Power Index

The Power Index combines the student's current overall average with their averages in the academic areas relevant to each major. It usually sits around 300, with about 400 as a maximum. The higher it is, the better the chance of a seat in a competitive major: students who excel in their first two years are rewarded with first choice.

Why AP courses matter early

Advanced Placement courses are weighted at 1.10 in the cumulative average: a 90 appears as 90 on the transcript but counts as 99. The weighted average feeds the Power Index, as well as awards, National Honor Society admission and college applications. Taking AP courses in 9th and 10th grade, where possible, can make the difference for the most demanded majors.
