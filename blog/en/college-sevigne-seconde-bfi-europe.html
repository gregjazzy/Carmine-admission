Collège Sévigné: entering seconde, the World (BFI) or Europe track

Collège Sévigné, rue Pierre-Nicole, Paris 5th. Photo LPLT, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0

Seconde (age 15, the first year of lycée) is Sévigné's other main door: every year, around 35 new students join the school at this level. They choose between two tracks, and that choice shapes their whole lycée.

World-bilingual or Europe

The World-bilingual track prepares the French international baccalaureate, the BFI, a full French bac with part of the teaching in the section's language. It is for genuinely bilingual students. The Europe track is for non-bilingual students: reinforced English, preparation for the European section, discovering Europe, an introduction to law and geopolitics. The school presents it as an ambitious track, not a second-tier route.

A seconde place at Sévigné

I help you choose between the World and Europe tracks, then prepare with your child an application and tests worthy of a lycée that takes only about thirty new students a year. My aim: that they get in on the track that will take them furthest.

The Mathilde Salomon scholarships

Sévigné has created scholarships named after its most famous headmistress. Since September 2025, five full scholarships a year are reserved for new seconde students from outside, awarded on social and merit criteria and funded by donations; five other students receive a partial scholarship. Recipients are supported by tutoring. For a brilliant troisième student whose family cannot afford the fees, it is a real door, prepared in troisième.

Is your child in troisième? Tell me about their plans: I will tell you which track to aim for, and how to prepare the application.

What the application must show

For the World track, the school also asks for an English questionnaire completed by the student's current English teacher: the real level of English is decisive. For both tracks, troisième reports, solid French and maths, and a coherent project are what count. Sévigné claims "excellence without competition": it is looking for curious, committed students, not just averages.

How I prepare applicants

I follow students at Sévigné and the other leading Paris schools, and every year I support around fifteen families with their admissions. For seconde at Sévigné, I start with an assessment of your child in French, maths and English, which guides the choice of track. I then help them build their application, and build with you the list of other lycées to aim for, including the state international sections.
