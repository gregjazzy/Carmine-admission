HKIS assesses each applicant's ability to succeed in an atmosphere of high expectations, self-discipline and responsibility to the school community. To judge that, the Admissions Committee reads every file against the same six criteria, adapted to the child's age.

The six criteria

English comes first

HKIS teaches in English, and English proficiency appears both as a criterion in its own right and inside every assessment, from the youngest children's receptive and expressive English to the oral language and writing components of the adaptive test from Grade 3. A family whose child is still building English should treat it as the priority of the year before applying.

"Proven success in similar environments"

This criterion is often overlooked. HKIS looks at whether a child has done well in a school like HKIS: English-medium, demanding, with an emphasis on independence. It sits well with the priority given to applicants coming from accredited American-curriculum schools abroad. A child moving from a very different system is not excluded, but the file needs to show how they would adapt.

Criteria and priority are different

Meeting the criteria makes an applicant qualified. Whether a qualified applicant receives a place then depends on available space and on the priority system: debenture holders, then community-connected families, then local and regional applicants. Strong criteria cannot override a full grade, and priority cannot replace the criteria.

A complete file

Only complete files are reviewed: application fee, Family Questionnaire and every required document by the deadline, 1 October for the Primary School and 1 November for Grades 6 to 12.
