Ranking Bronx Science among your choices

The Bronx High School of Science, New York. Photo Bxsstudent, Wikimedia Commons, public domain

Many families think of the SHSAT as a race to a cutoff. In fact, two things decide where a student lands: the score, and the order in which they ranked the specialized high schools when they registered. The order is fixed in October, before anyone knows the score.

How the two combine

Students are processed one by one, from the highest score down. Each receives an offer from the highest-ranked school on their own list that still has a seat. A student who does not list a school can never be offered it. Listing a school does not commit the student to it, and listing more schools never lowers the chance of getting a higher choice.

Choosing the order honestly

The only good list is one ranked by genuine preference. Bronx Science's research programme and science focus suit some students; each of the other seven schools has its own size, location and character, which suit others. Commute matters too: Bronx Science's students travel two hours a day on average. A family that ranks schools by prestige alone risks an offer the student does not want.

Not sure how to order your list? Let's go through the schools together.

When it is entered

The ranking is part of SHSAT registration in MySchools, which runs in October; for the fall 2026 test, from 6 to 30 October. Families should decide before the window opens, not on the last day. Once registration closes, each registered student receives a test ticket with the date and place of the test.
