Harrow scholarships and bursaries: who can apply, and for what

Harrow uses two words that families often confuse. A scholarship acknowledges excellence and carries a fee remission of 5 percent. A bursary is financial help, assessed on the family's means, and can cover up to the whole fee. A boy can hold one, the other, or both, and the routes to each are different.

Scholarships: six fields, by invitation

Harrow awards some 40 to 45 scholarships a year, in Academic, Sport, Drama, Music, Art and Design Technology and Engineering, for entry in Year 9 or Year 12. There is no all-rounder scholarship. Awards are made on an overall judgement that weighs the boy's character and suitability for boarding, his academic ability and potential, the needs of the School and the field of applicants that year.

Scholarships are not open to all comers. Only boys already screened through admissions are invited: those holding a place, those on the waiting list, and a small number applying through the scholarship-only route when the normal deadlines have passed.

The Academic Scholarship

A boy sits the Academic Scholarship only on the recommendation of his current school, ideally from his head. The assessment takes place in January and February of Year 8 and covers English, mathematics, analytical reasoning, philosophy and applied ethics, general linguistic skills, science, and at least three subjects among history, geography, French, Spanish, Latin and Greek. Results come in March of Year 8. Past papers going back several years are published, which makes this the most preparable part of Harrow's whole process.

It also has a practical advantage: a boy who performs well in these examinations is unlikely to be asked to sit Common Entrance in June.

Aiming for an Academic Scholarship

We prepare able boys for Harrow's Academic Scholarship papers, from mathematics and analytical reasoning to philosophy and ethics, working from the published past papers.

Music, art, drama, design, sport

  • Music: at least Grade 5 with distinction on the main instrument, often two instruments; a pre-audition with the Director of Music six months to two years before the assessment is required.
  • Art: a portfolio of 15 to 20 pieces and a sketchbook, an interview and a sustained observational drawing; candidates must be recommended by the Director of Art before November of Year 8.
  • Design Technology and Engineering: a portfolio of graphical work, CAD and completed objects showing flair.
  • Drama: a practical workshop day and an interview, looking for collaborators as much as leading actors.
  • Sport: usually county or academy standard in one sport, assessed in October or November of Year 8.

Every scholar must still pass Common Entrance at the required standard, or do well in the Academic Scholarship examinations.

Bursaries, and the rule for families abroad

Means-tested bursaries can cover up to 100 percent of fees and are considered for strong candidates at the point of application; the registration form lets families state the percentage of fees they would need. Harrow states that this does not disadvantage the application. Specific awards exist too: Year 7 Awards for boys at local primary schools in the UK, the Churchill Places in Year 12 for exceptional boys who need substantial support, and awards for sons of serving British forces and of Old Harrovians.

The limit is clear: as a UK charity, Harrow is unable to consider bursary applications from overseas. A family living abroad should plan on full fees, with a scholarship as the only possible reduction.

What a scholarship is really worth

At 5 percent of fees, a Harrow scholarship is worth about £3,300 a year at 2026/27 rates. Its real value lies elsewhere: it is Harrow's own recognition of a boy's excellence, and, for the academic award, it can spare him Common Entrance. For an able boy, aiming for it is worth the work.

How I prepare scholarship candidates

For the Academic Scholarship, I work through the published papers, subject by subject, with particular care on mathematics, analytical reasoning and the philosophy and ethics paper, which surprises boys who have never been asked to argue a position. For music or sport candidates, my role is academic: making sure the Common Entrance or scholarship papers do not undo a strong performance in their field.
