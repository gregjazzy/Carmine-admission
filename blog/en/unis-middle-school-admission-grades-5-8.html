UNIS Middle School admission: entering in grades 5 to 8

United Nations International School, on the East River, Manhattan. Photo Transpoman, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

New York independent schools November 2026 6 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

Book a conversation

The UNIS Middle School runs from 5th to 8th grade, one year earlier than the 6th-grade start most New York schools use. Called M1 to M4 inside the building, these four years have a single decision date, February 10, but two quite different ways of meeting the child. Which one your child faces depends on the grade, and that should influence when you apply.

Two evaluations, one division

In a group activity at ten, a child shows how they work with others they have never met: whether they listen, build on someone else's idea, take a turn without taking over. In a one-to-one conversation at twelve or thirteen, the child has to talk about themselves, their school, what they read and what they care about, to an adult, in English, for half an hour. Some children are far more comfortable in one setting than the other, and parents usually know which.

Grade 5: the quiet advantage

Most New York schools start their middle years in 6th grade; UNIS starts in 5th. For a child who is not already in the Junior School, that makes 5th grade a natural way in, at a moment when few other schools are recruiting. The child arrives at the start of a division, with a cohort that is forming, and has four years to settle before the demands of Tutorial House and the IB. For a family planning a move to New York, aiming for 5th rather than 6th is often worth considering for that reason alone.

The ISEE level is set by the grade applied to, so a 5th or 6th grade applicant takes the Lower Level: 128 questions over 2 hours 20 minutes, plus an unscored essay. It is long for a ten-year-old, and stamina is part of what needs preparing.

Grades 7 and 8: language, and the conversation

From 7th grade every UNIS student adds a further language, chosen among Arabic, Chinese, German, Italian, Japanese and Russian, on top of the French or Spanish begun earlier and English. A child entering in 7th grade therefore starts that new language with everyone else, while a child entering in 8th joins classes already a year in. Languages are taught in three or four levels per grade, so a beginner is placed correctly, but the workload of a second new language in a new school is real and worth planning for.

The 30-minute conversation is where children from other systems are most often underestimated. A pupil who answers in two words because that is how teachers are addressed at home, or who has never been asked what they think of a book, can come across as passive when they are simply unused to the format. This is the part of the process that benefits most from practice, and the least from memorised answers.

Hesitating between applying for 5th or 6th grade? Ask me; the answer depends on the child.

What the school is checking

Behind both formats sits the same question: will this child thrive in a large, busy school of around 1,400 students that treats middle schoolers as increasingly independent? UNIS describes the Middle School around independence, inquiry and analytical skills, with advisory structures to support students through adolescence. A child who needs constant adult direction to get started will find it harder than one who organises themselves, and the evaluation is designed to see that difference.

Class sizes run from 4 to 24 students, depending on the subject and level, with language groups often the smallest.

Timeline for September 2027

Open Ravenna and apply in the autumn; book the meeting and tour together; sit the ISEE in October or November with a December slot in reserve, or check that MAP results from the past year are strong enough to send; ask teachers for the ISAAGNY form before the end of November; make sure reports leave the current school in January. The decision arrives on February 10.

How I work with Middle School applicants

For a ten-year-old, I combine test preparation paced for their age with short group exercises that rehearse listening and contributing. For a twelve-year-old, the balance shifts toward the conversation: talking about a book, a project, a place they have lived, with ease and in full sentences, in English. In both cases I start from a timed diagnostic test, because a profile in hand saves weeks of unfocused work.
