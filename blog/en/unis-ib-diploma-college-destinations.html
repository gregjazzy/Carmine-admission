UNIS, the IB Diploma and where its graduates go to college

United Nations International School, on the East River, Manhattan. Photo Transpoman, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

Book a conversation

In 1967, UNIS was one of the founding schools of the International Baccalaureate Diploma Programme, alongside about ten other schools worldwide. Nearly sixty years later the Diploma is still the backbone of its high school, and it shapes where graduates go: every year, UNIS students enroll at universities in ten or more countries beyond the United States. For a family choosing a New York school with university in mind, that international reach is the main thing UNIS offers that an American curriculum school does not.

The IB at UNIS

Every Tutorial House student takes IB courses, and most complete the full Diploma. The program spans the sciences, history, economics, philosophy, anthropology, psychology, the arts and computer science, with the Extended Essay, Theory of Knowledge and the Creativity, Activity, Service projects that make the Diploma distinctive. For multilingual students, UNIS teaches several languages at Language A level, including French and Spanish, which opens the way to the bilingual diploma.

The demand is real. An IB student writes, researches and argues at a level many schools reserve for university, and the last two years are the busiest of the whole school. Students who arrive in 9th or 10th grade have time to build up to it; those who arrive in 11th start immediately.

Where they go

Over the past five years, UNIS graduates have enrolled at Harvard, Yale, Princeton, Columbia, Penn, Brown, Cornell, Dartmouth, Duke, Johns Hopkins, Chicago, Georgetown, Berkeley, UCLA and NYU among many American universities. In the United Kingdom, the list includes Cambridge, Imperial College, LSE, UCL, King's College London, St Andrews and Edinburgh. In Canada, McGill, Toronto and British Columbia. In Europe, Bocconi, TU Delft, Trinity College Dublin, ESADE, IE, the École hôtelière de Lausanne, and the joint degrees Columbia runs with Sciences Po and with Trinity.

The list is long rather than concentrated: UNIS sends students where their profile and their languages fit, and the strength of the counseling office lies in that range.

What the college office does

Counselors see families from 9th grade, but the real work starts in 11th, when each student is paired with a dedicated counselor and meets them regularly through two years. Two moments stand out. "Saturday with Admissions" lets 11th graders and their parents review mock applications and sit in on a simulated American admissions committee run by real admissions officers. And each fall more than a hundred universities from around the world visit the school, which gives seniors direct contact in small groups. A spring fair adds representatives from more than 150 colleges.

This is solid support. It is also shared by a whole class, and for the most competitive applications, especially the American selective colleges and Oxbridge, families often want more individual work on essays, testing and interviews.

Reading the IB as a family

For American universities, the IB is a demanding curriculum that admissions offices know well. For British universities, offers are made directly on predicted IB points. For many European universities, the IB is the standard international entry qualification. That is why a UNIS graduate's options are wide: the same diploma speaks to all three systems.

How I support UNIS students after admission

Once a student is at UNIS, my work shifts: IB mathematics and sciences when the pace becomes heavy, standardized testing for American applications, and the essays and interviews that decide the most selective admissions.
