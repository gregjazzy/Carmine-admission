Mandarin and languages at HKIS

Families choosing a school in Hong Kong often weigh how much Chinese their child will learn. At HKIS, the answer has three parts: compulsory Mandarin in the early years, a choice later, and, since 2025-26, a bilingual immersion pathway for the youngest. Admission, however, depends on English.

The structure

GradesLanguages
Reception One to Grade 5Mandarin compulsory, taught in ability groups by grade level
Grades 6 to 12Modern language as an elective: Mandarin or Spanish
Reception One, from 2025-26Dual Language Immersion pathway, in two classes in its first year, teaching content in Chinese and English

How Chinese is taught

The Chinese Studies programme aims at both language proficiency and cultural competence, for students of all backgrounds and levels. It uses the American Council on the Teaching of Foreign Languages proficiency guidelines: students move through levels according to how much and how well they can use the language. Literacy work is aligned with the Common Core standards used for English, so that reading and writing skills transfer between the two languages. Chinese culture is one of the school's Student Learning Results, woven into the curriculum and community life.

English decides, Mandarin adds

Admission to HKIS is assessed in English. I prepare children's English for the assessment, whatever their level of Mandarin.

The dual-language pathway

Introduced in 2025-26, the Dual Language Immersion pathway extends Chinese Studies for the youngest children. Alongside the second-language pathway, where students are placed by proficiency, it delivers academic content in both Chinese and English and builds literacy in both. It began in two Reception One classes and follows the same academic philosophy and standards as the mainstream Primary programme.

Why English still decides admission

Whatever a child's Mandarin, the admissions criteria put English proficiency first, and every pre-entry assessment examines it, from the youngest children's receptive and expressive English to the oral and written components of the adaptive test from Grade 3. A bilingual child strong in Chinese and still developing English should invest in English before applying.

For families arriving from abroad

The Chinese programme is designed for all backgrounds and proficiency levels, and Primary students are grouped by ability, so a child new to Mandarin starts at their own level. In Middle and High School, the choice between Mandarin and Spanish lets a student continue a language already begun elsewhere.
