Alongside need-based assistance, Columbus Academy offers three scholarship programmes with different rules. Two reward merit for students entering ninth grade; the third, funded by the Malone Family Foundation, combines academic giftedness and financial need from seventh grade. Columbus Academy is the only school in Ohio chosen by the foundation to offer it.

I help families position strong applicants for these awards. Here is how each one works.

Three programmes

ScholarshipForBasis
Merit scholarshipsNew incoming ninth gradersTest scores, transcripts, recommendations and a review of all first-round ninth-grade applications
Malone Family Foundation ScholarshipGrades 7 to 12Academic giftedness combined with financial need
Tom Morris '54 Alumni Memorial ScholarA rising ninth grader each yearMerit, chosen by a committee of alumni, for leadership and potential impact

Merit awards are renewable through graduation as long as the recipient keeps a solid academic and social record. No scholarship is awarded for athletics: Ohio High School Athletic Association bylaws forbid athletic scholarships.

How merit finalists are chosen

For ninth grade, the Upper School admissions committee reviews all first-round applications and selects merit finalists from among the students it intends to admit. There is no separate application: the ordinary file is the scholarship file. That makes two things decisive. The first is applying in the first round, by February 1. The second is the strength of each component, since test scores, transcripts and recommendations are all named in the selection.

The Malone scholarship

The Malone Family Foundation supports top students with limited financial resources at a select group of independent schools nationwide. At Columbus Academy, Malone Scholars are academically gifted students in Grades 7 to 12 who show financial need and exceptional promise; they receive substantial financial support and enrichment opportunities. For a gifted student whose family could not otherwise consider the school, it is the most important door.
