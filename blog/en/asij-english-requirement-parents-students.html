English at ASIJ, for parents and children

ASIJ sets an English requirement for parents as well as for children, and it is one of its two eligibility conditions, alongside living in Tokyo. At least one parent must speak, read and write English fluently. The school's reason is practical: English is the medium of all communication at ASIJ, from teachers' messages to parent meetings, and the school wants parents able to follow and support their child's education.

I work with bilingual and Japanese families considering ASIJ. Here is what the requirement means.

What a high level means

ASIJ is explicit that its students come from many language backgrounds and that a native speaker is not required. It is equally explicit that success in its program needs a high level of English. A child can speak English easily at home or with friends and still find a textbook, a written argument or a timed reading passage hard. The gap shows most in academic vocabulary, reading speed and the ability to write at length. Language proficiency tests are not accepted as the standardized test for the application; the school wants an academic test, which measures exactly that.

Timing matters for older children

Language support at ASIJ runs from Grade 1 to Grade 8. A child entering Middle School with English still developing can be supported; a child entering High School needs to be ready to work in English across all subjects from the first day. For families thinking about a move to ASIJ later in school, the years before Grade 9 are when building academic English makes the biggest difference.
