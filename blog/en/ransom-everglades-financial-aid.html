Nearly one student in five at Ransom Everglades receives help with tuition. The school's budget for aid and remission exceeds $9.5 million each year, and the typical grant is close to $43,000, or three quarters of the tuition. Families apply for aid on a separate track, and an applicant's finances play no part in the admission decision.

What the committee counts

The school looks at income and assets, with allowances and certain debts, corroborated by two years of tax returns, and considers part of a family's discretionary income available for tuition. Its committee expects some financial sacrifice and distinguishes necessary spending from lifestyle choices, which it will not offset. It may count the earning power of a parent who is not working without a documented reason, if there are no small children at home. When parents are separated, both biological parents and their current spouses fill out applications, and the school does not accept that one parent has disclaimed responsibility.

Renewal each year

Each year the family files again, with new tax returns. If its finances have not changed much, its contribution should stay about the same until graduation; the aid office reviews eligibility annually. Current families file earlier than new ones, by November 15, and receive their decision with the re-enrollment agreement in late January.
