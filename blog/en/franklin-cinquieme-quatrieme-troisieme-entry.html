Franklin: entering cinquième, quatrième or troisième

After sixième, Franklin opens no new classes. A place in cinquième, quatrième or troisième (ages 12 to 14) only exists if a student leaves, so the question is not only "does my child have the level?" but "will there be a place, and who will get it?".

The calendar

Applications for cinquième, quatrième and troisième open on 15 September and close on 31 March, for the following September. The file is submitted online; it is checked, then reviewed by the director or the préfet, who offers an appointment or declines. As for sixième, there is no written test, and the appointment does not mean admission.

The 31 March date is misleading. Places become available throughout the year, and a complete file submitted in the autumn is read while there is still a choice. Applying in March often means applying for a place already given.

A place mid-collège

When places come from departures, every application is compared with the students already there. I prepare applications of excellence that stand that comparison, and students ready for the appointment with the préfet.

What makes the difference

The director and the préfet read the reports with one question in mind: will this student keep up with a class already under way? Franklin's students have classes on Saturday mornings from cinquième, study Latin in cinquième, and many started German in the bilingual track in sixième. A new student must show they will hold that pace: consistent results, comments that speak of work and seriousness, a family committed to the Jesuit project.

Is your child in sixième, cinquième or quatrième elsewhere? Tell me about them: I will tell you whether Franklin is realistic, and when.

Joining mid-way

I have students at Franklin, and every year I support around fifteen families towards the leading Paris schools. For entry mid-collège, my work starts with a frank reading of the file: does it hold up against the students already there, and in which subjects must it still rise? Then I prepare students able to carry the appointment, and, in parallel, other collèges that genuinely suit them, because mid-collège no application is ever certain.

Arriving, and keeping up

A student entering quatrième at Franklin joins a class with its own habits, demands and pace. The first weeks matter: Latin to catch up if needed, Saturday tests, the writing method expected in French and mathematics. Families who prepare the arrival over the summer avoid a difficult first term, which would then weigh on entry to the lycée.
