Maximizing Your Chances of Getting Into Jeannine Manuel

Photo: École Jeannine Manuel

EJM Admission February 2026 · updated November 2026 5 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. Has worked with students of École Jeannine Manuel for more than ten years.

Book a conversation

Many candidates turned down by École Jeannine Manuel were not weak students. They started too late, aimed at the wrong year group or submitted a file that told no story. These are things a family controls. Preparing children for this admission every year, and following students of the school for more than ten years, I have learned to spot the levers that depend on you. Here they are, in the order they are decided.

Start a year ahead

Applications open at the end of September for the following school year and close in mid-January for families living in France. That leaves barely four months. Measuring a child's level, closing a gap, gathering the documents and preparing the assessments do not fit into that window if you start in November. Ideally, take stock a year before the target start date, in the spring or early summer.

Aim at the right year group

The school opens whole classes at only five levels: moyenne section, grande section, CP, sixième and seconde. Elsewhere, a place only exists if a student leaves. Choosing the entry level is therefore the first lever, and often the most powerful: waiting a year to aim at an entry year is sometimes better than applying now for a place that may not exist. The differences between levels are covered in admission in sixième, quatrième or seconde.

Making the most of the application

The right entry level, the right timing, a file that tells a clear story: that is the work I do with every family aiming for Jeannine Manuel. Let's start with a first conversation about your child.

Measure the real level, not the school reports

Excellent grades at a school abroad or an international school say little about the level expected at Jeannine Manuel. Written French often falls behind after a few years abroad, even for a French-speaking child, and fluent spoken English does not guarantee the written English the school expects, a solid level from middle school and near-native in lycée. Only an honest measure shows which gap to work on.

The actual level, not the perceived one: I give your child three tests at the school's level, in French, English and mathematics. Ask for an assessment.

Mathematics deserves its own attention. The French curriculum asks students to prove, not just to calculate, and a bright student from the American system or the IB often calculates very well without ever having written a French-style argument.

Proofs, written method, French conventions: let's talk about your child's mathematics and what remains to prepare before the test.

A complete file, early

An application only goes forward to pre-selection once it is complete. The documents that cause most delay are those that depend on other people: the letter from the current school, required unless that school is under contract with the French state; the letter about the family, written by a relative, a friend or a professional contact; the speech and language assessment for grande section and CP. Asking for them as soon as applications open keeps a file from waiting while others move ahead.

Put the school reports in context

Grades that dip in the year of a move do not doom a file, as long as they are explained. A file that shows a drop without a word leaves the school to interpret it, rarely in the candidate's favour. A few lines on the change of country or system, and a trajectory that climbs again, are often enough.

Prepare the interviews, the parents' too

The school meets both parents, and expects from them a strong international outlook, commitment to its mission and close involvement in their child's education. That meeting is prepared like the child's: being able to say simply why this school, and what the family will bring to it. For the child, the aim is to talk naturally about what they enjoy and where they come from, not to recite.

The interview is prepared, not improvised. Tell me about your child and we will work on how they tell their story.

Never depend on one school

Even well prepared, an application to Jeannine Manuel remains uncertain. Wise families present their child to two or three other schools in parallel, chosen for the child rather than by default. Sometimes the alternative turns out to suit them better.

Every year, I see the vast majority of applicants leave without a place. My job is to do everything I can so that your child is not one of them, by working on each of these levers. The full procedure is in the guide to getting into Jeannine Manuel.
