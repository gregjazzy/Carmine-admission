École des Roches: admission, step by step

École des Roches, Verneuil-sur-Avre. Photo Djampa, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

Boarding schools November 2026 5 min read

The École des Roches, founded in 1899 in Verneuil-sur-Avre, Normandy, was France's first "new school". Today it is an international boarding school of about 240 students from more than fifty countries, on a 50-hectare campus just over an hour from Paris, and it works in partnership with the École Jeannine Manuel. Its admission is more flexible than at the leading Swiss boarding schools, but it is not a formality.

Six steps

It starts with an online enquiry, answered within 24 hours. A member of the admissions team then holds an interview with the family to discuss the child's academic profile, goals and the most suitable programme: middle school, French baccalaureate, BFI, IB Diploma or an intensive French year. Next comes the formal application, with recent school reports, a language assessment and a letter of motivation from the student. The academic team reviews each file and sends a decision letter, with a conditional offer and, if needed, a placement test. The family confirms the place with the enrolment contract and enrolment fees.

An application that leads to the right programme

I help you choose the programme that will truly serve your child, then prepare with them their file, letter of motivation and assessments. My aim: that they join the École des Roches on the track where they will succeed best.

What really matters

Most students take a language and academic assessment: it is used to place them on the right track. For the IB Diploma, the school requires strong written and spoken English. For the intensive French year, no prior level is required. The heart of the application is therefore the choice of programme: a student placed on a track that does not suit them, the IB without enough English or the French bac without solid French, starts at a disadvantage. That choice is built during the initial interview, and needs preparing.

French bac, BFI or IB for your child? Tell me about them: I will tell you which track serves them best.

The calendar

Students generally join middle school around 11 or 12, and the school holds open days throughout the year. Mid-year admissions are considered case by case, depending on places and the student's profile. As a full boarding school, all students in the year-round programmes live on campus.

How I support families

I have students at the École des Roches, and for more than ten years I have followed students at the École Jeannine Manuel, its partner; every year I support around fifteen families with their admissions. For the École des Roches, I start with an assessment of your child in French, English and maths, which shows which programme suits them. I then prepare their letter of motivation and assessments with them, and help you compare the school with other possible boarding schools.
