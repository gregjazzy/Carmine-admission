Collège Sévigné: the bilingual primary school, from nursery to CM2

Collège Sévigné, rue Pierre-Nicole, Paris 5th. Photo LPLT, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0

The Collège Sévigné primary school is not on rue Pierre-Nicole but on boulevard Arago, in the 14th arrondissement. It is small: three nursery classes, two classes per year group from CP to CM2 (ages 6 to 10), around three hundred pupils. And since 2016 it has been bilingual, which many families still do not know when they think of Sévigné as a classic Latin Quarter secondary school.

Real bilingualism, half and half

In the first year of nursery, English takes up two mornings a week. From the second year, teaching is split equally: half in French, half in English, by half-days and in different classrooms depending on the language, with native English-speaking teachers. From CP to CM2, every subject is worked on in both languages across each cycle, and English is taught in half-groups two hours a week to match each child's level.

The school takes bilingual children, but also French-speaking children who want to build very strong English. The curriculum remains the French national one: Sévigné is under contract with the state.

What sets this school apart

Three teaching choices come up again and again with the families I meet. Mathematics is taught along the lines of the Singapore method, worked out with the secondary teachers. Philosophy starts in the last year of nursery, in workshops, then in half-groups from CP: a feature unique in Paris. And the school goes out a lot: one morning a week of outdoor class in nursery, residential trips from CP, a week of English immersion in CM1-CM2.

Choosing a bilingual primary school in Paris

I have students at Sévigné, and every year I support around fifteen families towards the leading Paris schools. In primary, the choice seems less binding than in collège; it is not, because it decides the child's written English at ten, and so the doors that open in sixième, at Sévigné in the bilingual track as at Jeannine Manuel or the Alsacienne. What I bring is an honest comparison between these schools, and a reading of the child's real level to know where they will be most at ease.

And after CM2

In collège, Sévigné offers two tracks: bilingual English, for children who attended a bilingual primary, and English +, open to all. A child who did primary on boulevard Arago arrives naturally equipped for the first. Both tracks and their tests are described in Sévigné: entering sixième.
