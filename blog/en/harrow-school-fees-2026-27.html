Harrow School fees 2026/27: what a family really pays

For 2026/27, Harrow charges £21,990 per term, including VAT, or £65,970 for the three terms of the year. That figure is broader than many fee lists: it covers board, tuition, textbooks, a stationery allowance and laundry. What it does not cover is the series of deposits and fees around entry, and, for families living abroad, two further items that change the first-year budget considerably.

Around the offer: what is paid and when

ItemAmountWhen, and on what terms
Registration fee£450On registration, non-refundable

All amounts include VAT. Fees are paid in advance by the first day of each term.

A first year for a family abroad, in one figure

Take a boy whose parents live outside the UK and who needs a Child Student visa, entering in September 2027 at current rates. Before he arrives, the family will have paid the registration fee, the acceptance fee and the two deposits, £6,450 in all, plus a full term held as the overseas deposit, £21,990. His first year then costs £65,970 in fees and £3,000 in visa administration. Extra tuition in any subject is charged on top, as are additional English lessons for a boy who needs them after the free assessment. Fees are set each year, so a family planning five years should budget for increases.

The overseas deposit is the item that surprises: it is a full term paid more than a year early, and it only comes back on the final bill, at the end of the boy's time at Harrow, or if he withdraws with proper notice before starting.

A place earned, not bought

At Harrow, the fees follow the offer, never the reverse. We prepare boys for the Pre-Test, the Harrow Test and the interview, so that the investment begins with a place earned on merit.

Scholarships and bursaries: not the same thing

A Harrow scholarship is recognition of excellence, academic, artistic or sporting, and it carries a 5 percent fee remission: about £3,300 a year at current fees. A bursary is means-tested and can cover up to 100 percent of fees. As a UK charity, however, Harrow does not consider bursary applications from overseas, so for families living abroad, the scholarship is the only fee reduction available, and a modest one.

For UK families, Harrow is clear that indicating a need for a bursary on the registration form does not disadvantage an application.

Why the price does not decide

With around 600 applications for 160 places in the Shell, Harrow does not need to choose by ability to pay, and its process makes that visible: the reference, the Pre-Test, the Harrow Test with its interview and group work, then Common Entrance. A family that can afford the fees has, at that stage, exactly the same chances as any other. The question that deserves energy is therefore not the cost but the file.
