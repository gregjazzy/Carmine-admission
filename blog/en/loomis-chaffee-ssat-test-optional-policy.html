Testing at Loomis Chaffee

Loomis Chaffee stopped requiring standardised tests in 2020, the one exception being English proficiency for some international students. The policy is real: about a third of applicants, a third of admitted students and a third of enrolled students have submitted scores since then. The decision is therefore a genuine choice, and worth making carefully.

The rules

Applying forPreferred test
Grades 9 and 10SSAT (school code 4672) or ISEE
Grades 11 and postgraduatePSAT, SAT or ACT
All candidatesScores sent by the testing agency, received by 1 February
Everyone, tests or notThe Test Optional Form on the admission portal

An application is not considered complete, and is not reviewed, until the Test Optional Form has been submitted.

Submit or not?

Loomis encourages students who have taken a test to send it, because scores help it understand academic preparation and place students in the right courses. But it adds a clear condition: if the scores do not accurately represent the student's ability, do not send them. Students who choose not to submit are not penalised; decisions then rest on grades, courses, the application, the interview and recommendations. The school does not publish average SSAT scores, so that capable students do not use them to rule themselves out.

Without scores: a third recommendation

Loomis strongly recommends that applicants who do not submit testing send an additional academic recommendation, from a teacher who knows their academic work well. It replaces part of what a score would have shown, and gives the committee a more complete picture.

International students

International applicants do not need to submit SSAT or ISEE scores. Those whose first language is not English and who have studied fewer than two years in an English-speaking school must, however, show proficiency through the TOEFL or IELTS, or Duolingo where those are unavailable, before they can schedule an interview.
