How to get into Westminster School

Westminster School sits in the precincts of Westminster Abbey, in the middle of London, and is one of the most academically selective schools in the country. It is also changing. Girls have been part of its Sixth Form for more than fifty years; from 2028 they will join at 13+ too, and by 2030 every year group, from Reception at the Under School to the Upper Sixth, will be mixed. For families choosing a route in, the first question is not "how good is my child" but "which door".

Three doors, three sets of rules

Entry pointPlacesWho it suits
11+, Westminster Under School (Year 7)About 40Children at primary schools ending in Year 6; a through place to Westminster School in Year 9 with no further test
13+, Westminster School (Year 9)About 125, from around 500 applicationsChildren at prep schools that run to Year 8, with parents living in the UK
16+, Sixth Form (Year 12)75 to 85, from 600 to 650 candidatesStrong GCSE students, and the only door for international boarders

A child applying at 11+ cannot apply at 13+ at the same time. Westminster's advice is simple: primary schools that end in Year 6 point to 11+, prep schools that end in Year 8 point to 13+.

The 13+ route

Registration opens on 1 October of Year 5 and closes on 30 September of Year 6. In October or November of Year 6, candidates sit the ISEB Common Pre-Tests at their own school: mathematics, English and reasoning, online, with scores adjusted for age. Westminster also gives real weight to the report it requests from the current school in November. Around 250 candidates are then invited for further 40-minute papers in mathematics and English in early January, and an interview in January or February. Offers come by the end of February of Year 6, as unconditional places.

"Unconditional" has a precise meaning here: the school believes the child will comfortably reach the standard required at 13, and expects continued good conduct and progress, confirmed by an unreserved reference from the prep school in March of Year 8.

The rule that surprises families abroad

Westminster does not take international pupils before the Sixth Form. Its policy is that the school is not a suitable boarding environment for children under 16 whose parents live outside the UK, so at 13+ at least one parent must live in the UK, and the school does not sponsor Child Student visas at that age. Boarding at Westminster is weekly and term-time only, with school on Saturday mornings, and boarders under 16 spend weekends with their parents. For a family abroad, the realistic routes are therefore a move to London, or Sixth Form entry.

What Westminster looks for

At every entry point, Westminster selects on academic grounds: ability, potential and genuine intellectual interest. At 11+ it goes as far as recommending that parents avoid outside tuition, which it believes can blunt the freshness and eagerness it wants to see. At 13+ it says the Pre-Tests need no special preparation. The message is consistent: the school wants to meet children who think well, read widely and enjoy a challenge, not children trained to a test.

The useful preparation is therefore of a particular kind: broad, ambitious reading; mathematics practised as problem solving rather than procedure; and enough experience of talking about one's interests that an interview with two adults feels like a conversation.

Money

For 2026-27, day fees at 13+ are £16,456 a term and boarding fees £23,094. King's Scholars, the academic scholars chosen through The Challenge in Year 8 or at 16+, pay the day fee while boarding in College. Means-tested bursaries of up to 100 percent of day fees exist for families who meet strict UK residence conditions.
