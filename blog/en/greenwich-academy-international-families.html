Greenwich Academy welcomes applications from girls who live outside the United States, and families relocating to the area for work apply every year. It is, however, a day school that is not certified to issue the I-20 form: it does not sponsor student visas. A family must therefore have, or arrange, an immigration status that allows its daughter to attend school before she can enrol.

I work with families relocating to the United States. Here is what changes for them.

Settling the status first

Because the school does not issue visas, the question of status comes before the application, not after the offer. Families relocating on a work visa usually have dependent status for their children; families without that should seek advice early. The school itself recommends speaking to a consulate or an immigration attorney before starting.

Reading a foreign file

A transcript from another country, in another grading scale or language, is harder for an admission committee to read than one from a neighbouring school. A short note from the current school explaining the scale, and an English translation where needed, make the file easier to weigh. For Groups VI and above, recommendations come from the English and math teachers: in a school where English is not the language of instruction, the family should check which teacher is best placed to write. Aid is limited to US citizens and permanent residents, so most relocating families should plan on full tuition, from $54,260 to $60,820 in 2026-27.
