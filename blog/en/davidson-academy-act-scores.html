The ACT is the second route to Davidson Academy eligibility through achievement testing. From sixth to eighth grade, an ACT or SAT score is required; from ninth grade, an IQ score is also accepted instead. Its thresholds follow the student's age-based grade, ignore any grade skip, and apply only to a sitting from the last twelve months, documented by the complete official report.

ACT or SAT?

The ACT's "three of five" rule and its Science section change the calculation. A student who reasons well with data and experiments can use Science as one of the three qualifying scores, which the SAT does not offer. A student whose strength is concentrated in mathematics may find the SAT's "two of three" rule, with Math as one of only three scores, easier to satisfy. A diagnostic on both, taken a few months ahead, usually makes the choice obvious.

Science reasoning matters beyond eligibility, too: it carries 30 of the 145 points in the Academic Readiness Assessment, and the Academy itself suggests the science reasoning section of ACT preparation books as a useful resource.

Practical points

Remote, proctored practice ACT and PreACT sittings run by Northwestern's Center for Talent Development are accepted, with one skipped session required before retesting. In fifth grade and below, the PreACT is also accepted, with its own thresholds: three of English 26, Math 24, Reading 26, Science 22 and Composite 25.
