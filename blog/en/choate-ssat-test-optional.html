Choate: test-optional, should you send the SSAT?

The Andrew Mellon Library, Choate Rosemary Hall, Wallingford. Photo Daderot, Wikimedia Commons, public domain

US boarding schools November 2026 5 min read

Book a conversation

Choate has made testing optional. For many families, that is a relief; for those truly aiming at the school, it is one more decision, and it should be prepared like a test. I have students at Choate, and every year I support around fifteen families towards the most selective schools and boarding schools in the world.

What the school says

For grade 9, the SSAT or ISEE, taken from June of the application year; for grades 10 and 11, SSAT, ISEE, PSAT, SAT or ACT. An applicant without scores is not penalised: they are judged on grades, interview, recommendations and activities. Choate publishes no average or cutoff, and keeps the highest score in each section across sittings. For non-native speakers, a TOEFL, IELTS or Duolingo is strongly recommended, with published guidance: TOEFL 100, IELTS 7, Duolingo 130.

What the school does not say

"Optional" does not mean "without effect". When a score is sent, it is read with the rest of the file: a good score confirms reports that are hard to compare, those of a school abroad for instance; an average score can weaken a strong file. It is all in the choice.

How to decide

Does the score confirm the reports?

For a student from a system the committee knows less well, a good score is often the simplest way to prove their level.

Has your child never sat the SSAT? Tell me about them: a practice test already says a lot.

Several sittings

Since Choate keeps the best score per section, a second sitting can be worth it. It still has to be planned in time.

English

For a non-native speaker, the English test often matters more than the SSAT. A TOEFL below the published guidance weighs heavily.
