Testing at Choate: send a score or not

The Andrew Mellon Library, Choate Rosemary Hall, Wallingford. Photo Daderot, Wikimedia Commons, public domain

US boarding schools November 2026 6 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

Book a conversation

Choate does not require a test. An applicant who sends no score is judged on grades, transcripts, coursework, the interview, recommendations and activities, and is not penalised. There is no average score and no cutoff. For a family, that turns the test from an obligation into a decision, and decisions are harder to get right than obligations.

Which tests, for which grade

Entry gradeAccepted testsValid from
Ninth gradeSSAT or ISEEJune of the application year
Tenth and eleventh gradesSSAT, ISEE, PSAT, SAT or ACTJune of the previous year
Postgraduate yearPSAT, SAT or ACTJune, two years before
Non-native English speakersTOEFL, IELTS or Duolingo, strongly recommendedJune of the application year

Scores go directly from the testing agency where possible; for the PSAT, SAT and ACT, a school counselor may submit verified scores instead. The codes are 2392 for the SSAT, 070245 for the ISEE, and 1883 for the SAT and the TOEFL.

Choate keeps the best of each section

When a family sends scores from several sittings, Choate reads the highest score in each section across dates. This applies to the SSAT, ISEE, PSAT, SAT and ACT. In practice, a second sitting cannot lower what the committee sees, and it can raise it. That changes the arithmetic of preparation: an autumn sitting followed by a second one in December or January, with focused work on the weakest section in between, is often the best plan for a child who is close.

Prepare first, decide after

I prepare children for the SSAT and ISEE at the level the most selective schools expect, then help the family decide, results in hand, whether the score belongs in the file.

Three files, three answers

The child at a well-known school with strong grades. The committee can place the transcript without help. A strong score adds a little; an ordinary one can take away more than it adds. This is the case where not sending is most often the right choice.

The child whose grades are hard to read. A small school, a new school, a curriculum abroad, a school that does not give letter grades: here the reader has no easy way to compare. A strong SSAT is the simplest proof available, and it often does more for the file than any other single document.

The child who has never taken a timed test in English. The question is not yet whether to send, but what a practice test shows. Until the family knows that, any decision is a guess.

Has your child already sat a practice SSAT? Tell me the results, or that there are none yet.

What a score says beside the transcript

A score is never read alone. It is read with the recommendations, the transcript and the current grades. A high score with modest grades raises a question about effort; modest scores with excellent grades raise one about the grades. The family should ask, before sending, which question the score will raise, not only whether it is "good".

For reference, the middle half of Choate's class of 2026 scored between 1280 and 1510 on the SAT, and between 27 and 34 on the ACT. That describes students at the end of their time at Choate, not applicants at fourteen, but it gives a sense of the room.

The English test is a different matter

For a non-native speaker, or a student who has spent fewer than two years in a school taught in English, a TOEFL, IELTS or Duolingo result is strongly recommended. The minimum guidance is TOEFL 100, IELTS 7 or Duolingo 130. These are demanding levels for a fourteen-year-old, and a result below them weighs heavily. For these children, the English test often matters more than the SSAT.

Fee waivers

Because the test is optional, Choate has very few SSAT fee waivers. Families who need one write to the admission office.

My approach to testing

I have students at Choate, and for seven years I have prepared around fifteen families a year for the most selective schools. On testing, I begin with a practice test under real timing, then work section by section, with the weakest section first. Only when the real scores are in do we decide together, against the rest of the file, whether to send them.
