Applying from Grade 4 or 5

The University of Nevada, Reno campus, home of the Davidson Academy. Photo NevadaMarCom, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

The Davidson Academy is a middle and high school, but students can apply before sixth grade. The school strongly recommends that applicants have at least fifth grade in progress, and students in third grade or below cannot be considered. For a fourth or fifth grader, the question is not only whether the scores qualify, but whether the child is ready to work three years ahead in every subject at nine or ten.

I work with young, very advanced students and their families. Here is what early application involves.

Tests for fourth and fifth graders

TestRequirement
SATSixth-grade thresholds: two of RW 630, Math 650, total 1280
ACTSixth-grade thresholds: three of English 28, Math 27, Reading 30, Science 25, Composite 27
PSAT 8/9Two of RW 600, Math 570, total 1170
PreACTThree of English 26, Math 24, Reading 26, Science 22, Composite 25

IQ results are accepted as supplementary information at this level, not as the qualifying score. Remote proctored sittings of these tests are offered through Northwestern University's Center for Talent Development, with one skipped session required before retesting.

Readiness at nine or ten

The Academy's lowest math class is Algebra I, so a fifth grader admitted must be ready for it. The readiness assessment compares applicants with current Davidson students of the same age at the same point in the year, so a fifth grader is measured against the Academy's youngest students. Writing is assessed in depth, with expository and argumentative writing that few children of that age have been taught.

Maturity matters as much. A full day of assessment, with feedback and revision, asks a young child to stay focused, accept correction and keep working at their own pace.

Applying now or waiting

Applying early has a cost if the timing is wrong: a student who is not ready is declined and must reapply a later year. Waiting a year can mean a stronger test result, an Algebra I foundation and more practice in writing. The right choice depends on the child, and on whether the test scores clear the sixth-grade bar comfortably or only just.
