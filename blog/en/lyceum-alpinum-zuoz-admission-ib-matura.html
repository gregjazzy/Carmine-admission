Lyceum Alpinum Zuoz admission: the test, and the real choice between IB and Matura

At Zuoz, admission is not a competition. The school reviews files as they arrive, invites shortlisted candidates to a test and an interview. The difficulty lies elsewhere: in the timing of boarding places, and above all in the choice of programme, which commits the child for four years and decides which universities will be open to them.

How admission works

The school takes new students from grade 7 to grade 11, roughly ages 12 to 17, and exceptionally in grade 12. The file includes the registration form, the two most recent report cards and a student questionnaire; teacher recommendations count too. The CHF 1,600 application fee must be paid before testing. Shortlisted candidates then sit a test and an interview, in Zuoz or online, receive the decision and, if admitted, the contract and the deposit invoice. The place is only secured once the contract is signed and the deposit paid, and the offer lapses if the contract is not returned within two weeks.

Admission is rolling, but a boarding place is only guaranteed with registration at least six months before term, ideally six to eight. A trial day in the boarding house is possible before deciding: for a child who has never boarded, it is the best test there is.

IB or Matura: the real decision

Zuoz splits its students roughly evenly between two tracks. On the international side, Pre-IGCSE in grades 7 and 8, IGCSE in grades 9 and 10, then the IB Diploma in grades 11 and 12, in English. On the Swiss side, the Matura, over four or six years, in German, or bilingual German-English over the last three years, or bilingual Italian-German for Italian speakers.

What international families rarely see is that Zuoz's Matura is first and foremost the valley's track: day students from the Engadine make up more than 70% of it. It opens every Swiss university without an entrance exam, ETH Zurich and St. Gallen included, but it demands real German. The IB is the natural route to the UK, the US or Canada; for Swiss universities, each one's subject and grade conditions need checking.

For a child who does not speak German and is aiming for ETH or St. Gallen, the school offers a German Intensive Year: a year of immersion to reach the B1 level required to start the Matura. It is an extra boarding year: a decision to make with eyes open, not by default.

Who thrives at Zuoz

The school has about 340 students, 220 of them boarders, from more than 45 countries, with a 10% cap per nationality. Classes have 15 to 18 students, lessons last 80 minutes, and every evening there is supervised study of 40 to 90 minutes. The setting is sporty and structured: an afternoon of games each week, one compulsory activity each weekend day, ten weekends a year when everyone stays on campus. An independent, sporty child who is at ease in a group finds their place; a child who needs a city around them may find the Engadine winter long.

The destinations match: between 2022 and 2025, Zuoz students were admitted to Oxford, Cambridge, Imperial, UCL, Harvard, Cornell, Duke, ETH Zurich, St. Gallen, Bocconi and ESCP.
