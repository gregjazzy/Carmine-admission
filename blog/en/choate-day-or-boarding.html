Day or boarding at Choate

Choate has 647 boarders and 214 day students this year. For a family in Connecticut, that means two doors into the same school, and the family has to choose one of them when it applies. The decision looks practical. It also shapes the application, the cost and the child's four years.

The rules

  • Only residents of Connecticut may apply as day students.
  • An applicant chooses day or boarding when submitting the application, and cannot apply as both.
  • A change after submission must be requested in writing to the admission office.
  • Both pools are competitive. Neither is a side entrance.
  • The day option is recommended for students within a reasonable commute; circumstances that make commuting especially hard are taken into account, if the family explains them.

Where day students live

Most come from New Haven County. Others come from Hartford County, from towns such as West Hartford, Farmington, Bristol, Southington, Wethersfield and Glastonbury, and from Middlesex County: Middletown, Cromwell, Portland, Durham, Haddam, Killingworth. A family in one of these towns is in the zone where Choate expects a day application, and a boarding application from there needs a reason.

Day or boarding, the same bar

The door is different; the level expected is not. I prepare children for the most selective schools at the top of their ability, whichever way they apply.

What a day student's day includes

Day students are not visitors. They can stay on campus until curfew, in spaces built for them such as St. John Hall and the Lanphier Café, and they take part in clubs, sports and evening activities like boarders. Weekend activities and overnights on campus are open to them. The difference is the night and the early morning: the day student sleeps at home, and the family remains part of the daily rhythm.

What boarding adds

Boarders live in dorms designed for each stage. Ninth graders have their own residences, with study hours and a fixed lights-out. Tenth and eleventh graders usually spend two consecutive years in the same dorm, and seniors and postgraduates live together. Adults live in the dorms, and student prefects help the younger ones settle. It is a community that runs twenty-four hours a day, and for a child who is ready, that is the point: learning to run their own time, with adults close by.

The price difference

In 2026-2027, a day year costs $57,610 and a boarding year $74,530, both including the $1,000 technology fee. The gap is $16,920 a year, close to $68,000 over four years. Financial aid applies to both, and need is calculated on the full cost of attendance, so the gap is not always what the family ends up paying.

How to choose

Three questions settle most cases. How long is the journey, every day, in winter? A commute that works in September may not work in January, and a tired day student misses the evenings that make Choate what it is. Is the child ready to live away from home at fourteen? Some are, and thrive; some are not yet, and a day place at the same school is a better start. And what does the family want its own role to be over four years? The answer to that last question is rarely written down, and it should be.

One thing the choice should not depend on: a guess about which pool is easier. Both are competitive, and a file built on that guess is built on nothing.
