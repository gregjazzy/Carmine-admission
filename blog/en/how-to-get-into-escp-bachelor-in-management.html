How to get into ESCP's Bachelor in Management

ESCP's Montparnasse campus, rue Armand-Moisant, Paris. Photo MobileTV, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

ESCP is best known for its Master in Management, ranked sixth in the world by the Financial Times in 2026. Yet the school also admits students straight from secondary school, into its Bachelor in Management: three years, three countries, teaching in English, and an admissions process that looks nothing like a French competitive exam or a standard university application.

I know this school very well. I have helped several students get in, and every year ESCP students work with me during their bachelor. So I see both sides: what the school looks for in an applicant, and what it demands once they are admitted. Behind the image of a welcoming international bachelor, the reality is a little different: the academic level is real, and selection has its own codes.

Three campuses in three years, and the first is settled at admission

The Bachelor in Management is taken on three campuses, one per year. Year one is in Berlin, London, Paris or Turin, year two in Berlin, Madrid, Paris or Turin, year three on any of the five. Teaching is in English, with some courses in French and language classes throughout. The degree carries the French "grade de licence", and up to two further degrees, British and German, can be added when the requirements are met.

The first-year campus comes with the offer; the next two are decided during year one. Places on each campus are limited, and a student admitted late may not get the one they wanted. The route across Europe, which is the whole point of the programme, is therefore largely decided at the moment of applying.

Parcoursup or a direct application: the choice that closes a door

ESCP accepts four routes: its own online application, Parcoursup (the French national platform for post-secondary admissions), UCAS and the Common App, and only one per applicant. A student taking the French baccalaureate who wants Paris among their possible first-year campuses must apply through Parcoursup. One who applies directly on the school's website, to move faster or because a classmate did, gives up Paris for year one without realising it.

Conversely, a student who wants to start in London, Berlin or Turin should look closely at the direct application. It runs in rounds from autumn to early summer, with an answer about a month after each deadline, long before Parcoursup results come out in June. A family weighing several schools abroad can hold a firm offer by the winter of the final year. Which route to take depends on the campus in view, the rest of the application list, and the point at which the file is strongest.

Example: the calendar for the September 2027 intake, Paris campus, direct application. Rounds close on 18 November 2026, 14 January 2027, 17 February, 29 March, in April and on 20 May, with results no later than 18 December, 12 February, 18 March, 23 April, in May and on 15 June. Turin opens earlier, with a first round closing on 27 October 2026.

The file: three years of grades and a text in English

The application includes transcripts for the last three years, translated into English, a personal statement in English, a CV, and an English test score unless the student is exempt: IELTS 6.5, TOEFL iBT 4.5 or C1 Advanced at 180. An academic reference can be added but is not required.

Three years of transcripts means the first of those years counts as much as the last. An average year followed by an excellent one should be presented and explained; it does not vanish on the assumption that the panel only reads recent grades. The optional reference is worth providing whenever a teacher knows the student well enough to be specific: in a pile of files where everyone has good grades, it is often the only document that shows how the student actually works.

The personal statement is the piece French schools prepare least. The panel expects a personal text, written in English from the first draft, explaining why three years in three countries make sense for this particular applicant. A statement about the school's ranking, network and partners tells the panel nothing new. One built on something real, a language learned, a project carried through, gives them a reason to keep this file rather than the next.

The interview, where the codes show

Applicants shortlisted on their file have an online interview with at least two people, including faculty, usually within two weeks of their round's deadline. The panel assesses motivation, the fit between profile and programme, and personality: adaptability, maturity, openness to living across several countries.

Students from the French system are used to oral exams, where you show what you know. Here the panel wants a conversation in English, concrete examples rather than intentions, the ability to talk about yourself without reciting or underselling, and a clear-eyed idea of what living alone in London or Turin at eighteen involves. Two weeks between deadline and interview is short, so the interview should be prepared alongside the file.

Results come with an unconditional or conditional offer, a deadline to reply and the first-year campus. The place is secured with a deposit. Through Parcoursup, the school urges admitted students to confirm quickly, since campuses fill up.

The level, once you are in

A management bachelor taught in English has a reputation for being easier than a French "classe prépa". At ESCP, though, year one includes two maths courses, Fundamentals of Mathematics 1 and Statistics & Probability, followed in year two by Fundamentals of Mathematics 2 and Advanced Statistical Methods. A course is passed only with an average of 10 out of 20 and at least 8 out of 20 in the final exam, and a student who still fails after the resit is excluded from the programme.

This is often where year one is won or lost, especially for students who dropped maths in their final year. Because I work with ESCP students in these courses every year, I know what they demand; I explain it on my page on maths and statistics tutoring for ESCP students. An applicant who arrives with solid foundations sits those first exams with a real margin.
