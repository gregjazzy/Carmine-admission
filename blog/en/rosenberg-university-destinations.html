Institut auf dem Rosenberg: where its students go after school

What becomes of Rosenberg students after high school? Families considering the St. Gallen school ask early, and rightly so: at this level of investment, what comes next matters as much as the boarding years. I have helped students win places at Rosenberg and Aiglon, and fifteen at Le Rosey, and I guide students all the way to the most selective universities. What we observe among Rosenberg students fits in one sentence: they all go on to university, but not all of them where they could.

A 100% university acceptance rate

Every Rosenberg student is admitted to university. It is the result of guidance that starts long before the final year: the individual development plan, which follows each student from Grade 9, culminates in dedicated college counselling, with counsellors able to support an application to any university in the world, and professional interview training for Oxbridge or the Ivy League.

The figure is reassuring, but it does not tell the whole story. Being admitted somewhere is not the same as being admitted where you were aiming. What separates trajectories is the target chosen and the quality of the file presented.

The programme you choose shapes the universities within reach

Rosenberg is unusual in offering almost every diploma under one roof: the IB, APs, A-levels and its own high school diploma, with close to 500 courses to choose from. It is an immense strength, and a trap for anyone choosing without a compass. A-levels speak naturally to British universities, APs to American ones, the IB to both; a poorly thought-out combination at fifteen can complicate an application at seventeen. The right programme is chosen by starting from the target university, not the other way round.

What leading universities look at beyond grades

At Rosenberg, everyone knows it: academic results are only one part of a successful application. Achievements outside the classroom, Talent & Enrichment courses, internships, awards, experiences, have become requirements for admission to leading universities. That is what Stanford, Harvard or Oxford read: a student who has done something with their talents, and knows how to write about it.

The school provides these opportunities. The student still has to choose them coherently and turn them into a story that holds, from an American essay to a British personal statement.

Oxbridge and the Ivy League, two different preparations

Rosenberg students aiming for the top often hesitate between the two sides of the Atlantic, and many apply to both. Yet the two processes have almost nothing in common. Oxford and Cambridge expect a precise project in one discipline, very high predicted grades, often an admissions test, then an academic interview in which the candidate is asked to think through a problem they have never seen. American universities read a whole profile: essays, activities, recommendations, the coherence of a path. Running both at once has to be prepared from Grade 11, with a tight timeline and clear priorities.

Thinking about university from the day of entry

For a family choosing Rosenberg, the best way to prepare for university is to think about it from admission onwards: which programme, which subjects, which activities outside the classroom, and in what order. A student who arrives in St. Gallen with a direction, even a provisional one, gets far more out of the school's system than one who discovers their options along the way. It is one of the first questions I work on with the families I accompany, often before the application to Rosenberg is even submitted.
