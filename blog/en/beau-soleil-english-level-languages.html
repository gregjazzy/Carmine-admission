English level and languages

Beau Soleil is an international school in a French-speaking canton, and its language policy reflects both. Much of the teaching is in English; French is the language of the village and, for many students, a second academic language. A family's first question is usually what level of English their child needs.

How much English?

There is no single threshold. The level required depends on the grade a child is joining: older students generally need stronger English to manage their academic programme, especially in the IB years. Every applicant sits a written English placement test, and the admissions team invites families to describe their child's current level so that readiness and support can be discussed.

French as an asset

Because the school is in the canton of Vaud, it encourages its younger students to learn French, with classes from complete beginner to native level. In the senior years, that investment can pay off academically: a third of the 2026 IB graduates earned a Bilingual Diploma, combining subjects in English and French.

Is your child's English ready for the grade they would join? Let's check it together.

Arriving with English still developing

A child whose English is still developing will find the first months more demanding, especially in the older grades, where coursework is heavier. Preparing English before arrival, through regular reading and writing, makes the difference between coping and thriving.

A multilingual community

Students are paired, where possible, with a roommate of a similar age and a different nationality. Daily life therefore happens in English across many accents, with French in the village and the student's own language at home.
