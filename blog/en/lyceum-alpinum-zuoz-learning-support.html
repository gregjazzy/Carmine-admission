Swiss boarding schools

Lyceum Alpinum Zuoz: learning support, dyslexia and pastoral care

The Lyceum Alpinum, Zuoz, Upper Engadine. Photo Asurnipal, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

Book a conversation

Many families considering a Swiss boarding school have a child who is bright but fragile, dyslexic, distracted, or simply uneven. The question is not whether the school is demanding, they all are, but whether it knows how to support. On that point Zuoz has resources few boarding schools state so clearly.

Psychologists on site

The school has a team of psychologists who see students individually, for their academic goals as for personal difficulties, in confidence. It is licensed to carry out in-house assessments for dyslexia, dyscalculia, ADHD and other conditions, which allows it to grant exam arrangements and suitable classroom support, and to inform teachers directly. The psychologists also help students with exam anxiety.

The right setting for your child

For a child who needs support, the choice of boarding school matters more than anything. I know which ones truly provide it, and I prepare an application that presents your child at their true worth.

Everyday support

Every evening from Monday to Thursday, a 60-minute study session lets students do homework with tutors on hand, and an hour on Sunday evening prepares the week. Weekly homework help is offered in most subjects, from mathematics to economics. Students struggling in a subject get individual tutoring; those falling below their class average are asked by their teachers to take it up.

Does your child have particular needs? Tell me about them: I will tell you which boarding schools truly suit them.

Choosing with full knowledge

Every year I support around fifteen families towards the most selective boarding schools and schools, in Switzerland and France, and fifteen of my students have been admitted to Le Rosey alone. For a child with a learning difficulty, I always compare Zuoz with Rosenberg, which builds the whole timetable around the student, and with other boarding schools, some of which turn away severe difficulties. The right choice depends on the nature and degree of the difficulty, and on what the child needs to show their worth: a communal, sporty setting, or deep individual tailoring.
