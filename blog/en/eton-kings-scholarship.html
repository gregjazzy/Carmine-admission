The King's Scholarship at Eton

Eton was founded in 1440 for seventy King's Scholars, and there are still seventy of them. They live together in College, one of the school's twenty-five boarding houses, under the Master-in-College and the Dame. About fourteen scholarships are awarded each year, usually held for five years, on the strength of a single written examination taken in Year 8.

It is the most academic way into Eton, and the most misunderstood. I prepare candidates for it, and the first conversation with a family is often about whether their son should sit it at all.

The papers

Every candidate sits five compulsory papers and at least three optional ones. The examiners mark him on the compulsory papers and his best three options.

The examination is very demanding by design: it exists to find the most able boys of their year. Most papers offer a wide range of questions so that real ability can show, and the examiners reward boys who are outstanding even in a limited field. The General papers in particular let a boy who has had little tuition show what he can do. Past papers from 2019 to 2025 are published, and older ones can be requested.

Prepared at scholarship level

The King's Scholarship is written for the strongest thirteen-year-olds of their year. I prepare candidates at that level, paper by paper, and help families decide early whether it is the right route for their son.

Two candidates, two very different bets

A boy who already holds a conditional place from Stage 2 sits the King's Scholarship with a net beneath him. If he does not win a place in College, a satisfactory performance still guarantees his entry to Eton; if he performs poorly, he may be asked to sit Common Entrance in full or in part before he can join.

A boy without a conditional place takes an all-or-nothing exam. He does not need to have been registered to apply, which makes the King's Scholarship the main door for boys who missed the Year 5 deadline. But if he does not win a scholarship, Eton does not offer him a place. His current head teacher is, in Eton's own process, the key adviser on whether the attempt makes sense, and a frank conversation with the school is worth having before the application goes in.

Is your son in Year 7 and strong across the board? Tell me about him: the decision to sit the King's Scholarship is best made early.

An honour, not a discount

The scholarship itself carries no automatic reduction in fees. Financial help comes only through the means-tested bursary, and the rules are strict: a family whose son went through the 13+ process without applying for a bursary in Year 6 cannot apply later on the strength of a King's Scholarship. Families who do need help submit the bursary application by 28 February and learn in April, before the examinations, what support would follow a scholarship.

Dates and conditions

A candidate must be thirteen on 1 September of the year he would join, or have turned fourteen in August of that year where Eton has already agreed to defer an August-born boy by a year. Applications open in early October of Year 8 (for 2027 entry, in early October 2026) and close in the second week of January. The form needs a recent head-and-shoulders photograph and the unabridged birth certificate, with a certified translation if it is not in English. The examinations take place in late April or early May. Eton also runs open afternoons for boys in Years 7 and 8 to see College and learn about the examination.
