Eton: the King's Scholarship

Eton was founded in 1440 by Henry VI for 70 scholars, the King's Scholars. They still exist: 70 boys live in a house of their own, College, and about 14 new scholarships are awarded each year, usually for five years. It is the most prestigious route into Eton, and the only one still open to a boy who was not registered at 10.

The exam

It takes place in late April or early May, for entry in September, at 13. Every candidate sits compulsory papers, currently English A and B, Mathematics A, Science and General I, and at least three optional papers among French, Latin, Greek, Mathematics B, General II and History-Geography-Divinity. The school says so itself: the exam is intentionally very demanding, designed to find the most able candidates. Recent past papers are published.

Preparing for the King's Scholarship

The King's Scholarship rewards a mind, not a syllabus. I prepare your son for the maths, science and English papers, and for the way of thinking those papers look for.

What families should know

No registration needed. A boy can apply without having been registered in Year 5. But if he does not win a scholarship and had no conditional place, he will not be offered a place.

An honour, not a discount. The King's Scholarship carries no automatic fee remission; means-tested support can be added, to be requested from Year 6.

A route for boys already admitted. For a boy with a conditional place who does not get into College, a satisfactory performance in the exam guarantees his place.
