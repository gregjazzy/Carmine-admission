Getting into the École alsacienne: admission, test and calendar

The École alsacienne, Paris 6th. Photo Celette, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

Paris schools June 2026 5 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

The École alsacienne is the only private school of its standing in Paris that publishes how selective it is. Not hearsay: figures, on its own website. Around 1,400 applications in 2020 and 2021 for around 200 places.

One applicant in seven. That is discouraging, and also very useful, because once you know the figure you stop building a strategy around a single school.

I know the École alsacienne from the inside: I have had, and still have, students there, and every year I see which children it takes, and why. Its figures are public; what it really looks for in a child is not.

How many places, and at which level

The school's second piece of transparency is even more useful than the first. It publishes the number of places per year group, so you can see where it is possible to get in and where it is not.

In practice, there are five real ways in: moyenne section (the middle year of nursery school, age 4), CP (the first year of primary, age 6), CM1 (age 9), sixième (the first year of collège, or lower secondary, age 11, Year 7 in the UK, 6th grade in the US) and seconde (the first year of lycée, or upper secondary, age 15). Everywhere else, you are applying for places that may never exist. The school also states that siblings only get priority in nursery and CP: at every other level their file is assessed like anyone else's, which means that even having a brother or sister there is no free pass.

My aim: that they get into the École alsacienne, or into the school of your choice if another suits them better.

The calendar: seven weeks, from November to early January

The École alsacienne opens its admissions campaign once a year, in November, for entry the following September, and closes it in early January. For September 2027 entry, it runs from Friday 13 November 2026 to Sunday 3 January 2027.

The school warns that, given the number of applications, no late applications will be accepted. Watch the page from the end of October of the year before entry, and assume you have about seven weeks.

Selection: a written test, and an interview that may not be what you expect

The application is made entirely online, through a single form that you can fill in over several sessions. After that, the process depends on the year group.

On one point, the École alsacienne is unlike any of its neighbours, and its own words say it better than I can.

In nursery and CP, it is the parents who are interviewed, not the child. But from CE1 (age 7) onwards, the school writes: "we do not see the parents", except in particular situations, and "we recruit pupils, not their parents".

Compare this with Saint-Jean de Passy, where both parents must attend the interview. The difference goes well beyond logistics: the two schools see admission in different ways. One assesses a family's plans. The other assesses a child, alone, for ten minutes, in front of a teacher. The preparation is different, and anything the parents might say carries no weight at all.

What the school does not say

The École alsacienne is known for its kindness and its refusal of the cult of performance, and rightly so. But that reputation misleads many families: they conclude that a happy, curious child is enough. Yet the school receives seven applicants for each place, and its test and interview decide between children who are almost all happy and curious. What a teacher looks for in ten minutes, how a child should talk about what they love, the real level expected in the French and maths test, and the kind of student who truly thrives there up to the bac: none of this is written down. It is what I know from the inside, and what I share with the families I work with. To place it among the other leading schools, see best schools in Paris.

A child alone, ten minutes facing a teacher: that can be prepared. Tell me about your child and I will prepare them for the written test and the interview.

Fees: the only school where your tax assessment is part of the application

The application fee is €100, non-refundable, charged by card when you apply. According to the school, it covers processing the application and organising the interviews and tests. Families in bands 1 to 4 of the 2026-2027 scale, families applying for a bursary and pupils from the Charcot partner schools are exempt.

Tuition itself is indexed to taxable income, in eight bands. The first band is worth knowing about, because it runs against everything people imagine about a school in the 6th arrondissement.

Discounts can be combined: 15 to 30% depending on the number of children at the school, 1 to 3% depending on the payment method, 10% for teachers. The school publishes that 13% of its pupils received assisted tuition in 2024-2025. Fees are set by the school each year, so you apply on the basis of the current year's fees.

With only one school in mind, a refusal leaves little time. Tell me about your child: I will tell you which other schools suit them, and which to aim for at the same time.

A trap for pupils in state schools

One last warning, which many families discover too late. A pupil at a Paris state school who applies for seconde must go through the education authority's placement procedure and put the École alsacienne as first choice. An offer of admission is useless if the choices were not listed in the right order.
