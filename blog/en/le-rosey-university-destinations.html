Le Rosey: where its students go after graduation

The Château du Rosey, Rolle. Photo Eric Bajart, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0

Book a conversation

Where do Le Rosey students go after graduation? It is often the real question behind choosing the school. Families tend to imagine an almost automatic path to Harvard or Oxford. The reality is more interesting, and more useful for planning what comes next. Fifteen of my students have been admitted to Le Rosey, and I also guide students all the way to the most selective universities: here is what we observe.

What the enrolments show

Over 2019-2025, counting only the universities where students actually enrolled, not mere offers, three poles emerge. London first, with 27 students at King's College, 22 at UCL, 8 at Imperial and 5 at the LSE. The United States next, led by NYU (17), Babson (17), Northeastern (15), Chicago (11) and Georgetown (9). Continental Europe last, with the Lausanne hotel school EHL (24), IE in Madrid (22) and Bocconi (5).

The most prestigious universities appear, but in small numbers: 7 students at Oxford, 2 at Cambridge, 4 at Harvard, 3 at Stanford, 7 at Penn, 6 at Columbia, 2 at Yale. Each year, about 10 to 15% of students are admitted to one of the eight Ivy League universities, Stanford, MIT or Oxbridge, and over 30% to top institutions such as the LSE, UCL, Duke, Berkeley or Imperial.

What these numbers mean

First observation: Le Rosey opens the doors of the great universities, but not for everyone. Roughly one student in eight reaches the world's most sought-after universities. That is remarkable, but it also means seven in eight go elsewhere, often to excellent institutions, sometimes after aiming higher.

Second observation: London weighs more than people think. King's College and UCL together took 49 students, as many as NYU, Babson and Northeastern combined. Third observation: a significant share chooses specialised schools, hospitality in Lausanne, business in Madrid or at Babson, music at Berklee, design at Parsons.

The school's guidance, and its limits

Le Rosey has four university counsellors, who know admissions in the UK, the US, Switzerland and France. Every student has a dedicated counsellor, and from classes 3 and 2 a programme called Horizons helps them build their profile, write about themselves and choose their subjects. It is a real asset. But four counsellors for a whole year group cannot carry every file towards Stanford or Oxford with the precision of one-to-one support.

What makes the difference for the most selective

For Oxbridge or the Ivy League, what separates those admitted from the rest is rarely grades alone. Subject choices from class 3, an intellectual project pursued over time, essays that say something true, preparation for Oxford interviews or admissions tests: all of this is built over two or three years, not in the final year. And the two systems read different files: British universities expect a precise project in one discipline, American ones a broader profile, where commitment outside the classroom weighs as much as results.

Thinking about university when choosing the school

For a family still choosing a boarding school, these figures carry a practical lesson. Le Rosey prepares students for both the International Baccalaureate and the French Baccalaureate, and the choice between the two, made well before the final years, shapes which universities are realistic and how the file will be read. A child who already leans towards London, the United States or a specialised school should make that choice with the destination in mind, not by default. It is one of the first conversations I have with the families I work with.
