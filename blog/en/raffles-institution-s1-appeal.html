The S1 Posting Appeal

The Secondary 1 posting results arrive in December. A boy whose PSLE score was strong enough for Raffles Institution may still be posted elsewhere, because the allocation also depends on choices and places. The S1 Posting Appeal Exercise is his remaining route into Year 1 that year.

Who may appeal

ConditionDetail
StatusBoy who is a Singapore citizen, permanent resident or international student
PostingNot posted to Raffles Institution in the S1 Posting Exercise
ScorePSLE score meeting RI's cut-off for his cohort that year
ExcludedStudents admitted to a secondary school through DSA

How it works

Applications are made through an online form that is accessible only during the appeal period, after posting results. Raffles assesses appeals on academic merit, and each depends on vacancies being available. The school does not disclose details of its selection, gives no reasons for non-selection, and treats its decision as final. Applicants are told the outcome by email.

A score that leaves options

The appeal only opens to boys at Raffles' cut-off. I prepare boys for the PSLE so that the score keeps every door open.

Two sources to read

The Ministry of Education explains that the appeal process depends on the reason for the transfer request, and points families to each preferred school's own website. Raffles publishes its conditions on its admissions pages and answers common questions about school life on its official question page. Reading both before December saves time when the window opens.

Preparing for the window

  • Before the results: read the Ministry's appeal guidance and Raffles' appeal page, so the form holds no surprise.
  • On posting day: check the result early; the appeal period is short.
  • In the meantime: accept the posted school's arrangements, since an appeal may not succeed.

If the appeal fails

Raffles admits a small number of boys in Year 3, from Secondary 2 in mainstream schools, on excellent results, co-curricular achievement, placement tests and an interview. Boys who entered their current school through DSA, or who are already in an Integrated Programme, are not considered. A boy who goes elsewhere in Year 1 and does well therefore keeps a later chance.
