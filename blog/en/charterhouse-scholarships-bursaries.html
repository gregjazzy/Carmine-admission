Charterhouse scholarships and bursaries

Charterhouse, Godalming, from the air. Photo Tim Sheerman-Chase, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 4.0

Scholarship is older than almost anything else at Charterhouse. The school's first boarding house, founded in 1614, is called Gownboys, after the original scholars who wore academic gowns. Today the school offers a wide range of awards at 13+ and 16+, all earned through open competition on merit, and separate means-tested bursaries for families who need help with fees.

The awards and when they are assessed

After registration, the school sends scholarship application forms ahead of the assessment dates. For children on the Year 8 route in 2026-27, co-curricular scholarship applications are due by Friday 11 December 2026, and academic ones in the spring of 2027.

The Academic Scholars Pathway

When Charterhouse makes 13+ offers after the assessment day, candidates who show exceptional academic potential may be invited to join the Academic Scholars Pathway: a series of intellectually demanding events and experiences at Charterhouse that prepare them for the academic scholarship in Year 8. The invitation is itself a signal, and it comes from the assessment day, not from a separate application.

What a scholarship is, and is not

A scholarship at Charterhouse recognises excellence and potential, and it carries weight beyond school, when pupils later apply to universities or enter professional life. It is first of all an honour. Where a family also has financial need, a scholarship can be supported by a bursary.

Bursaries

Bursaries exist to make Charterhouse possible for talented pupils whose families could not otherwise afford the fees, a tradition the school traces back over 400 years. They are means-tested, subject to available funds, and reviewed every year, so an award can change with the family's circumstances. Enquiries go to the admissions office.

Preparing for an academic scholarship

The academic scholarship is assessed in April of Year 8, more than a year after the 13+ offer. That year is the real preparation: wide reading, mathematics beyond the syllabus, and the habit of writing and arguing at length. Children who arrive at the scholarship papers having only revised for the Pre-Test are rarely the ones who win.
