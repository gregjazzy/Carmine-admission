UWCSEA is the only international school in Singapore with boarding houses on its own campuses, and it has more than fifty years of boarding behind it. Today, more than 300 boarders from over fifty nationalities live there, and about 30% of them are UWC scholars. The rules for joining them are precise.

Who can board

  • Age. From the August 2027 intake, boarding is open only to students entering Grade 9, around 14, or above.
  • The 90% rule. Boarding is for students whose families will not be living in Singapore for 90% or more of term time. It is not a weekday option for local families.
  • Full boarding only. There is no weekly, weekday or single-term boarding.
  • Commitment. Boarders in Grades 9 and 10 commit to at least one full school year; boarders in Grades 11 and 12 commit to the full two years of the IB Diploma.
  • Both campuses. Boarding applicants are considered for Dover and East automatically, and cannot apply through a company's nominee programme.

How boarders are chosen

The academic criteria are the same as for day students: a positive approach to learning, the right level of English and a profile that can progress with confidence. Boarding applicants then add an interview of 30 to 45 minutes, usually online, with the Director of Residential Life or a houseparent, to check they are socially and emotionally ready for residential life. High School applicants also take part in the Virtual Immersion Programme, and boarding applicants attend only one session even though they are considered for both campuses.

The houses

On Dover Campus, boarders live in two low-rise buildings forming three houses named after Kurt Hahn, Maya Angelou and Nelson Mandela. On East Campus, they live in Tampines House, a fourteen-storey building. Houseparents, generally teachers, live in the houses with their families; one is always on duty with a 24-hour emergency number. Each new boarder joins a "house family" of students of different ages, led by a houseparent.

A day

Breakfast in the campus canteen, then lessons alongside day students; the houses are closed during the school day, with more flexibility for Grades 11 and 12. After school come activities, sport, service and clubs, then free time, themed dinners, and supervised study led by assistant houseparents, with an EAL tutor on each campus. Younger boarders hand in phones and laptops in the evening; the older ones stay up a little later. Then the Wi-Fi goes off.

Rules and safety

Every boarder must have a Singapore mobile number. Leaving campus requires an online request at set times, and overnight stays need a host who has been screened in advance and parental approval. Passports are kept in the boarding office, the boarding house on each campus has a medical clinic with a registered nurse, and each campus has a counsellor.
