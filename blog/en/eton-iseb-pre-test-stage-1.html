Eton Stage 1: the ISEB Pre-Tests

UK boarding schools November 2026 6 min read

Every boy registered for Eton at 13+ takes the same first step, in the autumn of Year 6: the ISEB Common Pre-Tests, sat online at some point between early September and 30 November. On their results, and on a report from his current school, Eton decides in mid-December which boys come to Stage 2.

For many families, this is the first time their son is measured against the whole field. I prepare boys for these tests every year, and the same misreadings come back each autumn.

Four tests that adjust to the child

The Pre-Tests cover maths, English (reading comprehension and grammar), verbal reasoning and non-verbal reasoning. They are adaptive: each question depends on how the boy answered the ones before, so that the test climbs quickly to the level where he starts to make mistakes. Results are standardised by age against a sample of children applying to independent schools, which means a boy born in August is not compared with one born in September.

Two consequences follow. First, there is no comfortable middle of the paper: a strong boy spends most of the test on questions he finds hard, and that is normal. Second, the reasoning papers measure something many schools never teach directly. A boy can be top of his class in maths and still lose time on his first sequences of shapes, simply because he has never seen the format.

Stage 1 without surprises

I prepare boys for the four Pre-Tests so that the format no longer costs them anything and their real level carries them through, at the standard Eton expects of a candidate it will want to meet.

The part families forget: the report

Eton asks each boy's school for a reference in September of Year 6, to be returned by 31 October. It covers his academic strengths, his interests and his character. It is written before the test results exist, by people who have watched him for years, and it travels with his scores into the Stage 1 decision.

A report is not something parents write, but it is something they can make sure is informed. A school that knows the boy is applying to Eton, knows what he does outside lessons and knows the deadline writes a different reference from one that discovers the request in late October.

Where and when he sits it

The tests are usually taken at the boy's own school, which must register as an invigilation centre. Arranging this is the parents' responsibility: if the school cannot host the tests, Eton runs some sittings itself, usually in mid-October, and otherwise a test centre can be booked through VICTVS. Families register their son on the ISEB portal from June of Year 5, not before; for the 2026-27 session, it opened on 9 June 2026 and testing began on 1 September.

The timing is a choice. A boy can sit in the first week of September, after a summer away from school, or in November, after two months of lessons. The four tests can also be taken on one day or spread over several. These decisions look administrative; for some boys they are worth real marks.

One sitting, every school

The Pre-Tests are shared. A boy sits them once, and every school he gives his ISEB applicant number to sees the same results. For a family applying to Eton and two or three other leading schools, Stage 1 at Eton is also the first round everywhere else. That raises the stakes of a single autumn morning, and it is a strong reason to prepare once, properly, rather than separately for each school.

How I prepare boys for Stage 1

I have students at Eton and work each year with around fifteen families aiming for the most selective schools. Stage 1 preparation starts with an honest measure of where a boy stands in each of the four papers, set against the level the tests reach for a strong candidate. The work that follows is targeted: the format where he loses time, the reasoning he has never been taught, the reading speed that holds him back. The aim is a boy who walks into the test knowing what is coming, and a file, with its report and dates, that is in order well before the deadline.
