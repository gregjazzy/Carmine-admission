How to get into Bangkok Patana School

Bangkok Patana School has been part of Bangkok's education for more than sixty years. It is Thailand's first and largest British international school, not-for-profit, with over 2,200 students aged 2 to 18 from around 65 countries. It follows the English national curriculum to Year 11, then the IB Diploma in Years 12 and 13.

I have students at Patana, and the first thing I tell families is that there are two admissions processes, not one.

The non-Thai process, step by step

Everything starts with documents. The current teacher sends a recommendation directly to the school, with a writing sample from the child. The family uploads the last two years of school reports, identity documents, photos and signed forms, including a Letter of Guarantee and a doctor's letter, plus year-specific forms such as option choices in Year 10 and Year 12. Anything not in English must be professionally translated before the process starts.

The school will not process an application until all of this has arrived. It then reviews the file and arranges assessments, subject to places. Students who are not native English speakers are assessed by an English as an Additional Language specialist on campus; the youngest children are seen in person. A formal offer letter from the Head of School follows a positive decision, and the family secures the place with a non-refundable initial payment of THB 50,000.

English, the deciding test

For many families, the EAL assessment is the step that decides. I prepare children for it, spoken and written, and help families assemble a complete file.

Full year groups

The school says plainly that many year groups are full and that places in certain age groups are regularly oversubscribed. When no place is available, a complete application goes into a waiting pool, where the date of application is only the last of six factors. Families are encouraged to check availability with the admissions office and to apply early.

Moving to Bangkok? Tell me about your child, their age and their English.

A few conditions

Students must live in Bangkok with a parent or legal guardian, and non-Thai students need a valid non-immigrant visa before their first day. No single nationality may exceed 20% of the student body, so balance within a year group can matter too.
