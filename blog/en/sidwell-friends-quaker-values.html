Sidwell Friends is a Quaker school, founded in 1883, and its Quaker character is not decorative. The belief that there is "that of God" in everyone shapes how teachers treat students, how the community gathers, and what the school asks of its students outside the classroom. Families of all faiths and of none send their children there; what they share is a way of learning and living together.

I help families understand what this means before they apply. Here is how it shows in practice.

Truth through dialogue

At Sidwell Friends, truth is described not as a set of facts passed from teacher to student but as something revealed through dialogue, inquiry and reflection. In place of formal doctrine, students write queries, guiding questions they use to examine their lives and actions. In the classroom, teachers value the particular gifts of each student. The mission statement asks students to "let their lives speak".

Values visible in the admissions process

The admissions process mirrors these values. The file opens with the family's own words before any score; the parent meeting is a two-way conversation; the applicant video exists so that a child who is less comfortable in a formal interview can still be heard in their own voice. A student who listens, reflects and speaks honestly about what matters to them fits this community better than one who performs.

The same values guide the school's financial aid, which aims to make a Sidwell education accessible to families of all incomes.
