Confirmed places and Common Entrance

The entrance to Millfield, Street, Somerset. Photo Colin Smith, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 2.0

UK boarding schools November 2026 5 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

Book a conversation

For pupils at prep schools that prepare for Common Entrance, Millfield works differently from schools that wait for 13+ results. It runs its own interview and computer-based reasoning test early, and confirms places around eighteen months before entry, which for most pupils means around Year 7. The aim is explicit: to lift some of the pressure of Common Entrance, and to give families certainty well ahead. That arrangement raises the same questions every year.

"Once the place is confirmed, Year 8 no longer matters."

Not quite. Pupils at Common Entrance schools are expected to perform to their potential in the exams. The place itself does not depend on the result, but the results are read, and the admissions team stays in regular contact with every candidate's current school.

"Common Entrance results are used for nothing."

They are used, primarily for setting in Year 9. Millfield teaches in small sets, around twelve pupils on average, and the Common Entrance papers help place each new pupil in the right group for each subject. A pupil who underperforms may start Year 9 in a set below their real level, which is a slower start than necessary.

The early stage, done well

"A confirmed place comes with a scholarship decision."

No. Scholarships run on their own calendar. For September 2027 entry into Year 9, applications close on 1 December 2026, and assessments run from 25 to 29 January 2027, by discipline: music, sport and co-curricular, art and product design, then academic. Scholarship candidates hear about two to three weeks after their assessment.

Is your child in Year 6 or 7 at a prep school? Tell me where they are.

"The early assessment favours children who have been drilled."

The reasoning test has no syllabus and cannot be revised in the usual sense; it takes just under an hour on screen. The interview, about thirty minutes with a senior member of staff, looks at academic interests, activities and experiences. Familiarity with the test formats helps a child stay calm; beyond that, what makes the difference is a child who has something real to say.

"Pupils outside Common Entrance schools are at a disadvantage."

No. Children at state schools or international schools follow the same steps: registration, a reference from their school, interview and reasoning test, with UKiset sometimes asked of candidates abroad. Common Entrance simply does not apply to them, and their Year 9 setting relies on other information.

After the offer

About 200 pupils join Millfield in Year 9, the school's largest intake, and follow the Nine at Millfield programme, designed to welcome them into the school as a whole. In the term before entry, families receive joining information and the name of the house.
