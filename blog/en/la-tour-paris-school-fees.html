La Tour (Paris 16th) school fees, languages included

Paris schools November 2026 5 min read

La Tour is a Catholic collège-lycée under contract with the French state: basic schooling costs a few thousand euros a year. But the school has a feature that changes the maths: much of what makes its reputation, the anglophone section and the language sections, is billed on top. To compare La Tour with other schools, you have to add things up.

The base

In 2026-2027, the annual contribution is €2,186 in collège and €2,516 in lycée. On top come common flat-rate fees, €135 in collège and €126 in lycée, and fees specific to each year group, covering trips, retreats, mock exams and books: from €127 in troisième to €524 in seconde. A €54 per family subscription completes the bill. Lunches are optional: €1,763 a year for five a week, €382 for one.

Languages, the real variable

The anglophone section, "anglophone studies", costs €1,966 a year in sixième, €1,904 from cinquième to seconde and €1,472 in première and terminale, plus a €100 to €150 photocopy and book fee. Advanced or reinforced English costs €801 a year. Bilingual sections in other languages, Spanish, Italian, Arabic or Chinese, cost €1,477, and language options between €432 and €1,207 depending on hours.

In practice, a year of sixième in the anglophone section, with lunch every day, comes to around €6,500. The same student without the anglophone section or lunches pays around €2,600.

Admission fees

An application costs a €60 non-refundable fee. After the appointment, enrolment is confirmed by signing the schooling contract and paying a €600 advance, not refunded if you withdraw, except in cases of force majeure. Payments are then made by direct debit over nine months. The school has a solidarity fund and bursaries, awarded confidentially: no financial reason should prevent enrolment.

What the price does not tell you

I have students at La Tour, as at the other leading Paris schools, and every year I support around fifteen families with their admissions. The anglophone section surcharge only pays off if the child has the level to truly benefit; otherwise, advanced or reinforced English is often enough. That is the first question I ask, along with the real place languages will hold in the child's plans after the bac.
