How to get into NCSSM

The North Carolina School of Science and Mathematics is unusual on almost every count. It is a public high school, part of the University of North Carolina System, that houses juniors and seniors on two campuses, Durham and Morganton, and charges nothing: no tuition, no room and board, no meals, no textbooks, no application fee. It is open only to North Carolina residents, and it admits students for their last two years of high school.

Who can apply

  • Grade: students in their second year of high school, 10th grade, for entry as juniors.
  • Residency: bona fide legal residents of North Carolina, or children of active-duty military parents stationed in the state. Every applicant completes the state's Residency Determination Service process.
  • Programs: Residential in Durham, Morganton or both; NCSSM Online, a two-year supplemental honors program taken while staying at one's own school; and Summer Ventures. A single application covers all of them.

What the application contains

  1. The online application on CFNC.org, open from 15 October to 5 January, with essays.
  2. The residency determination through RDS, by 30 January.
  3. Three evaluations, by 28 February: a 9th or 10th grade STEM teacher, a 9th or 10th grade English teacher, and the current 10th grade counselor.
  4. Transcripts and report cards, submitted between 15 and 28 February.
  5. For Residential applicants, the NCSSM Math Assessment on one of two Saturdays in February.

The SAT and ACT play no part: for the Class of 2029, scores are neither required nor accepted.

How candidates are chosen

Applications are read by committees of North Carolina educators, counselors, retired teachers and others, working in teams of three, each assigned to a congressional district. They score every file on a rubric, out of 102 points for Residential candidates and 93 for Online. The criteria are academic rigour, community involvement, critical reading and writing, enthusiasm for STEM, need for NCSSM and enthusiasm for learning, work ethic and initiative, and, for Residential applicants, the math assessment.

What awaits graduates

Residential graduates who complete their requirements with a weighted GPA of at least 3.5 are guaranteed admission to any UNC System university to which they apply, and can apply for a grant covering in-state tuition at a UNC institution. Graduates come from all thirteen of the state's congressional districts.
