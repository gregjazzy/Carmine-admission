Financial aid at Milton

At Milton Academy, every dollar of aid is need-based. There are no merit scholarships, and no on-campus work is required of students who receive aid. The school aims to meet 100% of the demonstrated need of each enrolled student, and 37% of Upper School students receive aid, with an average grant of 77% of tuition.

Does applying for aid hurt the application?

Milton reads applications in two phases. The first is need-blind: admission officers evaluate each candidate without regard to ability to pay. Only the final phase is need-aware, when the committee weighs its decisions against the funds available. A family that needs aid should therefore apply for it.

How need is calculated

Need is the difference between the cost of attendance, tuition and books, and what the family can pay. The assessment considers income and expenses, assets and liabilities, family size, cost of living and the number of children in tuition-charging schools. Income is only one factor; Milton wants families across a broad economic range, including those who need only some help.

  • Parents seeking aid are expected to be employed, unless they care for a child under five, provide live-in care for an elderly dependant, or have a prohibitive disability.
  • When parents are separated or divorced, both complete the application, and the income and assets of both, and of any new spouses, are considered.
  • Where a family truly cannot contribute, Milton provides complete funding.
  • For those who qualify, aid also covers some trips, music lessons and other costs outside tuition.

Admission first

Aid follows admission, and the first reading ignores ability to pay. I prepare Milton candidates for the interview and the file, so that the decision is earned on the student.

The application

New families apply through Clarity by 31 January. The form takes under thirty minutes, can be completed on a phone, and costs $65 to submit. Families who meet the deadline are considered before those who apply late; a family that cannot meet it should call the financial aid office. A preliminary aid assessment arrives with the admission decision on 10 March.

International families

Aid is open to students who are neither US citizens nor permanent residents. In addition to the application, they submit the tax documents for the last two years, translated into English where needed, a letter from the employer setting out the terms of employment, a certificate from each bank stating all balances, and statements for November and December.

After the first year

Families reapply each year, by 15 December. If nothing has changed significantly, the award should be comparable. Families who enrol without aid can apply later only by showing a significant change in their situation, with a letter to the Director of Financial Aid. Aid is not reviewed until the current year's tuition and fees are fully paid.
