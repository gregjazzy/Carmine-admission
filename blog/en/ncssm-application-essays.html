The NCSSM essays

NCSSM does not accept SAT or ACT scores, which leaves the essays with an unusual amount of weight. They sit inside the single CFNC application, which covers the Residential program, NCSSM Online and Summer Ventures. There are two sets: one for Residential and Online applicants, one for Summer Ventures. A student applying to several programs completes the relevant sets in the same application.

What the essays are scored on

The selection committees score applications on a published rubric. Three of its criteria rest heavily on what the student writes:

  • Critical reading and writing ability: a thoughtful engagement with ideas and the construction of one's own, rather than reproducing information. The essays are where it shows.
  • Enthusiasm for STEM: genuine curiosity about how the world works and how to improve it, shown through the application, the essays and the record.
  • Need for NCSSM and enthusiasm for learning: whether NCSSM offers something essential that the current school cannot, and whether the student's curiosity has outgrown what is available.

Work ethic, initiative and community involvement also come through, in the examples a student chooses.

Essays that think

I work with NCSSM applicants on their essays: choosing real examples, building an argument, and writing in a voice that is clearly a fifteen year old's, not an adult's.

Showing enthusiasm without claiming it

Every applicant says they love science. The essays that score show it: a question the student could not let go of, an experiment that failed and what they changed, a book or video that sent them down a path, a problem they built something to solve. Specific detail, and what the student did next, is what separates curiosity from a statement of interest.

Writing about "need" without criticising a school

A common mistake is to explain need for NCSSM by listing what the current school lacks. Committees, many of them North Carolina educators, read that poorly. A better approach starts from the student: what they have already done with the resources available, where they have hit a ceiling, and what precisely, a research course, a mentorship, a group of peers, they would pursue at NCSSM.

Practical points

  • Preview the questions early and draft offline, then paste into CFNC.
  • Finish well before the winter break: the application closes on 5 January with no exceptions.
  • Keep the student's voice. A polished essay that sounds like a parent wrote it undermines the very criterion it is meant to show.
