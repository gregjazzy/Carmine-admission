CDNIS scholarships recognise and support students with strong academic results, leadership and a commitment to their community. There are two, and they work very differently: one is for students joining the Upper School, the other for students already at CDNIS.

The CDNIS Scholarship

No family applies for it directly. It is offered by invitation to new Upper School students who have shown a consistent record of high performance in their previous schools. Recipients must:

  • qualify for admission and meet every entrance requirement to the highest standard;
  • bring excellent academic records together with achievement in the arts, sport or community service;
  • to keep it, maintain an excellent academic record and exemplary conduct each year.

In practice, the route to the award runs through the admissions assessment and the file: an applicant who ranks Excellent and brings evidence beyond the classroom is the one the school can invite.

The Jockey Club Scholarship

Each year, the Hong Kong Jockey Club funds an award for three students at the end of Grade 10 who are returning to CDNIS for the IB Diploma years. It covers half of the annual tuition for each of Grades 11 and 12, which at 2026/27 rates means about HK$130,750 a year. Criteria are excellent academic ability, a leadership role in co-curricular activities, and involvement in service inside or outside school.

Merit awards, not a fee plan

Both are merit scholarships, limited in number and awarded on achievement and alignment with the school's values. They are not a substitute for a financial plan: families should budget for the full fees, including the capital levy or a debenture, the entry fee and the deposit.

Preparing a candidate the school can invite

For seven years, I have worked with around fifteen families a year aiming at very selective schools. For a CDNIS Upper School applicant, I prepare the assessment and help the family present achievements beyond grades clearly, which is what an invitation depends on.
