Applying to Eton from abroad

Eton takes boys from all over the world, through exactly the same process as boys from English prep schools. Nothing in the assessment is adapted for families abroad, and nothing is closed to them. What changes is everything around the assessment: the calendar has to be translated into another school system, the tests have to be sat somewhere, and the costs include lines that English families never see.

I work every year with families outside the UK who are aiming for Eton and the other leading English boarding schools. These are the points where they most often lose time.

Translating the school year

Eton's calendar is written in English school years, which run from 1 September to 31 August. A boy is in Year 5 during the school year in which he turns ten, and registration closes on 31 August of that year. A boy born between 1 September 2016 and 31 August 2017, for example, must be registered by 31 August 2027 and would join Eton in September 2030, whatever class he is in at home.

The trap is a school system that starts in January or groups children differently. A family that counts in local classes can believe it has a year in hand when the deadline has already passed. The only safe reference is the boy's date of birth.

Eton, from anywhere

Families abroad face the same assessment as English prep-school boys, with less of the context. I prepare candidates for each stage at the level Eton expects, and handle the calendar so that distance never costs a boy his chance.

Sitting the Pre-Tests outside the UK

The ISEB Pre-Tests can be sat anywhere in the world. Usually the boy's own school hosts them by registering as an invigilation centre, and international schools often do; otherwise an approved centre such as the British Council, or a centre found through VICTVS, can be used. It is the parents' responsibility to arrange this, between June of Year 5 and the testing window in the autumn of Year 6.

The tests are in English, and they include a paper in reading comprehension and grammar, standardised against children applying to English independent schools. A boy who speaks English fluently but has never been taught in it can find this paper harder than his conversation suggests. His school will also be asked for a report on his strengths, interests and character by 31 October, and a school unfamiliar with English admissions needs to know what is being asked of it.

Stage 2 and after: being there

Boys who pass Stage 1 are invited to Eton itself for Stage 2, in the spring of Year 6, for an individual interview and an online test. In Year 7, families with a conditional place meet several House Masters to choose a house. A boy on the waiting list returns in the summer of Year 7. Each of these steps is a journey, and the dates are given by the school, not chosen by the family.

Once at Eton, boys go home every two or three weeks, for a Short Leave weekend or a Long Leave at half term. For a family on another continent, those weekends need a plan from the first Half.

The costs specific to overseas families

The fee is the same for everyone: £21,891 per Half in 2026/27, including VAT. Two lines are added for families abroad. If at least one parent normally lives overseas, the family pays an additional deposit of one full Half's fees on accepting the place, credited on the final bill and refunded in full if the boy withdraws with a full term's notice before his year of entry. And if Eton sponsors the boy's UK student visa, there is a charge of £3,000 a year, paid termly, to cover the cost of that sponsorship.

At sixteen

Boys at schools overseas can also apply for one of the twenty or so fee-paying Sixth Form places, with the overseas equivalent of six GCSEs at grade 7 as the minimum. The application is made in October, the tests are held at Eton in November, and the pastoral interview for shortlisted boys takes place online. There is no financial support on this route.

How I prepare families abroad

I have students at Eton and for seven years have worked with around fifteen families a year, many of them outside the UK, aiming for the most selective schools in England, Switzerland and the United States.
