Columbus Academy describes itself plainly as an expensive place, spending almost twice as much per student as nearby public schools. It also runs what it presents as the largest tuition assistance budget among independent schools in Central Ohio, and more than a quarter of its students receive need-based aid. For many families, the real price is the net figure after assistance, which they learn on the same day as the admission decision.

I go through these numbers with families considering the school. Here they are for 2026-27.

Tuition covers most instructional costs, lunches, most materials and routine diagnostic testing. Textbooks, special trips and project materials are billed separately, and families outside the district pay $1,455 for transportation. Explorers and PreK have their own rates.

How assistance works

Families apply through Clarity by February 1, 2027, the same day as the admission deadline. Awards are based on what families report about assets, income, expenses and debts, and Clarity waives its fee automatically below an income threshold. Separated or divorced parents both complete the process, and awards are made per student, not per household. Aid covers tuition only, with occasional partial help for larger costs such as the eighth-grade trip to Washington. It must be renewed every year; with a similar financial situation, families can expect a similar award.

The admissions committee has no access to the financial application, and both decisions are communicated together on March 1. Assistance is not available for the Explorers program.

Payment plans

Tuition is paid by automatic bank transfer, in full in August, in two parts (60 percent in August and 40 percent in January, for a $100 fee), or monthly through May for a $200 fee; families with need-based aid pay a reduced plan fee. Tuition refund insurance is optional at 1.37 percent of the tuition obligation.
