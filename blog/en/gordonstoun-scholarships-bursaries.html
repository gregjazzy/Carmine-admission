Gordonstoun scholarships and bursaries

At Gordonstoun, a scholarship and a bursary are two separate things that are designed to meet. The scholarship recognises ability, potential and drive, and carries no automatic fee reduction. The bursary is financial help, means-tested and never repaid. The link between them is explicit: bursary funds go primarily to scholars. For many families, then, the scholarship weekend is where both questions are settled.

Entering the Prep, Year 7 or 8

Prep scholarships are awarded in three categories: Academic, All-Round and Music. They are assessed during the Prep School Scholarship and Taster Days, held in early October of the year before entry; in 2026, on 2 and 3 October, for entry in September 2027. A Prep scholarship can run until the end of Year 13, with reviews at the move into Year 9 and into Year 12.

Entering the Senior School, Year 9

The Year 9 Scholarship and Taster Weekend takes place at the end of August, a full year before entry: 28 to 30 August 2026 for September 2027. Six categories are offered: Academic, Adventure, Art, Dance and Drama, Music, and Sport. The Adventure scholarship is the one families rarely find elsewhere, and it fits a school where every pupil sails and goes on expeditions. A Year 9 award lasts until the end of Year 13, with a review at the start of Year 12.

Arriving at the weekend ready

I prepare scholarship candidates for the assessments of the weekend in their chosen field, and for speaking about it, so that the child's real level is what the assessors see.

Entering the Sixth Form, Year 12

The Year 12 Scholarship Weekend falls in late November of Year 11, from 26 to 28 November in 2026. Its categories are Academic, Music, Sport, Art, Drama and Dance, and Product Design. A Year 12 scholarship covers both Sixth Form years.

All assessments take place in person at Gordonstoun. Registration for each event closes before it takes place; once it has closed, families can ask the admissions office to be placed on a reserve list.

What a scholar receives

  • Free one-to-one music lessons, for music scholars.
  • Coaching or mentoring in the chosen field.
  • Access to national leadership events and development opportunities.
  • Priority consideration for a bursary.

The award holds as long as the scholar stays in good academic and behavioural standing.

Bursaries

Bursaries can cover up to 100% of the fees, though most are between 10% and 25%. The largest go to Sixth Form pupils, so that support reaches as many families as possible. More than a third of Gordonstoun pupils receive some form of fee assistance. Families connected to the Armed Forces or the FCDO receive a separate 10% remission automatically.

Preparing a scholar

I have pupils at Gordonstoun, and for seven years I have taken on around fifteen families a year aiming at the most selective schools. A scholarship candidate at Gordonstoun needs two things: a level clearly above the year group in the chosen field, and the ease to show it over a weekend among other strong candidates. I work on both, starting well before the weekend.
