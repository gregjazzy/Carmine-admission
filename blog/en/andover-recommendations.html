Andover asks for four recommendations and allows a fifth. They all go through Gateway to Prep Schools, and the rules about who may write them are stricter than families expect. Most of the difficulty is not in the rules themselves but in the situations they create: a teacher who arrived in September, a teacher who does not write English, a child who has changed schools twice.

Who writes what

RecommendationStatusThe rule to know
Principal or counselorRequiredSent through Gateway, like all the others
English teacherRequiredMust be the current teacher, not a former one
Math teacherRequiredMust be the current teacher, not a former one
Personal recommendationRequiredAny adult who knows the child well; may be a former teacher in any subject
Special interestOptionalFor a talent the others cannot describe

An optional arts or athletics supplement can also be added on Gateway. Any recommender may write in their own language: an official translation helps, but is not required.

Recommenders chosen, and well briefed

I help families decide who should write, how to ask, and when, so that each recommendation adds something the rest of the file cannot say. It is part of building a file of excellence for the most selective schools.

Book a conversation

The English teacher arrived in September

Because the English and math recommendations must come from current teachers, a child who has a new teacher this year will be described by someone who has known them for a term at most. There is no way round the rule, so the answer is timing and information. Ask in the autumn, late enough for the teacher to have seen real work and early enough to leave time to write, and give them, discreetly, a sense of what the child has done before: last year's comments, a project they are proud of. And use the personal recommendation for the former teacher who knew the child best.

The teacher does not write English

This is common for children at schools abroad, and Andover has removed the obstacle: the recommendation can be written in the teacher's native language. A translation is helpful, not required. The real risk is elsewhere. A teacher who has never written for an American school tends to write a few lines in the register of a report card. It is worth explaining, when asking, what a recommendation is for and what kind of detail helps: a moment in class, a piece of work, how the child compares with others the teacher has taught.

Choosing the personal recommender

This is the only required recommendation the family chooses freely, and it is often wasted on someone important who barely knows the child. The best personal recommender has seen the child commit to something over time: a former teacher, the director of a youth orchestra, a coach, the adult who runs a project the child cares about. What they write should describe the child in a setting the school letters do not reach.

When the special interest letter helps

The optional special interest recommendation is useful when the child has a real talent that no other writer can describe with authority: an instrument at a high level, a sport, a long-running scientific project. It adds nothing if it repeats the teachers. One rule of thumb: if the family cannot say in one sentence what this letter will add, it should not be requested.

The child who changed schools

Andover needs grades for the first term of the current year and the two years before. A child who has attended more than two schools in the past three years can send the additional reports through an optional form provided for that purpose. Families in this situation should gather the documents early: a school the child left two years ago may be slow to respond.

How I organise the recommendations

I have students at Andover, and for seven years I have worked with around fifteen families a year aiming at the most selective schools in the United States, England and Switzerland. With each family I draw up the list of recommenders in the autumn, decide what each should be able to say, and help prepare the request to each one. Then I read the rest of the file so that the essay, the graded paper and the video do not repeat what the recommenders will already say.
