Harvard-Westlake tuition and aid

St. Saviour's Chapel, Harvard-Westlake, Los Angeles. Photo Keithready, Wikimedia Commons, public domain

US schools November 2026 5 min read

Harvard-Westlake is a day school, so its costs are simpler than a boarding school's: tuition, a one-time fee for new students, and the everyday expenses of a school day in Los Angeles. Its financial aid, by contrast, goes further than most families expect.

The costs for 2026-27

ItemAmount
Tuition$54,900
New student fee, once$2,500
Bus service, optional$3,800 to $4,000, with a 50% fee credit for upper school students
Other estimated costs: books, meals, activitiesAbout $2,500 to $3,500, varying by student

A first year without the bus therefore costs roughly $60,000 to $61,000 in total, and later years a little less.

Who receives aid

About 21% of students, 340 in 2026-27, receive need-based financial aid, for a total of $16.8 million. On average, grants cover 71% of tuition, and the average family pays $16,000. Every grant is based on demonstrated need and tailored to the family's circumstances; there are no merit awards. The programme has grown steadily: in 1995-96, 13% of students received aid.

Admission first

Aid follows admission. I prepare Harvard-Westlake applicants for the questionnaire, the interview and the ISEE, so that the place is earned on the student.

What a grant includes beyond tuition

  • New student orientation and class retreats.
  • A $1,500 draw account for the cafeteria and school store, and a $1,500 computer stipend.
  • Books and course materials, required athletic uniforms and equipment, club equipment and the yearbook.
  • Help with SAT and ACT preparation, reduced school transport, and access to leadership, study-travel and summer programmes, and required trips.

For an aided family, this means the true cost of attendance is close to the family contribution, without a stream of extras.

Wondering whether your family would qualify? Let's talk about it.

Apply at admission, or not at all

The financial aid application is due with the admission application, on 15 January, and the admission application fee is waived for families applying for aid. Decisions on aid come with the admission decision on 12 March. Students who do not apply for aid at the time of admission are not eligible later; a small fund exists only for families hit by unforeseen financial reversals.

My view

For seven years, I have worked with around fifteen families a year aiming at very selective schools. At Harvard-Westlake, a family that might need help should apply for aid from the start: the window does not reopen. The admission itself depends on the student, and that is where I put the work.
