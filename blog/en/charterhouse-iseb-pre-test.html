The ISEB Pre-Test for Charterhouse

Every candidate registered for 13+ entry at Charterhouse through the Year 6 or Year 7 route sits the ISEB Common Pre-Test. The test does not make the admission on its own. It decides who is invited to the Interview and Activities Assessment at Charterhouse, and so it closes the door for some children before the school has met them.

The test in four lines

  • Format: a series of online tests, age-standardised, so a younger child in the year group is not penalised for being younger.
  • Subjects: mathematics, English, verbal reasoning and non-verbal reasoning.
  • When: the autumn term of Year 6 for UK candidates; the autumn of Year 7 for international candidates, sat at their own school, with results shared by December.
  • Read with: a reference from the child's current school.

The reference matters as much as families think it does not

Charterhouse reviews the Pre-Test results together with the school's reference. A good score with a lukewarm reference is weaker than families expect, and a slightly lower score with a reference describing a curious, hard-working, kind child can still lead to an invitation. It is worth making sure the current school knows the child well and knows what Charterhouse values: ambition, independence, moral courage, kindness and a willingness to try everything.

Where good pupils lose marks

Reasoning. Verbal and non-verbal reasoning are not taught as subjects in most schools, and almost nowhere outside England. A strong pupil who has never seen a series of shapes to complete or a word analogy can lose marks that have nothing to do with ability.

Adaptive questions. The tests adjust in difficulty as the child answers, so questions get harder quickly for a strong candidate. A child not warned of this can lose confidence half-way through.

English for non-native speakers. English and verbal reasoning together weigh heavily. That is exactly why Charterhouse recommends the Year 7 route to international families whose first language is not English.

What comes next

Candidates selected after the Pre-Test are invited to Charterhouse in the spring term for group activities, interviews and a piece of creative writing. Preparation for that day should begin as soon as the Pre-Test is over, not after the invitation arrives.
