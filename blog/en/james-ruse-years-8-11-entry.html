Selective schools in Australia

Entering James Ruse in Years 8 to 11

After Year 7, James Ruse still takes students, but only into places left by departures, and through a process unlike any other NSW selective school. The Department's general Years 8 to 11 form is not used. Instead, the school runs its own three stages: an Edutest examination, an application by invitation, and an interview. There is no entry in Year 12.

I prepare students for this route, which suits families who arrive in Sydney after Year 6 or whose child has grown into a selective profile later. Here is how it works.

Year 8 has changed

From 2028, there is no direct application for Year 8 at any NSW selective high school; vacancies are filled from the Department's reserve list. Demand explains why: in 2026, more than 1,100 applications were submitted across the state for fewer than ten Year 8 places. From 2029, only students who sat the Year 7 placement test, are still on a reserve list after the reserve decision date and attend a comprehensive public high school can be offered a Year 8 place. Students already in Year 7 at another selective school cannot move across. The three-stage route described below therefore concerns mainly Years 9 to 11.

Our experience with James Ruse

We have a track record of admissions to James Ruse, and we put that experience at your family's disposal: a student prepared for Edutest, a file that shows a balanced and resilient learner, and an interview approached with confidence.

For the 2027 intake, registration with Edutest ran from June 17 to July 24, 2026, and the exam was held on Saturday August 15 at James Ruse, from 9 a.m. to about 12:30, at a cost of $180. Calculators are not allowed. One exam serves every participating school the family registers for, and the first school registered becomes the test venue. Students overseas or interstate can arrange remote testing with Edutest at least three weeks before, at extra cost. Results are reviewed, and families learn within about four weeks whether their child is invited to Stage 2.

Stage 2: the invited application

Invited families submit the school's application form with supporting documents: school reports, of which all results and comments are read with emphasis on English, mathematics and science; an additional information form; results from external academic competitions or tests, including the most recent NAPLAN; and evidence that the student is balanced and resilient enough to thrive in a selective school, such as extracurricular activities, sport, leadership roles and service. A panel of the school's educators decides who is interviewed.

That last criterion is worth reading carefully. James Ruse is not only looking for the strongest test result; it is looking for students who will cope with the pace and the peer group of a highly selective school.

Stage 3: the interview

Shortlisted students meet the principal or senior staff, and those who succeed at interview are offered a place. Students who join are full-time, take at least 12 units in Year 11 and 10 in Year 12, and cannot always enter accelerated courses already under way.
