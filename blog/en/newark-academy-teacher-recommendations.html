Grades and test scores say what a student has achieved. Newark Academy's teacher recommendations are meant to show something else: how the student collaborates, thinks and contributes as a classmate and community member. The school describes them as a partnership with the applicant's current teachers.

What teachers can show

A teacher sees what no form captures: how a student behaves in a discussion, works in a group, responds to a setback or a hard problem, and treats classmates. Newark Academy looks for curious, motivated, passionate and collaborative students; recommendations are where that description is either confirmed or not.

A file that hangs together

I help families organise the recommendations and any extra letter so that each adds something new, alongside the test and the conversation.

Book a conversation

Read with the transcript and the test

Recommendations are not read alone. Newark Academy uses them, together with the transcript and the SSAT or ISEE, to judge academic readiness and fit. The prior year's transcript is due by 15 December, with a release form the family downloads, and current year grades join the file by 11 January. A recommendation that describes real engagement gives life to the numbers beside it; one that contradicts them raises questions the committee will notice.

Asking well

  • Ask in the autumn, not in December, so teachers have time before the holidays.
  • Choose current teachers who know the student's work this year.
  • Give context: a short note on the student's interests and why Newark Academy helps a teacher write specifically.
  • Follow up gently in early January to confirm the recommendations have been sent.

Unsure which teachers to ask? Let's think it through.

When an extra letter helps

A supplementary letter is useful when someone outside the classroom knows a side of the student the file would otherwise miss: a coach who has seen leadership on a team, a music teacher, a mentor in a research or community project. It should add information, not repeat what the teachers say.
