The Milton interview

Book a conversation

No application to Milton Academy is complete without an interview, and the interview must take place by 15 January. It is a two-part personal interview with a member of the admission team, and the candidate's parents or guardians take part in the visit. Families choose between two formats.

The campus visit adds something a screen cannot: time with a current student who shows the school as it is, and a chance for the candidate to arrive at the interview with something real to say about it.

Booking

To book, the family first fills in the request for information form, receives an activation link for the admission portal, and schedules the interview there. Appointments fill up quickly. A family that requests information in September has the widest choice; one that waits for December may find only slots in the last days before the deadline.

A conversation, well prepared

I prepare Milton candidates for the interview through real conversations: about their reading, their work, what they do outside class and why Milton, on campus or on screen.

What helps the candidate

  • Something to talk about. A book, a subject, a project, an activity they care about, described in detail. Milton values students for whom being interested in school is a pleasure; the interview is where that shows.
  • Knowledge of the school. Why Milton in particular: its closeness to Boston, its houses, a programme, a conversation with a student guide.
  • Ease with an adult. A few practice conversations with someone new make a visible difference, especially for quieter candidates.

The parents' place

Parents take part in the visit and the interview process. Their role is to support, not to speak for the candidate. Admission officers want to hear the student; answers prompted or completed by a parent weaken what the child has said.

On Zoom

For a virtual interview, the practical details matter: a quiet room, a stable connection, the camera at eye level, and a candidate who has practised looking at the lens. Families abroad should check the time difference carefully when booking.

How I prepare candidates

I have spent seven years preparing the children of around fifteen families a year for interviews at very selective schools. For Milton, the work is conversation, not rehearsal: finding the subjects that bring the candidate to life and helping them talk about them with ease, whatever the format.
