Choate essays and recommendations

US boarding schools November 2026 7 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

Book a conversation

A Choate file is a set of voices. The child writes, the parents write, the counselor writes, two teachers write, and up to two more people may write if the family chooses. Each of them sees the child from a different place. When the file works, each voice adds something; when it does not, five people say "hard-working and kind" in five different ways.

Because Choate requires no test and sets no minimum grade, these pages carry more weight here than at schools where a score anchors the reading. I have students at Choate, and the written file is where I spend the most time with families.

The child's essays

The applicant essays sit in Part 2 of the Gateway application. They are the only place in the file where the child speaks in writing, in their own words, and the reader expects a thirteen- or fourteen-year-old's words, not an adult's. An essay that has been polished by a parent or a tutor is recognisable at once: the vocabulary is too even, the structure too neat, the conclusion too wise.

The essays that work start small. A moment, a question, an argument the child lost, a thing they made that did not work the first time. From there the child says what they thought and why, and the reader learns something no transcript could show.

The Parent/Guardian Statement

The other half of Part 2 is written by the parents, and it is the hardest page in the file for many of them. The natural reflex is to list achievements, which the rest of the file already covers. What the reader cannot find anywhere else is the child seen from home: how they behave when something goes wrong, what they do with a free afternoon, what has changed in them over the last two years. A statement that is honest about a difficulty and what the child did with it is worth more than a page of praise.

A written file that sounds like the child

I work on the essays with the child, without ever writing them, review the parent statement with the family, and help choose recommenders who will add something. The result is a file of excellence in which each voice has its own part to play.

The counselor and the transcript

In Part 3, a school official completes the Principal/Counselor Recommendation and the Transcript Report. The transcript covers current grades and the last two years. Students already in high school also send their school's profile, if one exists, so the reader can place the grades. If the current term's grades are not yet available, grades in progress are accepted, but an indication of this year's performance must reach Choate by February 1.

The English and math teachers

Choate asks for recommendations from the child's current English and mathematics teachers. These two letters are the closest thing in the file to an academic assessment, and the family does not choose the subjects, only, sometimes, the moment. A teacher who has known the child for six weeks writes less than one who has known them for a year; if the current teachers are new, it is worth knowing early. And if a recommendation from a current teacher is simply not possible, the family writes to the admission office, which is what Choate asks.

Do your child's teachers know them well enough to write something specific? Tell me about the situation.

The two optional recommendations

A third teacher recommendation, from a current or previous teacher, and a personal recommendation from an adult who knows the child well and can speak to their character, are both optional. They help only when they add something: a music teacher who has seen two years of work, a coach, the adult who runs a project the child has carried. A third academic letter that repeats the first two adds weight without adding information.

Additional personal recommendations go directly from the writer to the Choate admission office. Supplementary material, such as a portfolio, is uploaded to the Choate portal, and Gateway allows multimedia links in the activities section.

Teachers at a school abroad

A teacher who has never written for an American boarding school tends to write too little, in the register of a school report. It is perfectly fine to explain, when asking, what a recommendation is for and what kind of detail helps: a moment in class, a piece of work, a comparison with other students they have taught. That conversation should happen in the fall, not in the week before the deadline.

My work on the written file

For seven years I have worked with around fifteen families a year aiming at the most selective boarding schools in the United States, England and Switzerland. On the written file, I work with the child on finding the subject of each essay, then on successive drafts that stay in their voice. I read the parent statement with the family, and I help decide who should recommend the child and how to ask. Everything is checked against what the rest of the file already says, so that each piece brings something new.

The essays take several drafts and the recommendations take time. Write to me in the fall, not in January.
