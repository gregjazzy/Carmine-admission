NMH need-based scholarships

Northfield Mount Hermon calls its financial aid need-based scholarships, and the word matters: there is nothing to repay. More than 40 percent of families receive one, the school has distributed $14.5 million, and it commits to meeting 100 percent of the demonstrated need of every admitted student. That commitment covers international students as well as American ones.

I work through the aid question with many of the families I accompany to NMH. Here is how it works in practice.

What the averages say

The average award in 2026-27 is $57,472 for a boarding student and $35,802 for a day student. Set against the tuition, that is about 72 percent of the boarding rate and 69 percent of the day rate. These are not token grants. An average aided boarding family pays about $21,900 in tuition rather than $79,382, before fees and other costs.

An average says nothing about a given family, since each award follows that family's demonstrated need. The only way to know a family's figure is to apply.

How to apply

All families, new and returning, apply through Clarity. Filing costs $65, and a waiver can be requested within the Clarity application itself; a family that is refused one but believes it qualifies can contact NMH's aid office directly. The deadline for fall 2027 is January 15, 2027, the same day as the admission deadline.

Two rules catch families out. First, NMH reviews an aid application only once the admission application is complete, so a missing recommendation can hold up the aid file too. Second, every parent or guardian must provide income and tax documents, including where the parents live in separate households. An award cannot be made without information from all of them, and gathering it from a parent who is not involved in the application can take weeks.

Unsure whether your family would qualify? Tell me about your situation: the time to decide is the autumn, not January.

When the answer comes

Admission decisions are released on March 10, 2027, and aid awards follow within twenty-four hours. A family therefore knows both the decision and the net cost at almost the same moment, and can compare schools on what it will actually pay.

International families

Because NMH's commitment to meeting full need extends to international students, families outside the United States should not rule aid out. The practical work is in the documents: foreign tax returns or income statements, often in another language and another fiscal year, have to be gathered and submitted through Clarity on the same January 15 deadline.

Late applications

For the current school year, NMH still accepts applications on a rolling basis as space allows, with limited financial aid, including for scholar-athletes. The lesson is simple: aid is at its fullest for families who apply by January 15. A late application can still succeed, but with fewer resources behind it.
