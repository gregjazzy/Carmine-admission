From sixth grade, the student becomes the main actor in a Columbus Academy application. They write a supplement, spend a shadow day on campus, have a short interview with the admissions team and sit the ISEE, while their parents have their own conversation with the school and their teachers send recommendations.

The pieces the student controls

The supplement is the school's first view of the student in their own words, and it must be done before the shadow day can be booked, so it cannot be left until January. A supplement that says something specific, about a subject, a project or a question the student cares about, gives the interviewer something to start from.

The shadow day is both a visit and an assessment. The student attends classes with current students and has a short interview with a member of the admissions team. Students who ask questions, take part when invited and can talk about their own interests in a few sentences leave a clear impression.

Ninth grade and scholarships

For new ninth graders, the first-round file also serves as the scholarship file: the Upper School committee selects merit finalists from all first-round ninth-grade applicants it intends to admit, based on test scores, transcripts and recommendations. Applying by February 1 is therefore essential for a strong ninth-grade candidate.
