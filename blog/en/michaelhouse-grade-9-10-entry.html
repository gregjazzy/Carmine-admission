Joining Michaelhouse in Grade 9 or 10

Michaelhouse is built around a five-year journey that starts in E Block, Grade 8. Most boys arrive then, and the scholarships, the open days and the weekend events are all designed for that intake. A later door does exist, into D Block (Grade 9) or C Block (Grade 10), but it works differently: it opens only when a place becomes available.

The rules, briefly

  • No guaranteed intake. A boy is admitted to Grade 9 or 10 only if a place has become free in his year.
  • The same four criteria. Previous academic performance, a personal interview with the Rector, the most recent school report and a confidential report from his current Head.
  • No scholarships. Scholarships and exhibitions are reserved for E Block entry.
  • Possible financial help. Discretionary awards and financial assistance can, in some cases, be made to boys entering Grades 9 or 10, if space and funding allow.
  • The same fees. Board and tuition cost R417,000 for 2026 whatever the year, with the acceptance fee on taking up the place.

Situation one: the family missed the Grade 8 deadline

Registrations for a Grade 8 intake close on 31 January of Grade 7, and later applications go onto a waiting list. A family in this position should keep the Grade 8 application alive on that list and, at the same time, ask about Grade 9. The boy's file, especially his Grade 7 and Grade 8 reports, will be read for either.

Situation two: the family is relocating

A move to South Africa, or within it, often falls in the middle of a school career. Because entry depends on a place being free, the earlier the admissions office knows, the better. A boy coming from another system should bring reports that make his level legible, and be ready to talk about the change at interview.

Situation three: he was not ready at 12

Some boys, and some families, are not ready for full boarding at the start of high school, and become ready a year or two later. Michaelhouse can still be a destination, but the boy must show the Rector that the decision is now his, and that he can join boys who have lived together in their House for a year or more.

Arriving mid-journey

Within each of the ten Houses, the Cop and Cack system pairs younger boys with older mentors, and the resident staff know each boy personally. A later arrival benefits from both, but still has to find his place in a year group that started together. Boys who throw themselves into a sport or a cultural programme from the first week usually settle fastest.
