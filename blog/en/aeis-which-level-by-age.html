Families moving to Singapore usually start from their child's current class abroad. The AEIS starts somewhere else: from the age the child reaches in the year they would join a Singapore school. The ministry's online checker asks two questions, date of birth and intended year of entry, and returns the tests the child can sit and the levels they could be placed at.

I help families read this checker and choose the level to sit. Here is what it shows.

Primary and secondary are not alike

At primary level, a child who does not reach the level applied for can be offered a lower one: a nine-year-old who sits Primary 3 may be placed in Primary 2. At secondary level, the outcome is the level sat or nothing. Where a teenager has a choice, between Secondary 1 and 2 at fourteen for example, the decision weighs a higher level against a safer one, and depends on how close the child is to Singapore's English and mathematics at each level.

Reading the checker correctly

For children who reach eleven or thirteen in the year of entry, and for any birth date close to a boundary, the checker gives the options directly and is worth running with the exact dates. A family that applies to the AEIS for a January start and one that waits for the S-AEIS a few months later use the same year of entry, but not the same range of levels: the S-AEIS stops at Primary 4 and Secondary 2.
