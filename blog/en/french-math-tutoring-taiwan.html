French Curriculum Math Tutoring in Taipei: Support for LFT Students

Private Tutoring January 2026 10 min read

Taipei is one of the most unusual expatriation postings for a French family. The community is tiny. The Lycee Francais de Taipei is a small school where everyone knows everyone. And when your child starts struggling in mathematics or physics-chemistry, the reality hits hard: there are absolutely zero local tutoring options for the French curriculum. None. Not a single French-speaking tutor specialized in the French program on the entire island. This is the most extreme situation in the AEFE network, and it is precisely the situation that online tutoring was designed to solve.

As an admissions consultant who has guided more than 1,600 students, including families in some of the most isolated postings across Asia, I understand the scale of this problem. Parents in Taipei often work in the semiconductor industry or technology sector -- brilliant, demanding professionals who understand perfectly well the importance of mathematics in their children's academic trajectory. But they are powerless against the void of local options. This void is exactly what we fill with our online tutors, specialized in the French program and available in the Taipei timezone.

The Lycee Francais de Taipei: a small school with big ambitions

The LFT is an institution on a deeply human scale, in the most literal sense. With student numbers far below those of the major French schools in Hong Kong, Shanghai, or Singapore, the LFT offers a family-like environment where every student is known and individually monitored. This is a genuine advantage in terms of pastoral care. But it is also a structural constraint.

With small cohorts, the LFT cannot offer the pedagogical diversity of a larger institution. Specialization options in Premiere and Terminale are more limited. Teachers, however competent, are few in number and cannot provide individual support beyond their class hours. And crucially, in such a tight-knit group, social dynamics take on disproportionate significance: a struggling student is immediately visible, which can generate additional psychological pressure that parents often underestimate.

LFT families are typically technology sector expatriates. Engineers at TSMC, executives in semiconductor companies, researchers. These are parents who understand the value of mathematics and science. They themselves excelled in these subjects. And this is precisely why watching their child struggle with French-curriculum math becomes a source of disproportionate anxiety. They know what is at stake.

Why French curriculum math is uniquely challenging in Taipei

Taiwan has an extraordinarily high-performing education system in mathematics. Taiwanese students consistently rank among the top performers in international olympiads and PISA mathematics assessments. But this excellence does not transfer to the French program. The two systems operate in entirely different paradigms.

The Taiwanese system emphasizes problem-solving, method memorization, and speed of execution. The French program demands formal proof, logical construction of reasoning, and the ability to justify every step of a demonstration. A student who excels in the Taiwanese system can find themselves completely lost when facing a French mathematics exercise that asks not merely for the answer, but for a proof of why that answer is correct. This fundamental difference renders any local tutor incapable of effectively supporting an LFT student.

Difficulties by grade level

At the college level, problems often emerge during transitions. A family arriving in Taipei with a child in Quatrieme discovers that the French mathematics program has requirements in algebra and geometry that the student may not have encountered in their previous schooling, especially if they come from an English-speaking or Asian system. Catching up is possible at this stage, but it requires targeted support that the LFT alone cannot always provide.

In Seconde, the program intensifies considerably. Reference functions, equations and inequalities, vectors, probability -- all of this forms a foundation that must be perfectly mastered to approach Premiere with confidence. For LFT students, the difficulty is amplified by the total absence of any supplementary support available locally.

In Premiere and Terminale mathematics specialization, the pace becomes unforgiving. Numerical sequences, limits, derivatives, integrals, counting methods, continuous probability -- the program is dense and each chapter builds on those that preceded it. A student who has accumulated gaps in college or Seconde enters a downward spiral that is extraordinarily difficult to break without qualified external help.

Physics-chemistry: an even more pronounced isolation

If finding a French-curriculum math tutor in Taipei is impossible, finding a physics-chemistry tutor is doubly so. The physics-chemistry specialization program in Premiere and Terminale combines mechanics, waves, thermodynamics, organic chemistry, and analytical chemistry in a demanding conceptual framework that relies heavily on mathematical competence. Taiwanese science tutors, trained in a completely different pedagogical tradition, cannot support a student through French-style physics demonstrations or energy balance problems as the curriculum requires them.

Online tutoring: the only credible option for LFT families

In Taipei, online tutoring is not one option among many. It is the only option. And this reality, far from being a handicap, has become an advantage. LFT families who work with our online tutors gain access to French-program specialists they could never find locally, even in much larger cities.

Our tutors are experienced professionals, trained in the French mathematics and physics-chemistry curriculum. They know the official frameworks, the baccalaureate expectations, the recurring pitfalls of every chapter. They are accustomed to working with AEFE students, which means they understand the specificities of French education abroad -- the small classes, the sometimes compressed curricula, the heterogeneous student profiles.

Taipei's timezone (GMT+8) is perfectly compatible with our scheduling. Sessions take place in late afternoon local time or on weekends, via video conferencing with interactive whiteboards. The pedagogical experience is rigorously equivalent to in-person instruction, with the additional advantage that every session can be recorded and reviewed as many times as needed.

The Carmine approach: tutoring anchored in post-bac strategy

What fundamentally distinguishes our support from a standard tutoring service is the integration of academic work into a strategic reflection on the student's future. When we work with an LFT student, we are not simply helping them understand derivatives or fluid mechanics. We are building an academic profile that will serve their post-baccalaureate ambitions.

LFT families often have high ambitions for their children. Scientific preparatory classes, EPFL, selective Anglo-Saxon universities, top engineering schools. Each of these destinations requires a specific level of achievement in mathematics and science. A student targeting EPFL must achieve grades above 16/20 in scientific specializations. A student aiming for MPSI preparatory class must demonstrate mastery beyond the official program. A student applying to American universities needs a transcript where hard sciences are an asset, not a weakness to explain away.

At Carmine, every hour of tutoring fits into this strategic logic. Our tutors communicate with our admissions guidance team to ensure that academic work directly serves the student's strategic objectives. This is a coherence you will not find in any standard tutoring agency.

Do not wait: the urgency of action in a context without local alternatives

In Taipei more than anywhere else, waiting is a fatal mistake. In a city where local options exist, a parent can afford to temporize, try a tutor, switch if it does not work. In Taipei, there is no local plan B. If your child begins to fall behind in mathematics or physics-chemistry, every week without support is a week of additional gaps accumulating.

The French mathematics program is relentless in its cumulative logic. A poorly understood concept in Seconde will make an entire chapter incomprehensible in Premiere. A missed chapter in Premiere will make Terminale insurmountable. The families who react at the first signs of difficulty -- a declining grade, a persistent misunderstanding, a loss of motivation -- are the ones whose children find their way back to excellence. The others endure a progressive deterioration that becomes increasingly difficult to fight against.
