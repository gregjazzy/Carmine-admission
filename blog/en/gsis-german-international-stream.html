German Swiss International School runs two schools under one roof. The German International Stream follows the core curricula of the German state of Thuringia, like the German schools across South-East and East Asia, and leads to the German International Abitur. For German-speaking families it is the natural choice; for others it is more open than many assume, at certain ages.

I work with families weighing the two streams. Here is how the German stream works.

Does a child need to speak German?

Entry pointGerman required?
Kindergarten DKG1-DKG2No; German language programmes are provided
Pre-primary DVOR and Primary K01-K03No; German language programmes are provided
Secondary K06No; German language programmes are provided
All other regular classesYes, a good command of German

The windows are precise. A non-German-speaking child can enter in Kindergarten, in the first primary years or in K06; at K04, K05 or from K07 onward, a good command of German is expected. Families relocating with a child who is learning German should aim for one of the open entry points rather than hope for an exception.

How admission works

For the German stream, a meeting with the Head of Department is mandatory for every applicant, and entrance assessments are required for certain year groups. Applications are accepted year-round; when a year group is full, the child joins the waitlist, and the application date affects their position. Admissions priority applies as in the English stream, with German, Swiss and Austrian passport holders in the first group. During the application process a family can still transfer its application between streams; once a child is enrolled, a change of stream is not supported except in exceptional cases.

School life in the GIS

Classes have at most 20 children in Kindergarten and 26 in Primary and Secondary, with an average of 14. English is the first foreign language from K01; Mandarin is added from K03, and French or Latin from K06. Kindergarten and DVOR are at the Pok Fu Lam campus; Primary from K01 and Secondary are at The Peak. The day starts at 7:40, ending at 12:30 in Kindergarten, 1:15 in Primary and 3:20 in Secondary, with afternoon activities available. There is no school uniform.

The German International Abitur

The stream leads to the German International Abitur, a bilingual diploma recognised by universities worldwide. Recent GIS graduates have gone on to Cambridge, King's College London, the University of Toronto, ETH Zurich, the University of Zurich, and universities in Berlin, Hamburg, Münster, Rotterdam and Maastricht, among others.
