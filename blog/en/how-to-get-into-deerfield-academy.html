How to get into Deerfield Academy

The Main School Building, Deerfield Academy, Massachusetts. Photo John Phelan, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 3.0

Book a conversation

Most schools leave families to guess what they value. Deerfield says it in one sentence, about one piece of its file: a graded essay that shows thoughtful teacher commentary and the student's effort to improve is more valuable than one that simply received a high grade. That sentence is the key to the whole application. Deerfield is not looking for the child who has already arrived; it is looking for the child who is visibly learning.

I have students at Deerfield. What follows is how its application is built, and how to read it in that light.

A school of 650, and a way of life

Deerfield, in western Massachusetts, considers 650 students its ideal size: large enough for a broad program, small enough that every student is known. The school describes itself through its habits as much as its courses. Family-style sit-down meals are served seven times a week, with tables reassigned every four weeks. There are no Saturday classes, but there is an academic dress code and a "phone-light" culture. Every student takes part in a cocurricular activity each term. And from fall 2027, with a new dorm, every student will board.

The application in five steps

  1. Inquiry and Candidate Profile. The Gateway application opens on July 1. The profile should go in well before January 15, with a $60 fee, or $125 for families living outside the United States. It unlocks the Deerfield Admission Portal, which has its own checklist beyond Gateway's.
  2. The interview. Required, one-on-one with an admission officer, on campus or by Zoom.
  3. The test. Required. For grades 9 and 10, the SSAT or ISEE; an English test on top for some students.
  4. Three pieces of writing. A short answer, a personal essay and a graded essay, in a section of the application that is unique to Deerfield.
  5. Grades and four recommendations. Current math, English and third academic teachers, plus the principal or counselor.

A Deerfield file of the highest standard

Interview, test, the three essays, the choice of recommenders: I prepare each part of a Deerfield application to the level of the most selective boarding schools, with the school's own priorities in mind.

Reading the file the way Deerfield reads it

Once the sentence about the graded essay is understood, every other piece makes sense. The three teacher recommendations, all from current teachers, describe a learner in class this year. The interview, one-on-one, shows how the child talks about what they are working on. The SSAT or ISEE is sent with its writing sample, a text produced under time pressure without help, which the school can set beside the essays. The file is designed to show the child as they really work, not at their most polished.

That has a practical consequence. Families who try to make every piece look perfect often weaken the file, because perfection hides exactly what Deerfield is trying to see. A graded paper with a B+ and a teacher's searching comments, followed by a revised version, can do more than an A with "excellent" in the margin.

What Deerfield does not want

Deerfield is unusually direct about supplemental material. Most applicants do not need to send any, additional materials do not necessarily strengthen an application, and duplicate or excessive submissions may not be reviewed. Performing arts portfolios go through SlideRoom, athletic videos through the portal's Media Links form. A family that sends everything is not showing commitment; it is ignoring the instructions.

Entry points and timing

Deerfield takes applications from students in grades 8 to 12, for entry to grades 9 to 12 or a postgraduate year, and admits for September only. A student may apply to repeat their current grade, which the school suggests discussing during the interview. The priority deadline is January 15; decisions come on March 10, and enrollment contracts are due on April 10.

How I prepare candidates for Deerfield

Every year I work with about fifteen families aiming at the most selective schools in the United States, England and Switzerland, and I have done so for seven years; several of my students are at Deerfield. For this school I start with the child's recent schoolwork, because the graded essay is chosen from it and because it shows how they respond to feedback. From there I prepare the test, the short answer and personal essay, and the interview, and help choose the three teachers. Throughout, the aim is the same as Deerfield's: a child who is seen learning.
