Winchester College: Founder's Scholarships, bursaries and awards

Winchester distinguishes clearly between recognising talent and paying for it. Most of its titles, Scholar, Exhibitioner, Music or Sport Award holder, carry honour, teaching and opportunities, but no automatic reduction in fees. Money comes from two other sources: a merit award with a fixed value, and a large means-tested bursary programme. Knowing which is which, and when each must be requested, avoids a costly misunderstanding.

Founder's Scholarships: 25 percent, on merit alone

Founder's Scholarships recognise academic excellence, intellectual curiosity, analytical rigour and flexibility of mind. They are open to new pupils joining in Year 9 and in Year 12, and in the Sixth Form to day pupils as well as boarders. Each carries a 25 percent reduction in fees and is awarded purely on merit, without any means test. At 13+, four are offered each year to the top performers in Election, and they are held in College.

The rule that matters most to families: a Founder's Scholarship and a means-tested bursary can be held together and add up. A boy with a 25 percent bursary and a Founder's Scholarship pays half the fees.

Means-tested bursaries: from 5 to 100 percent

Winchester spends more than £5 million a year on bursaries, supporting more than 140 pupils, and awards range from 5 to 100 percent of fees. They can also cover extras: the laptop, trips and extra-curricular activities. Targeted awards add to the general programme:

  • The Paddy Dear Bursary Award, a £10 million endowment created by an Old Wykehamist who was himself on a bursary, for 13+ and 16+ families who qualify for support of between 20 and 70 percent: those who can contribute but not pay the full cost.
  • The Wykeham Award, for Sixth Form entrants from UK state secondary schools.
  • The Keniston-Cooper Maths Bursary Award, for state school Sixth Form entrants planning Further Mathematics.
  • Specific awards for exceptional golfers and for pupils from Northern Ireland.

Talent first

Founder's Scholarships reward academic merit alone. We prepare candidates for Election and for the Sixth Form assessments where those awards are decided.

When to ask, and why timing is decisive

A bursary must be requested at registration, by ticking the box on the form, and Winchester advises doing so in the main round, by 1 October. For 13+ applicants, a family whose son passes the initial assessment in Year 6 is then invited to complete an online application with details of income, expenditure and circumstances, followed by a telephone meeting with Bursary Administration Ltd, the company Winchester uses to process applications. The decision arrives with the offer of a place, and the award is reviewed each year.

The College is firm on the timing. Support for a pupil already at the school is considered only in exceptional cases of hardship after a significant, unexpected change. For Election candidates applying only for a scholarship, the bursary request must be made before or at the same time as the Election application; otherwise Winchester assumes the family will not need support at any point.

Music and Sport Awards

Music Awards bring performance opportunities, expert teaching and structured lesson time; Sport Awards bring specialist coaching, strength and conditioning, mental-skills training and a dedicated sports tutor. Neither carries an automatic fee reduction, but individual tuition is free for music and sport award holders, and awards can be combined with a bursary. Assessments for 13+ entrants take place in Year 8.

Reading the numbers

For a family that does not need a bursary, the only reduction available is the Founder's Scholarship, and it goes to a handful of the very strongest candidates each year. For a family that does, the programme is large, more than £5 million a year, and asking costs nothing in the admission process. The mistake is to hesitate: the form must be ticked at registration.
