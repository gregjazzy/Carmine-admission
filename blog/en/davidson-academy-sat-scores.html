For most Davidson Academy applicants, eligibility starts with a test score, and the SAT is the most common one. From sixth grade to eighth grade, an SAT or ACT score is required; IQ results only supplement it. The thresholds are set for the student's age-based grade, without grade skips, and come from consultation with national academic talent search organizations.

I prepare young students for the SAT every year. The Davidson thresholds and how to read them follow.

A qualified applicant meets or exceeds at least two of the three scores for their grade. Students below sixth grade must meet the sixth-grade scores. Results must come from a test taken within the past year, and the full official score report must be submitted with the application; late reports are not considered.

Reading the table

The "two of three" rule gives some flexibility: a sixth grader with 680 in Math and 630 in Reading and Writing reaches 1310 and qualifies on all three counts, while one with 720 in Math and 600 in Reading and Writing reaches 1320 and qualifies on Math and total. But the thresholds rise quickly, and by tenth grade a perfect Math score and 780 in Reading and Writing are expected. The school adds that scores which only just meet the minimum may not lead to an assessment invitation, and that applicants who meet the bar in every area tend to do better in the assessment.

Testing young

An eleven-year-old sitting the SAT faces a test written for high school juniors. The mathematics goes beyond what most sixth graders have been taught, and the reading passages assume adult vocabulary and stamina. Davidson Institute and Northwestern University's Center for Talent Development offer remote, proctored practice PSAT 8/9, PreACT, SAT and ACT sittings whose scores are accepted; a student must skip one session before retesting. Scores from these sittings get no preference, and their dates may not align with Davidson deadlines.
