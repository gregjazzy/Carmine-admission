Conditional place or waiting list at Eton

The letter that follows Stage 2 does not end the story. It opens one of two quite different stretches of time. A boy with a conditional place has two years to keep it. A boy with a waiting-list place has about a year to win one. Families tend to relax in the first case and despair in the second, and both reactions cost something.

I follow boys at Eton and work with families at both points. What each outcome means in practice comes down to a few things.

A conditional place: what still has to happen

The condition is an examination in Year 8, at twelve or thirteen. A boy confirms his place by passing one of three: the King's Scholarship, Common Entrance or the Eton Entrance. Which one he sits is a real choice, best made with his current school, which knows how he performs in long written papers.

The King's Scholarship deserves a particular word. For a boy who already holds a conditional place, a satisfactory performance guarantees entry even if he does not win a scholarship. The risk is lower than it looks, and an able boy can aim high without putting his place in danger, provided he does not perform poorly, in which case he may still be asked to sit Common Entrance in full or in part.

From offer to first day

A conditional place is a promise that still has to be honoured in Year 8. I prepare boys for the confirming examination, whether King's Scholarship or Common Entrance, at a level that leaves nothing to chance.

Year 7: choosing a house

During the first two terms of Year 7, parents and boy meet several House Masters to find the right boarding house among the twenty-four Oppidan houses (King's Scholars live in College). Each house holds about fifty-five boys, around eleven per year, with its own House Master and Dame and its own character. This is where a boy will live, eat, make his closest friends and be looked after for five years, and the meetings are worth preparing as carefully as the interview was.

The acceptance fee, £3,840 for boys joining in 2026, is due by the end of March of Year 7; £500 of it is returned when the boy leaves. Families living overseas also pay an additional deposit of one Half's fees on accepting the place. Families with a bursary update their financial details in September of Year 8 so that the award can be reassessed.

Your son has just received a conditional place? Tell me about him and the school he is in: the choice of confirming exam is the next decision.

A waiting-list place: a second assessment

Boys on the waiting list come back to Eton in the summer term of Year 7. They have another interview and sit the online test again. Eton admits some of them every year, and the number changes from one year to the next.

The reassessment uses the same instruments as Stage 2, a year later. That is the useful fact. Whatever held a boy back the first time (a test where he ran out of time, an interview where he stayed on the surface) is what he works on during the intervening year. A year is a long time at eleven, and the boys who come back noticeably more mature, more articulate and more secure in their reasoning are the ones who give the school a reason to change its mind.

No place

A boy who receives no offer at Stage 2 has not lost every route to Eton: the King's Scholarship in Year 8 and the Sixth Form at sixteen remain open, both demanding. In the meantime, his ISEB Pre-Test results remain valid for the other schools on his list that use them, which is one reason a family's list should never have Eton alone on it.

How I support families after Stage 2

I have students at Eton and each year accompany around fifteen families towards the most selective schools. After Stage 2, my work depends on the outcome: preparation for the confirming examination for a boy with a conditional place, a precise plan for the year ahead for a boy on the waiting list, and for others a clear view of the routes still open and of the schools where he will succeed.
