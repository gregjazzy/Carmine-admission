Institut auf dem Rosenberg: classes of eight, tailored timetables and the IDP

Institut auf dem Rosenberg, St. Gallen. Photo Rene Nueesch, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 3.0

Boarding schools November 2026 5 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

Book a conversation

What sets Rosenberg apart from the other leading boarding schools is not its setting: it is the way it teaches. Eight students per class on average, one adult for every two students, a different timetable for each student, and learning organised by level rather than by age. For some children, it is exactly what they need. For others, it is too much freedom.

A path built for each student

At Rosenberg, timetables are 100% personalised: each student builds their own combination of subjects from close to 500 courses. A student who excels in maths can take a class well above their age; another can strengthen a weak subject without slowing down in the others. The school also runs daily drop-in and revision sessions in every subject, and a media centre staffed in the evenings for study support.

Making the most of a tailor-made school

I help you see whether Rosenberg's teaching truly suits your child, then prepare their admission: English, maths, personal statement, interview. My aim: that they join a school that will help them grow at their own pace.

The individual development plan

Each student has an individual development plan, built from their goals, strengths and ambitions, and followed by specialists and a mentor. Parents are part of it: they share their vision with the team, which tracks progress and adjusts. A dedicated app brings together grades, the calendar and communication with the family. It is very close monitoring, which reassures many parents.

Does your child do better with a tailor-made framework? Tell me about them: I will tell you whether Rosenberg suits them.

For which child?

This approach particularly suits a child with an uneven profile: well ahead in one subject, behind in another, or passionate about a field that standard programmes leave aside. It also suits a child who needs strong individual attention. It does, however, require maturity: such a free path assumes knowing what one wants, or being well guided to find out.
