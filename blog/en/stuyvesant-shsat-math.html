The SHSAT math section

Half of the SHSAT composite score comes from mathematics. For a student aiming at Stuyvesant, whose cutoff is the highest of the eight testing schools, the math section is where a strong student can build a real margin, and where an unprepared one can lose it.

What the section covers

AreaTypical skills
Ratios and proportional relationshipsRates, percentages, scale, unit conversions, proportional reasoning in word problems
Number system and expressionsOperations with fractions, negatives and exponents, simplifying and evaluating expressions
Equations and inequalitiesLinear equations and inequalities, setting up an equation from a situation
Statistics and probabilityMean, median, data interpretation, simple and compound probability
GeometryAngles, area, volume, the coordinate plane, properties of figures

The section has 50 questions, in multiple-choice and other formats, and shares the 180 minutes of the test with the ELA section; students choose which to start with.

What the adaptive format changes

From fall 2026, the SHSAT is computer-adaptive. The test starts at average difficulty; correct answers lead to harder questions, incorrect ones to easier ones, and the estimated score updates after every response. In the math section, students must answer each question before moving to the next, and cannot return to earlier questions. Every student still answers 50 questions on the same standards.

The practical consequence is a change of habit. The old strategy of skipping hard questions and coming back no longer works. A student has to decide on each question, in sequence, and keep their pace without the safety net of a second pass.

Where strong students lose points

  • Word problems read too fast: the arithmetic is right, the question answered is not the one asked.
  • Content not yet taught: some probability and geometry topics come late in middle school curricula.
  • Arithmetic slips under time pressure, especially with fractions and negatives.
  • Getting stuck: spending five minutes on one question, which the sequential format no longer forgives.

A preparation plan

  1. A diagnostic on the five content areas, to find gaps.
  2. Targeted teaching of missing content, with plenty of word problems.
  3. Timed sets on screen, answering in sequence without returning.
  4. Full practice tests on the city's SHSAT portal in the weeks before November.
