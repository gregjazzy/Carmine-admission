École des Roches: the admission interview, how to prepare

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

Book a conversation

At Les Roches, the interview does not come at the end. It opens the process: it is how the school discovers the family, the child and their plans, and where the programme they will be offered takes shape. Many families treat it as a first contact. It is already selection. I have students at Les Roches, and every year I support around fifteen families towards the most selective boarding schools and schools.

Who is seen, and why

After a first enquiry, a member of the admissions team arranges an interview with the family. It covers the child's academic profile, their goals, and the most suitable programme: collège, French baccalaureate, BFI, IB or the intensive French year. Then come the formal application, with recent reports, a language assessment and a motivation letter from the student, then the decision.

Preparing for Les Roches' interview

A family that knows what it has come for, a child who talks about themselves accurately: I prepare both, for Les Roches as for the other boarding schools in view.

What the school looks for

Les Roches is a 240-student boarding school in the countryside, with a strong communal life. The interview looks at whether the child will thrive there, and whether the family's plans fit what the school offers. The school publishes neither its questions nor what tips the balance. That is where preparation makes the difference.

Topics to prepare

Plans after lycée. France, the UK, North America: this points towards the bac, the BFI or the IB, and a family that has thought it through helps the school offer the right programme.

Boarding. Has the child lived away from home before? What do they expect from house life, sport, activities? A child who can picture it reassures.

The motivation letter. It must sound like the student. Interview and letter answer each other.

Is your child torn between programmes? Tell me about them: I will tell you which serves them best.

The costly mistakes

Arriving without a plan

A family that does not know what it expects from the school lets the school choose for it, and not always the most ambitious programme.

A child who just goes along

A teenager who does not want to board shows it. Better to have heard them before, and talked about it frankly.

A letter written by an adult

It shows, and it serves the student worse than a simple, sincere letter.

What I do for your family

I prepare families for this interview as a real selection step: the plan, the choice of programme, how the child talks about themselves and their motivation letter. I also take stock of their level in French, English and maths, so that the programme aimed for is realistic. For the full procedure, see École des Roches admission.
