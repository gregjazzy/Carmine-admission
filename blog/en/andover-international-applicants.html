Applying to Andover from abroad

Andover's international community is large: around 130 students who are not US citizens, from some 46 countries, plus about 80 American and dual citizens who live abroad. Together they make up roughly one student in six. The application is the same as for everyone else. For a family abroad, a few practical points change, and one that is unusual among leading schools: aid.

Five things that are different from abroad

  1. The fee. The application fee is $125 for international students, against $60 for others.
  2. English. A student at a non-English-medium school abroad takes the TOEFL iBT, IELTS or Duolingo, in addition to the required SSAT, ISEE, PSAT, SAT or ACT. Most admitted international students score at least 100, 7.5 or 135.
  3. The interview. It can be done virtually or as a recording, so no journey is required.
  4. Recommendations. A teacher who does not speak English may write in their own language; a translation helps but is not required.
  5. Aid. International families are welcome to apply, and admission remains need-blind.

Need-blind for international families too

This is the point that surprises families most. At some schools, aid for international students is limited or affects the decision. At Andover, international families apply for aid through the same Clarity application, admission does not take the request into account, and the school meets the full demonstrated need of the students it admits. The practical difference is in the documents: families outside the United States submit their own country's income documents, and must not upload US tax forms.

Distance changes nothing in the standard

Wherever the family lives, the preparation is the same, online and at the highest level: the English test to the level Andover's admitted students reach, the SSAT or ISEE, the graded paper, the essay, the video and the interview.

Grades from another system

Andover reads the first term of the current year and the two years before. A school abroad sends its own reports, in its own scale. Andover itself grades from 0 to 6 and does not rank its students, so its readers are used to scales that are not American; still, a short school profile explaining the grading, and a counselor who places the child in their class, help the reader enormously. A child who has attended more than two schools in three years can send the extra reports through a dedicated optional form.

The written pieces in a second language

The graded writing sample must be schoolwork the child has already handed in. For a child at a school that teaches in another language, that may mean a paper not written in English. It is a legitimate choice, but it needs thought: the reader has to be able to follow the argument. The essay and the video, on the other hand, are in English, and they will be read beside the English test score. A gap between them is noticed.

Meeting Andover from abroad

Admission officers travel. This autumn, Andover took part in a Ten Schools Admission Organization reception in Zurich, and holds regional "Conversation with Andover" events in cities around the world. Virtual tours run through the winter, and the Zoomin' With Shuman series answers questions about the application online.

After admission

Andover has an international student coordinator who advises international students and their families, issues the I-20 forms needed for the student visa, and runs a one-and-a-half-day orientation before the school year begins. The International Club meets every two weeks. Families abroad can pay school bills through Convera or Flywire.

What I do for families abroad

Several of my students are at Andover today, and a good share of the fifteen or so families I have worked with each year for seven years live outside the United States. For an international applicant, I begin by measuring the child's English against the levels Andover's admitted students reach, and their mathematics at Upper Level SSAT level. That sets the length of the preparation. Then I prepare both tests, choose the graded paper with the family, and work on the essay, the video and the interview in English.
