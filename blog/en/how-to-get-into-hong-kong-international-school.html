How to get into HKIS

Hong Kong International School is an American-curriculum, not-for-profit school serving students from Reception One, pre-kindergarten, to Grade 12, in four divisions. Its High School alone, on the Tai Tam campus, has more than 800 students. Admission follows a clear sequence, but the decisive feature is a priority system that shapes who is even invited to assessment.

The four steps

  1. Application on OpenApply, with a HK$2,000 non-refundable fee and the Family Questionnaire. The file counts as complete only when the fee, the questionnaire and every required document are in.
  2. Supporting documents, requested once the application has been reviewed, and varying by grade.
  3. Pre-entry assessment, for selected applicants only, invited according to priority, the number of applications and the expected places in each grade.
  4. Admissions Committee decision, communicated from March onwards, on a rolling basis.

Two deadlines

Division, 2027-2028Deadline
Primary School, Reception One to Grade 51 October 2026
Secondary School, Grades 6 to 121 November 2026

Applications with outstanding documents at the deadline are not considered. Later applications may be accepted case by case.

The priority system

Applicants fall into three groups. Debenture holders come first. Then come families already connected to HKIS, or arriving from abroad from American-curriculum or international schools. Last come applicants from schools in Hong Kong and Greater China, whose invitations to assess depend on volume and resources.

When no place is free, qualified applicants enter a wait pool organised by these priorities. A debenture places a qualified applicant at the top of the pool, but never guarantees admission.

What the committee weighs

HKIS assesses English proficiency, academic achievement or, for the youngest, developmental readiness, personal characteristics, fit with the school's Student Learning Results, and proven success in similar school environments. Report cards, teacher recommendations, assessments and personal statements give a holistic view.

Practical conditions

Every student needs a valid Hong Kong visa or right of abode before school starts, must live with at least one parent who is a full-time Hong Kong resident, and must have personal health insurance.
