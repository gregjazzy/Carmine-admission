Franklin: entering seconde, and what the lycée expects

Most students join Franklin in sixième. But the lycée also takes new students, and seconde (age 15) is a real door for a pupil coming from elsewhere, provided you understand how it works. It has no closing date. That is its main difficulty.

An open window that closes without warning

Lycée applications open on 15 September of the troisième year. No end date is announced: the school reviews applications and closes when its places are filled. A family that waits for first-term results, or for the spring, often turns up when nothing is left. The file is submitted online only, with its documents; it is checked, then reviewed by the director or the préfet (the senior staff member in charge of the year group), who offers an appointment or declines. There is no written test, and the appointment does not mean admission.

A place at Franklin's lycée

For seconde, Franklin chooses on collège reports and an appointment. I know what the school looks for: I prepare applications of excellence, submitted at the right time, and students at the top of their level when they meet the préfet.

What the school reads

With no exam, everything rests on the quatrième and troisième reports, and on what they say: consistency, level in the demanding subjects, teachers' comments. A brilliant but uneven student fares worse than a student who is solid everywhere. The appointment checks the student's and the family's commitment to Franklin's project, a Jesuit school that stands for intellectual, human and spiritual formation, and families are expected to have read its educational charter beforehand.

Preparing for seconde at Franklin

I have students at Franklin's lycée, and every year I support around fifteen families towards the leading Paris lycées. For entry into seconde, preparation starts in quatrième: those are the reports the school will read. My role is to say frankly whether the file is at the right level, to help the student bring it where it needs to be, and to prepare them for an appointment where they will have to talk about themselves accurately. Other lycées to aim for, Stanislas, Saint-Jean-de-Passy, the Alsacienne, are chosen in parallel, each with its own calendar.

What awaits the student

Franklin's lycée has its own rhythm. A written test every Saturday morning, oral tests after class or on Wednesdays, supervised study possible until 8 pm. Each year group has its préfet, who follows students and families, and individual meetings with the form tutor punctuate the year. An integration day opens seconde, on discernment and choice; a social action project marks première; a four-day retreat launches terminale.

In première, students freely choose three specialities out of seven: mathematics, humanities-literature-philosophy, physics-chemistry, life and earth sciences, economics and social sciences, history-geography-geopolitics-political science, and English. Guidance is close, with monthly sessions on studying in France and abroad, individual meetings and a careers forum in October of première. At the end there is also the school's own ECG prépa, one of the best in France for HEC, ESSEC and ESCP.
