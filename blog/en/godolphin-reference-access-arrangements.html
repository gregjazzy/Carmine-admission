The school reference and access arrangements

Two parts of the Godolphin 11+ file are handled away from the candidate: the reference written by her current school, and, for some girls, the evidence for access arrangements. Neither is visible on the day, and both are settled before the test.

The reference

Each Consortium school to which a girl applies asks the head of her current school for a reference, separately, before the entrance examination. The Consortium offers a common reference form that asks, among wider information on attitudes and character, for detailed commentary on the candidate's academic performance; schools may use their own form instead. The reference is one of the three approaches the Consortium uses, alongside the test and the interview.

A family does not write the reference, but it can help it be accurate: by making sure the school knows which schools the girl is applying to, and by keeping communication with teachers open through Year 6.

The detail that catches families

The normal-way-of-working condition is the one most often missed. Extra time granted for the 11+ must reflect how the girl already works at school, from the spring term of Year 5 at the latest, and usually every day. A report obtained in October of Year 6, with no history of support at school, will not meet it. Reports without standardised scores are not considered; reports from other specialists must include specific recommendations, not only a diagnosis.

At Godolphin specifically

Godolphin welcomes applications from girls with individual learning needs. Requests for extra time or other concessions are made in the relevant sections of the Examination Entry Form, issued in September or October, with written evidence. Arrangements granted for the 11+ apply to that test only; the senior school will reassess needs later, notably before GCSEs.
