Aiglon College: expeditions, houses and boarding life

Villars-sur-Ollon, home of Aiglon. Photo Bledard92, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0

Boarding schools November 2026 5 min read

To understand whether a child is made for Aiglon, look less at their grades than at their week. Aiglon is unlike any other boarding school: the mountains are not a backdrop, they are part of the curriculum. And that is what the school checks at admission, when it assesses an applicant's fit with its principles.

House life

The school has ten houses, five for boys and five for girls: two for the junior school, of up to about thirty pupils, and eight for the senior school, of around fifty each. Day students are fully part of them. Meals are family-style, the house meets in the evening, rooms are inspected daily, and evening prep is supervised by house tutors. Houseparents are there day and night. It is a dense, structured community life that teaches shared responsibility.

Showing they are made for the mountains

I prepare your child for Aiglon's interview so that they show what the school is really looking for: a wish for challenge, community life and responsibility. My aim: that the committee sees a future Aiglonian in them.

Expeditions, at the heart of the programme

Every student goes on expeditions regularly, from junior school to the final year. The programme is progressive: in Years 10 and 11, expeditions go further into the Alps, with longer journeys, winter travel and adventure sports, a trip to Costa Rica and, in October, a cultural stay in a French- or German-speaking city. The most ambitious aim for the "Skyline" expeditions, the school's most demanding peaks. In winter, students ski several times a week. And since the founder's time, meditation has been part of daily life.

Does your child love effort, the outdoors, group life? Tell me about them: I will tell you whether Aiglon fits them.

For which child?

Aiglon suits a child who wants to push themselves physically, enjoys group life and accepts a demanding framework, and wants to grow as much through experience as through study. It suits less a child who looks above all for comfort, or whom the mountains, the cold and nights outdoors worry more than they attract. That is exactly what the interview tries to see, and what a child must be able to express sincerely.
