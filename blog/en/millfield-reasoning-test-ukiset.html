The reasoning test and UKiset

Two tests can appear in a Millfield application. The first, a computer-based reasoning test, is sat by every candidate alongside the interview. The second, UKiset, concerns families outside the UK: some candidates sit it before their interview, and those unable to visit Millfield take it in place of the school's own test.

Can a reasoning test be prepared?

The Millfield test is designed so that it cannot be revised for: there is no syllabus to learn. That is fair, and it means families should not buy stacks of practice papers. What a child can do is arrive familiar with the kinds of task, so that the first minutes are not spent understanding instructions, and calm enough to work steadily on a screen for an hour. For a child who has never seen a non-verbal sequence or a verbal analogy, that familiarity matters. For one who has, more drilling adds little.

Ready, not drilled

I make sure children reach the Millfield test and UKiset familiar with each type of task, and spend the rest of the preparation where it counts most: the interview and, abroad, written English.

UKiset for international candidates

UKiset is open to students from 9½ to 18. A basic knowledge of English is recommended, and the test is in English throughout, so a child educated in another language meets two challenges at once: the reasoning and the language. The English section gives a CEFR level, which schools read alongside the reasoning scores. A test can be retaken after three months, and results remain valid for a year, which leaves room for a second attempt if the first comes too early.

What the tests do not decide alone

At Millfield, every candidate also has a 30-minute interview with a senior member of staff and a reference from their current school. The reasoning score opens or narrows the door; the interview and reference decide how a child is seen. A good score with a flat interview is not a strong application.

How I prepare a candidate

For seven years I have taken on around fifteen families a year aiming at highly selective schools. For a Millfield candidate, I check familiarity with the test formats early, then put most of the time into the interview and, for families abroad, into the written English that UKiset measures.
