Preparing the SHSAT math section

For many Brooklyn Tech candidates, math is the section they expect to be their strength. It often is, but only when the preparation has been steady. The section covers middle-school content; its difficulty lies in multi-step problems and in the adaptive rule that every question must be answered before moving on.

What is tested

Fifty questions in six domains: ratios and proportional relationships; numbers and expressions; equations; inequalities; statistics and probability; and geometry. Questions come as multiple choice and in other formats. The raw score is standardised and added to the English score to form the composite.

Habits that win points

  • Commit and move on: with no skipping, a stuck student loses time on the rest of the section.
  • Write the setup: translating the words into a short equation before calculating prevents most word-problem errors.
  • Check the question asked: many wrong answers are correct answers to a different question.

Is math your child's stronger section, or the one to build? Let's see on the practice test.

Math first, or second?

Students choose which section to begin with. A candidate who is confident in math may open with it to bank points while fresh; another may prefer to start with English and finish on familiar ground. Try both orders on full practice tests in October, then keep the one that produces the better composite.

Beyond the test

At Brooklyn Tech, mathematics does not stop mattering at admission. Grades in 9th and 10th grade, especially in subjects linked to the majors a student wants, feed the Power Index that decides placement; Advanced Placement courses count at 1.10. A student who arrives with secure algebra has an advantage in that second race too.
