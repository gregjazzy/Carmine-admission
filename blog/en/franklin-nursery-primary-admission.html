Franklin: getting into nursery or primary school

Saint-Louis-de-Gonzague, Paris 16th. Photo Ornd, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

Paris schools November 2026 4 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

Book a conversation

Many families think of Franklin for sixième. Those who think of it for nursery or CP (age 6) quickly find that the primary school is even harder to get into: few classes, few departures, and a stated priority for brothers and sisters of current students. For a first child, places are rare.

How registration works

Everything starts with online pre-registration, after reading the educational charter; no paper file is processed. For nursery, appointments start in the second half of September of the year before entry. For CP, CE1 and CE2 (ages 6 to 8), applications open in mid-September, and appointments start after the All Saints' holiday. In both cases the school gives priority to siblings. CM1 and CM2 (ages 9 and 10), which Franklin still calls huitième and septième, only open when pupils leave.

Example: the round for September 2027. In autumn 2026, Franklin announced no places in the last two years of nursery or in CM1-CM2, and applications for CP, CE1 and CE2 had already closed.

A place in the primary school

For a first child, a place at Franklin before sixième turns on very little: the moment of pre-registration and the appointment. I know how to approach it, and which other schools to keep in parallel so that nothing is lost.

The appointment

At this age there is of course no test. The school looks at the child, but above all at the family: its understanding of the Jesuit project, its consistency, the place it gives to human and spiritual formation. A family that comes for Franklin's reputation and knows nothing of its project is quickly spotted.

Aiming for Franklin for a child aged three to eight? Write to me: I will tell you at which level the door is most open.

Entering before sixième, or waiting

I have students at Franklin, and every year I support around fifteen families towards the leading Paris schools, from primary to lycée. For the primary school, the real question is often one of timing: trying for a very rare place early, or preparing for sixième, the main entry point, by choosing a primary school that leads there well. I know both paths, and I tell families which one serves their child best; it is a decision made early, and it shapes the CM1 and CM2 reports on which Franklin will later choose.

What the primary school costs

In 2025-2026, the full-rate family contribution was €2,435 a year from nursery to CE2 and €2,815 in CM1-CM2, adjusted to income, with flat-rate supplies and lunches on top. Details are in Franklin school fees.
