Le Rosey: the admission calendar, and why to apply early

The Château du Rosey, Rolle. Photo Eric Bajart, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0

Boarding schools November 2026 5 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

Book a conversation

At Le Rosey, when you submit the file matters almost as much as the file itself. Every age group and every nationality has a quota, and these quotas fill up through the year: an excellent candidate who applies late can end up on the waiting list for lack of space, not lack of ability. Fifteen of my students have been admitted to Le Rosey, and half of a family's work is making sure the right moment does not slip by.

Two systems depending on age

For younger children, in classes 9 to 4 (UK Years 4 to 9), admissions are rolling. As soon as the online file is complete, the child comes to spend a day on campus for the tests and interview, and the committee, which meets every week, usually decides within two weeks.

For classes 3, 2 and 1 (UK Years 10 to 12), where competition is fiercer, decisions are made in rounds. For September 2027 entry: files completed by 8 October 2026 receive an answer in early November; those completed by 26 November, in mid-December; those completed by 11 February 2027, in early March. At the first two rounds a file can be accepted, refused or deferred to the next round; at the third, it can also be wait-listed. After the third round, places are usually filled, and new files can only lead to the waiting list or a refusal.

The weight of quotas

Le Rosey is committed to the diversity of its community and applies strict limits by nationality and language group, at every level. For a French, Russian or Chinese family, this means the place is also contested against other candidates of the same nationality, and every month of delay reduces the odds. The best window to register is between June and September of the year before entry.

Can you apply too early?

Yes and no. Le Rosey does not admit anyone before the September preceding entry, and the campus day cannot take place more than fifteen months before the start. But an outstanding file submitted two years ahead may be offered a conditional place, provided the child keeps up the same level and good reports. And very early applications leave a useful record for when the file becomes official.

Four answers, and what they mean

An acceptance, sometimes linked to results in the current year. A refusal, which is not final: the child can reapply the following year. A place on the waiting list, meaning the candidate has the level but no place is left, because the class is full or the quota reached; a place can open up late, sometimes in the middle of summer. Finally, a deferral, when competition for a class or a language group leads the committee to compare all such files together at a later date, which the school gives the parents.

Neither connections nor donations

Some families believe a name or a generous gesture always opens a door. Not at Le Rosey, which accepts no donations at all: school fees are its only source of income. The number of beds is limited, and even the best-connected candidate will not get in if there is no room. Only the quality of the file counts, and the conviction that the child will flourish in Rolle. When space runs out, the school sometimes points families to Le Régent, its sister school.
