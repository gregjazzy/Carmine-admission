How to get into Bergen County Academies

Bergen County Academies, BCA, is a free public high school in Hackensack, New Jersey, open to residents of Bergen County. It brings seven specialised academies, from science and medicine to finance, culinary arts and the performing arts, into one school with an extended day, research laboratories and a senior-year internship. It reports an acceptance rate of 19.5 %.

I have students at BCA. The process runs as follows.

One chance, in 8th grade

Only 8th graders can apply, for entry into 9th grade, and a student can apply only once. There is no transfer into BCA later. The family's primary residence must be in Bergen County by 1 September of the application year, and the student must be registered in the home town's district.

What the committee weighs

The admissions committee reads each file as a whole: the application essay, the entrance test, standardised test scores from middle school, report cards from 5th to 8th grade, the teacher recommendations and, for those invited, the interview. The level of courses taken counts alongside grades. The criteria are the same for every academy, with an audition or portfolio added for the arts. The school is frank that it relies on measurable evidence, because character and passion are hard to quantify.

Two constraints few families expect

First, there is a limit on how many students BCA can accept from each school district, based on the size of the local high school, so a strong applicant competes mainly with others from the same town. Second, the academy chosen at application is final: an admitted student cannot change academy or transfer between them.

Is BCA the right fit?

The school day runs from 8:00 to 4:10, longer than a regular high school, with research, projects and clubs built into it. BCA itself asks families with heavy commitments outside school to think carefully about whether the extended day suits their child.
