How to get into Choate Rosemary Hall

The Andrew Mellon Library, Choate Rosemary Hall, Wallingford. Photo Daderot, Wikimedia Commons, public domain

US boarding schools November 2026 7 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

Book a conversation

Choate Rosemary Hall publishes no minimum grades, no average GPA and no average test score. It does not require a test at all. Families often read that as good news. In practice it means that a file at Choate has no single number to hide behind: the decision rests on what the committee can learn about the child from a handful of documents and one forty-five-minute appointment.

I have students at Choate, and I prepare applicants for it every year. This guide is about how those documents are read, and where good candidates quietly fall short.

The school in a few figures

Choate has 861 students this year, from ninth grade through a postgraduate year: 647 boarders and 214 day students. They come from 40 states and around sixty countries, and about 18% are international. Rosemary Hall was founded in 1890 for girls, the Choate School in 1896 for boys, on the same family property in Wallingford, Connecticut. The catalogue runs to more than 300 courses in six departments.

Without a score, the transcript has to speak for itself

When no test is required, the transcript and the teacher recommendations carry the academic case alone. That works well for a student at a school the committee knows. It works less well for a student whose grades come from a small school, a new school or a system abroad, where an A is hard to place. The admissions team is trained to read many curricula, but a reader can only credit what the file shows. This is where the optional score earns its name: for some children it is the clearest proof available, and for others it would only add doubt.

Where strong candidates lose ground

The children who fall short at Choate are rarely weak students. They are good students whose file says the same thing five times. The transcript says strong grades, the teachers say hard-working and kind, the essay lists activities, the interview repeats the essay. Nothing is wrong, and nothing stays with the reader.

What Choate wants to know fits in four short questions: what the child thinks about, what they care about, what they do to relax, what they hope to explore next. A file that answers those four questions, each in a different document, reads as a person. A file that answers "how good are your grades" five times reads as a transcript.

Choosing the entry grade

Most students enter in ninth grade (179 this year), but the tenth and eleventh grades are larger than the ninth, which means Choate keeps admitting students after the first year. There is also the postgraduate year, about fifteen to twenty students a year.

Two rules matter here. A family applies to one entry grade only, and any change must be requested in writing. And an applicant may choose to repeat their current grade, which Choate calls reclassification. Families do it to align the grade with a child's maturity, to have more years at the school, or to have more time before college. Math and language placement is done by test, so repeating a grade does not mean repeating the same courses. It is a family decision, and it is worth taking before the file is built, because the whole file reads differently depending on the grade.

How I prepare a Choate application

I have students at Choate, and for seven years I have worked with around fifteen families a year aiming at the most selective schools in the United States, England and Switzerland; since 2021 those students have been admitted to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford and the leading boarding schools. For Choate, the work starts with an assessment of written English and mathematics, which tells us both where the child stands and whether a test score would help. Then comes the interview, practised online in real conditions, the essays, written by the child and worked on until they sound like them, and the choice of recommenders. The aim is a file in which every piece adds something.

Where the class above ends up

Over the last five graduating classes, the most frequent destinations were the University of Chicago (66), NYU (47), Columbia (42), Cornell and Yale (41 each), and Georgetown (28), with 21 at Harvard and 22 at St Andrews. In the class of 2026, the middle half of SAT scores ran from 1280 to 1510. These figures are not a target for a fourteen-year-old. They describe the classroom a Choate student sits in for four years.
