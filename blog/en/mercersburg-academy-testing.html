Mercersburg's testing policy has three layers, and families often read only the first. The school calls its overall stance "testing preferred": scores are welcome and listed among application components, but whether they are required depends on the grade and on the aid a family hopes for.

Grade by grade

EntryTestStatus
Grade 9 or 10SSAT or ISEENot required for admission; required for merit scholarships
Grade 11, 12 or postgraduate yearPSAT, ACT or SATRequired
Access scholarshipsSSAT, ISEE or HSPTRequired
Non-native English speakersTOEFL, Duolingo or IELTSRequired, unless three years in an English-medium school

Useful codes: SSAT 5026, TOEFL 0877. Suggested English scores: TOEFL 5.0 or higher out of 6, Duolingo 135 or higher.

Should a Grade 9 applicant test anyway?

In most cases, yes. A score is preferred, it is the only way to be considered for merit scholarships, and access scholars generally score at the 85th percentile or above. A strong score also adds evidence of academic promise, which the school says it reads in transcripts, test scores and teacher letters together. A student who tests poorly despite strong grades can weigh whether to submit, but should decide after a real attempt, not before.

Later entry

For Grades 11, 12 and the postgraduate year, the PSAT, ACT or SAT is compulsory. These are the tests American universities know, so a student entering late is already preparing for college admission at the same time. Postgraduate applicants, who spend a year at Mercersburg after finishing high school, often use that year to strengthen exactly these scores.

Timing

Everything is due by 15 January. A first sitting in the autumn leaves time to retake before the deadline, and time for official score reports to reach the school. The Admission Office typically processes each item three to five days after it is submitted.
