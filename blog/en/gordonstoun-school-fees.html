Gordonstoun's fees are set by its Board of Governors and charged per term, with three terms in the year. For 2026-27, a Senior School boarder pays £21,365 a term, about £64,100 a year. Families often ask the same handful of questions about these figures; the answers below come from the school's own schedule.

Why does a Sixth Form newcomer pay more?

A pupil who joins directly in Year 12 pays a higher termly fee than one who moved up from Year 11: £23,000 rather than £21,365 to board. Over the two Sixth Form years, the gap is close to £9,800. A family hesitating between joining at 13 and joining at 16 should put that figure next to the rest of the decision.

The fee buys a place only once it is won

Do many families receive help?

Yes. More than a third of Gordonstoun pupils receive fee assistance, a balance the school keeps on purpose since Kurt Hahn wanted a socially and economically mixed community. Bursaries are means-tested, funded by the school and its supporters, and never repaid. They can reach 100% of fees, but most fall between 10% and 25%, and the largest go to Sixth Form pupils.

Does a scholarship reduce the fees?

Not by itself. Gordonstoun scholarships are honorary. They bring benefits such as free one-to-one music lessons for music scholars, mentoring and leadership events, and above all priority for bursaries: the bursary funds go primarily to scholars. For a family that needs help, applying for a scholarship is therefore the most direct route to it.

Is there a rate for military and diplomatic families?

Families connected to the Armed Forces or the Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office automatically receive a 10% remission on net fees. Families receiving the Ministry of Defence's Continuity of Education Allowance can find full boarding for less than £4,000 a year, depending on the child's age. A termly payment plan is also offered through School Fee Plan.
