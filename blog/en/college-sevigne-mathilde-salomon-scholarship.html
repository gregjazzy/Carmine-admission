Collège Sévigné: the Mathilde Salomon scholarship, and entering seconde with funding

Collège Sévigné, rue Pierre-Nicole, Paris 5th. Photo LPLT, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0

Few Paris private schools fully fund the schooling of students who are not yet theirs. Sévigné does, and it is one of the least known doors into a very good Paris lycée: the Mathilde Salomon scholarship, named after the headmistress who shaped the school at the end of the nineteenth century.

What the scholarship covers

Created in 2024, the scholarship covers all or part of the school fees of lycée students, from seconde to terminale. Since September 2025 it has also been open to students arriving from outside: five scholarship students are admitted each year into seconde with a full scholarship, and continue into première and terminale. Five other seconde students receive a partial scholarship. Sévigné takes around thirty-five new students into seconde every year, so a significant share of them arrive with support.

Recipients are chosen on social criteria and on the quality of their project, and each is given tutoring to settle in. The stated goal is for 10% of Sévigné's lycée students to hold a scholarship by 2030.

An application that convinces

A Mathilde Salomon scholarship is won on the quality of the student's project as much as on their circumstances. I know how to present an application of excellence, and a student who carries their project with accuracy.

Other support

For families already enrolled, a six-member committee confidentially reviews requests for one-off help with school fees, lunches, study or trips: around twenty families benefit each year, from nursery to terminale. State grants for collège and lycée also apply, since Sévigné is under contract.

Applying for the scholarship

I have students at Sévigné, and every year I support around fifteen families towards the leading Paris lycées. A scholarship application is first an application to the lycée: the school record must hold up, the chosen track, Monde or Europe, must match the student's real level of English, and the project must be stated simply. That is often where the difference lies between two candidates in similar situations, and it is what I work on with families.

Why Sévigné does it

This is not a public relations exercise. Sévigné was born in 1880 from a double commitment, opening excellence to girls and to religious minorities, and has since stood for "excellence without competition" and against social exclusiveness. The scholarship continues that history. For a family, it is above all a real and little-known chance to enter a lycée that prepares the French International Baccalaureate.
