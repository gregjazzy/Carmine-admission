French Curriculum Math Tutoring in Dubai: Support for LFIGP Students

Private Tutoring January 2026 8 min read

Dubai is home to one of the largest French expatriate communities in the world. Thousands of families live, work, and educate their children in the French system here. The Lycee Francais International Georges Pompidou -- the LFIGP -- is the anchor of this community, with multiple campuses spread across the emirate. And yet, despite the size of this Francophone population, finding a mathematics tutor who genuinely understands the French curriculum remains a formidable challenge in Dubai.

The reason is straightforward: Dubai's tutoring market is overwhelmingly oriented toward Anglo-Saxon programs. IB, GCSE, A-Levels, American curriculum -- these are the systems that local tutors are trained in. The French mathematics program, with its specific methodology of formal proof and structured reasoning, is a world these tutors do not know.

The LFIGP: a flagship of the AEFE network

The Lycee Francais International Georges Pompidou is one of the largest institutions in the AEFE network worldwide. With its campuses at Oud Metha, Academic City, and Sharjah, it educates several thousand students from kindergarten through Terminale. Each campus follows the accredited French program, with the same standards, the same exams, and the same expectations as any lycee in metropolitan France.

This scale creates a paradox. The size of the LFIGP gives families the impression that Dubai's Francophone ecosystem is large enough to generate its own academic support solutions. In reality, the vast majority of tutors who market their services to LFIGP families are generalists who do not master the specificity of the French math program. They can teach math. They cannot teach French math.

The distinction is fundamental. French mathematics is not simply a French-language version of what the Anglo-Saxon world calls "math." It is a discipline built around formal demonstration, where every claim must be proven, where the reasoning process is as important as the result, where the written presentation follows specific conventions that only a tutor trained in the French system can transmit.

Dubai's tutoring market: volume without relevance

Dubai is a city where everything is available for purchase, and academic support is no exception. Tutoring platforms proliferate. Advertisements from private tutors flood the French community's WhatsApp groups. Tutoring centers occupy storefronts in every neighborhood.

But volume is not quality, and more importantly, volume is not relevance. The overwhelming majority of these tutors are trained to support students in Anglo-Saxon or Arabic programs. They teach mathematics as a problem-solving exercise: apply the right formula, find the right answer, move on. This approach works perfectly for the IB program or A-Levels. It is entirely inadequate for the French program.

At the LFIGP, a student in 3eme solving a geometry problem must write a structured proof: hypotheses, citation of the theorem used, logical deductions, conclusion. A Terminale student studying a function must follow a rigorous methodology: domain, derivative, sign of the derivative, variation table, limits, graphical representation -- each step justified in writing. A local tutor trained in A-Levels knows neither this methodology nor this formalism. They cannot help the LFIGP student where it actually matters.

The return to France: a strategic imperative

Dubai is, by nature, a transient city. Many French families live there for a few years before returning to France. This return is a critical moment for children, particularly on the academic front. And mathematics is often the most painful point of friction.

A student returning to France after several years in Dubai must be able to integrate into a French college or lycee without a gap. If they are targeting a selective institution in Paris -- Jeanine Manuel, the Lycee International de Saint-Germain-en-Laye, Stanislas, Louis-le-Grand -- the mathematics level will be examined with particular attention. The admission tests at these institutions are written in French format. A student who has spent four years in Dubai with a local tutor who taught them math "the Anglo-Saxon way" will find themselves exposed when facing these exams.

Maintaining the French mathematics standard during an expatriation is not a luxury. It is a strategic necessity for families planning a return to France. And this maintenance can only be ensured by a tutor who knows the French program and its specific demands. For more detail on this topic, our article on returning to Jeanine Manuel from abroad covers the expectations of these selective institutions.

The challenges at each level

College (5eme - 3eme): building rigor from the ground up

The college years at the LFIGP lay the foundation for everything that follows in mathematics. This is where students learn to prove, to justify, to write with the precision the French system demands. Algebraic identities, factoring, algebraic manipulation, geometry theorems (Thales, Pythagoras) and their formal proofs: everything is established between 5eme and 3eme.

A student who accumulates gaps during these years enters the lycee in a precarious position. Seconde is often a shock for students with weak mathematical methodology, and gaps accumulated during college do not resolve themselves spontaneously. They compound.

Lycee (2nde - Terminale): the race toward the bac

At the lycee level, every grade counts. The transcripts from Premiere and Terminale feed the Parcoursup application for formations in France. The mathematics specialization, chosen by virtually every student targeting scientific or business studies, carries a major coefficient at the Baccalaureat. The exam is demanding: long-form written exercises, formal proofs, open-ended problems, time management under pressure.

For an LFIGP student targeting a prepa scientifique in France, a grande ecole de commerce through competitive exams, EPFL in Switzerland, or a top American or British university, the specialite mathematiques bac grade sends a decisive signal. A 12/20 communicates mediocrity. A 17/20 tells admissions committees: this student commands mathematics at the highest level of rigor.

Physics and chemistry: the same problem, the same solution

Everything we describe for mathematics applies word for word to physics and chemistry. The French science curriculum demands rigorous written work, systematic use of symbolic expressions before any numerical application, dimensional analysis, and methodical justification of every result. Local tutors in Dubai do not teach physics this way. They teach physics for the IB program or A-Levels, where the writing methodology is radically different.

An LFIGP student struggling in physics and chemistry needs the same type of support as a student struggling in math: a tutor who knows the French program, who masters French writing methodology, and who can teach the subject exactly the way it is taught in class at the LFIGP.

Online tutoring: the timezone advantage

Dubai offers a considerable advantage for online tutoring with European-based tutors: the timezone. At GMT+4, Dubai is only two to three hours ahead of France. A lesson scheduled for 5pm or 6pm Dubai time corresponds to 2pm or 3pm in France -- a perfectly comfortable time for our tutors.

This proximity is a major asset compared to families based in Asia (Singapore, Hong Kong, Shanghai), where the six-to-seven-hour gap complicates scheduling significantly. In Dubai, sessions can be arranged with maximum flexibility, including weekends and during LFIGP school holidays.

Our tutors are specialists in the French program. They know the textbooks, the exercise types, the grading methodology. They understand what LFIGP teachers expect, because they teach the same subject in the same format. The initial diagnostic precisely identifies the student's gaps, and the progression plan is built to address them methodically and efficiently.

An LFIGP student targeting EPFL needs a profile of scientific excellence. A student considering a prepa HEC must present math grades that place them at the top. A student applying to Columbia, UCL, or McGill needs transcripts that speak for themselves. Our tutors understand these stakes. They do not work in a vacuum. They work with the post-bac horizon in view, with an acute awareness of what every point gained in mathematics means for the student's future.
