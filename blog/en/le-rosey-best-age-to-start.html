Le Rosey: what is the best age to start?

The Château du Rosey, Rolle. Photo Eric Bajart, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0

Boarding schools November 2026 5 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

Book a conversation

At Le Rosey, the question of entry age is asked differently from elsewhere, because the school also asks it of parents. Some children are ready for boarding at eight, others prefer to wait, and parents must be ready too: willing to give their child real independence and not try to control everything from afar. Fifteen of my students have been admitted to Le Rosey, at very different ages, and this choice often weighs as much as the preparation itself.

Eight to twelve: the Junior Section

The youngest join the Junior Section, designed for children aged eight to twelve, with very attentive support within the school as a whole. At this age, it is not even essential to master French or English, the school's two languages. It is the most flexible entry point, for a mature child and a family ready for an early separation.

Twelve to fourteen: when most arrive

Most arrivals happen between twelve and fourteen, and many of these students then stay until graduation. From twelve, a child needs to understand one of the school's two languages. It is often the best balance: old enough to live boarding life fully, young enough to enjoy several years at Le Rosey before exams.

Not everyone stays to the end. Some students, mostly younger ones, spend two or three years at Le Rosey before moving on to an equally prestigious school in the UK or the US. After fifteen, however, it is extremely rare for a student to leave before graduating. Arriving at twelve or thirteen therefore often commits the family for the rest of the child's schooling, and deserves to be thought through as such.

From class 3, the door narrows

For classes 3, 2 and 1, the equivalent of UK Years 10 to 12, competition becomes markedly tougher, and the school decides in rounds rather than on a rolling basis. By fifteen, a child must master one of the two working languages. An excellent student can run into a full class or a nationality quota that has already been reached.

Is your child fourteen or fifteen? Write to me: timing already matters a great deal.

The last two years: an entry the school discourages

Can you join Le Rosey for the final two years to prepare the International Baccalaureate? It is possible, but the school itself advises against it. IB places are few and fiercely contested, and every year excellent candidates are turned away. Above all, spending only your two busiest years in a new school brings little. Families who do it once often enrol younger siblings earlier.

How quickly the answer comes

Age also changes how long a family waits. For younger children, a decision usually comes within two weeks of the file being complete. For the upper classes, it comes at fixed rounds: early November, mid-December or early March, depending on when the file was completed. A family aiming at class 3 or above should therefore plan its calendar a year ahead, while a family with an eight-year-old can afford a little more flexibility.

Neither too early nor too late

Le Rosey admits no one before the September preceding the year of entry, and the campus day cannot take place more than fifteen months before the start. An outstanding file submitted two years ahead may, however, be offered a conditional place. To give yourself every chance, register between June and September of the year before entry. The right age also means the right year to apply.
