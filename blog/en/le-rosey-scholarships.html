Le Rosey: scholarships, who gets them and how

The Château du Rosey, Rolle. Photo Eric Bajart, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0

Le Rosey does award scholarships, but they are so rare that it is worth knowing exactly what they look like before building plans on one. Each year, the school and the Rosey Foundation award between three and five, all partial: the school states that it awards no full scholarships.

What the school looks for in a scholarship student

Academic excellence matters, without being the only criterion. The school describes the qualities it expects: results that place the student in the top 5% of their age group, good French or English and a taste for learning several languages, the potential to contribute strongly to the school's sport, arts or humanitarian work, and the wish to take on responsibilities with the maturity to handle them. It is looking for students who want to play a leading role in society and whose parents cannot fully fund a place.

An application worthy of a scholarship

I prepare your child for Le Rosey's exams and build with them an application that shows what the school expects from a scholarship student: excellence, commitment, character. My aim: that they come across as one of the rare applicants it will choose to support.

The calendar, and its conditions

Applications are accepted only between 1 September and 31 December, by mentioning the request for aid when registering online, with a CHF 500 application fee. A request received from January onwards is carried over to the following school year. Scholarships concern entry from class 3 to class 1, the final years before the diploma. The family must provide its financial information and make a significant contribution to tuition as well as covering all optional expenses. Scholarship status is not made public, and it is reviewed every year.

An alternative worth knowing

Le Rosey's sister school, Le Régent International School in Crans-Montana, has a more generous IB scholarship programme for a limited number of students. For a brilliant student whose family cannot afford Le Rosey, it is often a more realistic route.
