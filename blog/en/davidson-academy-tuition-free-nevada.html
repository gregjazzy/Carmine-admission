The Davidson Academy is a public school. For residents of Nevada, there is no tuition. Courses and textbooks are paid for through a combination of public and private funding, and courses at the University of Nevada, Reno that count toward the Academy's graduation requirements are covered at no extra cost. For a school that teaches profoundly gifted students three years ahead in every subject, that is unusual, and it changes the family's calculation completely.

I work with families who are weighing a move to Reno for Davidson. Here is what the school covers and what it does not.

What is covered and what is not

ItemCost to the family
Davidson Academy tuitionNone for Nevada residents
Courses and textbooksCovered
University courses that count toward graduation requirementsCovered
Some classes, activities and clubsOccasional small fees
University courses beyond graduation requirementsIn-state per-credit tuition, with a need-based scholarship fund available

As a public school serving grades 6 to 12, the Academy cannot issue 1098-T tax forms for university tuition.

The real cost is the move

Eligibility requires being, or intending to become, a Nevada resident, and admitted students must physically live in Reno before the first day of school in August. Students study on the university campus in a public day school; there is no boarding. For a family elsewhere in the United States or abroad, the decision is therefore about relocating a household, with everything that implies for work and siblings.

That is why the order of steps matters. The sensible order is to apply, go through the assessment, and commit to moving once the offer is in hand. The timeline allows it: applications close on January 20 and the school year starts in August.

Davidson Academy Online

The Davidson Academy also runs an online school for students across the United States and Canada, with its own tuition and aid. Families who cannot move to Reno should look at it separately; its admissions and costs are not those of the Reno day school.
