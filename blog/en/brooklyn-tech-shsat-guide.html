The SHSAT for Brooklyn Tech

Every Brooklyn Tech student entered through the same door: the Specialized High Schools Admissions Test, scored by an independent company. The test serves eight schools at once and, from fall 2026, takes a new, computer-adaptive form. These are the questions families ask most often.

The test in one table
SectionsEnglish Language Arts, 50 questions; Mathematics, 50 questions
Time180 minutes, standard
FormatComputer-adaptive, on Department of Education devices
ScoreEach section standardised, then added into a composite
UseComposite, ranked list and seats decide offers

Does it matter which section comes first?

The student chooses which section to start with. A student who settles faster with numbers may begin with math; one who reads best when fresh may begin with English. The choice is worth testing in practice, not deciding on the day.

Can a student skip and come back?

No, except in one case. Each question must be answered before the next appears. Within an English passage set, earlier answers in that set can be changed until the set is submitted.

Is one section worth more?

No. Each section's raw result is converted to a standardised score, and the two are added. A balanced profile and a lopsided one with the same total are treated identically; a very weak section, however, is hard to make up.

Which section should your child start with? Let's find out in practice.

Is coaching necessary?

The official NYC SHSAT Portal offers a Student Readiness Tool and practice tests in the new format, and should be every family's first resource. Beyond that, what helps most is solid middle-school mathematics, wide reading, and timed practice that mirrors the real conditions.

Where is it taken?

Eighth graders at public middle schools ending in 8th grade test at their own school. Others test at a central site assigned by district; for districts 13, 14, 15, 16, 19, 23 and 32, that site is Brooklyn Tech itself. Everyone uses Department of Education computers, never a personal device. Eligible students with IEPs, 504 Plans or English learner status may receive accommodations such as extended time.

When do we see the result?

Offers come first, in March; scores follow. In spring, families can review the test through Test View.
