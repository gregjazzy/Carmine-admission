Northfield Mount Hermon asks for two teacher recommendations: one from the child's current English teacher and one from the current math teacher. The family does not write them. Yet they are among the pieces that most shape the decision, because they describe the child in a classroom, which is exactly where NMH will place them.

I help families handle this part of the file every year. It cannot be written for the teacher, but it can be prepared.

Current teachers, and only them

The word "current" matters. A favorite teacher from last year, or a tutor who knows the child well, cannot replace the English or math teacher of the present school year. For a child who changed schools in September, that means a teacher who has known them for a few weeks when the request goes out. The request is made through the application platform, SAO or Gateway, which sends the teacher a link and lets the family track whether the letter has arrived.

Since the teachers are this year's, they can only be asked once the year has begun. The useful window is September to early November. A request in December reaches a teacher who has already written many others and has a holiday break before the January 15 deadline.

What NMH reads in a teacher's letter

NMH teaches three core courses per semester in seventy-minute blocks built on discussion, labs and projects, and each teacher works with about thirty students in a semester. The people reading the letters know exactly what that classroom demands. A letter that says the child earns top marks and hands work in on time tells them little. A letter that describes how the child takes part in discussion, how they react when they are wrong, whether they help others think, and how they handle a long task, tells them whether the child will thrive in that schedule.

The math letter is often the more revealing one. A strong math student who explains their reasoning to classmates and enjoys an open problem reads very differently from one who is fast and accurate but silent.

Not sure which teachers to ask, or how? Tell me about your child's school year.

How to ask

A short, respectful conversation before the platform's email arrives makes a real difference. It explains why the family is applying to NMH and what kind of school it is, so that the teacher understands what to describe. It does not suggest content or ask for praise. Teachers write better letters when they know the school and have time; they write worse ones when they are surprised by an automated request.

For a child at a school outside the American system, the English teacher may be teaching English as a foreign language, and may never have written for an American boarding school. A brief explanation of the format, and of the fact that the letter should be in English, avoids a short or generic text.

Additional letters

A family can add further recommendations or an updated transcript through NMH's SchoolAdmin parent portal. An extra letter is worth sending when it shows something the file lacks, a coach or a music teacher who has seen the child commit to something over years, not when it repeats the classroom picture.

How I help families with NMH recommendations
