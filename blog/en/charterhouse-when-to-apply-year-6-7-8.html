Year 6, 7 or 8: when to apply

Every 13+ pupil joins Charterhouse in the Fourth Form, Year 9. But the application can begin in Year 6, Year 7 or Year 8, and the three routes are neither equal nor interchangeable. Choosing the right one is the first strategic decision a family makes.

Every route ends with an offer conditional on a positive reference, for performance and behaviour, from the child's school during Year 8.

Why Year 6 for UK families

Charterhouse gives two reasons: competition for places is strong, and the Year 6 route allows an early offer. In practice, a child who applies in Year 6 knows by the end of March of that year, two and a half years before arriving, and the family can plan Years 7 and 8 around a confirmed place. A child who waits faces fewer places later.

Why Year 7 for many international families

For families abroad whose first language is not English, Charterhouse recommends the Year 7 route because it gives the strongest preparation for the ISEB Pre-Test. The test includes English and verbal reasoning, and an extra year of English makes a measurable difference. It is still an early route: the Pre-Test is sat in the autumn of Year 7, at the child's own school, with results by December.

How the Year 8 route works

The Year 8 route has its own format. Candidates take tests on a secure digital platform, from anywhere with Wi-Fi, within a set window, then attend an assessment day at Charterhouse, at which point the school asks for references. For 2026, families who registered by 31 August were tested from 3 to 10 September with an assessment day on 29 September; those registering between 1 September and 19 October were tested from 23 October to 3 November, with an assessment day on 17 November. A pupil at school outside the UK who cannot travel can be interviewed virtually.

The Year 8 route is also open to children who were unsuccessful a year earlier. Charterhouse recognises that children develop at different rates, and the Director of Admissions offers personal advice before any retake.

A practical rule

Register for the earliest route that suits the child. A family can still change course later; it cannot go back to a route whose deadline has passed.
