Applying to Charterhouse from abroad

At Charterhouse, the test for being an international applicant is simple: a pupil currently studying outside the UK will usually be treated as international, whatever their nationality. A British child at school in Singapore is international; a child of any nationality at a UK prep school is not. The distinction matters because the recommended route and timing change.

The route for families abroad

For international families whose first language is not English, Charterhouse recommends the Year 7 pathway, as the one that gives the strongest preparation for success in the ISEB Pre-Test. Registration closes on 30 September of Year 7. The Pre-Test is sat in the autumn of Year 7 at the child's current school, with results shared by December. Selected candidates then attend the Interview and Activities Assessment in the spring term of Year 7, and offers follow, usually by the end of March. Some candidates may be offered a place on a reserve list.

Later entry from abroad

  • Year 8 route and 14+: tests on a secure online platform, from anywhere with Wi-Fi, then an assessment day; a pupil abroad who cannot travel can be interviewed virtually.
  • Sixth Form: online tests in late September and early October, then an assessment day in November; international candidates can ask to take scholarship assessments online.

Hong Kong and mainland China

Applicants from Hong Kong and mainland China apply through Charterhouse's partner, the education consultancy Academic Asia. Sixth Form candidates should register their interest with Academic Asia by late August of the year before entry; they are interviewed at the annual Top Schools Event held in Hong Kong each September.

The real challenge: reasoning in English

The Pre-Test includes verbal and non-verbal reasoning, which are rarely taught outside England, and English, which is also the language of every other paper. For a child taught in another language, the gap is usually less about intelligence than about familiarity. The extra year of the Year 7 route exists precisely to close it, provided it is used for regular, targeted practice.

After the offer

Each September, Charterhouse becomes home to more than 1,000 pupils from around the world. International families are invited to an online welcome seminar before arrival, and once at school each pupil is supported by House staff, a personal tutor and an international pupil committee. A dedicated International Students Co-ordinator advises families on visas, guardianship and travel.
