Winchester College fees 2026-27: boarding, day and the extras

Winchester College, the Chapel and Chamber Court from above. Photo Tim Sheerman-Chase, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 4.0

For 2026-27, Winchester charges £20,700 per term for a boarder and £15,318 per term for a day pupil, VAT included. Day places exist only in the Sixth Form, where girls and boys can join as day pupils or boarders; from 13 to 16, every boy boards. Over three terms, that is £62,100 a year for boarding. The College does not charge for books or stationery used in the curriculum. What surprises families is less the fee than the sequence of payments around it.

The extras that do appear

  • A laptop, required and supplied by the school on joining, its cost spread over the first three bills and announced the summer before entry.
  • Exam entries, typically £500 to £1,000 for each set of GCSE or A level examinations.
  • Music lessons, from £417.60 per term for 30 minutes a week to £784.80 for an hour (2025-26 rates), free for music award holders; LAMDA drama lessons from £30 for 30 minutes.
  • Sports kit, a one-off starter kit around £45, with specialist equipment for some sports.
  • Trips such as exchanges, sports camps and expeditions, charged at cost.

Admission comes first

At Winchester, the fee follows a place won in Year 6 through ability testing, writing and interview. We prepare boys for each of those stages, so that the investment begins on merit.

The first year for a family abroad

Take a boy whose parents live outside the UK, entering Year 9 in September at current rates. By the time he arrives, the family will have paid £480 at registration, £2,000 at acceptance (later credited), and a £20,700 deposit in Year 8, returned only when he leaves. His first year then costs £62,100 in fees, £1,440 if Winchester supports his visa, the laptop, music lessons if he plays, and trips. Fees are reviewed each year.

Two further points for families abroad. Winchester supports a limited number of Child Student visa applications and does not sponsor visas for day boys. And families can pay several years of fees in advance, up to five, through a scheme that offers a discount; the deposit is still due.

Reductions: what exists and what does not

Scholarships and Exhibitions won at Election, and Music and Sport Awards, carry no automatic fee reduction. The exception is the Founder's Scholarship, worth 25 percent and awarded on merit alone, to four boys at 13+ and to Sixth Form entrants. Means-tested bursaries, from 5 to 100 percent, must be requested at registration; support for pupils already at the school is reserved for exceptional changes in circumstances.

Where the effort should go

Places at Winchester are decided by the General Ability Test, the writing assessment and the interview in Year 6, not by ability to pay. A family that can meet the fees has no advantage there, and a family that needs a bursary loses nothing by asking. The useful energy goes into the boy: reading, writing and talking well before November of Year 6.
