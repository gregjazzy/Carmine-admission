École alsacienne: tuition fees 2026-2027

The École alsacienne, Paris 6th. Photo Celette, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

Paris schools November 2026 6 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

Book a conversation

People readily imagine that a school in the 6th arrondissement, among the most renowned in France, costs as much as an international school. It is the opposite. Since September 2026, the École alsacienne has set its tuition according to each family's income, from completely free to €1,447 per term for the highest incomes, about €4,340 a year.

Every year I see families discover this rate with relief, then realise, a little late, what it implies: at this price, money is no longer the obstacle. The place is.

Eight rates, based on income

The rate depends on the two parents' reference taxable income from two years earlier. For September 2026 entry, that is the 2025 tax notice, covering 2024 income.

Discounts can be combined: from 15% for two children to 30% for five, 1 to 3% depending on the payment method, 10% for teachers' families. By comparison, Jeannine Manuel's state-contracted lycée costs more than €10,000 a year, and the IB Diploma at a Paris international school €29,000 to €41,400.

Price is not the obstacle. The place is.

I prepare applications of excellence and bring your child to the École alsacienne test and interview at the top of their level. My aim: that they win one of the places at a school that so many families now aim for, whatever their income.

What comes on top of tuition

The rate does not tell the whole story. On top come €80 per term of sundry costs (insurance, medical service, library, supplies), a €200 deposit for the tablet provided from cinquième to terminale, compulsory school trips, from Alsace in sixième to Rome in cinquième and London in terminale, €560 to €740 each in 2024-2025, the canteen if the child has lunch at school, and a €100 application fee, waived for families on the first four rates. For those families, the parents' association can also cover all or part of the trips.

Weighing the Alsacienne against other schools on cost? Let's talk: I will help you compare what each one costs, and what it offers.

What this model changes for admission

By adapting its fees to income, the school pursues a deliberate policy of social diversity: 13% of its students receive aided tuition, and its Charcot bursaries fully fund students from partner schools. It is a fine ambition. For applicant families, it has a less visible consequence: a school of this calibre, at this price, attracts even more applicants, for a number of places that does not change. There are already seven per place on average; the new rates give no reason for that figure to fall.

Another point families from abroad often discover late: if income is not declared in France, the school asks for the last three payslips and an employer's certificate, translated into French. Without them, the highest rate applies.
