How to get into Harrow School

The Old Schools, Harrow School, Harrow on the Hill. Photo Bernard Burns, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0

UK boarding schools November 2026 7 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

Book a conversation

Harrow takes around 160 boys into the Shell, its Year 9, each September, and receives about 600 applications for those places. A further 20 or so join in the Sixth Form. Almost everything is decided in Year 6, when a boy is ten or eleven, more than two years before he arrives on the Hill. Families who discover the process in Year 7 have, in most cases, already missed it.

The route at 13+

The two filters that matter

The first is the Pre-Test together with the reference. A small number of boys are stopped at this point; most go on. What lifts a boy into the second round is the combination: a solid Pre-Test score and a head who writes about him with conviction. A brilliant score with a cautious reference, or the reverse, is a weaker file than families imagine.

The second is the Harrow Test: twenty minutes with a House Master, an hour of computerised English and mathematics, and twenty minutes of group work in a classroom. This is where 600 become 160. There are no past papers for it, and the interview carries real weight, because Harrow is choosing boys who will live in a House for five years, not only pupils who will sit in its classrooms.

What the four results mean

  • A1: a place in a named House. The boy meets his House Master one to one from the summer of Year 6.
  • A2: a guaranteed place, with the House allocated later, usually in Year 8.
  • B: the waiting list.
  • C: no offer.

Every offer is conditional on sitting Common Entrance in June of Year 8, or performing well in the Academic Scholarship examinations. Common Entrance has no pass mark as such, but Harrow hopes for an average of at least 65 percent across subjects, and the results decide the sets a boy starts in.

What Harrow says it is looking for

Bright boys with an enthusiastic attitude to school life, the potential to lead, and a personality that will contribute to the community; boys who will thrive as boarders and have a good behavioural record. Harrow is explicit that it gives no preference to particular prep schools and that registering a boy at birth brings no advantage. In practice, that puts the weight back on the boy himself: his reference, his Pre-Test, and how he comes across to a House Master in twenty minutes.

Late applicants, and the Sixth Form

A boy registered after 30 September of Year 6 and before 1 January of Year 7 is a late applicant. He competes for fewer places and is invited to the Harrow Test only with a very strong reference and an equally strong Pre-Test. For boys at school overseas, late applications are not assessed at all, which makes the September deadline absolute for families abroad.

The other door is Year 12, with up to 20 places, tests in A level subjects and interviews, for boys predicted top grades at GCSE.

How I prepare boys for Harrow

The work starts in Year 5, so that nothing in Year 6 is a surprise.
