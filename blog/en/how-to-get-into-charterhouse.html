How to get into Charterhouse

Charterhouse, Godalming, from the air. Photo Tim Sheerman-Chase, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 4.0

Charterhouse, founded in 1611, calls itself academically selective, and admits by competitive assessment, interview and a reference from the current school. Yet its Director of Admissions describes the approach as recruiting the "complete child": selection is not based on academic criteria alone, but also on character, interests, talents and personality. Both halves of that description matter for a family preparing an application.

I have pupils at Charterhouse. Here is how the process is organised.

The 13+ route the school recommends

For UK families, Charterhouse strongly recommends starting in Year 6, because competition is strong and this route allows an early offer. Registration closes on 30 September of Year 6. That autumn, every registered candidate sits the ISEB Common Pre-Test, read together with a reference from the current school. The candidates selected are invited to an Interview and Activities Assessment at Charterhouse in the spring term. Offers come during that term, usually by the end of March, and must be accepted with a deposit within eight weeks. Every offer remains conditional on a positive reference, for work and behaviour, when the child moves into Year 8.

What the "complete child" means in practice

At 13+, Charterhouse looks for children with the potential to become academically ambitious, independent learners, and with the personality for life in a busy boarding school: independence, moral courage and kindness. It does not expect every child to excel at everything, but it wants a "try everything" attitude. The Pre-Test measures the first part. The assessment day, with group activities, interviews and a piece of creative writing, is where the rest shows.

That has a practical consequence. A child who prepares only for the Pre-Test can reach the assessment day and then disappear in a group task. A child who joins in, listens and writes freely is showing exactly what the school describes.

Later routes

A Year 7 route exists, and is the one Charterhouse recommends for international families whose first language is not English. A Year 8 route offers limited places, including for children who were unsuccessful a year earlier; its tests are taken online, followed by an assessment day. At 14+ and 16+, the process is shorter and the places fewer.

How I prepare a Charterhouse application

I have pupils at Charterhouse, and for seven years I have worked with around fifteen families a year aiming at the most selective schools in England, the United States and Switzerland. For Charterhouse I begin in Year 5 where possible: an assessment against the Pre-Test, then preparation in maths, English and the two kinds of reasoning, and in the spring, practice for the group activities, interviews and creative writing of the assessment day.
