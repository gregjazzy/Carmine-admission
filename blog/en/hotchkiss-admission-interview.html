The Hotchkiss admission interview: a dialogue, not a test

The main building of The Hotchkiss School, Lakeville, Connecticut. Photo Adrien Delessert, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 2.5

US boarding schools November 2026 6 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

Book a conversation

Hotchkiss does not require an interview, but it recommends one, and an applicant has every reason to take it. Interviews run from mid-September to mid-January, on campus or virtually, with no appointments during Thanksgiving or winter break. The school describes the conversation as a dialogue rather than an evaluative experience. That description is accurate, and it is also the reason the interview matters: in a dialogue, a student shows who they are whether they mean to or not.

What the interviewer wants to talk about

Hotchkiss says it hopes to go beyond grades and test scores and focus on the reasons behind the student's boarding school search: interests, talents and hopes for the future. In practice, three threads come up again and again in conversations of this kind:

  • Why boarding school, and why now. A student who can explain what they are looking for, in their own words, is more convincing than one repeating a parent's reasons.
  • What they do with their time. Not a list, but one or two interests explored in depth: what they have built, read, played or organised, and what they learned.
  • How they handle independence. A moment when something went wrong and they dealt with it; a responsibility they carry.

Why the campus visit matters

Hotchkiss strongly encourages families to interview on campus, because seeing the school is the best way to judge fit and because a visit raises questions an on-campus interviewer can answer. Virtual interviews are offered and are a reasonable choice for families far away. But for a student weighing Hotchkiss against two or three other New England schools, walking the campus often settles more than any brochure. Families who want to meet a coach or a department head arrange that directly; the admission office does not coordinate it.

Making the interview a conversation

We prepare students to talk about their interests, their reasons for boarding school and their own growth with ease and specifics, in English, so that the Hotchkiss dialogue shows them at their best.

Scheduling

The inquiry form must be completed before an interview can be booked, and the Candidate Profile in Gateway should be submitted beforehand if possible, so the interviewer knows the basics. Saturday slots fill quickly. A student applying for the January 15 deadline should aim for an October or November interview, which leaves time to finish the essays afterward with the conversation fresh in mind.

The one case where it is required

Homeschooled applicants must interview before the committee reviews their file. They also submit a statement of educational philosophy, a description of their program for the past two years, and recommendations from the adults who teach them, including a parent if the parent is the main teacher.

Preparing without a script

The interviews that go badly are rarely the ones where the student says something wrong; they are the ones where the student says nothing particular. Preparation should therefore be about material, not answers: choosing two or three real stories, practising telling them in a few sentences, and getting used to follow-up questions. A student who arrives with a question of their own about Hotchkiss, one that shows they have looked closely, turns the dialogue into what the school says it should be.

How I prepare students for the Hotchkiss interview

I run mock interviews in English, built on the student's own activities and reasons for applying, and we review them together: where they were vivid, where they were vague, where a better example existed. For students from other systems, the main work is learning to talk about themselves at length, which many have never been asked to do.
