UNIS admissions test: ISEE, SSAT or MAP?

United Nations International School, on the East River, Manhattan. Photo Transpoman, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

Book a conversation

From fifth grade upward, every applicant to the United Nations International School needs a standardized score on file: the ISEE, the SSAT, or the MAP results their current school already holds, provided the test was given within the past year. Below fifth grade there is no test at all; the child is watched in a classroom. That third option, MAP, is what sets UNIS apart from most New York day schools, and it turns the test into a decision the family makes rather than a hurdle it simply clears.

Most families make that decision without noticing they have made it. This article is about making it on purpose.

The score should be in before January, when the teacher forms and school reports are also due. A family that starts thinking about testing over the winter break has, in practice, one attempt left.

MAP: convenient, and double-edged

If your child's school runs MAP Growth, as a good number of international schools do, you can skip the Saturday at a test center entirely. The school sends the MAP report with the academic records and the testing question is settled.

The catch is how MAP is taken. It is an adaptive test sat at school, in an ordinary week, usually with no preparation and no sense that anything rides on it. Children treat it accordingly. The percentiles it produces describe a normal Tuesday, and UNIS will read them as they stand. Before you let the school forward it, ask for the most recent report and look at reading and mathematics separately. If both sit comfortably high, use it and spend the saved weeks elsewhere. If one of them is soft, a prepared ISEE or SSAT will almost always tell a better story, and nothing obliges you to send MAP.

ISEE or SSAT for UNIS

UNIS states no preference, so the choice comes down to the child and to the calendar.

The ISEE level depends on the grade the child is applying to, not the grade they are in. Applicants to 5th and 6th sit the Lower Level: verbal reasoning, quantitative reasoning, reading comprehension and mathematics achievement, 128 questions in 2 hours 20 minutes, then an unscored essay. From 7th grade, the Middle and Upper Levels run 161 questions in 2 hours 40 minutes on the same structure. A wrong answer costs nothing on the ISEE, so every question gets an answer. The SSAT is built differently, and its own guidance treats guessing as something that can help or hurt; a careful child who hates leaving blanks often does better on the ISEE for that reason alone.

Retakes favour planning ahead. The ISEE can be taken once per season (fall runs August to November, winter December to March), so a first sitting in the autumn leaves a second chance in December, still before the January deadline. The SSAT allows several paper sittings in a testing year, but its at-home version is open only in the United States and Canada. For a family still abroad, it often comes down to which test is offered near home on a usable date.

What the score actually weighs

UNIS publishes no cut-off, and families who look for one waste their energy. The score is read next to the school reports, the ISAAGNY form filled in by current teachers, and the meeting on campus. Entry is competitive without being ferocious in most grades, which means a test is rarely a wall. It is, however, the one piece of the file that reads the same for everyone.

That matters most for children coming from abroad. A report card from Seoul, Geneva or Lagos is hard for an admissions office in Manhattan to calibrate; a percentile is not. For an international applicant, the test therefore carries a larger share of the file than it does for a child moving across town from another New York school, and that is where preparation pays.

The same goes for English. A child schooled in French or Spanish can be fluent in conversation and still lose ground on verbal reasoning and timed reading, where the vocabulary is precise and the clock is tight. UNIS has strong support for children still learning English, but the test is taken before any of that support begins.

Timing for September 2027

Work backwards from the January deadline. A first ISEE in October or November, a possible second in December, the campus meeting booked through Ravenna around the same period, teacher forms requested early so that nobody is chasing a busy teacher over the holidays. Mid-year applications follow the same steps with no fixed calendar, and here MAP is often the quickest way to complete a file in a hurry.

How I prepare a child for the UNIS test

I start with a full practice test under real timing, because the profile it reveals decides everything else: some children lose their points in quantitative reasoning on unfamiliar question types, others in reading at speed. The work then goes where the points are, with timed sections, a written essay corrected line by line, and, from 7th grade, practice for the thirty-minute conversation, where a child has to talk about themselves with ease in English. My students have gone on to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford and the most demanding schools in the United States, England and Switzerland; the method is the same, adapted to UNIS.
