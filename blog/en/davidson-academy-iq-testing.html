IQ testing for Davidson

Many families first hear of the Davidson Academy after an IQ assessment. The school does accept IQ testing, but its role depends on the student's grade. From ninth grade up, an IQ score can establish eligibility on its own. From fourth to eighth grade, the SAT or ACT, or for younger students the PSAT 8/9 or PreACT, is required, and IQ results serve only as supplementary information.

I work with families who arrive with an IQ report and want to know what it means for Davidson. Here are the rules.

Accepted tests and indices

TestQualifying result
Stanford-Binet, Fifth Edition (SB5)Standard score of 145 or more on Verbal, Nonverbal or Full Scale
WISC-V145 or more on one of GAI, FSIQ, NVI, EGAI, VECI or EFI, or on two of Verbal Comprehension, Visual Spatial, Fluid Reasoning and the Quantitative Reasoning Index
DAS-II145 or more on Verbal Ability, Nonverbal Reasoning or General Conceptual Ability

All three are individually administered tests, and the testing must have been completed within the past five years. The complete report, with the tester's observations, the tests given, the scores, the explanation of findings and the recommendations, must be submitted as a single attachment with the application.

What the grade rule means

A ten- or twelve-year-old with a WISC-V General Ability Index of 150 is clearly in Davidson's population, but that score alone does not make them eligible: an SAT, ACT, PSAT 8/9 or PreACT result meeting the grade's thresholds is still needed. The IQ report then strengthens the file. For students in ninth grade and above, the IQ route opens fully, as an alternative to the SAT or ACT.

The WISC-V rules reward a specific profile. A student whose full-scale IQ is pulled down by processing speed or working memory can still qualify on the General Ability Index or on two of the reasoning indices.

Beyond the score

Eligibility is only the first filter. The Academy considers every score and invites to the Academic Readiness Assessment the students who show overall readiness, then asks for 80 percent on a day of curriculum-based work. A very high IQ with weak expository writing or mathematics below Algebra I readiness will struggle at that stage. The report's own observations and recommendations often point to what needs work.
