UWCSEA fees and UWC scholarships

UWCSEA publishes its fees per student and per year, reviewed annually and billed in three terms. For 2026/27, the bill has three layers: tuition, which rises with the grade, a development levy charged to every student, and, for boarders, a residential fee.

The annual fees

2026/27, per yearTuition
K1 to Grade 1S$39,069
Grades 2 to 5S$40,743
Grades 6 to 8S$46,866
Grades 9 and 10S$47,313
Grades 11 and 12S$49,926
  • Development levy: S$9,537 in the first year, then S$5,166 a year, for the upkeep and renewal of the campuses.
  • Boarding: S$45,288 a year on top of the school fees, plus an amenities fund of S$348.

A Grade 11 boarder in a first year at UWCSEA therefore faces about S$105,000 in fees, before extras.

Before the first day

  • Application fee, non-refundable: S$627 for a day application to one campus, S$1,254 for both campuses, S$841 for a boarding application, which always covers both.
  • Enrolment fee: a one-off, non-refundable S$4,992.
  • Acceptance: paying the enrolment fee and the first term's fees accepts the offer. UWCSEA bills a term ahead instead of taking a deposit, so new families are typically billed for the second term before their child starts.

The fee follows the place

At UWCSEA, the place is decided on the file, the statement, English and, for older students, the Virtual Immersion Programme. I prepare each of them with the family.

What comes on top

Outdoor education is part of the programme and charged separately: for example, a six-day trip to Thailand in Grade 8 cost about S$1,937 at 2024/25 prices, and Grade 9 expeditions of 7 to 18 days between S$1,700 and S$6,000. From Grade 6, students need their own MacBook. IB examination fees, about S$1,200 to S$1,800 depending on subjects, are billed in Term 3. Uniform, school buses and canteen meals are extra too.

UWC scholarships

Full and partial scholarships exist at every UWC school and college, but UWCSEA does not award them itself. They are allocated through the more than 150 UWC national committees around the world, which select students on promise and potential, assess the family's means, and set the amount according to need. A family interested in a scholarship contacts the national committee of its home country. For Singaporean students, the UWC Singapore national committee runs selection for citizens and permanent residents applying to the IB Diploma at UWCs worldwide. Scholars make up about 30% of UWCSEA's boarding community.

My view

For seven years, I have worked with around fifteen families a year aiming at very selective schools. At UWCSEA, the fee itself is rarely the barrier for the families I see; the competition for places is. For families who need help, the scholarship route goes through the national committee, with its own process and calendar.
