Lycée International de Saint-Germain-en-Laye entrance tests: what you need to know

Schools near Paris November 2026 5 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

Book a conversation

The Lycée International is a state school, and many families expect an administrative admission. It is the opposite: every enrolment first goes through admission into one of the fourteen sections, and that admission turns on a test. I follow students at the Lycée International, and every year I support around fifteen families towards the most sought-after bilingual schools in the Paris region.

What the procedure says

Admission is granted by the education authority, on the proposal of the head, once the student's ability to follow the section's lessons has been checked. That check is made on file and through an exam mainly in the section's language. In elementary school, the test is oral or written depending on age; in collège and lycée, there is a written and an oral test. Every procedure begins with tests run by the section itself.

Two examples. In the American Section, students from sixième to terminale sit a written exam in English, in groups, lasting 45 to 90 minutes depending on age; selected candidates then have a 30-minute interview with a teacher. In primary, the assessment is individual, 30 to 60 minutes. In the British Section, collège and lycée students sit written English comprehension and composition exams, then an oral.

Preparing for the section test

I prepare each child for the test of the section they are aiming for, written and spoken, with work timed to the date of their test. Let's start with a first conversation about your child.

What the sections do not say

The British Section says it plainly: it does not publish past papers, because it wants to measure natural aptitude and language, not "coached" knowledge. No section publishes the level expected at each age. Yet that is what decides, and it is what I know through my students.

Why bilingual children fail

Native speaking, lagging writing

The sections require native or near-native language, written and spoken. A child who speaks English at home but has always been schooled in French often writes well below their age in that language.

Is your child's writing at the level of a native speaker their age? Tell me about them: I measure it before the test.

Reading

Vocabulary and reading ability are among the criteria. A child who reads little in the section's language pays for it in written comprehension.

The oral after the written test

Selected candidates sit an oral. A child who has never talked about themselves, their reading, what they love, in that language, facing an adult, loses their footing.

When to start

Example: the American Section round for September 2027. Applications from 1 December 2026 to 8 January 2027 for sixième to terminale, and until 22 January 2027 for nursery to CM2; written exam on 27 January 2027 on site, 23 January online; primary tests between 22 February and 2 April 2027.

A writing gap takes several months to close: you need to know where the child stands from September.
