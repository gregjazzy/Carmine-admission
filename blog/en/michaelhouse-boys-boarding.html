Boarding at Michaelhouse

Many boarding schools also take day pupils, or let weekly boarders leave on Fridays. Michaelhouse does neither. Every boy boards, for five years, from E Block to A Block, and that is its central strength: a shared identity that is not diluted by day boys leaving at four o'clock or weekly boarders leaving at the end of the week.

Ten Houses around six quadrangles

A boy belongs first to his House. There are ten, laid out around six interlinked red-brick quadrangles covered in ivy, so that walking from one part of the school to another means crossing other Houses. Friendships therefore form across Houses as easily as within them. Each House is run by a team: a Housemaster who lives close to the boys, assistants, a House Mother, tutors and interns, who know each boy personally.

Seniors who look after juniors

Within the House, the long-standing Cop and Cack system pairs older boys with younger ones, as mentors. A new E Block boy arrives with someone a few years ahead of him who is responsible for showing him how things work. Beyond the House, there is a counselling department and a medical centre open 24 hours a day.

Teachers who live on campus

The whole academic staff is resident. A boy's teachers are also the adults he sees at matches, in the dining room and in the evening, which lets them know his ambitions and difficulties beyond what a classroom shows. For a boy who works better when someone notices him, this changes a great deal.

The afternoons and weekends

The campus covers 680 hectares of the KwaZulu-Natal Midlands. Boys can choose among sixteen sports, with eleven playing fields, two artificial hockey surfaces, a cricket oval, pools, courts and a canoeing dam, and Michaelhouse fields more teams on a Saturday than most of its competitors. Seven cultural programmes run alongside: four marimba bands, an orchestra and jazz band, choirs, debating, chess, theatre and art, from painting to welding. Chapel is part of the week; the school's values are Christian, its admission policy non-sectarian.

Testing the idea before committing

Michaelhouse offers two ways for a family to try full boarding before the decision. At Mums@Michaelhouse, mothers of registered boys spend a night at the school and meet current mothers. At Weekend@Michaelhouse, registered boys explore the campus, share meals and sleep in a boarding House. How a boy comes back from that night is often the most useful information a family will get.

After the five years

More than 6,900 Old Boys are alive today, bound together in what Michaelhouse families know as the Club for Life. Each boy who leaves is paired with a mentor who matriculated within the previous seven years, to help with the first steps after school.
