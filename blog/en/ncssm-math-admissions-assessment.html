The NCSSM Math Assessment

Every applicant to NCSSM's Residential program, in Durham or Morganton, takes the NCSSM Mathematics Admissions Assessment. It used to be called Discovery Day. Online applicants do not take it, and their rubric tops out at 93 points rather than 102.

The format

The day itself is now short: several testing sessions run through the day, students leave as soon as their session ends, and there is no campus tour.

Why it is harder than the syllabus suggests

A strong 10th grader has long since finished Algebra 1, and may assume the test is easy. The difficulty lies elsewhere. Thirty questions in forty minutes leaves about eighty seconds each, without a calculator. Fractions, negative numbers, exponents, area and angle work have to be quick and exact, and many students have not done them by hand for two years. The test rewards fluency and reasoning on familiar material, not advanced knowledge.

Fast, exact, without a calculator

How to prepare

  • Return to the foundations. Work through pre-algebra and geometry by hand: fraction arithmetic, proportional reasoning, linear equations, properties of shapes, angles and areas.
  • Work without a calculator. From December, put the calculator away for all practice.
  • Practise under time. Sets of questions at eighty seconds each, then a full forty-minute run, so the pace feels normal on the day.
  • Read every question twice. Most errors on this kind of test come from misreading, not from not knowing.

Where it fits in the rubric

The math assessment is one criterion among several on a rubric scored by district committees, alongside academic rigour in 9th and 10th grade, essays, evaluations and evidence of initiative. A strong result supports an application; it does not carry it alone. But it is also the one criterion a student can still improve substantially between January and February of 10th grade.
