Westminster School bursaries: who is eligible, and how it works

Free and subsidised places have been part of Westminster for generations, and the school still spends about £2 million a year on bursaries across the Under School and the senior school. A bursary can cover up to 100 percent of day fees, and a full bursary also pays for uniform, travel to school, equipment, books and compulsory expeditions, with pastoral support to help new bursary pupils settle. But eligibility is defined tightly, and the families who would gain most are sometimes the ones who learn the rules too late.

Who is eligible

All of the following must be true:

  • the child has the right to live and study in the UK when starting at the school;
  • at least one parent and the child have lived in the UK continuously for two years at the point of application;
  • the parents live inside the M25 at the point of application and throughout the child's time at the school;
  • the parents are British or Irish citizens, have settled or pre-settled status lasting until the child finishes, or have indefinite leave to remain.

In practice, Westminster's bursaries are for London families. A family relocating from abroad, or living outside the M25, is not eligible, however strong the child.

Where bursaries are offered

Bursaries are available at three entry points: 11+ (Year 7 at the Under School, the only Under School entry with bursary places), 13+ and 16+. They are for day places. Boarding bursaries are not given, except combined with a King's Scholarship.

Talent, then support

At Westminster, a bursary follows a place won on academic merit. We prepare able children for the 11+, 13+ and 16+ assessments where those places are decided.

The process, entry point by entry point

Everything begins at registration: the intention to apply for a bursary must be stated then, which triggers an eligibility check.

EntrySteps after registration
11+Part A initial assessment; Part B in depth once ISEB results are known; a home visit, in person or virtual. The child must still reach the January papers on merit.
13+Part B in-depth assessment if the child is offered a place in February of Year 6; because bursaries are indicated two years ahead, circumstances are reassessed in Year 8.
16+Part A; Part B only for candidates invited to interview; a parent interview in person on the candidate's interview day in late November.

Bursaries are reviewed every year and can rise or fall with circumstances. Those awarded at 11+ or 13+ can continue to the end of Year 13, subject to satisfactory academic progress. If a bursary holder also wins a Music or King's Scholarship, the scholarship's value is absorbed into the bursary.

Fees that can be waived

A family applying for a bursary that cannot pay the registration fee or the entrance fee may have them waived while the bursary is considered. A 100 percent bursary holder does not pay the £2,400 entrance fee at 13+.

If circumstances change later

For families already at the school who suffer a significant, unexpected change in finances, Westminster may offer temporary help through the means-tested George Herbert Hardship Fund.

What the bursary does not change

A bursary never lowers the academic bar. At every entry point, the child must first earn the place: the ISEB Pre-Tests, Westminster's own papers and interview at 11+ and 13+, the four subject exams and interview day at 16+. That is where preparation matters, and where a bright child from any background can show what Westminster is looking for.
