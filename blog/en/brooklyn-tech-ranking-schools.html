Families preparing for Brooklyn Tech often focus on the test and forget that they are building two lists at the same time. Both matter, because together they decide which school the student attends in September.

The two lists

How the specialized list works

The SHSAT results are ordered from highest to lowest. Going down that list, each student is placed in the highest school on their own ranking that still has seats. If the first choice is full, the second is tried, then the third, until the eight schools are filled. A student who leaves Brooklyn Tech off the list can never receive an offer there.

Where to place Brooklyn Tech

Brooklyn Tech suits students who want a broad, technical education and the chance to specialise later through its majors, from engineering and architecture to finance, law or neuroscience. For a family living in Brooklyn, the commute may also weigh in its favour. Rank it where the student genuinely places it, not by cutoff: a lower cutoff does not make it a "safety", and ranking a school first does not lower the chances elsewhere.

The general application is not a formality

If no specialized offer comes, or if the student declines one, the general match is final, and the student attends that school. That list deserves the same care: schools the student would be glad to attend, in a sincere order.

Accepting the offer

A specialized offer must be answered by the deadline or it is lost. Brooklyn Tech's guidance to admitted families is simple: accept the offer in the parents' MySchools account; detailed summer emails follow.
