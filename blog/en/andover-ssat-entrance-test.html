Testing for Andover

Andover requires a test score from every applicant, and it lets the family choose which test. That combination is less common than it sounds: some schools make testing optional, others prescribe a test by grade. At Andover the score is compulsory and the choice is free, which turns the first question from "should we test?" into "which test suits this child?".

Scores are sent directly by the testing agency. Andover publishes no minimum score for any of them.

A fact families enjoy: Andover helped create the SSAT

Andover counts the SSAT, along with Advanced Placement and School Year Abroad, among the initiatives it helped launch. The test most of its applicants take is, in a sense, partly its own. That does not make it a formality. In a pool of this strength, the score has to sit comfortably beside the rest of the file, and for a child at a school abroad it is often the only piece directly comparable with every other applicant.

How to choose between them

For a ninth-grade applicant, the real choice is between the Upper Level SSAT and the Upper Level ISEE. The two do not reward the same strengths. The SSAT's verbal section leans heavily on vocabulary and analogies, which favours wide readers in English. The ISEE has its own balance of verbal reasoning, reading and mathematics. Some children score noticeably better on one than the other. The only way to know is a timed practice test of each, before registering.

For older applicants, the PSAT, SAT or ACT can make more sense, especially if the child will need one of them for college anyway.

Registration traps

  • Late fees. Late registration charges for the SSAT and ISEE are substantial, and they are not covered by the test fee waiver.
  • Fee waivers. Families applying for financial aid can request a test fee waiver through the Andover portal.
  • Codes. Entering Andover's code at registration sends the score directly. A score sent by the family is not the same thing.
  • A second sitting. Leaving room for one means the first sitting has to happen in the autumn, well before February 1.

The English test, on top

A student at a school outside the United States that is not an English-medium school also takes the TOEFL iBT, the IELTS or the Duolingo English Test. Most international students admitted to Andover score at least 100, 7.5 or 135. For a thirteen- or fourteen-year-old schooled in another language, these are demanding levels, and they are reached over months, not weeks. For such a child, the English score can weigh as much as the SSAT.

What the room looks like

For the classes of 2025 to 2027, the middle half of Andover students scored between 1370 and 1530 on the SAT, with an average of 1438, and between 28 and 34 on the ACT. Andover offers only three official AP courses, yet 212 students of the class of 2026 sat 927 AP exams, 85% of them scoring a 4 or a 5. These students prepared for those exams largely on their own, which says something about the level of the classroom an admitted child joins.
