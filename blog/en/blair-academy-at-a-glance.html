Figures do not choose a school, but they describe it. Blair Academy publishes its own, and read together they sketch a particular kind of place: small, residential, selective and close-knit.

What the numbers say

With fewer than 500 students and a ratio of five students per teacher, Blair is small enough for every adult to know every student. The fact that nine in ten teachers live on campus means those relationships continue beyond the classroom, in the dormitories, on the playing fields and at meals. That matches the school's mission: to know its students so they can understand themselves and the world more fully.

The mission behind the numbers

Blair states its mission plainly: to know its students so that it can help them understand themselves and the world more fully. Through strong relationships with faculty and experiences designed to challenge them, students are meant to grow as skilled learners and engaged members of a diverse community, and to leave not only as prepared undergraduates but as global citizens contributing to the greater good. The small size and the resident faculty are the practical means to that end.

Campus and activities

The 463 acres hold athletic facilities on a scale few schools of this size offer: seven international squash courts, indoor and outdoor tennis courts, a 25-yard indoor pool, a nine-hole golf course, eight athletic fields and a three-mile lake for crew. Blair fields 32 varsity teams, and its arts range from film production and digital music to ceramics, painting and photography.

Selectivity and access

A 15 % acceptance rate makes Blair selective; a 36 % share of students on aid, with 100 % of demonstrated need met, makes it accessible. The two go together: the school chooses students first, then makes sure they can attend.
