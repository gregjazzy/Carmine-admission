The ISF Academy distinguishes two kinds of support. Scholarships reward academic excellence or exceptional talent; the Financial Aid Program reduces fees according to need. A family can be concerned by one, the other or both, and the two follow different logics.

I help families understand where their child fits. Here is how the system works.

Three scholarship pathways

PathwayGradesFocus
Academic PathwayGrades 6 to 11Academic excellence
Special Talent PathwayGrades 6 to 11Art, drama, music and sports
S.K. & Margaret Wong ScholarshipGrade 12The arts

The scholarship programme is open to all students who demonstrate academic excellence, exceptional talent and an outstanding commitment to the school community, and a single application form covers every pathway. That last criterion matters: talent alone is not the whole case, and the school looks for students who will give to the community as well as excel.

Financial aid alongside

Need-based aid works separately. The Financial Aid Program grants fee remission from 20 to 100 percent, and the admissions assessment is need-blind, so a request does not affect the decision. Families can apply at any point in the process, but only those whose complete application arrives before the financial aid deadline learn the outcome with their acceptance package.

Building a scholarship case

For the academic pathway, the evidence is mostly in the record: school reports, results in external competitions or examinations, and the level shown in the admissions assessments themselves. For the special talent pathway, it is in what the child has achieved and sustained: graded music examinations, performances, exhibitions, representative sport, with dates and levels stated plainly. In both cases, commitment to a community, a team, an orchestra, a club the child has helped run, belongs in the case, since the school names it as a criterion. A short, precise list of real achievements reads better than a long one padded with participation.

Scholarship and admission

A scholarship does not replace admission. Every applicant must pass the assessments in Chinese and English, the orals in both languages and the parent meeting. For a talented child whose Chinese or English is below grade level, the first task is the language, not the scholarship case.
