Aiglon College: full scholarships, who gets them and how

It is one of Aiglon's most distinctive features. Every year, the school awards a small number of fully funded scholarships to students whose families could not afford an Aiglon education. Since 1991 it has welcomed 134 scholars, and half of current scholars are funded through the generosity of parents and alumni.

What a scholarship covers

An Aiglon scholarship covers everything: tuition, exams, textbooks, university application support, boarding, meals, healthcare, skiing, expeditions, school trips, and an allowance for personal expenses, uniforms, technology and private lessons. The student lives the school exactly like everyone else.

An application worthy of a scholarship

I prepare your child to show what Aiglon expects from a scholar: excellent results, genuine commitment, a taste for challenge. I help them write their personal statement and prepare for the interview. My aim: that they are one of the few students the school chooses to welcome.

Who they are for

Scholarships are merit-based and means-assessed. The school looks for students with a proven record of high academic achievement, involvement in a range of activities, and a commitment to self-improvement and challenge. It assesses them like all applicants, against its principles: challenge, respect, responsibility, diversity and service. Priority goes to students entering Year 11 or Year 12; applicants must be at least 15 on 1 September for Year 11, and between 16 and 18 for Year 12. The scholarship is kept until graduation, subject to an annual review.

The calendar

For August entry, the first step is an online form due by mid-November: personal information, educational background, interests, aspirations, a personal statement and reports from the last two full academic years. Applicants who progress are contacted in December, and the family must then provide its financial information. Students linked to a partner school, organisation or charity apply through that partner.
