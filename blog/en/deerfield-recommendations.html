Deerfield recommendations

Book a conversation

Deerfield asks for more teacher voices than most boarding schools. Where many stop at English and math, Deerfield adds a third academic teacher, and all three must be current. Add the principal or counselor, and an optional additional letter, and the recommendations become the largest single part of the file written by people other than the family.

The five possible letters

  • Current math teacher: required.
  • Current English teacher: required.
  • Current third academic teacher: required; the family chooses the subject.
  • Principal or counselor: required. In some schools the same person also sends the grades, which Deerfield accepts.
  • Additional letter: optional, from a teacher past or present who can speak to the child's best qualities as a learner and classroom participant. Instructions are in the Deerfield Admission Portal.

The request links go out through Gateway, and Deerfield suggests sending them in the fall.

Grades that come with them

For a ninth-grade applicant, the school sends the first marking period of the current year and last year's grades. From tenth grade to the postgraduate year, it sends the first marking period and all previous high school grades.

The right voices, asked at the right time

I help families decide which third teacher and which additional letter will add the most, and how to ask, so that the recommendations show the learner Deerfield is looking for.

Choosing the third teacher

The subject matters less than the teacher. The best choice is the teacher who has seen the child work hardest, revise most, or change most this year, whether in history, science or a language. A teacher who can describe a specific moment, a question the child asked or a project they rebuilt, gives Deerfield the picture it is looking for: a student who learns. A teacher in a prestigious subject who knows the child only from tests gives much less.

When the additional letter is worth it

The optional letter is valuable when a teacher outside the required three has seen something the others have not: last year's English teacher who watched the child's writing transform, a science teacher who supervised an independent project. It is not the place for a family friend or a coach writing about character. Deerfield is specific: the letter should speak to the child as a learner and classroom participant.

No English or math class this year

Some students, often in older grades or unusual programs, are not taking English or math this year. Deerfield's answer is simple: request the recommendation through Gateway from last year's teacher in that subject, and inform the admission office of the situation. It is better to do this early and explain clearly than to send nothing and hope.

Teachers abroad

A teacher outside the American system often writes a few polite lines in the style of a report card. Asked early, and told that an American school wants specific observations about how the child thinks and works, the same teacher can write something genuinely useful. That conversation belongs in September, not in January.

How I organise the letters

My students include several at Deerfield, and for seven years I have worked with around fifteen families a year aiming at the most selective schools in the United States, England and Switzerland. In September I list with the family who will write each letter and what each should be able to say, then help prepare the requests. Later, I read the essays against what the teachers are likely to write, so that the file does not tell the same story four times.
