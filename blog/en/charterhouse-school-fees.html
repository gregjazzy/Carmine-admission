Charterhouse fees

Charterhouse, Godalming, from the air. Photo Tim Sheerman-Chase, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 4.0

UK boarding schools November 2026 5 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

Book a conversation

For 2026/27, Charterhouse charges £21,015 a term for boarders and £16,369 a term for day pupils. With three terms in the school year, that is roughly £63,000 a year to board and £49,100 as a day pupil. These are the only fee figures worth quoting; the rest of the cost depends on the family's choices.

The fees at a glance

2026/27Per termPer year (three terms)
Boarding£21,015about £63,045

A full schedule of fees is available on request from the school. Families should ask for it before accepting an offer, to see what is billed separately.

Before the first term

  • Registration: £450, including VAT, non-refundable, for every entry point.
  • Deposit: paid with the signed acceptance form when an offer is made; at 13+ through the Year 6 or 7 routes, within eight weeks of the offer.
  • 11+ route: one registration fee covers both Charterhouse and the chosen prep school, Edgeborough, Windlesham House or Amesbury, and admitted families sign one contract and pay one deposit.

A place is not for sale

At Charterhouse, the fees do not separate candidates; the Pre-Test, the assessment day and the school reference do. I prepare children for each, at the level the school expects.

Bursaries

Charterhouse has supported pupils whose families could not otherwise afford the fees for over 400 years. Bursaries today are means-tested, depend on funds being available, and are reviewed every year against changes in the parents' circumstances, so an award can be adjusted. Enquiries go to the Director of Admissions. Pupils on bursaries are full members of the school community.

Scholarships are something else

Scholarships at Charterhouse are awarded on merit, through open competition, in academic subjects, the arts, music, drama, design engineering and sport. They recognise excellence and can be supported by a bursary where there is need. A family should not count on a scholarship to pay the fees; it is an honour first.

Why the price is not the question

I have pupils at Charterhouse, and for seven years I have helped around fifteen families a year towards the most selective schools in England, the United States and Switzerland. At this level, the fee is rarely what decides. The Pre-Test result, the way a child takes part in the assessment day and what their school writes are what decide, and that is where I work.
