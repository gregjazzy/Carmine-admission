How to get into Harvard-Westlake

St. Saviour's Chapel, Harvard-Westlake, Los Angeles. Photo Keithready, Wikimedia Commons, public domain

Harvard-Westlake is a day school in Los Angeles, with a middle school and an upper school on two campuses that work as one school. It receives more applications from qualified students than it has places, every year, and recommends that every family apply to other schools as well. The numbers explain why.

Where the places are

GradeNew students
7thAbout 215, the main entry
8thOnly when a place opens, which is rare
9thAbout 90
10thAbout 5 to 10, depending on enrolment
11th and 12thOnly in special circumstances, such as relocation to Los Angeles

Students admitted in 7th grade move to the upper school automatically if they remain in good academic and behavioural standing.

The application, step by step

  1. Application form, with a fee that is waived for families applying for financial aid. Submitted by 13 November, it guarantees an interview invitation for 7th and 9th grade applicants.
  2. Student questionnaire: short answers about the applicant and their interests, due at least two days before the interview.
  3. Interview, on a weekday during school hours, in person with a student-led tour of the middle school campus, or on Zoom.
  4. ISEE, a single sitting between 1 August and 15 January.
  5. Recommendations from the current English teacher, math teacher and an administrator, completed between 1 November and the deadline, plus a transcript release form.
  6. Deadline for the application and financial aid: 15 January at 11:59 pm Pacific time.
  7. Decisions on the online portal, 12 March at 5 pm.

What decides

No single factor decides admission. Harvard-Westlake looks for kind, curious and engaged students eager to contribute to the school, and competitive applicants have a strong academic record. The ISEE is one element of a holistic reading, alongside grades, teachers' recommendations, the questionnaire and the interview.

Aid

About 21% of students receive need-based aid, and the average grant covers 71% of tuition. Families must apply for aid at the time of admission: students who do not apply then are not eligible later, apart from a small fund for unforeseen reversals.
