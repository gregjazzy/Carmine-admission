Step five of the Montgomery Bell application combines the student interview with three supplemental documents, all due on February 1. They are less visible than the ISEE or the shadow visit, and families tend to treat them as paperwork. They are not: together they are how MBA hears from the people who see the boy every day.

Three documents, three viewpoints

DocumentWho provides itWhat MBA learns
Transcript request formThe current schoolThe academic record over time
Teacher evaluation formA current teacherHow the boy works and behaves in class
Character Skills SnapshotThe boy, onlineA standardized view of qualities such as resilience and curiosity

The transcript

The family sends MBA's transcript request form to the current school, which returns the record directly. Nothing to prepare, except one thing: making the request early enough that a busy school office sends it before February 1.

The teacher evaluation

This is the piece most worth thinking about. The best evaluator is not necessarily the teacher of the hardest subject, but the one who knows the boy best this year and has seen him work through difficulty. MBA's Portrait of a Graduate names resilience, consistency, curiosity and integrity among its ideals; a teacher who can describe those in a specific boy, with specific moments, writes the evaluation MBA wants to read.

Every piece in its place

I help families choose the right teacher and plan the supplemental materials, so that the file reads as one consistent portrait of the boy, at the standard of the most selective schools.

The Character Skills Snapshot

The Character Skills Snapshot is an online assessment the boy completes himself. It looks at character qualities rather than academic knowledge. It cannot be crammed for, and trying to guess the "right" answers is a mistake: the result is read beside what the teacher writes and what the school sees on the shadow day and in the interview. Honest answers, given calmly and without rushing, are the right preparation.

Not sure which teacher should write the evaluation? Tell me about your son.

How the pieces fit together

MBA meets the boy twice, in the shadow visit and the interview, and reads about him three times, in these documents. A file is strongest when all five tell a coherent story: the teacher describes a boy who keeps going, the Snapshot points the same way, and in the interview he tells, in his own words, about a time he did exactly that.
