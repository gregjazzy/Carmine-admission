The Discovery program

The SHSAT score is the only way into the eight testing Specialized High Schools, with one exception. The Discovery program offers a summer route for certain students from disadvantaged backgrounds who took the SHSAT and scored within a set range just below the cutoff. Students who complete it successfully can enrol at a Specialized High School in September.

How it works

  1. The student takes the SHSAT in the fall and lists the schools they want.
  2. Eligible students find Discovery information in the SHSAT results section of their offer letter in March. Only those students can apply.
  3. They apply to a Discovery program at a school they listed on their SHSAT registration, and can participate only in the program on their application.
  4. They attend the summer program, about three to five weeks long, at that school or a nearby site.
  5. Students who meet the program's completion requirements can enrol in September.

Who can be invited

Eligibility is set by the city and includes economic criteria. Public and charter school students must attend a school with an Economic Need Index of 60% or more; students at non-public schools or who are homeschooled must live in a high-poverty area. Other criteria also apply, and a student must have scored within the range set for that year. Families cannot sign up for Discovery; it comes by invitation only.

What the summer asks

Each school sets its own program and requirements. In summer 2026, examples included classes in English, math and leadership skills; history, English, math, science and Spanish at the High School of American Studies; algebra, geometry, English, study skills and an introduction to Russian at Staten Island Tech. Completion conditions typically combined high attendance, often 90%, with completion of assignments, and sometimes passing grades or teacher recommendations.

Why the ranked list matters here too

A Discovery invitation applies only to the Specialized High Schools a student listed when registering for the SHSAT. A student who left a school off the list cannot be considered for its Discovery program. It is one more reason to list every testing school the student would truly attend.
