Institut auf dem Rosenberg: Talent & Enrichment and boarding life

Institut auf dem Rosenberg, St. Gallen. Photo Rene Nueesch, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 3.0

Boarding schools November 2026 4 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

Book a conversation

At Rosenberg, an essential part of education happens outside the classroom. The Talent & Enrichment programme is not a list of after-school clubs: the school presents it as a school within a school, with its own courses, partners and standards. I have helped students win places at Rosenberg and Aiglon, and fifteen at Le Rosey, and it is often this programme that wins families over, and later gives their children's university applications their strength.

A school within a school

The catalogue counts more than a hundred courses, from fashion design to artificial intelligence, which students choose according to their interests. Many are designed with leading companies and experts in their fields, and some take the form of interactive university-level courses. Athletics, in the broad sense, change with the seasons. Even the youngest, in primary school, take part, to discover the world beyond the classroom.

Beyond the school week

The programme spills over into weekends: excursions in Switzerland, husky sledding, and several times a year Talent & Enrichment trips to Rome, London or Paris. On community weekends, the whole school works together on projects around philanthropy or sustainability. The spirit sought is entrepreneurial: engaging passionately with the real world rather than collecting activities.

Why the programme matters at admission

A school that invests so much outside the classroom looks for students who will make the most of it. At the interview as in the personal statement, a candidate who shows a real passion, pursued over time, however modest, speaks directly to what Rosenberg wants to build. A child who has never chosen anything outside school will find it harder to convince the school they can make use of a hundred courses on offer.

Does your child have a passion they struggle to put forward? Let's talk: it is an asset at Rosenberg.

Why it matters for university

Rosenberg says it again and again: achievements outside the classroom, programme courses, internships, awards, experiences, have become admission requirements at leading universities. This is where a Rosenberg student can stand out from another brilliant applicant. Provided their choices are coherent: a path that runs from robotics to an internship at a technology company, then to a personal project, tells a story. Ten unrelated courses, however impressive, tell none.

Choosing with a common thread

The risk, with such a catalogue, is scattering. My advice to families is to choose a common thread each year, linked to the intended studies or to a deep passion, and to build around it. That thread will later sit at the heart of university essays and interviews. It also makes the years at Rosenberg richer: a student who knows why they choose a course gets far more from it than one who fills a timetable.

And boarding life

The rest of life at Rosenberg follows the same tailored spirit. Students can access their residences all day, meals can be individualised, and when a student feels unwell, their house tutor and the health coordinator arrange for them to see the school doctor, with specialists and regional hospitals a few minutes away. There is no school uniform but a dress code: formal dress from Monday to Friday during lessons, breakfast and lunch, suits for boys and elegant trousers, skirts or dresses for girls, then smart casual in the evenings and at weekends. It is a demanding framework, but one designed around each student. For a child coming from a school with a strict uniform and fixed timetables, this blend of freedom and formality is often the biggest adjustment of the first weeks.
