EIB de la Jonchère: a bilingual school from nursery to troisième

Paris schools November 2026 5 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

Book a conversation

EIB de la Jonchère, in La Celle-Saint-Cloud, in the Yvelines, about twenty minutes from La Défense, has a particularity in the EIB network: it keeps the same bilingual model from the first year of nursery to troisième (age 14). Half of teaching is in English, half in French, with the French curriculum and English based on the Cambridge programme.

What is required on entry

In nursery, no level is required. In CP, the child must master one of the two languages; from CE1 to CM2, both, otherwise they join an immersion class. In collège, requirements are set: A2 in French and English for sixième, B1 for cinquième, B2 for quatrième and troisième. Complete beginners can still enter sixième or cinquième through an intensive programme, with a "Booster" programme for the weaker language, at extra cost.

Preparing for La Jonchère

At La Jonchère, the assessment decides the class and track, from the second year of nursery. I prepare children to join on the track that suits them, without unnecessary reinforcement.

How admission works

Five steps: a first contact, a meeting with the admissions officer, on site or by video, the online file, then the child's assessment with the head, in French, English and maths, from the second year of nursery, after paying the €300 application fee. A final interview with the head precedes enrolment, which is only guaranteed after payment of the registration fee and the first term.

The cost

In 2026-2027: €14,100 a year in nursery and elementary, €16,995 in collège and in immersion classes from CE1 to CM2. A door-to-door school bus is offered as an option, within about 15 km, and a 25% reduction applies to the third child.

And after troisième

Students go on to all kinds of lycées. EIB Lycée Étoile, in the network, admits them on file, like other lycées. The balance between French and international families, roughly half and half, makes it a school where a child arriving from abroad settles quickly.

I follow students at EIB, and every year I support families settling in west Paris. For La Jonchère, I measure the child's real level in both languages against the requirements of their year, which avoids an unnecessary reinforcement programme, then prepare them for the assessment.
