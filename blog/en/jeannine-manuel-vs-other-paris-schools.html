École Jeannine Manuel vs other Paris schools: how to choose

EJM Admission October 2026 9 min read

When a family mentions Jeannine Manuel to me, another school almost always comes up in the same sentence. EIB, because it is bilingual too. Saint-Germain-en-Laye, because it is a state school. The American School, because the family may move back to the United States in three years. Or the École alsacienne, because a friend sent her children there and speaks highly of it.

I prepare children for the Jeannine Manuel entry tests, and with their parents I also build the list of other schools. We make these comparisons in the very first meeting. They are rarely decided on reputation, which all these schools have. They are decided on the child, on how long the family will stay in Paris, and on where the child will study after secondary school.

Is Jeannine Manuel private or state?

This is the first thing parents search for, and it explains most of the comparisons. École Jeannine Manuel is a private school that has held a contract with the French state for more than sixty years. In France, a private school "sous contrat" follows the national curriculum and has its teachers paid by the state, which keeps fees low. Jeannine Manuel follows the French programme except in English, science and Chinese, where it has its own. Students sit the French baccalauréat in its international version (the BFI), and the school also offers the IB Diploma in the final years, as a separate, more expensive track.

The contract is what makes the price. A year of primary school costs just over €10,000, while the English-speaking schools west of Paris charge three times as much. That is why Jeannine Manuel draws French families, bi-national families and expats alike: it is bilingual for the price of a French private school. The school describes itself as highly oversubscribed, and its waiting list is not ranked, so there is no such thing as being "first on the list".

Jeannine Manuel or EIB

This is the closest comparison, because on paper the two schools look alike: private, bilingual, under state contract for the French track, with the BFI or the IB in the final years and similar fees. They differ in shape. Jeannine Manuel is a single Paris school from kindergarten to the baccalauréat, where a child spends their whole schooling in one place. EIB is a group of eight schools, from the 7th to the 17th arrondissement and out to La Jonchère, and its primary schools do not all practise the same kind of bilingualism: Grenelle, Trocadéro and Wagram teach half in French and half in English, while Lamartine and Monceau follow the French programme with English added.

Two differences matter in practice. The IB is much cheaper at EIB than at Jeannine Manuel. And Jeannine Manuel runs an adaptation programme for children who speak little or no French, from 2nd to 9th Grade, which often makes it the best way in for a family arriving from abroad. For a family already living in Paris, I almost always advise applying to both.

Jeannine Manuel or a state international section

The Lycée international de Saint-Germain-en-Laye and the international sections in Paris, at Balzac, Camille-Sée or Montaigne, lead to the same diploma as Jeannine Manuel: the French international baccalauréat. They are state schools, so the French part of the teaching is free. Only sections run by associations, as in Saint-Germain, charge fees, which remain far below private-school prices.

They do not recruit the same child, though. An international section looks for a pupil who already has a second language of culture, strong enough to study literature and history in it, and checks this with a written and an oral exam. Jeannine Manuel is more open to a French-speaking child whose English is still being built, provided they can keep up. I compare Jeannine Manuel and Saint-Germain in detail in a separate article; for the Paris sections, see the British international section.

Jeannine Manuel or an English-speaking school

The American School of Paris, the International School of Paris, Marymount and the British School of Paris are not direct rivals of Jeannine Manuel. They teach in English, follow an American or British programme or the IB, and cost three to four times as much, before the entry fees added in the first year.

The choice between the two worlds depends above all on the system in which the child will go on studying. A family moving on to London or New York in two or three years is often better off staying in an English-language programme, so the child does not change systems twice. A family settling for the long term, or whose child will study in France, gains from a school that leads to the French baccalauréat. Jeannine Manuel keeps both doors open: according to the Good Schools Guide, its class of 2024 went on to France (65 students), the United Kingdom (63) and the United States (23). Each of these schools has its own page on the blog: American School of Paris, ISP and Marymount, British School of Paris.

Jeannine Manuel or a leading French school

Some families hesitate between Jeannine Manuel and the École alsacienne, Stanislas or a top state lycée. These are excellent schools, but they are not bilingual. If English is not the main criterion, the comparison moves elsewhere: the school's climate, the room left for independence, the pressure in class. If English matters, none of them is an equivalent of Jeannine Manuel. I describe them one by one in Best schools in Paris.

How I decide with families

I start from three questions. How long will the family stay in Paris? Where will the child study after the baccalauréat? And what is the child's real level of English in reading and writing, not only in speaking? The third one surprises parents most. Many children speak very fluent English and still struggle with a long text, and that is exactly what the Jeannine Manuel tests measure. I see it in the children I prepare.

With those answers, we build a list of three or four schools in the right order, with a calendar for each. Applying only to Jeannine Manuel, at a school that describes itself as highly oversubscribed, is a risk I advise no one to take. This is the work we do at Carmine: assess the child, choose the schools with the family, then prepare the tests and interview for each one.

Example: the admissions round for September 2027. Jeannine Manuel closes applications on 12 January 2027 for families living in France, with no deadline for families moving from abroad. The American School opens admissions in October and handles them on a rolling basis. The International School of Paris reviews files from January, then continuously. Marymount advises applying in January or February for September. The École alsacienne takes applications from November to early January.
