UNIS Tutorial House: high school admission, grades 9 to 12

Book a conversation

The high school at UNIS is called Tutorial House, or Tut House, and runs from 9th to 12th grade (T1 to T4). Every applicant follows the same route: a 30-minute individual conversation with staff, usually paired with a 45-minute tour; the ISAAGNY form from current teachers and official school reports, due in January; an ISEE, SSAT or recent MAP score; and a decision on February 19, 2027, a little later than the rest of the school. The process is simple. The choice of grade is not.

Each grade is a different decision

Tutorial House is built around the IB Diploma, which students take in the last two years. That shapes every entry point.

  • 9th grade (T1). The natural entry. Students arrive at the start of the division, with two years to adjust before the IB begins, and the college counseling program already introduces families to university choices in T1.
  • 10th grade (T2). Still a sound entry. It is the year in which students plan their IB courses with counselors, so a new student needs to settle quickly and make informed choices by spring.
  • 11th grade (T3). The IB starts. A student entering here begins a demanding two-year program in a new school, in English, and is paired at the same time with a college counselor. Possible, but only for a student already working at a high level and comfortable with the subjects they will choose.
  • 12th grade (T4). Joining in the final year of the IB is rarely realistic, and families should talk to the admissions office before investing in an application.

The conversation is the real interview

Thirty minutes, one teenager, one or two adults. UNIS uses it to confirm readiness, which in practice means three things: can this student talk about their own learning, can they hold a real exchange in English, and will they take responsibility in a division that treats students as young adults and expects them to go to teachers and counselors themselves when something goes wrong.

The students who do well are rarely the ones with polished speeches. They are the ones who can explain why they liked a particular book or experiment, say what they found hard this year and what they did about it, and ask a question of their own about the school. A teenager who has lived in several countries has material that others do not, but often undersells it because nobody has asked them to put it into words.

The test at this level

For 9th to 12th grade, the ISEE is taken at the Upper Level: verbal reasoning, quantitative reasoning, reading comprehension and mathematics achievement, 161 questions in 2 hours 40 minutes, plus an unscored essay. The SSAT and recent MAP results are accepted on the same footing. For a student coming from a strong school in another system, mathematics is rarely the problem; timed reading and vocabulary in English often are, and they are what an ISEE preparation should target.

English and the IB

English is the language of instruction and the IB courses are demanding: sciences, history, economics, philosophy, psychology at university-level depth. A student who is still building academic English can be supported, but the honest question for an 11th-grade entrant is whether they can write an IB essay in English within months. In 9th grade there is time; in 11th there is very little. That is often the deciding argument for applying a year or two earlier than the family first planned.

On the other side, students with strong home languages have a real asset: the IB bilingual diploma, and UNIS teaches languages at Language A level for native speakers in several of them.

Calendar for September 2027

Autumn: Ravenna account, application, conversation and tour booked together, ISEE sat in October or November. Before the end of November: ISAAGNY form requested from current teachers. January: reports and scores in. February 19: decision.

How I prepare Tutorial House applicants

With teenagers, I work on the two things that move a high school file: an Upper Level ISEE score that matches the student's real ability, through timed reading, vocabulary and the quantitative sections; and a conversation in which they speak about their studies and interests clearly, without a script. I also help families decide the entry year with the IB timeline in mind, which is often the most valuable advice of all.
