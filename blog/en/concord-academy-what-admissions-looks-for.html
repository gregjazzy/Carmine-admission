What Concord Academy admissions looks for

Concord Academy calls itself an independent school for independent-minded students, and its admissions criteria follow from that. The committee does not publish score thresholds; it publishes a list of what it hopes to learn about each applicant.

The nine qualities, and where they show

QualityWhere the committee sees it
Academic performanceGrades, transcripts, SSAT or ISEE
Intellectual curiosityTeacher letters, student answers, interview
CharacterRecommendations, interview
MaturityInterview, counsellor letter
Community involvementStudent application, counsellor letter
Potential for growthTrends in grades, teacher letters
Talents, hobbies and passionsStudent and parent statements, optional supplements
Positive relationships with adults and peersRecommendations, interview
Individual expression and open-mindednessWritten answers, interview

The school behind the list

CA sets high standards but says intrinsic motivation takes a student further than anything else, so its curriculum is broad and flexible and students are encouraged to direct their own learning. Every student meets an advisor one-on-one each week. Its values are explicit: individual expression, common trust, equity, and being competitive with oneself while collaborative with others. The nine qualities describe a student who will use that freedom well.

What this means in practice

A student who has done well because they were told to may struggle to show curiosity in an interview. A student who has chased one interest of their own, read beyond a syllabus, built something or started a club has material for every part of the file. Potential for growth also matters: a record that improves, or a teacher who describes a student rising to a challenge, can count as much as a flawless transcript.

Small and large at once

CA presents itself as small enough to know everyone and large enough to find a niche, with more than 200 courses, over 100 clubs and affinity groups, a new performing arts centre and competitive athletics. Students who arrive with an interest tend to find a place to develop it.
