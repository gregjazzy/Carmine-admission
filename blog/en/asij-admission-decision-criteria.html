ASIJ publishes the criteria it uses to decide, and most of them are not about grades. A strong student can be refused for reasons that have nothing to do with the quality of their file, and a family that understands the criteria before applying can present its situation clearly instead of hoping the reports speak for themselves.

I help families in Tokyo and abroad read these criteria. Here they are.

The stated criteria

CriterionWhat it means in practice
Fit with the schoolThe family's educational goals and beliefs match ASIJ's philosophy and teaching approach
BalanceThe school keeps a mix of backgrounds across each grade and division
ResourcesASIJ can provide the support and opportunities the child needs, including learning or language support
English-medium needThe founding principle: serving students with an established need for an education in English
SiblingsThe school intends to accept all the children of a family
Additional considerationChildren of alumni, and families in the Corporate Contribution Program

The English-medium principle

This is the criterion families in Japan most often misread. ASIJ was founded to serve children who need to be educated in English, typically because their family is international, moves between countries or plans an English-speaking path through school and university. Close to half of ASIJ families are bicultural, with parents from the United States and Japan, and American families make up about half of the student body. For a family settled in Japan, the application should make that need visible: the child's schooling so far, the family's languages, its plans.

Resources and support

The school runs a learning-support program from Kindergarten to Grade 12 for mild needs, and English language support in Grades 1 to 8. Each applicant is reviewed individually against what the school can provide. Families must declare any support their child has received, from speech therapy to English as an additional language, and submit the reports before a decision is made; the school can revoke an offer if relevant reports were withheld. Being open early is both a rule and the best way to have the right conversation about fit.
