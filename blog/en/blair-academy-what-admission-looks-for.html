What Blair admission looks for

Blair Academy, Blairstown, New Jersey. Photo Mcollins109, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

US boarding schools November 2026 4 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

Book a conversation

Blair Academy is unusually explicit about the students it wants. Its admission office lists the qualities in one paragraph, starting not with grades but with character: "applicants of high character who are kind individuals". Reading that list carefully tells a family where to put its effort.

The qualities, and where they show

QualityWhere the committee sees it
High character, kindnessInterview, teacher recommendations, optional personal recommendation
Strong academic abilityTranscripts for three years, test scores if submitted
Passion for learning, curiosityInterview, student questionnaire, English and math recommendations
Work ethic, commitment to growthGrade trend, teacher comments
Engagement, collaborationActivities, special-interest form, interview
Inclusivity, respect for viewpointsQuestionnaire, interview, recommendations

Well-rounded, but not generic

Blair's stated aim is to enrol well-rounded students who will thrive and enrich the community. With 32 varsity teams, arts from film production to ceramics, and 90 % of faculty living on campus, the school expects students to take part in many sides of life. That does not mean listing every activity; it means showing genuine involvement in the ones that matter to the student.

Academic ability, read in context

Strong academic ability is on the list, but the school notes that test scores do not tell the whole story and that it looks thoroughly at every part of the application. A transcript showing growth, comments from teachers who see real curiosity, and an interview where the student talks about ideas with enthusiasm all count alongside numbers.

Character is not a slogan

Kindness and character are hard to fake in a two-hour visit with students and an admission officer. They show in small things: how a candidate speaks about classmates, how they treat the student guiding the tour, what they choose to talk about. Families can help most by letting the student be themselves, rather than coaching a polished version of them.
