How to get into Brooklyn Tech

Brooklyn Tech, in Fort Greene, is one of New York City's eight test-based specialized high schools. Like the others, it admits on the SHSAT alone. It also adds a second competition once students are inside: in 10th grade, they compete for one of the school's majors, which shape their last two years.

The back-up matters

Every student taking the SHSAT also receives a match from the general high school application. If no specialized offer comes, or if the student declines one, that match is final. A family should therefore rank the other schools on that application as carefully as the specialized ones.

From the SHSAT to the majors

I prepare students for the SHSAT, then, once admitted, for the 9th and 10th grade results that decide their major.

The second selection

In 10th grade, every student ranks the majors after a series of presentations. Placement depends on preference and on a Power Index built from the student's grades, in which Advanced Placement courses are weighted. Once placed, a student cannot change major. Admission to Brooklyn Tech is therefore the start of a second race, not the end of the first.

Who can apply

New York City residents in 8th grade, or in 9th grade for the first time, from public, private, parochial or charter schools or home schooling. Students from districts 13, 14, 15, 16, 19, 23 and 32 who test at a central site do so at Brooklyn Tech itself.
