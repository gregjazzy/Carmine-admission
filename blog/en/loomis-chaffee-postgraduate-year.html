A postgraduate year at Loomis Chaffee

A postgraduate year, or PG year, is a thirteenth year at a boarding school after finishing high school. Loomis Chaffee enrolls 20 to 25 postgraduates a year, for students who would benefit from another year of demanding coursework, a chance to strengthen key academic areas, and more time to think about their college options.

Who it is for

Most Loomis postgraduates go on to play sport at a very high level in college, and preparing for that is a main reason they choose the year. But the school is clear that the programme is for anyone who values a year of maturation, rigorous courses and the chance to explore interests unavailable before. The students who do best embrace growth academically, athletically and socially, are intellectually curious, and want to live in a diverse community.

Diploma or certificate

Postgraduates apply for one of two statuses. A diploma candidate must meet all Loomis Chaffee diploma requirements, beyond the credits recognised at admission. A certificate candidate must pass English each term and earn at least four full credits during the year, from courses across every discipline. Most PGs choose the certificate, which gives the most flexibility in choosing courses.

What a PG year can change

A year at Loomis adds a full set of grades in demanding courses, new teacher recommendations and, for athletes, a season with a strong programme. For a student whose high school record undersells them, or who needs time to mature before college, those additions can matter as much as the extra year itself.

How I help
