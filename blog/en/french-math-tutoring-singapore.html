French Curriculum Math Tutoring in Singapore: Support for LFS Students

Private Tutoring January 2026 8 min read

Singapore is globally celebrated for its mathematics education. Singaporean students consistently top the PISA and TIMSS rankings. The "Singapore Math" method is studied, replicated, and revered by education systems worldwide. For French expatriate families living in Singapore, this reputation creates a dangerous illusion: if the math culture here is this strong, surely my child is well-supported in mathematics.

This is a misconception. And it is one that costs students at the Lycee Francais de Singapour dearly, year after year. Because Singaporean mathematical excellence and French mathematical rigor are not the same thing. They are, in many ways, fundamentally different disciplines.

The Lycee Francais de Singapour: French standards in an Asian context

The Lycee Francais de Singapour (LFS) educates approximately 2,800 students from kindergarten through Terminale. It is an AEFE-accredited institution that follows the French national curriculum in its entirety. Students sit the Brevet des colleges, then the Baccalaureat, under the same conditions and with the same expectations as students in metropolitan France. The standards are identical. The exams are identical. The grading is identical.

But the LFS operates within a Singaporean ecosystem where mathematics tutoring is an industry, where centers like Kumon and The Learning Lab offer math support on every corner, where children are immersed in a culture of numerical performance from an early age. This environment creates a false sense of security. Parents assume that because Singapore is a math powerhouse, their child cannot possibly have a math problem.

The reality is more nuanced. The Singaporean approach to mathematics emphasizes problem-solving through visual modeling, mental calculation, and the efficient application of strategies to arrive at correct answers. The French approach demands formal demonstration, structured proofs, and rigorous written reasoning. Finding the answer is not enough. A French math student must prove why the answer is correct, citing theorems, chaining deductions, leaving nothing implicit. A student who thrives in the Singaporean paradigm can struggle profoundly when faced with a French math paper -- not because they lack mathematical ability, but because the ability being tested is of an entirely different nature.

Why local tutors cannot solve this problem

The instinctive response for most parents is to find a math tutor in Singapore. The tutoring market is vast. Options are abundant. Some local tutors are genuinely excellent mathematicians and educators.

But none of them know the French curriculum. None of them understand what a "demonstration par l'absurde" looks like in French format. None of them can teach the conventions of a French geometric proof, with its specific vocabulary, its structured hypotheses, its explicit theorem citations. None of them know what a "tableau de variations" is, or how to write a complete function study in the formalism required by the French Education nationale.

Hiring a Singaporean math tutor for an LFS student is like hiring a world-class sushi chef to prepare a French tasting menu. The expertise is real, but it does not apply to the problem. The student ends up strengthening their computational skills and problem-solving techniques -- useful abilities, certainly -- but gains nothing in the areas that actually determine their grade at the LFS: formal rigor, proof construction, and French mathematical writing.

The challenges by level: from college to baccalaureat

College (5eme - 3eme): building the foundations of rigor

The college years are where French mathematical methodology is built from the ground up. Students learn to write geometric proofs, to manipulate algebraic identities (identites remarquables), to factor complex expressions, to work with formal algebraic notation. This is also where gaps begin to form and, if left unaddressed, compound relentlessly.

A student in 4eme or 3eme at the LFS who falls behind in geometric demonstration or algebraic manipulation carries a deficit that amplifies each subsequent year. The French curriculum is cumulative by design: each year assumes mastery of everything that came before. A gap left open in 4eme becomes a wall in Seconde.

Lycee (2nde - Terminale): specializations and the baccalaureat

At the lycee level, the stakes escalate. In Seconde, mathematics becomes more abstract: functions, sequences, probability, vectors. In Premiere and Terminale, students who choose the mathematics specialization face a demanding program: derivatives, integration, limits, logarithms, exponentials, three-dimensional geometry, combinatorics and probability.

The Baccalaureat is the ultimate deadline. The specialite mathematiques exam carries a major coefficient in the final grade. The exercises demand impeccable writing, perfect mastery of French methodology, and the ability to manage time under pressure. For LFS students targeting selective post-bac pathways -- prepas scientifiques in France, EPFL in Switzerland, top-tier Anglo-Saxon universities -- the bac grade is decisive.

Physics and chemistry: the same gap, the same solution

Everything we observe in mathematics applies equally to physics and chemistry. The French science curriculum demands a methodological rigor that local Singaporean tutors simply do not possess. Writing a physics solution in the French system follows precise conventions: dimensional analysis, writing the literal (symbolic) expression before any numerical application, justifying every step of the reasoning process.

A Singaporean tutor teaches physics in the Singaporean or Anglo-Saxon way: direct formula application, numerical resolution, final answer. That is not what the French program expects. For an LFS student struggling in physique-chimie, the problem is identical to the mathematics problem: they need a tutor who knows the French program and its specific expectations.
