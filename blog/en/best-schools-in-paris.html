Best schools in Paris: the leading schools, seen from the inside

Photo Kajimoto, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 2.5

Choosing a school October 2026 · updated November 2026 7 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. Always has students in the leading Paris schools and prepares children every year to get into them.

Book a conversation

Picture two students in quatrième (year 9), same average, same curiosity, same energy. One enters a school that pushes them, compares them, pulls them upwards every week: they become formidable. The other enters the same school and fades within a term. It was not a question of ability. It was a question of school.

Paris has the most renowned schools in France, and their baccalaureate results all look alike. What separates them appears in no ranking: how they treat a student who doubts, the room left for one who thinks differently, what happens to a good student who discovers they have become average, and the schools their students go on to win places at. I know these schools from the inside: I always have students there, I often know their teachers, and I talk with their leadership teams. Here, for each one, is what it shows and what families say about it. Behind these portraits, the reality is often a little different: that is what I share with the families I work with.

The leading state lycées: excellence, free of charge

Louis-le-Grand

The summit, and behind it, classes préparatoires that send their students to the École normale supérieure and Polytechnique every year. The parents' association speaks of more than 2,000 applications for about 270 places in seconde. Students and families describe it as demanding and kind, a place where people help each other more than you would think. A child must arrive already very strong, and resilient: many go from top of their class to one student among others, and not all live it the same way.

Henri-IV

The other legend of the Montagne Sainte-Geneviève, and an experience former students describe in two opposite ways: some speak of an incredible atmosphere, others of pressure that wears you down. Everything depends on the class, the teachers and above all the student. For an independent teenager who loves intensity, it is a rare opportunity.

Charlemagne

A collège and lycée in the heart of the Marais, long among the most respected in Paris. Since the 2022 reform of Affelnet, entry into seconde depends mainly on the points score, and it has become very hard for a student without a social bonus. A very fine school, provided you come in through the right door.

Honoré-de-Balzac

The lycée of families from elsewhere: six international sections, from German to Portuguese, the French international baccalaureate, and one of the most active parent communities in Paris. Families describe a two-speed school whose excellence is concentrated in the international sections. For an already bilingual child who wants to stay in the French system, it is often the best choice in Paris, and it is free.

The leading private schools

Stanislas

An institution, with its own classes préparatoires where many students continue, and a boarding house former students describe as very close-knit. The setting is Catholic, the discipline strict, and the demands rise year after year until terminale. It suits the solid, hard-working student who accepts a framework and is aiming for prépa; an independent mind, or a child who needs to be listened to closely, will be less at ease. Applying to Stanislas needs to start early.

Saint-Louis-de-Gonzague, known as Franklin

The Jesuit school of the 16th arrondissement: real demands, a good atmosphere among students, and an economics classe préparatoire aimed at HEC as its natural next step. Its sixième opens applications for only two weeks a year, in September of the CM2 year. Families who know prepare from CM1; the others discover the date too late.

École alsacienne

The Parisian exception: a secular school of Protestant inspiration, known for its kindness and its refusal of the cult of performance, which nonetheless educates part of the country's intellectual elite. About 1,400 applicants for just over 200 places a year, and the school says it recruits students, not their parents. For a curious, original child who needs room to give their best, it is a dream. Fees depend on income, down to nothing.

Saint-Jean-de-Passy

Prépa-level demands from collège onwards, as its students say themselves. Passy produces rigorous, organised students, well armed for competitive exams. In return the school expects genuine commitment to its framework, and both parents are seen at the interview. For a child who likes clear rules and steady work, it is a remarkable education.

École Jeannine Manuel

The most international of the leading Paris schools, and one of the hardest to get into. Bilingual throughout, with an American BFI and an IB track: its class of 2024 split half in France and half abroad, 65 students in France, 63 in the United Kingdom, 23 in the United States. It sets no deadline for families arriving from abroad, and an adaptation programme welcomes children who speak little or no French. It does, however, require genuine bilingualism and a student at ease academically. Everything is detailed in how to get into Jeannine Manuel.

La Tour, Sion, Sévigné, Fénelon Sainte-Marie

Four more discreet schools, often better choices than people think. La Tour, in the 16th, is described as serious and attentive to each student, with an English-language section. Notre-Dame de Sion has the reputation of a family-sized, very demanding school, with an economics prépa. Sévigné, secular and humane, offers an American BFI. Fénelon Sainte-Marie has teachers families describe as deeply committed, and a human-sized prépa.

EIB and the Neuilly schools

The École internationale bilingue readily welcomes international families and non-French-speaking children, with primary schools half in French, half in English. In Neuilly, Sainte-Marie, Sainte-Croix and Saint-Dominique are the three Catholic schools that families in western Paris compete for, each with a very different way of balancing demands and support.

What these schools are worth relative to each other

What really decides, for a given child, is where each school stands relative to the others, and what the child will come out with. The same excellent average is not worth the same on the rue Notre-Dame-des-Champs and in Passy. Some schools keep a student going through a bad term and get them back on track; others let them go at the end of the year. Some lead almost all their students to the French prépas, others to London or American universities too, and their brochures do not always tell the truth about the proportion. Their bilingualism is not the same everywhere either. And there is the school on the rise, and the one living on its reputation.

One example, which many families discover too late: at Jeannine Manuel, CP is the last year in which the school requires no English; a year later, it turns beginners away. The rest, what can be seen from the inside, is written nowhere.

Few people see these schools from all these angles at once, from the inside and over many years: through the students I follow there, their teachers, and the leadership teams I talk with. That is what allows me to set them side by side for your child, and to find, among the best, the one where they will give their best.
