Best schools in Paris: the great schools, and the child each one suits

Choosing a school October 2026 12 min read

Picture two thirteen-year-olds with the same marks, the same curiosity, the same energy. One joins a school that pushes him, compares him, pulls him upwards every week: he becomes formidable. The other joins the same school and fades within a term. It was never a question of ability. It was a question of school.

Paris has the highest concentration of outstanding schools in France. Their baccalaureate honours rates all look alike, between 90 and 100%. What sets them apart appears in no ranking: how a pupil who doubts is treated, how much room there is for one who thinks differently, what happens to a strong student who discovers he has become average. I know these schools from the inside, through the students I teach there and the teachers I know in them. The portraits below say what each one promises, and for which child.

The great state lycées: excellence, free of charge

Louis-le-Grand

The summit. In the 2025 baccalaureate, 97% honours and 83 highest honours with distinction, by far the highest figure in Paris; behind them, preparatory classes that send students every year to the École normale supérieure and Polytechnique. The parents' association speaks of more than 2,000 applications for about 270 places in the first year of lycée. Students and families describe a demanding and caring school, where people help each other more than outsiders think. A student must arrive already very strong, and resilient: many go from top of the class to one strong student among others, and not everyone lives it the same way.

Henri-IV

The other legend of the Montagne Sainte-Geneviève, and an experience former students tell in two opposite ways: some describe an incredible atmosphere, others a pressure that wears them down. It depends on the class, the teachers and above all the student. Tellingly, its added value in the 2025 bac is 0: Henri-IV gets exactly the results expected from the brilliant students it takes in. For an independent teenager who thrives on intensity, it is a rare opportunity.

Charlemagne

A collège and lycée in the heart of the Marais, long among the most respected in Paris. Since the 2022 reform of Affelnet, the state placement system, entry into seconde depends mostly on the points scale, and it has become very hard for a student without a social bonus. A beautiful school, provided you come in through the right door.

Honoré-de-Balzac

The lycée of families from elsewhere. Six international sections, from German to Portuguese, the French international baccalaureate (BFI), and one of the most active parent communities in Paris. Families describe a school running at two speeds: excellence is concentrated in the international sections. For a child who is already bilingual and wants to stay in the French system, it is often the best choice in Paris, and it is free.

The great private schools

Stanislas

An institution: 99% honours at the bac, 166 highest honours, its own preparatory classes where many students continue, and a boarding house that alumni describe as very close-knit. The setting is Catholic, discipline is strict, and the demands rise year after year until the final year. It suits a solid, hard-working student who accepts a framework and aims for a prépa. An independent spirit, or a child who needs to be listened to closely, will be less at ease.

Saint-Louis-de-Gonzague, known as Franklin

The Jesuit school of the 16th arrondissement: 99% honours, real demands and a good atmosphere among students, and an economics preparatory class geared towards HEC as the natural next step. Its sixième (first year of middle school) takes applications for only two weeks a year, in September of the last year of primary. Families who know it prepare from the year before. The others find out about the date too late.

École alsacienne

The Parisian exception: a secular school of Protestant inspiration, known for its kindness and its refusal of the cult of performance, which nevertheless educates part of the country's intellectual elite. About 1,400 applicants for just over 200 places a year; from the second year of primary, the child is interviewed alone, and the school says it recruits pupils, not their parents. For a curious, original child who needs room to breathe to give their best, it is a dream. Fees depend on income, down to free.

Saint-Jean-de-Passy

Prépa-level demands from middle school, as its own students say. Passy shapes rigorous, organised students, well armed for competitive exams. In return, the school expects real commitment to its framework, and both parents attend the interview. For a child who likes clear rules and steady work, it is a remarkable machine.

École Jeannine Manuel

The most international of the great Paris schools, and probably the hardest to get into. Bilingual from start to finish, 99% honours at the bac, an American-track BFI and an IB programme: its 2024 graduating class split between France and abroad, 65 students in France, 63 in the United Kingdom, 23 in the United States. It sets no deadline for families arriving from abroad, and an adaptation programme takes children who speak little or no French. It does ask for genuine bilingualism and a student at ease academically.

La Tour, Sion, Sévigné, Fénelon Sainte-Marie

Four quieter schools, often better choices than people think. La Tour, in the 16th, is described as serious and attentive to each student, with an English-speaking section. Notre-Dame de Sion has the reputation of a family-like, small and very demanding school, with an economics prépa. Sévigné, secular and humane, offers an American-track BFI. Fénelon Sainte-Marie has teachers that families describe as deeply committed, and a human-sized prépa. All of them score 98 or 99% honours at the bac.

EIB and the Neuilly schools

The École internationale bilingue readily welcomes international families and children who do not speak French, with primary schools taught half in French and half in English, and 98% honours at its lycée. In Neuilly, Sainte-Marie, Sainte-Croix and Saint-Dominique are the three Catholic schools that families in western Paris compete for, each with its own very different way of balancing demands and support.

What the rankings will not tell you

Official figures show who earns honours. They do not show what matters most for your child:

  • how the school treats a student going through a bad term, and whether they are still there the following year;
  • the real level needed for the entrance tests, which school reports do not show;
  • the year in which it is easiest to get in, which is not always the one families aim for;
  • the school on the rise and the one running out of steam, what teachers say among themselves and brochures never do.

That is what I know about these schools, and it is what decides where your child will give their best.

The calendar to keep

The great schools recruit early: sixième applications in the autumn or winter of the last year of primary, international sections before mid-March, Henri-IV and Louis-le-Grand from January to early April for candidates outside a Paris collège. Details are in Middle schools in Paris and Best high schools in Paris; for a family arriving from abroad, start with Schools in Paris for expat families.

Sources for the figures. Honours rates and highest honours: 2025 lycée results indicators published by the French ministry (2025 bac). Destinations of Jeannine Manuel's 2024 class: Good Schools Guide, 2026. Applications and places: school websites and the Louis-le-Grand parents' association. Figures change every year.
