IMSA is a residential school: students leave home at 14 or 15 and live on campus for three years. That is why its application gives parents a voice of their own. The parent or guardian statement, 200 to 300 words, lets the family describe the student from a perspective no teacher has.

What the statement is for

IMSA wants to learn more about what makes the applicant unique, their interest in STEM, their readiness to live independently and adapt to a new environment, and any other insight the family thinks useful. It asks parents to be open, honest and authentic, and is explicit that there are no right or wrong answers.

Three questions to answer

QuestionWhat helpsWhat does not
What makes this child unique?One or two concrete moments only a parent would knowA list of adjectives
Where does the interest in STEM come from?How it shows at home: questions, projects, books, experimentsRepeating grades and awards already in the file
Is the child ready to live away from home?Honest signs of independence, and how the child handles changeClaiming total maturity, or avoiding the question

Readiness, honestly

The question of independence matters more at IMSA than at a day school. Students live in residence halls, in wings of 18 to 24 students supervised by a resident counselor, and many cannot go home every weekend. A parent who describes how their child has handled a summer camp, a move, or responsibility at home gives the admissions team something real. Acknowledging what the child is still learning, and how the family supports it, often reads as more credible than unqualified praise.

Practical points

  • Respect the 200 to 300-word range.
  • Write it yourself, in your own words; the student's essays are where the student's voice belongs.
  • Avoid repeating the application: the statement adds a perspective, not a summary.
  • If circumstances affected the student's record, the optional statement is the place to explain them.

How it fits

The parent statement is one part of a holistic file alongside grades, an SAT or ACT score, three teacher evaluations, a counselor form, essays and activities. It rarely decides an application alone, but it can confirm what the rest of the file suggests, or give it the human dimension it lacks.
