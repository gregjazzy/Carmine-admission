The Discovery program

The SHSAT is the only criterion for Bronx Science, but it is not the only route. Each of the eight specialized high schools runs a summer Discovery programme for some disadvantaged students whose scores fell within a certain range below the cutoff. Completing it successfully leads to a place in the fall.

Six steps from the test to September

The disadvantage criteria

Criteria focus on economic disadvantage. Students at public or charter schools generally qualify through their school's Economic Need Index, at 60 % or above; students at private schools or taught at home, through living in a high-poverty area. Other criteria can apply; families should read the official Discovery page each year.

Every point counts

For students close to the cutoff, a few extra points can mean a direct offer rather than a summer route. I prepare SHSAT candidates to gain those points.

No separate application

There is no way to apply to Discovery directly. A student must register for the SHSAT in October, sit it in November and list the school; the invitation, if any, follows from the score and eligibility. Discovery is a door opened after the test, not an alternative to it, and families should plan for the SHSAT exactly as anyone else would.

Leaving Bronx Science off the list

A student can be invited to Discovery only at a school they listed when registering. A family that leaves Bronx Science off the list, thinking it out of reach, also closes this door. Listing it costs nothing and keeps both routes open.

Weeks of classes before 9th grade

Discovery is a real academic commitment: several weeks of classes at Bronx Science during the summer, with attendance and performance requirements. Students who complete it begin 9th grade with classmates who entered by the test, and with a first experience of the school's pace.
