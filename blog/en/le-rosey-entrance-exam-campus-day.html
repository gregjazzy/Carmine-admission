Le Rosey: the entrance exam and the day on campus

The Château du Rosey, Rolle. Photo Eric Bajart, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0

Boarding schools November 2026 6 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

Book a conversation

At Le Rosey, almost everything is decided in a single day. Once the online file is complete, the applicant is invited to spend a day on the Rolle campus. They discover the school, sit exams and meet an academic advisor. It is on what the school observes that day, as much as on the school reports, that the admissions committee decides.

Every year I prepare children for this day. It is less intimidating than families fear, and more decisive than they think.

Three tests, one of which families forget

The school tests applicants in maths, in English or French, and in the child's mother tongue. That third test often surprises families. Yet it says a great deal about Le Rosey's spirit: more than half its students have neither French nor English as their mother tongue, and the school teaches nearly thirty mother tongues. It wants a child who is solid in their own language, not only in the school's.

For the language of instruction, requirements rise with age: among the Juniors, aged 8 to 12, mastering French or English is not essential; from 12, they must understand one of the two; from 15, they must master one. Academically, the school expects every applicant to be able, in time, to follow the International Baccalaureate or French bac programmes.

Arriving in Rolle ready

I prepare your child for Le Rosey's maths and language tests and for the interview with the academic advisor, to the level of a school that turns away excellent applicants every year. My aim: that they go through the day with confidence, and that it works in their favour.

The interview, and what the day reveals

The interview is with an academic advisor, and the school also meets the family. But what strikes you, preparing children for this day, is that it is one continuous observation. The school says it favours active, curious and ambitious applicants, able to live in a boarding school and share their lives with students from seventy countries. It explicitly turns away spoiled children used to always getting their way. A whole day is more than enough to tell the two apart.

Has your child ever spent a day at a boarding school? Tell me about them: I will tell you how to prepare them for Le Rosey's.

How the committee decides

The exams are marked by Le Rosey's teachers. The complete file is then read in detail by three people, including the Headmaster, and discussed at the weekly admissions meeting, which brings together the Director General, the Headmaster and the admissions leadership. For younger applicants, the decision usually comes two weeks after the file is complete. It may be an acceptance, a refusal, a place on the waiting list, or a deferral when competition is too strong, notably because of language and nationality quotas.

How I prepare for this day

For seven years, I have supported around fifteen families a year with their admissions to the most selective schools, in France and Switzerland, and fifteen of my students have already been admitted to Le Rosey. For Le Rosey, My support rests on an assessment in maths, English and French at the level of its exams, to see where your child stands and what they lack. I prepare them for the tests, the interview and this day of observation, without moulding them: the school is looking for a lively child, not a candidate reciting answers. And I help you choose when to apply, which matters as much as the preparation.
