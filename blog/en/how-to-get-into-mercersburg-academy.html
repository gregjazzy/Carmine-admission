How to get into Mercersburg Academy

For more than 130 years, Mercersburg Academy has educated students in the town of Mercersburg, Pennsylvania, about ninety minutes from Washington's airports. It is a boarding and day school whose motto, "Hard work, fair play, and clean life", still shapes how it describes the students it admits.

I have students at Mercersburg. Here is the process, step by step.

Five steps

  1. Inquiry form. The first step, which pairs the family with one of the school's admission counsellors, who then guides the application.
  2. Interview. Required for every applicant before 15 January, usually combined with a campus visit.
  3. Testing. Optional for Grades 9 and 10 unless the student wants to be considered for merit aid; required for Grades 11, 12 and postgraduate year; English proficiency for non-native speakers.
  4. Application. Through the Standard Application Online or Gateway to Prep Schools; day students must use the SAO. It includes the student application, transcripts for the current and two previous years, three recommendations and test scores.
  5. Financial aid. A Clarity application by 15 January for families seeking need-based aid.

Interview, tests, letters

What Mercersburg looks for

The school seeks academic achievement and promise, shown through transcripts, test scores and teacher recommendations. It pays equally close attention to personal qualities: motivation, character, extracurricular interests and the ability to adapt to the Mercersburg community. In the interview, students are asked to share how they see themselves contributing to the school.

How competitive it is

Last year, nearly 800 students interviewed for approximately 150 places. Some applicants who are not admitted are placed on a waitlist; if a place opens, the committee chooses the student who best fits it. Decisions are announced on 10 March, and families reply by 10 April.

Cost

For 2026-27, boarding tuition is $81,600 and day tuition $53,340. Mercersburg covers the full demonstrated need of every admitted family, and half of its families receive merit- or need-based aid.
