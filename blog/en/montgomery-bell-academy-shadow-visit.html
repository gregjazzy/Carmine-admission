The shadow visit

The third step of a Montgomery Bell application is a shadow visit: the boy spends a school day following a current student, in class and around the campus on The Hill. It is the only step where MBA sees him among other boys, in the rhythm of a normal day, rather than across a table or on paper.

The day, hour by hour

A typical sophomore day runs like this; the order of subjects changes from day to day, but the shape is the same.

TimeWhat happens
7:30Optional help session, or breakfast in the dining hall
7:50Advisory: announcements, reflection, a start with the advisor
8:00 and 8:53Two academic classes, such as English and math
9:40Activity period: clubs, a break, assembly on Mondays
10:13 to 12:43Science, study hall, lunch, history
1:33 and 2:23World language, then an elective or the arts
3:10Athletics and extracurriculars: football, cross country, robotics, debate, mock trial

What MBA is watching

No one hands the boy a checklist, but the day naturally shows the qualities MBA names in its Portrait of a Graduate. Does he join in during class, or sit silently? Does he ask a question when he does not understand? At lunch, does he talk with the boys at his table? None of this requires brilliance. It requires a boy who is willing to take part in a place he does not yet know.

A boy ready to take part

I help boys prepare for the MBA shadow day as I prepare them for the interview: not with rehearsed lines, but with the confidence to join in, ask and talk, at the level of the most selective schools.

Preparing without making it a performance

The worst preparation is to tell a boy he is being judged all day; he will freeze or perform. The best is to explain what the day looks like, so that nothing surprises him, and to agree on two simple intentions: ask at least one question in class, and talk to the boys around him at lunch. For a boy coming from a co-educational school, it can help to talk beforehand about what a boys' classroom feels like.

Is your son at ease in a group he does not know? Tell me about him.

A day for him, too

The shadow visit is also the boy's chance to decide whether he wants to spend five or six years at MBA. A boy who comes home with opinions about the classes, the teachers and the other students has used the day well, whatever happens next. His reactions are useful to the family, and they often give him material for the interview.

Timing

The shadow visit comes after the application form and before the ISEE in MBA's sequence. Placing it in the fall leaves time to act on what the day reveals, and lets the boy talk about it in the February interview.

How I work with families

I have students at Montgomery Bell, and for seven years I have worked with around fifteen families a year aiming at the most selective schools in the United States, England and Switzerland. Before a shadow day, I talk with the boy about what to expect and what to look for, and afterwards we go over what he noticed, which becomes the start of his interview preparation.
