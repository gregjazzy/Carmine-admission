Election at Winchester College: the 13+ scholarship examination

Election is Winchester's academic scholarship examination, as old as the College itself. Since William of Wykeham founded the school, Scholars have lived in College, the original scholars' house, and wear black gowns that set them apart. Each year Election chooses about fourteen of them, plus about six Exhibitioners who live in other houses. It is taken in Year 8, instead of the ordinary Winchester Entrance examination, and it asks far more of a thirteen-year-old than an ordinary entrance test.

How a boy comes to sit it

A boy first goes through the normal registration and admissions process. Election is then an additional route for those of exceptional academic promise. The first conversation is with the boy's own school and teachers, whose professional judgement Winchester values, and with the College and Election secretary. Promising candidates are invited to visit College: lunch with the Scholars, a tour, a meeting with the Master-in-College. Only once the Master-in-College invites a boy to apply does the family receive the application form, and the current school is asked for a reference.

Dates for Election 2027

  • Application deadline: Thursday 21 January 2027.
  • Examinations and interviews: Monday 26 to Wednesday 28 April 2027, at the College.
  • Results and the Roll of Scholars and Exhibitioners: Tuesday 11 May 2027.

Families make their own arrangements for accommodation during the three days; the College does not house candidates.

Preparing for Election

We prepare candidates for Election with the published specimen papers, from Mathematics I and II to the General Papers, and train them for the two interviews where scholarly curiosity is judged in conversation.

The papers

Every candidate sits four compulsory papers: English, Mathematics I, Science, and General Paper I, a multiple-choice test of logical reasoning. Candidates from independent schools then take at least three further papers from Latin, French, Greek, History, Geography, Mathematics II and General Paper II; the Master-in-College recommends four or five, not all of them. Candidates from state schools take General Paper II, which usually has three questions, perhaps a set of data to interpret, a passage to comprehend or a structured essay, and may add any other paper.

A boy taught French, Greek or Latin is expected to sit at least one of them. The French assessment includes a spoken part, and spoken assessments can be offered in Spanish, German, Russian and Italian. Calculators are allowed in Science and Geography, not in Mathematics. The level is aligned with Key Stage 3, but the questions are written to test scholarly thinking rather than revised or rehearsed answers.

Two interviews

Each candidate has two separate interviews, one with the Master-in-College and one with a member of the College's academic leadership. They are designed to find scholarly qualities through conversation: how a boy reasons aloud, what he has read beyond the syllabus, how he responds when pushed on an idea. A boy with a strong music or drama talent can also ask for a short informal test during Election, or bring art or design work.

Families who will need a means-tested bursary must ask for it before or at the same time as applying for Election. Winchester expects that families who do not apply then will not need support later.

How to prepare

Winchester's own advice is to read broadly: literary fiction, non-fiction, poetry, broadsheet newspapers, good magazines and online writing. That is the foundation. On top of it, a candidate gains a great deal from working through the specimen papers published for recent years, especially Mathematics II and the General Papers, whose style is unlike ordinary school tests, and from practising thinking aloud in front of an adult who questions every step.

How I prepare Election candidates

I take candidates through the specimen papers subject by subject, with particular weight on mathematics and on the General Papers, and I run mock interviews in the spirit of a conversation with the Master-in-College: an idea from his reading, pushed and tested, until he enjoys defending it. Reading lists are built with him, around what already interests him.
