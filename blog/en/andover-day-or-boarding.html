Day or boarding at Andover

Until recently, the day option at many boarding schools was reserved for families living nearby. Andover changed that: since the 2025-2026 school year, any family may apply as day or boarding, without geographic restriction. The freedom comes with a rule. The choice is a one-time option, made when the application is submitted, and it cannot be revisited later in the process.

The numbers

This year Andover has 858 boarders and 309 day students, so roughly one student in four goes home at night. A day year costs $63,840 and a boarding year $79,800 in 2026-2027, a difference of $15,960 a year. Financial aid applies to both, and since admission is need-blind, cost should not push a family towards one option for the wrong reasons.

One school, five neighbourhoods

Students live in five residential clusters, which Andover describes as neighbourhoods. Different dorms and clusters have different personalities, and students are fiercely loyal to theirs. Ninth graders live in dorms with prefects, older students who live alongside them as mentors; upper-class dorms have proctors. Boarders eat home-cooked meals with their house counselor's family, and more than nine faculty members in ten live on campus. Each student, day or boarding, has a team of more than eighteen adults around them: advisor, house counselor, teachers, coaches.

Either door, the same standard

Day or boarding, the file Andover reads is the same, and so is the level it expects. I prepare children for the most selective schools at the very top of their ability, whichever door the family chooses.

What the day student shares, and what they miss

Day students take the same courses and follow the same afternoon requirement: every student takes part in an afternoon activity, whether a sport, community service or a life sport such as dance, yoga or Outdoor Pursuits. What a day student misses is the evening and the weekend that happen without them unless they stay: the late conversations, the dinners with the house counselor's family, the Saturday plans made on the spot. A day place works best for a family close enough that the child can stay late without the journey becoming a burden.

What boarding asks of a fourteen-year-old

A boarder at Andover carries five courses a term, at six to nine hours of work per course each week, plus the afternoon activity, plus the life of a dorm. There is no dress code, the town centre is ten minutes' walk away, and freedom is real. Some fourteen-year-olds thrive on it from the first week. Others need a year or two more before living away from home. The honest question is not which option is more prestigious, but which one this child is ready for.

Three questions to decide

  • How long is the journey, every day, in January? A commute that is easy in September can become heavy in winter, and a tired day student misses what makes Andover more than its classes.
  • Is the child ready to manage their own time? Boarding gives freedom and expects self-discipline in return.
  • What role does the family want to keep? For four years, day families share the daily rhythm; boarding families share holidays and weekends.

The choice should not rest on a guess about which pool is easier to get into. A file built on that guess is built on nothing.

How I help families decide

I have students at Andover, and for seven years I have worked with around fifteen families a year aiming at the most selective schools in the United States, England and Switzerland. Before the application is submitted, I help families weigh day against boarding with the questions above and an honest view of the child; Andover's admission team will also discuss it with families who ask. Then I prepare the child for what is the same in both cases: the test, the graded paper, the essay, the video, the interview.
