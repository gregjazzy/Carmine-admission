Le Rosey: IB or French baccalaureate, which to choose?

The Château du Rosey, Rolle. Photo Eric Bajart, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0

Boarding schools November 2026 5 min read

Every Le Rosey student finishes with an official examination: the International Baccalaureate, in English, French or both, or the French baccalaureate. The choice is made at the end of class 2, the equivalent of US grade 10. Many families think it is simple: IB for abroad, bac for France. At Le Rosey, almost the opposite turns out to be true.

Two diplomas, two sets of demands

The IB requires good written and spoken English or French. Students choose six subjects, three at Higher Level and three at Standard Level, which they can split between the two languages to earn a bilingual diploma; on top come Theory of Knowledge, the Extended Essay and the activities programme, with at least three mountain expeditions a year. The French bac requires good written and spoken French: three specialities in the penultimate year, two in the final year, the grand oral, and 40% continuous assessment.

The school's results are excellent in both. Over the last five years, a 100% pass rate at the bac, with more than 60% of honours; 100% of IB students earning the diploma, with an average of 36 points against a world average of 30, and more than two thirds earning a bilingual diploma.

The diploma that opens the right doors

My aim: that their diploma serves their plans, not the other way round.

Le Rosey's French bac does not lead to France first

This is the figure that surprises most. Of Le Rosey's 63 French bac graduates between 2020 and 2025, 35 went to the United States or Canada, 6 to the United Kingdom, 8 stayed in Switzerland, and only 8 went to study in France. About two thirds of the school's bac graduates go to the US or UK, to Penn, Georgetown, Tufts, NYU or London. The French bac there is not a fallback towards France: it is a passport, provided it is well presented to universities that know it less well than the IB.

IB or bac for your child? Tell me what they are aiming for after school: I will tell you which serves them best.

How to choose

The right choice depends less on the target country than on the student. A student very at ease in French, who enjoys the essay and the rigour of a national programme, will often do better in the bac, and can aim for the best American universities with it. A student who wants to keep six subjects, work in both languages and showcase a bilingual diploma will find the IB a more natural framework. And the school advises against arriving only for the two IB years: places are scarce.
