The UCAS personal statement: the three questions, written by a French student

UCAS · Personal statement October 2026 7 min read

A student at a French lycée has learned to write a dissertation: a question, a plan, an argument in which the writer disappears behind the subject. The personal statement asks the opposite: to talk about themselves, what they have read, done and understood, in English, in 4,000 characters. It is often the part of the UCAS application that costs them most, and the one where they get the register wrong.

UCAS personal statement: the three questions

Since applications for 2026 entry, the free text has been replaced by three compulsory questions. UCAS words them as follows:

  • "Why do you want to study this course or subject?"
  • "How have your qualifications and studies helped you to prepare for this course or subject?"
  • "What else have you done to prepare outside of education, and why are these experiences useful?"

Word count: the length

The limit is 4,000 characters in total, spaces included, with at least 350 characters per answer. The rest is shared out freely according to the course and the student's background. UCAS states that admissions staff read the three answers as a single text: do not repeat yourself from one to the next. And one personal statement covers all five choices, which is why it is better not to name a particular university and to aim for closely related courses.

What universities read

Cambridge writes that it reads the personal statement in full and does not give it a formal score. It adds that activities unrelated to the subject should not take up more than 20% of the answer in which they appear. LSE asks that at least 80% of the text focus on interest in the programme, and advises against discussing career plans in the first answer.

The message is clear: British universities select on the subject. A student who writes about their travels, sport and volunteering, as they would for an American university, wastes the space.

The three answers, written by a French student

Question 1. It calls for a precise idea, not a declared passion: a problem, a book, a concept that sparked the wish to go further, and what the student did with it. "I have always loved maths" says nothing; a line of reasoning discovered while preparing for the olympiads, and pursued beyond the syllabus, says a lot.

Question 2. A British reader does not know the French baccalauréat. Name the specialist subjects (spécialités), the maths expertes option, the BFI or the international section if there is one, and say what each has brought to the course applied for, rather than listing them. The French curriculum, which keeps many subjects until the final year, philosophy included, becomes an argument once it is explained.

Question 3. Reading, competitions, online courses, personal projects: everything done outside the classroom, provided the student shows what they took from it for the subject. For Oxford and Cambridge, competitions such as the Concours général or the olympiads belong here.

Examples, plagiarism and artificial intelligence

The "UCAS personal statement examples" found online are a trap. UCAS runs every statement through similarity detection software that compares it with previously submitted statements and with a library of examples collected from the internet. Above 30% similarity, the text is reviewed, and the chosen universities are informed. UCAS also writes that generating all or a large part of the text with an AI tool and presenting it as your own could be considered cheating; applicants declare that their statement has not come from AI software.

UCAS calendar for 2027 entry. Deadline 15 October 2026 at 6 pm UK time for Oxford, Cambridge, medicine, dentistry and veterinary science; 13 January 2027 at 6 pm for most other courses. Application fee: £34.50 for up to five choices. Dates and fees published by UCAS, checked on 3 October 2026.

Writing the text

The right calendar starts at the end of première (Year 12): note the reading, problems and ideas that mattered, so there is material in September. Then a first draft in French if that comes more naturally, rewritten directly in English, and several versions reviewed for substance before language. For the rest of the procedure, see UCAS with the French baccalauréat.
