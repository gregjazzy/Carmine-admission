Getting into Franklin: applications, calendar and interview

Saint-Louis-de-Gonzague, Paris 16th. Photo Ornd, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

Paris schools September 2026 6 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

Let us start with the point that sinks the most applications, because it cannot be made up: Franklin opens its sixième applications only from 15 to 30 September, during the CM2 year (the last year of primary), a year before entry. The website is explicit: after that, the form closes and no application can be submitted. Two weeks a year, the shortest window of all the leading Paris schools.

The rest, Franklin barely publishes. I know the school from the inside: I have had, and still have, students there, and every year I see what it expects from applicant families, beyond what its website says.

The application: entirely online, and nothing published

Applications can only be submitted through the school's online platform. The website is clear that no paper application will be processed. You need to have read the school's educational charter before you start.

The published process has four stages: submission of the application with its supporting documents, receipt and checking, review of the file by the director or the préfet (the senior staff member in charge of the year group), then a reply in the form of either an offer of an appointment or a rejection.

There is no written test, at any level. The interview is with the director or the préfet, and the school takes care to point out that being offered an appointment does not mean being accepted.

What Franklin does not publish

Neither the list of documents nor the application fee appear on the website: the document list only shows once an account has been created, and the fees are only found in the financial regulations. In 2025-2026, the full-rate family contribution ranged from €2,435 in nursery to €3,285 in the prépa, adjusted to income (see Franklin school fees). Nor does it publish its number of places or applications: at most an average of thirty pupils per class in collège, and a hundred and fifty teachers.

This silence has a consequence: families cannot compare Franklin with the other leading schools on public criteria. Yet that is where everything is decided: what the director or the préfet is really looking for in the interview, the school reports that get through and those that do not, what the school does with its students up to its prépa aimed at HEC, and what it is worth against Stanislas, Saint-Jean-de-Passy or the École alsacienne for a given child. None of this is written on a website. It is what I know from the inside, and what I share with the families I work with. To place Franklin among the others, see best schools in Paris.

Depending on the year group

For sixième, the date that matters is 15 September of the CM2 year: families who discover the window in October are a year late, and since there is no test, the CM1 and CM2 school reports are what count. During collège, places come from pupils leaving: apply as soon as applications open rather than waiting for 31 March. For lycée, the absence of a closing date means the school closes when it is full. And each leading Paris school has its own calendar: the École alsacienne sees the child alone and publishes its figures, Saint-Jean-de-Passy sees both parents, Franklin closes in two weeks.

For its classes préparatoires, on the other hand, the school publishes precise support rates for scholarship students: half at level 0 bis, sixty per cent at level 1, seventy-five at level 2, all of it from level 3, plus scholarships of two thousand five hundred euros.
