The Bronx Science cutoff score

Every spring, families look for one number: the score needed for Bronx Science. The 2026 answer is 525. But that figure is not a pass mark announced in advance. It is the composite score of the last student to receive an offer, and it depends on how everyone else scored and how they ranked their schools.

How a cutoff appears

Offers are made in descending order of score. The highest scorer receives their first choice, the next their highest choice with seats left, and so on. When Bronx Science's last seat is filled, the score of that student becomes the cutoff. Its level therefore reflects two things at once: the 750 places to fill in 9th grade, and the choices of the students who scored higher. These figures concern 9th grade only; entry in 10th grade, with a handful of places, works on its own numbers.

Aim above the line

I prepare students to reach a composite comfortably above the recent cutoff, because the line moves and a few points either side decide the school.

Why it moves

  • The pool changes: about 30,000 students sit the test each year, and their results differ from year to year.
  • Preferences change: if more strong students rank Bronx Science first, its cutoff rises.
  • The test changes: from fall 2026, the SHSAT is computer-adaptive, which may shift score distributions.

Using the number well

A sensible target sits clearly above the latest cutoff, not exactly on it. Equally, a student slightly below 525 on a practice test should not drop Bronx Science from the list: listing it costs nothing, and the ranking never lowers a student's chances at schools listed below it. For students from disadvantaged backgrounds who fall just short, the Discovery programme may offer another way in.
