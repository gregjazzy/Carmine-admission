Jeannine Manuel Entry Test: What You Need to Know

Photo: École Jeannine Manuel

EJM Admission February 2026 · updated November 2026 5 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. Has worked with students of École Jeannine Manuel for more than ten years.

Book a conversation

The entry test is the stage families underestimate most. For more than ten years, I have worked with students of École Jeannine Manuel, from their admission all the way to the university or grande école they are aiming for, and every year I prepare children for these tests. Here is what the school says about them, what it does not say, and why so many candidates lose their place there.

What the school says about its tests

The school does not give the same test at every age. It only describes the outline:

  • From moyenne section to CP (ages 4 to 6), no written test: the child takes part in small-group activities, and the school meets the parents.
  • From CE1 to CM2 (ages 7 to 10), the applications that are retained lead to a short academic assessment in Paris, in English, French or mathematics.
  • In sixième (age 11), after a pre-selection on the file, the school meets the candidates and their parents and asks them to sit additional assessments.
  • From cinquième to troisième (ages 12 to 14), the candidates retained may sit one or more written tests; only then are some of them invited in with their parents.
  • In seconde and première (ages 15 and 16), the school meets some candidates with their parents and gives them assessments.

In every case, the test comes after a pre-selection on the file. The children who sit it already have good school reports: the test is there to separate strong candidates.

What the school does not say

The school's website lists the subjects. It says nothing about the format of the tests, how long they last, the exercises set or the level expected at each age. Yet these are the details that decide the result, and they are what I know, from preparing children for these tests every year and following the school's students closely.

Why so many candidates lose their place

Mathematics, the French way

The school follows the French national curriculum, and in mathematics that curriculum asks students to prove, not just to find the right answer. A bright child from an English-speaking system or the IB often calculates very well, but has never written out a French-style argument. It is the most common gap, and the most costly.

Does your child do "the same maths" as the school? I give them a test at the school's level and tell you exactly what is missing. Ask for an assessment.

Fluent spoken French that hides weak writing

A child who speaks French at home can be at ease orally and far from the expected level in writing, especially if they have been schooled abroad. The school assesses them at the level of a student who has followed the whole French curriculum.

Fluent English that is not enough

The reverse applies to English. Speaking fluently does not mean reading and writing at the level of an American international section. From CE2 (age 8), the school requires written English, and in lycée near-native English.

To measure their written French and English at the expected level, I give them both tests alongside the maths one. Tell me about their situation.

When to start preparing

Example: the 2027 admissions round. Applications opened in September 2026 and close on 12 January 2027 for families living in France; there is no deadline for families moving from abroad. Most decisions arrive in March or April 2027.

Closing a gap in method in mathematics or in writing takes several months. As the file closes in mid-January for families living in France, you need to know where your child stands from the start of the school year in September, and start working straight away.
