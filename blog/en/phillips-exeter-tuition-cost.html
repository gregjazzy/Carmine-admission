Phillips Exeter Tuition: What a Family Really Pays

One of the academic buildings on the Exeter campus, New Hampshire. Photo Transmarinus, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0

US boarding schools November 2026 6 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

The number every parent finds first is $71,797, Exeter's boarding tuition for 2026-27. It is the sticker price, and at Exeter the sticker price tells you surprisingly little. Roughly half the school pays far less, the other half pays more once the extras are counted, and one decision made in the application season shapes the next four years of bills.

What Exeter publishes for 2026-27

An Exeter year, item by item:

Item2026-27
Boarding tuition$71,797
Day tuition$56,077
Books and supplies (estimate)$900
Student health plan for international students (F-1/J-1 visas, dual citizens, non-US residents)$2,595
Application fee$60

A full-pay boarder is looking at $72,697 a year before travel, laptop and incidentals; an international boarder at about $75,300 once the mandatory health plan is added. Over four years at today's prices, that is roughly $291,000 and $301,000, and tuition goes up most years. Exeter does not publish the enrollment deposit or the payment schedule. Ask the business office before you commit.

Day tuition is not a lever most families can pull

The $15,720 gap between boarding and day tuition is real, but day status is not a free choice. Exeter's application rules require families in certain nearby towns to apply as day students, and allow families in a second list of "choice towns" to pick either. The school calls that pick a one-time decision. Living outside those towns, you apply as a boarder, unless you are prepared to move to one of those towns.

How financial aid actually works

Exeter states that admission is need-blind and that it meets 100 percent of demonstrated need with grants, not loans. Forty-nine percent of students receive aid, about $30 million a year in total.

The average award says more than the percentage. For 2026-27 it stands at $60,036 for a boarder and $39,715 for a day student, which covers about 84 percent of boarding tuition. The aided half of the student body is heavily subsidized; the other half pays full freight. There are no merit scholarships: every dollar is need-based.

On top of that, tuition is free for families earning under $150,000, but the conditions are narrower than the headline. The family must live and work in the United States, its income must come from W-2 wages only, and its assets must be consistent with that income. A household with business, partnership or self-employment income does not qualify automatically, even below the threshold. It goes through the standard aid review on Clarity like everyone else.

It is decided once, with the application, and it carries into the following years. I work through it with you well before January 15. Tell me about your situation.

The decision that costs families the most

It is skipping the aid application "to improve our chances". Many parents believe full-pay applicants have an edge. Exeter says the opposite, since admission is need-blind, and the rule that matters most is this one: aid not requested at the time of application may not be available in later years unless the family's circumstances change significantly. A family that goes full pay in year one as a tactic can end up locked out of aid for years two to four.

If your finances are borderline, apply for aid. If you are confident you will never need it, do not apply. What you should not do is play it as a signal.

International families

Families outside the United States can apply for aid with official income documents from their own country and a certified translation, which Exeter accepts. Exeter does not state whether international applicants are reviewed need-blind as well. If you will need aid, ask the admissions office in writing before you apply; the answer changes the strategy. The $150,000 free-tuition rule does not apply, since it requires living and working in the United States.

What the real budget includes

Beyond tuition and books: travel home at the end of each term, a laptop, winter gear for New Hampshire, and the cost of applying itself, including test registration and possibly a campus visit for the interview, although Exeter also interviews online. Each item is modest. Together they are the line families forget when they compare Exeter with another school.

If you are weighing Exeter against Andover, Choate or St. Paul's, compare net cost for your child, after aid, over the years they would actually attend. Sticker price against sticker price tells you very little.
