Phillips Exeter: the admission interview

The central quad of the Exeter campus. Photo Transmarinus, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0

US boarding schools November 2026 4 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

Book a conversation

At Exeter, every applicant has an interview. The school puts it simply: it wants to know the child, what they are passionate about, their favourite activity, the last book they read, anything that helps it understand what they love to do.

Three formats

The family chooses: an interview on campus, in New Hampshire, an online conversation, or a video of recorded answers to questions sent by the school. The interview is booked in the applicant portal, from July.

Preparing for Exeter's interview

Exeter's interview cannot be recited: it can be prepared. I work with your child on talking about their passions, their reading and themselves, in English, naturally.

What the committee looks for

Exeter teaches around an oval table of twelve students, where everyone must think aloud and listen to others. The interview gives a foretaste: the committee wants a curious child, able to develop an idea, to say why they love what they love, and to react to an unexpected question.

What families underestimate

What follows the answer

"What was the last book you read?" Everyone has an answer. The difference lies in what follows: what the child thought of it, what surprised them, what stayed with them.

Can your child talk about themselves, in English, to an adult they do not know? Tell me about them.

The video format

More comfortable on the surface, it is more demanding: no follow-up, no dialogue, a fixed answer time. A child who has never tried it loses some of their footing.

Over-coaching

A child reciting learned answers is spotted within minutes. Prepare, do not mould.

What I do for your family

For the interview, I work with your child in short sessions, in the chosen format, until they talk about themselves with ease. For the test, see the SSAT or ISEE.
