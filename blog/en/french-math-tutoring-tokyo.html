French Curriculum Math Tutoring in Tokyo: Support for LFIT Students

Private Tutoring January 2026 10 min read

Your child attends the Lycee Francais International de Tokyo and their mathematics grades are slipping. You need a tutor who understands the French curriculum. In Tokyo, this search quickly becomes an exercise in frustration. Japanese private tutors, no matter how accomplished, teach a fundamentally different program. The handful of French-speaking tutors available locally often lack the training and experience to support a Terminale student through the mathematics specialization. And local tutoring agencies have never heard of the French baccalaureate.

This is a problem I know intimately. After guiding more than 1,600 students through demanding academic trajectories, including a significant number of expatriates across Asia, I can confirm that French families in Tokyo face one of the most isolated tutoring situations in the entire AEFE network. The LFIT is an excellent school. But when a student falls behind in mathematics or physics-chemistry, the local support options are virtually nonexistent. This is precisely the gap our online tutoring service was built to fill -- specialized expertise in the French curriculum, delivered wherever families need it.

The LFIT: academic excellence in an isolated context

The Lycee Francais International de Tokyo is one of the most respected institutions in the AEFE network across Asia-Pacific. It serves a small but particularly affluent and demanding French community: senior executives at major corporations, diplomats, researchers stationed in Japan's capital. Academic expectations are high, and the LFIT delivers, with baccalaureate pass rates consistently above 95%.

But the Tokyo context creates a paradox. The Japanese education system is one of the most rigorous in the world for mathematics. Japanese students excel in computation, geometry, and logical reasoning. Yet this rigor does not translate to the French program. The methodology is radically different. Japanese mathematics prioritizes technical precision and speed of resolution. French mathematics demands formal proof, structured reasoning, and the ability to construct rigorous demonstrations. A Japanese tutor, however brilliant, simply cannot teach the French approach to mathematical argumentation. They were not trained in this framework.

Why French curriculum math is a challenge in Tokyo

The French mathematics program is designed as a progressive escalation, where each year builds on the achievements of the previous one. The problem is that this progression is unforgiving. A gap in Troisieme becomes a wall in Seconde. A misunderstanding in Seconde becomes a crisis in Premiere specialization. And by Terminale, when the baccalaureate stakes become concrete, it is often too late to repair two or three years of weak foundations.

At the LFIT, as in all French schools abroad, class sizes tend to be smaller than in metropolitan France. This is theoretically an advantage. In practice, it also means less pedagogical diversity, fewer teachers available for supplementary support, and a strong social pressure within a tight-knit group where everyone knows everyone. A struggling student at the LFIT has nowhere to hide -- and nowhere easy to find help.

Breaking points by level

In Sixieme and Cinquieme, difficulties are typically transitional: families arrive from Japan or elsewhere with heterogeneous backgrounds, and the French program demands a uniformity that leaves some students behind. In Quatrieme and Troisieme, algebra becomes abstract, functions appear, and the first serious gaps emerge. This is where support should begin -- but rarely does.

In Seconde, the program escalates with reference functions, vectors, and probability. For a student whose Troisieme foundations are shaky, this is the tipping point. In Premiere and Terminale mathematics specialization, the pace accelerates sharply: sequences, limits, derivatives, integrals, continuity, advanced probability. LFIT students targeting preparatory classes, EPFL, or selective universities need not merely to keep up but to excel. And this is where the absence of local support becomes a genuine handicap.

Physics-chemistry: the second front

Mathematics gets most of the attention, but French curriculum physics-chemistry presents equally serious challenges. The specialization program in Premiere and Terminale is dense, conceptually demanding, and deeply intertwined with mathematical competence. Newtonian mechanics, optics, thermodynamics, organic chemistry -- all of this requires a solid mathematical foundation and the ability to reason physically in a way that local Japanese tutors, trained in a different paradigm, cannot effectively transmit. Finding a competent physics-chemistry tutor for the French program in Tokyo is even harder than finding one for mathematics.

Why act now

The most common mistake among expatriate families in Tokyo is waiting. Waiting for the situation to become critical. Waiting for the mock baccalaureate results. Waiting until Terminale to realize that the Seconde foundations were never solidified. The French mathematics program does not forgive passivity. Every lost term is a term that will need to be recovered under increasingly difficult conditions.

If your child is at the LFIT and you are observing signs of difficulty in mathematics or physics-chemistry -- declining grades, growing confusion, loss of confidence -- the time to act is now. Not next term. Not next year. Now. A precise diagnostic of the situation, an adapted work plan, a competent tutor: this is what makes the difference between a student who endures the program and a student who masters it.
