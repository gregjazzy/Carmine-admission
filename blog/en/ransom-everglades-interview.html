The interview is a required item on the Ransom Everglades checklist, and the school treats it as one of the main sources it uses to decide. Its admission office says it evaluates candidates on entrance tests, records, recommendations and the interview, and that it looks beyond grades and scores for personal qualities that suggest a student will contribute to the community.

What the school is looking for

Ransom Everglades describes students who think deeply and are doers, who engage in discussion, and who find their place in academics, the arts, athletics or co-curricular life. The Character Skills Snapshot names the qualities it values: curiosity, teamwork, initiative, responsibility, resilience, self-control, open-mindedness and social awareness. An interview is where a student can show some of these through examples rather than claims: a project they kept working on, a time they changed their mind, something they started.

Preparing without rehearsing

A good interview is a conversation, not a recital. The useful preparation is to help a student identify two or three subjects they can talk about with genuine interest, to practise answering a question and then adding a detail or an example, and to come with one or two questions of their own about the school. Visiting the campus beforehand, on a tour or open house, gives a student something concrete to say about why Ransom Everglades. For the Upper School, rereading the statement before the interview helps keep the two consistent.
