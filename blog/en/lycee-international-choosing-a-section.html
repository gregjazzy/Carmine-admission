Lycée International: choosing a section among the fourteen

Schools near Paris November 2026 4 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

Book a conversation

At the Lycée International, you do not enrol in the school, you enrol in a section. There are fourteen: German, American, British, Chinese, Danish, Spanish, Italian, Japanese, Dutch, Norwegian, Polish, Portuguese, Russian and Swedish. The choice is no formality: it decides the test, the child's community and, in lycée, part of their BFI exams.

Who the sections are for

They take children of foreign families living in France, binational children, and French children who were schooled in a country of the language or have a sufficient level in it. The first criterion everywhere is command of the section's language. The section teaches language and literature and part of history-geography in its language; the rest is in French, at the pace of the national curriculum.

The right section for your child

For a binational or trilingual child, the choice of section shapes their schooling up to the BFI. I know what each requires, and I help you choose the one where they will succeed best.

What a section costs

The French part of teaching is free, as in any state school. Section teaching has a cost, set by each section: in 2026-2027, from €4,051 to €5,746 a year in the British Section and from €5,100 to €6,750 in the American Section when the family pays itself, roughly double when an employer pays. For other sections, ask the section for its grid.

Could your child aim for several sections? Tell me about them: I will tell you which to choose.

How to choose

The simple rule: the strongest written language, not the one best spoken at home. A child who speaks English and Italian but has only ever written in Italian stands a better chance in the Italian section than in an English-speaking one, and will take a stronger BFI there. The choice also depends on plans after the bac: an English-speaking section naturally prepares for British or North American universities.

What I do for your family

I follow students at the Lycée International, and every year I support around fifteen families towards the most sought-after bilingual schools in the Paris region. For a child torn between two sections, I measure their writing in each language before deciding, then prepare them for the chosen section's test. Each section's contacts are on the Lycée International website: the section is who you contact first.
