In the English International Stream at German Swiss International School, secondary applicants for Years 7 to 10 sit a computer-based CAT4 and written English and mathematics papers. The stream is described by the school as very selective: applicants must pass every part of the assessment, and English is expected to match that of a native speaker of the same age. Invitations to assess follow admissions priority, so applying early still matters at secondary level.

I prepare secondary applicants for GSIS every year. Here is what the entry involves.

The assessment

ComponentWhat it covers
CAT4Verbal, non-verbal, quantitative and spatial reasoning, on computer
Written EnglishReading and writing at the level of a native speaker of the same age
Written mathematicsMathematics expected for the year group, in English

Papers are confidential and no samples are published. A child who does not pass is deferred for at least two years before being assessed again, which at secondary level can mean moving from Year 7 to Year 9, close to the start of IGCSE.

What native-level English means at twelve

At secondary level, native-level English is a demanding bar. It means reading a demanding text and writing a structured, accurate response with the vocabulary and control of a strong native speaker of the same age. A child who speaks English fluently but has studied mainly in another language often writes less precisely than they speak, and that gap shows in a written paper more than in conversation.

Mathematics taught in another system raises a second question: whether the child has covered the same topics by the same age. Checking this against the curriculum the EIS follows is the first step.

Joining the secondary school

Secondary students attend The Peak campus. In Years 7 and 8 the EIS has three classes of 27 per year group. German is taught as the first foreign language from Year 2 to Year 11, and French is available as a second foreign language from Year 7, alongside Mandarin. The stream leads to the IGCSE and then the IB Diploma. Once admitted, students cannot change stream.
