Why a boys' school

Montgomery Bell Academy is the only all-boys school in Middle Tennessee, and it does not treat that as a detail. Its whole education is built around the idea that boys learn best in a setting designed for them. For a family choosing between MBA and co-educational schools in Nashville, the question is not abstract: will this particular boy thrive there?

MBA's case for a boys' school

MBA argues that in a single-sex setting, boys learn with focus and confidence, free from many social distractions. They become more curious, more willing to take risks and more supportive of one another. Teachers understand how boys learn, and the curriculum draws on themes that resonate with boys: courage, adventure, honor and responsibility. Whether a boy is mastering poetry or physics, the aim is that he feels safe enough to try.

Gentleman, scholar, athlete

The school's Portrait of a Graduate turns those words into nine qualities. As a gentleman: integrity, courage and kindness. As a scholar: curiosity, creativity and understanding. As an athlete: resilience, consistency and teamwork. These are presented not as boxes to tick but as lifelong pursuits, and they shape the school day, from morning advisory with an adult mentor to afternoon athletics.

Where its graduates go

The class of 2026 had 138 graduates. They are attending 66 different colleges in 30 states, the District of Columbia, Scotland and England; 15 will compete in NCAA athletics, and 30 received National Merit recognition. Each boy works one-on-one with a dedicated college counselor from the spring of his sophomore year.

Who thrives there

MBA describes the boys who do well as curious, driven and eager to embrace challenges. In practice, that is a boy who likes to be pushed, enjoys competition without being crushed by it, and responds to adults who expect a lot of him. A boy who is still finding his footing can also flourish, if he is willing to take part; the school's emphasis on mentors and advisory is there for that.

The boys for whom it is a harder fit are those who would rather stay at the edge of things. The shadow visit is the best test: a boy who comes home energised by the day has answered much of the question.

Junior School and High School

MBA takes boys from seventh grade. Entering in the Junior School gives a boy six years on The Hill; entering in ninth grade, four. The application is the same in structure, with the ISEE at Middle or Upper Level depending on the grade.
