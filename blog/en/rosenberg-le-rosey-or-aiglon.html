Rosenberg, Le Rosey or Aiglon: which Swiss boarding school to choose

Institut auf dem Rosenberg, St. Gallen. Photo Rene Nueesch, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 3.0

Rosenberg, Le Rosey and Aiglon are three of Switzerland's most renowned boarding schools, and families aiming for one often look at the other two. They share high standards and an international outlook. But I know them through my students, and they do not suit the same child.

Three ways of educating

Rosenberg, in St. Gallen, is the tailor-made school: eight students per class on average, one adult for every two students, a timetable unique to each student, and almost every possible path, IGCSE, IB, A-levels, AP, German International Baccalaureate. Le Rosey, between Rolle and Gstaad, is the school of bilingualism and tradition: French and English, IB or French bac, Codes and a very structured community life. Aiglon, in Chesières, is the mountain school: English-speaking, IGCSE then IB, with expeditions, skiing and house life at the heart of the programme.

The right boarding school, then the right preparation

I help you choose the boarding school that truly fits your child, then prepare their application according to each school's own rules: fifteen of my students have been admitted to Le Rosey, and I have students at Rosenberg and Aiglon. My aim: that they get in where they will thrive.

Three different admissions

Rosenberg first reads an application, including a personal statement in any format, before inviting applicants to an interview and its exams, and makes direct or conditional offers. Le Rosey sets exams in maths, language and the mother tongue during a day on campus, decides weekly and applies strict nationality quotas. Aiglon uses an interview and online assessments, decides monthly, and says its assessments need no preparation. Each has its own calendar, and a family applying to all three must keep them going in parallel.

Rosenberg, Le Rosey or Aiglon for your child? Tell me about them: I will tell you which one fits.

For which child?

Rosenberg suits a child with an uneven profile or strong passions, who needs a path cut for them and strong individual attention. Le Rosey suits a child who wants to keep French, or aim for the bac, and thrives in a very international community with clear rules. Aiglon suits a child who wants to step fully into an English-speaking curriculum and grow through effort and the mountains. The prestige is comparable; it is the child who decides.
