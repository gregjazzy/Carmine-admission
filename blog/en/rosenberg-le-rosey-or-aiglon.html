Rosenberg, Le Rosey or Aiglon: which Swiss boarding school to choose

Institut auf dem Rosenberg, St. Gallen. Photo Rene Nueesch, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 3.0

Boarding schools November 2026 5 min read

Rosenberg, Le Rosey and Aiglon: three of the most sought-after boarding schools in Switzerland, and three schools the same family often looks at together. They share high standards and an open outlook, but they are not looking for the same child. I have helped students win places at Rosenberg and Aiglon, and fifteen at Le Rosey, and the best way to tell them apart is to start from the child, not the school.

Three children, three schools

Take three profiles I meet often. The first has very strong passions, an uneven school record, and gets bored when forced into a pace that is not their own. The second is French-speaking, wants to stay that way, does well within a clear framework and loves group life. The third dreams of fresh air, independence and an entirely English-language education. Each could succeed at all three schools. But each will flourish more in one of them.

Rosenberg, for the child who wants to build their own path

In St. Gallen, teaching is organised by ability rather than age, every student has a personal timetable, and close to 500 courses lead to IGCSEs, the IB, APs, A-levels or the school's own diploma. It is the school for the first profile: the child who needs their strengths followed, accelerated where they excel and supported elsewhere. From Grade 9, each student's choices are guided by an individual development plan, and a dedicated talent and enrichment programme takes learning out of the classroom. The campus only opens to families once the application is complete.

Le Rosey, for the child who wants to stay bilingual

Between the Rolle estate and the Gstaad winter campus, Le Rosey prepares students for the International or the French Baccalaureate, in French and English, in a fully boarding school with clear codes of conduct. It is the school for the second profile, and often the natural choice for a family that wants to keep a door open to university in France. Language matters more with age: up to twelve, a child can arrive speaking neither French nor English; from fifteen, they must master one of the school's two languages.

Aiglon, for the child who grows through effort

Above Villars, Aiglon leads to the International Baccalaureate in an English-language curriculum, with classes of twelve on average and a life shaped by the mountains. Its pathways, challenge, respect, responsibility, diversity and service, are not decorative: they are also used to select students. Around three in four students board, and the school also takes younger children, with about a hundred in its junior section. It is the school for the third profile, and for families who want to visit before applying: Aiglon offers an initial visit of about an hour and a half.

The criterion people forget: how you get in

The three admissions have almost nothing in common. Rosenberg first reads a file, with a personal statement free in form, then invites candidates to an interview and its exams. Le Rosey tests on campus, including in the mother tongue, and works with nationality quotas. Aiglon asks for two confidential recommendations and runs assessments of reasoning and writing. A child can be at ease in one format and much less so in another, and that too belongs in the choice. When several schools are on the list, the order of applications and visits has to be planned from the start.
