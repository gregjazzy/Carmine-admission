Our Preparation Program for Jeanine Manuel Entry Tests

EJM Admission January 2026 9 min read

Two numbers tell the entire story. The general admission rate for external candidates at Ecole Jeanine Manuel: 10% to 15%. The success rate for candidates prepared by Carmine Admission: 70%. This is not marketing. It is the documented outcome of a methodology built over years and refined across more than 1,600 students.

The question families should be asking is not whether preparation matters. It obviously does. The question is: what kind of preparation produces a fivefold increase in admission probability? The answer is not more hours of tutoring. It is a fundamentally different approach.

Educational engineering, not tutoring

Most families, when they decide to prepare their child for the EJM entry tests, look for a tutor. Someone to help with math. Someone to practice French essays. Someone to improve English. This is the wrong framework.

A tutor addresses symptoms. They help a student with the problem in front of them. What the EJM admission process demands is something entirely different: a structural realignment of the student's academic profile to match a specific institutional framework. This requires engineering, not instruction.

At Carmine Admission, we do not tutor. We engineer admissions. Every preparation program begins with a precise diagnostic, follows a phased methodology, and is calibrated to the specific requirements of the target institution. For EJM, this means understanding not just what the tests evaluate, but how the institution thinks, what it values, and where the inflection points are in the selection process.

For a detailed understanding of the test format itself, see our complete guide to EJM entry tests.

The five-phase approach

Phase 1: The Diagnostic

Every preparation begins here, and every preparation is different because every student is different. The diagnostic is a comprehensive evaluation that maps the student's current academic profile against the precise requirements of the EJM tests.

This is not a generic assessment. It is a multi-dimensional analysis that evaluates:

  • Mathematical proficiency and methodology: not just "can they solve equations" but "can they write a proof in the French format, referencing theorems by name, with every step justified?"
  • Academic French level: grammar accuracy, essay structure capability, vocabulary range, reading comprehension of literary and journalistic texts
  • Academic English level: written expression sophistication, analytical reading, argumentative writing
  • Test-taking competencies: time management under pressure, ability to complete a full paper within constraints, resilience when facing unfamiliar problems
  • Psychological readiness: confidence level, anxiety management, motivation alignment

The diagnostic produces a precise gap map. This map is the foundation of everything that follows. Without it, preparation is guesswork. With it, every hour of work is targeted and efficient.

Phase 2: Mise a Niveau (Leveling Up)

This is the intensive remediation phase. Based on the diagnostic, we build a personalized curriculum that addresses each identified gap in order of priority and impact.

For most international students, the priority stack looks like this:

  1. Mathematics methodology: learning to write French-style proofs, mastering notation conventions, building calculator-free computation fluency
  2. French academic writing: grammar remediation, essay structure training, vocabulary building
  3. Content gaps: specific topics in the French math curriculum that the student has not encountered in their previous system
  4. English academic precision: for students coming from francophone systems, building genuine academic English proficiency

The mise a niveau is not a passive process. It is demanding. Students work through structured exercises, receive detailed corrections, and are required to redo work until it meets the standard. This is not popular with everyone. But it is effective. The difference between "understanding a concept" and "being able to demonstrate it under exam conditions" is the difference between the student who is prepared and the one who is not.

Phase 3: Simulations

Once the foundational gaps are closed, the student enters the simulation phase. This is where preparation meets reality.

Simulations are full-length practice tests conducted under authentic exam conditions: timed, silent, no calculator, no dictionary, no interruptions. Each simulation is followed by a thorough debrief that analyzes not just what the student got wrong, but why they got it wrong and what the corrective action is.

We track performance metrics across simulations: accuracy by topic, time allocation efficiency, error patterns, and stress response indicators. This data-driven approach allows us to continuously refine the preparation. If a student consistently loses time on geometry proofs, we intensify that specific training. If their French essays are strong but their grammar exercises leak points, we redirect resources accordingly.

Students typically complete between 4 and 8 full simulations before the actual test. By the time they walk into the exam room, the format is familiar, the time pressure is normalized, and the anxiety is manageable.

Phase 4: Interview Preparation

Candidates who pass the written tests are invited to an interview. The interview at EJM is bilingual, probing, and decisive. It is where the school evaluates the human being behind the test scores.

Our interview preparation is not a coaching session where the student memorizes answers. It is a structured process that develops the student's ability to speak authentically about themselves, in both French and English, with depth and self-awareness.

We work on:

  • Structured storytelling: how to present a life experience as a coherent narrative that reveals character
  • Bilingual fluency: the ability to switch naturally between French and English without loss of precision or confidence
  • Self-reflection: helping the student articulate what they have learned from their experiences, not just what they have done
  • EJM-specific knowledge: understanding the school's mission, values, and culture well enough to explain, specifically, why this school

Phase 5: Application File Optimization

The application file is often treated as administrative paperwork. It is not. It is a strategic document that frames the candidacy before the tests and interview even take place.

We work with families to ensure that every element of the file -- transcripts, motivation letter, activity descriptions, recommendation letter guidance -- tells a coherent story. The story must align with what EJM values: bilingualism, adaptability, intellectual curiosity, and international mindedness.

The four qualities we build

Our preparation is not just about passing tests. It is about developing four qualities that EJM explicitly values and that the admissions process is designed to detect:

Adaptability

The ability to function effectively across systems, cultures, and languages. For students who have lived internationally, this quality already exists -- but it needs to be articulated. For students who have lived primarily in one country, it needs to be demonstrated through other means.

Excellence

Not perfection, but a visible commitment to high standards. This manifests in the precision of a math proof, the polish of an essay, the thoughtfulness of an interview answer. Excellence is a habit, and habits are built through practice.

Resilience

The ability to face difficulty without collapse. EJM is a demanding school. It admits students who can handle pressure. Our simulations are designed to build this resilience: the experience of struggling with a difficult problem, managing frustration, and continuing to perform.

Engagement

Genuine intellectual curiosity and active participation. EJM does not want passive recipients of education. It wants students who contribute, question, and create. Our preparation helps students identify and articulate the activities, interests, and passions that demonstrate engagement.
