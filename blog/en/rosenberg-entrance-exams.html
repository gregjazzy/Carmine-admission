Institut auf dem Rosenberg entrance exams: what you need to know

Institut auf dem Rosenberg, St. Gallen. Photo Rene Nueesch, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 3.0

Families aiming for Rosenberg think first about the fees, then about the personal statement. The entrance exam comes last to mind, and that is often where the application is lost. I have students at Rosenberg as at Le Rosey and Aiglon, and every year I prepare children for the tests of the leading Swiss boarding schools. What the school says about its exams fits in a few lines; what it does not say decides the outcome.

Where the exam comes in

The application starts online: reports from the last three years, a personal statement, a copy of the passport, any useful document, and a CHF 1,000 application fee. Only applicants who pass this first reading are invited to a personal interview and the entrance exams. The school then reviews the results in light of the whole profile, and replies with a direct or conditional offer.

So the exam does not sort applicants at random: it separates children whose file has already convinced. An average result therefore weighs more than families imagine.

What the school does not say

Rosenberg publishes neither the exact subjects of its exams, nor their length, nor the level expected at each age. Since teaching there is organised by level rather than age, in classes of eight, with students sometimes taking courses well above their age, the bar cannot be read from an ordinary curriculum. That is precisely what I know, having followed students at the school and prepared applicants for the leading Swiss boarding schools.

The gaps that cost a place

Spoken English that hides fragile writing

Most of Rosenberg's programmes, the IB, A-levels, AP, are taught in English. A child at ease orally, schooled in French or another language, can be far from the written English expected of a future student on these programmes. It is the most common gap.

Is your child's written English at the level? Tell me about them: I measure it against the leading boarding schools.

Solid maths, but not at the level

A student among the best at their school can struggle with tests designed to spot those who will follow accelerated teaching. Good grades at another school say little about the level expected in St. Gallen.

An exam prepared like a school test

Rosenberg reads results alongside the profile. A child who does reasonably well but shows nothing of what they would bring to the school, neither in their personal statement nor at interview, leaves their place to a more distinctive candidate.

Does your child have a strong file? Write to me: let's see what can still tip the balance.

When to start

Rosenberg accepts applications all year, with a season from October to June for the late-August start, and early files have a clear advantage. A complete file in the autumn means the child must be ready for the tests from the winter: closing a gap in written English or maths takes several months, not a few weeks.
