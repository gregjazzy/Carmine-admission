What NCSSM costs

NCSSM is free. The sentence is short, and it is accurate. For its Residential program, in Durham or Morganton, its Online program and Summer Ventures, there is no application fee and no charge for tuition, meals, room and board, or textbooks. The state of North Carolina funds the school for its residents, as part of the University of North Carolina System.

What remains for a Residential family is the everyday cost of a teenager living away from home: travel to and from campus, personal spending, clothing and the like.

Financial assistance on top

For families enrolled in the Residential or Online program, NCSSM offers four tiers of assistance, assigned from the family's documents:

  • Access to all benefits, with a stipend.
  • Access to all benefits, without a stipend.
  • Reduced benefits.
  • Waived benefits only, for example for families in North Carolina's Medicaid program, giving waivers for standardised test and some college application fees.

Eligibility is shown through documents such as certification of TANF, SNAP or FDPIR benefits, or proof of all household income for the past three months. Tax returns are not accepted for this purpose. Families apply through their Blackbaud account, and a determination usually takes seven to ten business days. Summer Ventures students are not eligible.

Free, but selective

NCSSM costs nothing, which makes its selection the real barrier. I prepare applicants for the essays and the math assessment that decide it.

After NCSSM

The value continues beyond graduation. Residential graduates who meet the requirements are guaranteed admission to UNC System universities to which they apply, and can apply for a UNC tuition grant covering in-state tuition, though not room, board or fees.

Why "free" changes the competition

Because cost is not a barrier, NCSSM draws strong applicants from every corner of the state, and its graduates come from all thirteen congressional districts. The competition is entirely academic and personal: grades in 9th and 10th grade, essays, evaluations, initiative, and the math assessment for Residential applicants.
