Phillips Exeter: the SSAT or ISEE, what you need to know

The Academy Building, the historic heart of Phillips Exeter. Photo Quintin Soloviev, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 4.0

Exeter never gave up on testing. Every applicant sends a score, and for the main entry, grade 9, the Upper Level SSAT or ISEE is required. I have students at Phillips Exeter, and every year I support around fifteen families towards the most selective schools and boarding schools in the world.

What the school says

For grade 9, the Upper Level SSAT or ISEE is required. For grades 10 and 11, one test among the SSAT, ISEE, PSAT, SAT, PreACT or ACT. The test must have been taken no earlier than one year before the January 15 deadline, and for the SSAT or ISEE, results must be sent by the testing company itself. The school adds a point families read too quickly: these tests include a writing sample, and the admissions readers look at it very closely, as the most accurate measure of how the child writes.

What the school does not say

Exeter publishes no minimum or average score. The figures circulating on forums do not come from the school. What matters is the gap between the score and the rest of the file: a weak score casts doubt on the reports, a very high score does not make up for lukewarm recommendations.

Why good students fail

The writing sample

Many families prepare the scored sections and neglect the writing sample, unscored by the testing company but read by Exeter. That is often where a file loses credibility.

Does your child write English at the level of an American student their age? Tell me about them: I measure it before the test.

Vocabulary

The SSAT verbal sections assume a very wide English vocabulary. A non-native speaker, even a strong student, quickly loses points there.

The window

A test taken too early, more than a year before January 15, does not count. A test taken too late leaves little room for a second attempt.

When to start

For a January application, preparation starts no later than the previous spring, with a first sitting in the autumn and, if needed, a second before December.
