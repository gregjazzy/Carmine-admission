The Exeter entrance test: SSAT, ISEE or SAT

The Academy Building, the historic heart of Phillips Exeter. Photo Quintin Soloviev, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 4.0

Many American boarding schools have made testing optional. Exeter has not: every applicant sends a score, at every grade. The test is one part of a file that also includes an interview, two essays, four letters and two years of reports, but it is the only part that compares the child directly with thousands of others, and it is the one families most often prepare too late or for the wrong exam.

I prepare candidates for these tests every year, alongside the rest of the Exeter file. Here is what applies and how to choose.

One test, depending on the grade

Entry gradeAccepted tests
9SSAT or ISEE, Upper Level
10 and 11SSAT, ISEE, PSAT/NMSQT, SAT, PreACT or ACT

For ninth grade, the SSAT or ISEE result must be sent directly by the testing organisation using Exeter's code; a score forwarded by the family is refused. For the older entries, a PDF of the official score report or the score on the school transcript is also accepted.

SSAT or ISEE: two different tests

Both are accepted for ninth grade and neither is preferred. They are not the same exercise, though, and the choice should follow the child's profile.

SSAT Upper LevelISEE Upper Level
Verbal60 questions in 30 minutes: 30 synonyms, 30 analogies40 questions in 20 minutes
Mathematics50 questions in two 30-minute sections, no calculatorQuantitative Reasoning (37 questions, 35 minutes) and Mathematics Achievement (47 questions, 40 minutes)
Reading40 questions in 40 minutes36 questions in 35 minutes
Writing25 minutes, unscored, sent to schools30 minutes, unscored, sent to schools
Total3 hours 10, with an unscored experimental section2 hours 40, plus two breaks

The SSAT verbal section rests heavily on vocabulary, through synonyms and analogies, which makes it hard for a child whose English was learned at school. The ISEE weighs mathematics more, with two separate sections, and asks for speed: forty verbal questions in twenty minutes leaves thirty seconds each. A strong mathematician still building vocabulary often does better on the ISEE; a fast reader with wide vocabulary often prefers the SSAT. A diagnostic on both settles the question better than any rule.

The test, prepared at Exeter's level

The children who get into Exeter score in the upper reaches of these tests. I identify the right exam for the child, then prepare each section at that level, in the order that brings the most points.

The window and the retakes

A score counts only if the test was taken no earlier than one year before the January 15 deadline. Inside that window, the two tests allow retakes differently. The SSAT counts its year from August 1 to July 31 and allows up to six standard paper sittings, plus one Flex, at Middle and Upper Level; the computer-based version in a Prometric centre is currently limited to one sitting a year. The ISEE allows one sitting per season (autumn from August to November, winter from December to March, spring and summer from April to July), so three a year at most.

In practice, a first sitting in early autumn leaves time for a second before the deadline, provided the result reaches Exeter by January 15. A child who sits for the first time in January has no margin left.

Testing outside the United States

The SSAT at Home exists only for students physically in the United States or Canada on test day. Elsewhere, the SSAT is taken on paper in an international centre or on computer in a Prometric centre, at $339 for the Upper Level. The ISEE is offered online or on paper depending on location. For families abroad, the real constraint is the calendar of the nearest centre, which is worth checking before the summer.

The writing sample

Neither test scores the essay, but both send it to the schools, and Exeter's readers look at it very closely: it is written alone, in a set time, and is treated as the truest picture of how the child writes. The SSAT offers a choice between a personal and a general prompt. Preparing for this half hour is part of preparing for the test, and it is also the yardstick against which the application essays will be read.

For older applicants: the SAT or the ACT

A student applying as a one-year senior or a postgraduate cannot use the SSAT or the ISEE and needs a PSAT, SAT, PreACT or ACT score. These are tests built for university admission, with high-school mathematics, and they call for a different preparation. For tenth and eleventh grade, all six tests are open, and the right choice depends on the child's age and on which test they will need again later.
