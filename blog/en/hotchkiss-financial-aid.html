Hotchkiss financial aid: 100 percent of need, no loans, no merit

The main building of The Hotchkiss School, Lakeville, Connecticut. Photo Adrien Delessert, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 2.5

US boarding schools November 2026 6 min read

Hotchkiss's financial aid policy can be summed up in three commitments: it meets 100 percent of a family's demonstrated need, it does so entirely with grants rather than loans, and it awards no merit scholarships. More than $13.9 million goes to aid each year. A third of students receive it, 205 in all, including 54 newly enrolled students; more than a quarter of aided students attend tuition-free.

How need is calculated

Hotchkiss looks at a family's income and assets to determine what it can contribute to educational expenses, taking into account family size, the number of children in tuition-charging schools, and living costs. When parents are divorced or separated, both the custodial and non-custodial parent must apply, and a stepparent's income and assets are considered, with regard to their obligations to their own children. A single Clarity application can cover several children.

The place comes first

Aid at Hotchkiss is decided after admission. We focus on what earns the place: testing, essays and interview, prepared at the level the school expects.

Named scholarships, without a separate application

Every family that applies for aid is automatically considered for Hotchkiss's named, need-based scholarships, assigned by the Financial Aid Committee before notifications on March 10. Among them: the Goodnow Family Scholarship; the Christopher Redlich Scholarship, for high-achieving low-income and first-generation students, which can cover full tuition and adds stipends for summer travel and internships, plus mentoring; and the Louis G. Dreyfus Scholarship, for children or grandchildren of US Foreign Service Officers.

Applying for aid from outside the United States? Ask me what to prepare.

International families

International families apply through the same Clarity platform, with documents from their own country: tax records where they exist, employer earnings statements, and three consecutive months of recent bank statements, with specific documents for Canadian filers. Hotchkiss cannot offer international applicants a waiver of the aid application fee. The process is the same, but gathering documents from abroad takes longer, so international families should start in the fall.

Deadlines

New applicants: January 15, the same day as the admission application. Returning students: December 1. Hotchkiss is clear that meeting the deadline is imperative and that funds are not reserved for late applicants; a family that misses it should still apply, as limited funds may remain.

What aid does not do

Because aid is purely need-based, it never rewards achievement and never compensates for a weak application. The decision on admission comes first. The work that matters for a family is therefore the same whatever its means: testing, writing and interview.

How I help

I prepare the student for every part of the application Hotchkiss weighs equally, and I help families, especially those abroad, plan the aid paperwork alongside the admission calendar so that both are complete by January 15.
