Applying to NMH from abroad

Northfield Mount Hermon's admission process is the same for every applicant: one of two platforms, two current teachers, a required interview, a January 15 deadline. For families outside the United States, three things change: a required English test with demanding minimums, a different interview route for applicants from two countries, and a bill and aid process that include lines American families do not see.

I work every year with families abroad applying to NMH and other New England boarding schools. Here is what to plan for.

The English test: required, and set high

Applicants whose first language is not English must submit a TOEFL iBT or TOEFL Home Edition, an IELTS Academic or a Duolingo English Test score, sent directly by the testing organization. EIKEN results are also considered. The requirement can be waived for a student who has completed the last two academic years at a school where English is the main language of instruction.

TestRecommended minimum
TOEFL iBT or Home Edition100 under the scale used before January 21, 2026, or 5.5 on the new scale
IELTS Academic7 overall
Duolingo English Test125

These are high bars for a thirteen- or fourteen-year-old who learned English at school. A TOEFL of 100 assumes a student who can follow a seventy-minute discussion class and write at length in English from the first week. For many international applicants, this test, not the SSAT, is the real preparation of the year, and it should start well before the autumn.

The interview from abroad

International applicants who cannot travel can interview by video with NMH's admission office, booked by phone or email. Applicants from mainland China and South Korea whose first language is not English must instead go through InitialView, which records a live, unscripted interview and sends it to NMH with a writing sample. The appointment must be made with InitialView at least two weeks before January 15. Because the format is recorded, and paired with writing done on the spot, it shows a child's real English more plainly than any prepared document.

Living outside the United States and thinking of NMH? Tell me about your child: the English test is the first date to set.

The international bill

International students pay the same $79,382 for tuition, room and board as American boarders, with $2,675 in fees instead of $1,625. The difference covers the school's health insurance, which F-1 students must take, and an international student fee for maintaining the Form I-20 and SEVIS records and other services. Textbooks, about $500, a required laptop and travel come on top, as does tuition refund insurance unless the year is paid in full by July 1. For a family paying from another currency, that July 1 date is also the moment to plan the transfer.

Aid is open to international families

NMH offers need-based scholarships to domestic and international students alike and commits to meeting the full demonstrated need of every admitted student. International families apply through Clarity by January 15, like everyone else, with income and tax documents from every parent or guardian. The award arrives within twenty-four hours of the March 10 decision.

A postgraduate year as a bridge

NMH presents its postgraduate year partly as a path for international students who want to strengthen their English and experience an American school before applying to American universities. For a student finishing secondary school abroad, it is a year of senior-level courses, a dedicated postgraduate advisor and college counseling, inside the senior class.
