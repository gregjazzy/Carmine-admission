The Choate admission timeline

Book a conversation

Choate's calendar has three dates every family knows: January 15, February 1 and March 10. The difficulty lies elsewhere. Each official date is the end of a piece of work that has to begin months earlier, and the one appointment that cannot be repeated, the interview, sits in the middle of the season with no fixed day. Here is the year as it actually unfolds.

The inquiry form comes first, and it matters

Nothing starts until the inquiry form is in. It puts the family on the mailing list and, more importantly, it is what triggers the email invitation to book an interview. A family that skips it and goes straight to Gateway loses weeks, and the interview season does not wait.

The calendar, held for you

I build the year with the family, from the first assessment to March 10, and prepare the child for each step in the right order, so that nothing is decided in a hurry.

The interview has no date, which is the problem

Choate interviews between September and February, online only, and each applicant gets one appointment. Because there is no fixed date, families tend to book either too early, in September, before the child has practised, or too late, in the crowded weeks before the deadline. The best moment is the one when the child is ready, and that moment has to be planned backwards: a few weeks of practice, then the booking.

January 15 and February 1 are not the same deadline

An application submitted on Gateway by January 15 receives full consideration. The supporting pieces, recommendations, transcripts and scores, may arrive later: if everything is in by February 1, the decision comes on March 10. Current-year grades can be grades in progress, as long as an indication of this year's performance reaches Choate by February 1.

That two-week cushion is useful, and it is also a trap. The family controls the January 15 pieces. It does not control a teacher who has not sent a recommendation, or a school abroad that is slow to produce a transcript. Those requests should go out in the fall, not in January.

After the deadline

A family can still apply after January 15, as long as Gateway is open. If every piece arrives by February 1, Choate will try to give an answer on March 10; after that, files are read as time allows. Applications that arrive after March 10 rarely lead to an offer, and Gateway closes for the coming fall on June 15. A late file is possible, then, but it competes for whatever room is left.

How I plan the year with a family

I have students at Choate, and I work every year with around fifteen families applying to the most selective boarding schools; that experience is mostly about time. I begin with an assessment that tells us whether a test is worth preparing, then set the order of the work: recommendations requested in the fall, interview practised before it is booked, essays drafted early enough to be rewritten. Families who apply to several schools at once get a single calendar that holds them all.

March 10 and after

Admission and financial aid decisions are posted on the Choate portal on the same day. Exeter, among others, also answers on March 10. A family that has applied to several schools may be weighing more than one offer at once, so the comparison should be prepared in February, not improvised in March.
