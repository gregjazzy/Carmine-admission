Le Rosey: the student profile it looks for

The Château du Rosey, Rolle. Photo Eric Bajart, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0

Boarding schools November 2026 5 min read

Greg MitteletteFounder of Carmine Admission. 1,100 students supported over 14 years, with admissions to Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, EPFL and Bocconi.

Book a conversation

Many families think Le Rosey picks the children of the most prominent families. The school says the opposite, with rare frankness: in its view, the worst reason for wanting to come is to show off the parents' wealth and prestige. What it looks for is of another order, and that is what decides, once the academic level is there.

The portrait the school draws

Le Rosey says it favours active, curious and ambitious applicants. It wants children who are keen to share their lives with students from seventy countries, who will respond well to academic demands, and who will aim to play a dynamic role in society, starting with their school. Children, too, who genuinely want to improve and are ready for the workload that requires.

Academic level, language and suitability for boarding remain the decisive criteria. But the school insists: talent in sport or the arts, or involvement in school life, is a crucial part of an application, and it encourages families to include certificates and evidence, from music exams to sporting competitions.

Showing who your child really is

I prepare your child for Le Rosey's exams and help them show, in their file and during the campus day, what the school looks for: energy, curiosity, commitment. My aim: that the committee sees a future Roséen in them.

What the school turns away

It says this just as clearly. Le Rosey is not suited to children with a severe learning disability, although it supports minor or temporary difficulties. It rejects violent, antisocial or racist attitudes, and any evidence of drug or alcohol use. And it turns away spoiled children used to always getting their way: life and discipline there are demanding, and the change would be too hard. The school also expects the family to share the values of its Codes, based on respect for others and their differences.

Does your child have the profile of a future Roséen? Tell me about them: I will tell you what will work in their favour, and what to work on.

Why the profile shows on the campus day

A file can say many things. A whole day on campus, with tests, an interview and meeting students, shows more. It is there, more than anywhere, that the difference shows between a curious, open child and a child waiting to be looked after. Preparing an applicant well is not teaching them a role: it is helping them be, on that day, the best version of themselves.
