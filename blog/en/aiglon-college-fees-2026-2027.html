Aiglon College: fees 2026-2027

How much does Aiglon cost? The question comes up in almost every first conversation I have with families, and the answer is easy to find. What is less understood is what that price buys, and above all what it does not. I have helped students win places at Aiglon and Rosenberg, and fifteen at Le Rosey, and none of them got in because their parents could pay.

The 2026-2027 figures

Aiglon publishes its fees every January. For 2026-2027, boarding costs CHF 168,000 a year in Years 12 and 13, the International Baccalaureate years, CHF 156,800 in Years 10 and 11, CHF 151,200 in Year 9 and CHF 119,800 in Years 7 and 8. For younger children, boarding, available from Year 5, costs CHF 100,800. Day places exist at every age, from CHF 49,700 in the junior years to CHF 99,400 in the final two years.

One-off charges come on top at entry: a non-refundable CHF 3,000 registration fee when the application is submitted, a CHF 15,000 deposit to secure the place, returned only after the student leaves, CHF 4,000 for the school's capital investment, CHF 2,000 for alumni programmes, and CHF 4,100 to 5,400 for uniform and clothing. Siblings receive a reduction of 5 to 15% depending on how many children are enrolled.

Two details often surprise families. The CHF 15,000 deposit is only returned once the student has left and the account is settled, never before the September of the following academic year, and without interest. And for boarders in Years 5 to 8, pocket money of CHF 1,360 a year is charged in advance with the fees. VAT is already included in the amounts. None of this changes a family's decision, but it is better known before signing.

What the price does not pay for

Being able to pay these fees opens no doors. Aiglon selects students on their ability to follow a demanding curriculum, to thrive in the mountains and to find their place in an international community, measured against five pathways: challenge, respect, responsibility, diversity and service. Many families who apply can afford the fees, which is exactly why the price separates no one. What separates candidates are the assessments, the teachers' recommendations and the interview.

The real cost of a failed application

The CHF 3,000 registration fee is not refunded. But the real cost of a poorly prepared application lies elsewhere: a lost year, a disappointed child, a waiting list that drags on, or a fallback school chosen in a hurry. Families who think about this before submitting almost never live through that scenario. Decisions are made monthly, so a well-prepared file is answered quickly, and so is a weak one.

Comparing with other boarding schools

At this level, comparing prices between the leading Swiss boarding schools tells you little. Differences exist, but a family weighing Aiglon, Le Rosey and Rosenberg is first choosing a language, a curriculum and a way of life. Aiglon is English-speaking and leads to the IB through the IGCSE, in a mountain setting where effort and the outdoors are part of daily life. I help families make that choice around the child, then build an application that holds up, at Aiglon or elsewhere. To place the schools side by side, see Aiglon or Le Rosey.
