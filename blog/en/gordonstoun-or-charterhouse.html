UK boarding schools

Gordonstoun or Charterhouse

Charterhouse and Gordonstoun sometimes end up on the same list, usually because a family wants a co-educational British boarding school with a strong character and is willing to look beyond London. On price they are almost level: £21,015 a term to board at Charterhouse in 2026-27, £21,365 at Gordonstoun. On almost everything else, they differ. Five questions usually settle the choice.

1. How does each school select?

Charterhouse runs a structured, competitive process. On its recommended route, a child registers by 30 September of Year 6, sits the ISEB Common Pre-Test that autumn with a reference from their school, and, if selected, attends an assessment day of group activities, interviews and creative writing in the spring. Offers come by the end of March, two years before entry, and remain conditional on a good reference in Year 8.

Gordonstoun has no single entrance exam. Every applicant has an interview, the school reads the report and a referee, and it may request assessments in maths, English, reasoning or English as an additional language. The file then goes to the Principal's Office. A child with a strong test profile has an advantage at Charterhouse; a child who shines in conversation more than on paper is read more fully at Gordonstoun.

2. When does the decision happen?

At Charterhouse, the decisive moment for 13+ entry is Year 6, which suits families who plan early and children who are ready early. Gordonstoun's process is less tied to a single season, and its Year 9 scholarship weekend takes place at the end of August before Year 8. A family that discovers boarding late in Year 6 or Year 7 still has a realistic path to Gordonstoun.

3. What does a week look like?

Gordonstoun is a full boarding school seven days a week, with Saturday lessons, sail training for every pupil, a rescue or community service, and expeditions in the hills. Charterhouse, in Surrey, is also a boarding school with a rich life beyond lessons, but within easy reach of London, and without the sea at the centre of the timetable. A child who wants the outdoors at the heart of school life leans one way; a child who wants to stay close to home and city leans the other.

4. Which qualifications?

At 16, Charterhouse offers both A levels and the International Baccalaureate Diploma. Gordonstoun offers A levels, with more than twenty subjects, and BTECs in areas such as medical sciences and psychology. For a family set on the IB, the question answers itself.

5. Who is the school built for?

Charterhouse, founded in 1611, is academically selective and looks for children who can become ambitious, independent learners, with a "try everything" attitude. Gordonstoun, founded by Kurt Hahn in 1934, takes its motto, "there is more in you", literally: it admits a broad range of abilities and pushes each child through challenge, responsibility and service. More than a third of its pupils receive fee assistance, and a third come from overseas.

Applying to both

The two processes can run side by side, since they hardly overlap. For 13+ entry, Charterhouse comes first in the calendar, with registration by 30 September of Year 6; the Gordonstoun interview and any assessments can follow. Preparing for the Pre-Test also strengthens the maths, English and reasoning Gordonstoun may assess.
