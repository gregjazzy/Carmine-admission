Entering GSIS in Year 12

Year 12 is the last entry point into the English International Stream at German Swiss International School, and the only one where the assessment is an interview rather than tests. It opens the two-year IB Diploma Programme, which students complete in Year 13 before university. For a student moving to Hong Kong at sixteen, or changing school after IGCSE, it is a real door, but a narrow and demanding one.

I prepare Year 12 applicants every year. Here is what the entry involves.

What the school asks

ComponentDetail
InterviewWith the school, as the assessment for Year 12
TranscriptsRequested from the applicant's current school
Recommendation formCompleted by the current school
PriorityApplies as at every entry: passports and debentures, siblings, staff, alumni, then date of application

As in all EIS entries, English is expected at the level of a native speaker of the same age. With no written test, the interview, the transcripts and the school's recommendation carry the whole decision.

Joining the IB Diploma

In Years 12 and 13, GSIS students take the IB Diploma Programme: subjects balanced across disciplines, plus the core of Theory of Knowledge, Creativity, Activity and Service, and the Extended Essay. Students already at GSIS arrive from the school's IGCSE years, taught mostly to Cambridge International syllabuses with some Pearson Edexcel subjects. A newcomer joins them at the start of a two-year programme, but without their two years of shared preparation.

That shapes the interview. A strong applicant can explain which IB subjects and levels they intend to take, why, and how their previous studies prepare them; can talk about something they have read or investigated in depth, which is what the Extended Essay will ask; and can say what they would bring to the school beyond grades.

Records from another system

For a student coming from a national system, the transcripts must make sense to readers used to IGCSE grades. A short, factual explanation of the grading scale and of the courses taken helps. The recommendation form from the current school matters too: it should come from teachers who know the student's academic work well and can speak to how they will cope with the IB.
